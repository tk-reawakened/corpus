# Brayan

by Lisha on May 21, 2006
Okay, so I've been thinking the past couple of days about doing an article for the library on writing. If I did, would anyone read it? I don't know if I'm good enough a writer to be giving other people adivce (heck, I don't even know if I know HOW to give other people advice), but I'd be willing to give it a shot if people would read it. *shrug* On another note, this is THE longest entry yet. Just short of seven pages. Whew. Took forever. I am really happy with it, though it gets kinda soppy near the end. Oh well. We finally get to meet Brayan! He's not like you probably imagined him, that's for sure.... ***** May 21 Its been a long day. A very, very long day. Theres no doubting that, unfortunately. I rose early in the morning from a sleep that only barely happened. When I opened my eyes, the first words I thought were, The more frightening part was, I didnt know what I was going to do. I thought frantically about the possibilities as I got up and dressed. Quickly in my mind I changed before he comes to if he comes. I didnt want to be so final about the situation. I wanted there to be another choice. Grabbing my bag, I left the room and hurried down the stairs. I was surprised to accidentally run into - literally - Daehn. He reached out and grabbed my shoulder before I actually hit him. Woah, careful. Whats the rush? I pulled my bag, which had slipped, onto my shoulder again anxiously. Im just going to get some more magic, I said. Its running low, after yesterday. A little early, dont you think? he said, laughing. He turned his head and peered out the window. Well, not that early, I suppose. They should just be opening all of the shops now, I think. Judging from what Ive seen of how things work around here. Then he noticed my uneasy, nervous expression. Whats wrong? At first I was going to lie to him and say that nothing was wrong, I was absolutely fine, thank you very much. Then I figured that he would find out soon enough anyway, and besides, he deserved to know. Hanev wrote to his brother, Brayan, I confessed. They dont exactly get along. At all. Im afraid that I didnt have to finish. Daehn nodded slowly, understanding. I see. Why dont they get along? I hesitated. Daehn maybe had deserved to know about Brayan, but Keyas story wasnt mine enough to give. If Hanev wanted to talk about it, then he could. But I wouldnt, until then. Im not sure, I lied. I think that there may have been a fight. He knew I was lying. I could tell by the way he looked at me. But, thankfully, he said nothing more on the subject. Instead he nodded, then offered, I can go to buy the magic, if youd rather stay here. I shook my head and smiled. No, its fine. Youve done more than enough. And he really has. While Im upstairs working on spells or talking to Hanev, he buys what needs to be bought, prepares what needs to be prepared, does everything else that needs to be done, and then at the end of it all I still can only barely find the time to talk to him. He looked at me in a way that was something close to sympathetic. Are you sure? Positive, I answered with a smile. He nodded. Alright then. In that case, Ill see you later. You too. I went to the door and opened it, then turned around and said, I wont be too long, hopefully. Then I stepped outside the door and shut it behind me. The fresh air came as a shock. I really have been cooped up in that house the past few days, havent I? It was nice, though, after the initial shock. It was relaxing. It wasnt a long walk to the marketplace - actually, I think that more than half of it was going down the long pathway from Hanevs house. After I had walked down that, I was at the street, and suddenly things were so much louder and more crowded and more hurried. People hustled and bustled, running this way or that way, moving packages from here to there. I gaped. Im not used to any of this, and it isnt just because I hadnt been out into town much for quite awhile, though Im sure that had something to do with it. My town is small - very small - and this kind of activity is something I had only seen before once or twice in my life. , I thought to myself, And I stepped out onto the street, and became part of the crowd. I found that I got used to it quickly, though. Within the next few minutes I barely noticed the people all around me. Unfortunately, I was wrong when I thought that I would be able to easily find what I was looking for. I went from shop to shop, stand to stand, and every time I asked if they sold magic, the shopkeeper would say, Fraid not, dear. or, Im sorry, we dont carry that, but we have or even, No. Now if you arent going to buy something, get out of here.. Sigh. So frustrating. I was beginning to get tense, and wondered what time it was. I needed to get back before Brayan came As I began to get near the end of the street, I found a weaponry shop. I thought, The shop was small, but it was crammed with almost any type of weapon a person can think of. Some looked to be built for their beauty - others not so much. I breathed a sigh of relief when I saw several jars of magic lined up on the very edge of a shelf. I went over and took three of them, thankful to finally have found some. Then, as I made my way over to the desk, a knife caught my eye. I stopped to look at it. It was too long to be called a dagger, but too short to be called a sword. It was simple, with a plan wooden handle and an equally plain - but sharp - blade. It was halfway into an unadorned sheath, left partially out so the blade could be seen. I found myself closing my hand around the handle of the knife and taking it with me to the desk to buy. It was a spontaneous reaction, something I wasnt sure I meant to do. It was probably because I was afraid. I was afraid of what would happen if I couldnt defend myself - and Hanev - against Brayan. That was probably why, now that I think back to it. The shopkeeper gave sly-looking smile when he saw my purchase. Whats a girl like you going to do with things like these? he asked, laughing. I didnt answer him, but only paid him, then took the items and put them into my bag. The knife ended up on the side, and I could feel its shape against my leg. Feeling more than a little uncomfortable, I left the store. But just as I walked out of the door, a dog ran in front of it. It was going to fast - and it was a small dog, too - that I didnt see it. Andwell to put things simply, I tripped over it. Before I even realized I fell, I was on the ground. A young girl hovered by me, saying rapidly, Oh, Im so sry. Thas my puppy. twunt hap gen, miss. Hes ju so lil an all I smiled at her and said, No, its alright. as I put a hand to the place where I bumped my head on something - not sure what it was, but it had hurt, thats all I know. Are you alright? I heard another voice and looked up to see a dark-haired man, probably in his late twenties, looking down at me. The blood drained from my face as I recognized him. Brayan. Oh God. I quickly looked back down at the ground. Im fine, thank you, I said, as quietly as I could. My palms were sweating. Out of all the people that could have stopped, why on earth did it have to be him? What kind of an unlikely coincidence is that? Thats practically , for Gods sake! You dont have to act so shy, now, he said. Im not going to hurt you or anything. Dont look so scared. I didnt look at him. I didnt move. Soo he asked, his voice on the border of laughter. Do you intend to get off the ground anytime soon, or should I get a chair and wait for you? I said a short prayer in my head and stood, still looking down. You dont have to wait for me, I mumbled. But thank you anyway. He gave a short laugh. Well, if I intend to escort this fair maiden to wherever she is going at the moment, I will have to wait for her, wont I? I practically cringed in my place, but in my mind I was beginning to wonder if this really was Brayan, or if I had just mistaken someone else for him. This man acted nothing like Hanev made him sound like. , I thought as I quickly looked at his face and then looking down again, I dont need any escort, I said, rather coldly. But again, thank you for offering. Please, I insist, he said. Which way are you going? Before I even thought about it, I pointed in the direction of Hanevs house. As soon as I did it I screamed silently at myself, Then I realized that he would know, anyway, if he saw me at that house, and my hopelessness of the situation deepened. Really. I just so happen to be going that way too. Then, jokingly, I suppose you have no choice now, do you? You, I said frigidly as we both began to walk forward, are a horrible flirt. Now that is simply being prejudice, he said, sounding mock-appalled. You barely even know me. I dont need to know you to understand that, I muttered. Then, trying to force myself to be polite, I asked casually, Where are you going today? His joking expression disappeared, replaced by one that was unreadable and stoic. Im going to visit my brother. Havent seen him in oh, I suppose it must be around nine or ten years, Im guessing? So it Brayan. You dont sound too fond of him, I said cautiously, choosing my words with care. His expression darkened into one of anger and hatred. Weve had our fights. Ill just leave it at that, for both our sakes. Then his face turned joking again, and his voice again became light. And you? Where might you be going? I winced. How was I going to answer that? I knew he would find out soon enough anyway, but still Im staying with a friend for a few days. He isnt in such a healthy state, so I didnt want to leave him alone. Then I couldnt help adding, pointedly, His family is kind of broken, if I make any sense. His eyebrows lifted. I wondered if I should have added that last statement. But if he had suspected anything, he didnt mention it. Instead he only said, You make perfect sense. We paused to let a small family cross the street. Id think that your friends house would be somewhere around here? he said, nodding to the cluster of houses at the end of the street. No, I said, forcing myself to be calm. Its farther down. This time, instead of going up, his eyebrows went down and his brow furrowed. He was beginning to realize. I could tell. The family passed, and we walked on. You know, I said with careful nonchalance. You havent even introduced yourself yet. Thats very impolite. Oh. He smiled. Im sorry about that. He stopped and gave a small bow. Brayan Farlione, at your service, milady. There. It was confirmed. My heart sunk, even though I really knew it through the entire walk. We walked on. You havent introduced yourself either, he said, also sounding carefully nonchalant. Im Saphyra, I said shortly. And you dont have a last name? I gave him a forced smile. No. I dont. Really. Then he cleared his throat and said with hinting in his voice, Just tell me when we get close to your friends house. I will, I answered lightly. And then, under my breath, Because Im sure youll have no idea where it is. He cleared his throat again. Second time in two minutes. Well wouldnt it be somewhere around here? We were at the very last of the houses before the path to Hanevs house began. I shook my head, beginning to get truly nervous. Still, I had no other option. No. Its still farther down. He grimaced. Now, Saphyra he said, and I could tell that it was finally coming. And it did. Your friend wouldnt happened to be named Hanev, would he? I tried to look surprised. Yes, actually. How- You know what? he snapped, the joking gone from his voice. You are a horrible actress. We were walking on the path now, and almost halfway up it. You knew about me, didnt you? he asked. Was that why you were so afraid? Why, what has that brother of mine told you? I didnt answer. All I could think was, Saphyra! he shouted, furious. He hasnt told me anything, I said firmly. I didnt even know you were his brother. Only when I heard your last name. I didnt even know he a brother. Youre a horrible liar, too, he retorted sharply. Then he muttered, What I wont to him! My stomach was in knots, my palms were sweating, and I was trembling. I was so afraid for Hanev. I thought, mind replaying those lines over and over again. We didnt speak the rest of the way to the house. When we got there, Brayan grabbed the handle of the door and flung it open. The sound of the door hitting the wall echoed through the house, and Daehn came practically running down the stairs. He slowed to a stop when he saw us. Who are you? Brayan demanded when he saw him. No, never mind that. Just show me where Hanev is. Daehn shot me an astonished, questioning look, and I nodded slowly. I didnt want to. But I had to, for all of our safety. Brayan was enraged. To say the least. Daehn looked torn. Alright, he said hesitantly, then turned and began to walk up the stairs. Brayan followed, his hand wrapped tightly round my wrist, forcing me to follow too. Brayan marched into Hanevs room with his jaw clenched and his face darkened. When he saw Hanev, he stopped and glared. Then he pushed me away from him with such force that I nearly ran into the wall. What have you told her about me?! he bellowed. You disgusting son of a bitch! Hanevs wide eyes darted from me to Brayan, where they stayed. He was afraid. It could be seen clearly on his face. He was very afraid. I didnt tell her anything, he said with amazing calmness, so quietly and evenly that it could even partially disguise his fear. Partially. She has nothing to do with this. You, Brayan hissed, are so, so, for asking me to come here. Asking me! Youre more brainless than I remember you. He advanced slowly. As he did, my hand slowly, slowly crept toward the bag. The bag holding the knife. Why would you do that, Hanev, you idiot? Why? Ill kill you. Right now. Im already dying, Hanev answered simply, but with fear audible in his voice. I wanted to see you again before I do. Liar. Brayans voice shook. You ! Hanev didnt answer. He was completely silent. You realize that its all your fault, dont you? Brayan went on, voice still rising. Dont you realize that? Did you think that I would magically forgive you over the years? Is that why you wrote to me? Is that it? He advanced again, and this time he was right beside Hanev. He grabbed Hanevs shoulder - tightly - lifting him a few inches from the bed. Hanev clenched his teeth and winced. You were . Its fault that were like this. Its fault that were the only ones left. Its fault. ! Did you think I would just you for that?! I caught sight of Daehn slowly moving closer to Brayan, looking as if he was going to try to tackle him. I vigorously shook my head and mouthed, No! To my relief, he saw me. He looked hesitant, but he recoiled. It was then that I swept my hand into the bag and drew out the knife, unsheathing it as I pulled it out. Stop! I screamed, holding the knife aimed at Brayan. I slowly moved closer. Stop, I said again, more quietly. Brayan glared at me. You really are going to protect him? he asked in a breathless, shaky voice, still holding onto Hanevs shoulder. You really are going to protect ? Hes a murderer, you know. Did he tell you ? Yes, I answered, my own voice shaking just a little. I moved a step closer. Let go of him. Brayan hesitated, then let Hanev drop roughly. You still defend him? Even knowing what he did? His voice was dripping with hatred, and he glared at me the worst glare Ive ever seen. It was hard to believe this was the same man who had been flirting with me not an hour ago. I forgive, I answered. I forgive him. The glare intensified. I didnt know that was possible. Then youre just as bad as he is. Shut up! I snapped, and I took another threatening step forward. Get out of here. Now. Before I stab you through the heart. And I think at that moment, I was so furious that I would have. He glared at me again, and then slowly walked out of the room. Once he did, I still kept the knife poised for a few moments. Just in case. Then I let the knife drop and I turned to Hanev. He was clutching his shoulder, teeth clenched. Are you alright? I asked. He nodded slowly. Fine. Ill be fine. Are you sure? Yes. Ill be fine. I sighed and looked at the door. Then Im going to go make sure he left. Be careful, Daehn said. Hanev nodded in agreement. Hes. very angry, to say the least. I noticed him clutch his shoulder tighter. Ill be fine, I answered, and I reached down and let my hand close around the handle of the knife again. Then I went downstairs. I looked around quickly, to make sure he wasnt in one of the rooms, and he was. He was in the sitting room, the one with his pictures all along the walls. At that moment he was gazing at the one of Hanev, with the cuts on his face and throat. I raised the knife. I told you to get out, I sneered. He turned. He didnt seem fazed. His anger seemed to have left him as suddenly as it came, and it left a look of regret and sadness behind. Oh. Sorry. He glanced back at the painting, then at me again. You really do know, dont you? And you really forgave him? Yes, I said through tightly clenched teeth. He didnt seem to notice my hostility. Someday, he said. Someday you are going to regret ever crossing paths with Hanev. Youre wrong, I retorted defiantly. Ill regret it. Now  He nodded silently, then he left the room. I followed. Without a word, he opened the front door, and then closed it behind him. I looked through the window, and I saw him walk away. Finally. He was gone. I went back upstairs to Hanev then. I felt so bad for him. His face then was just unexplainable. It was sad, but so sad that it cant be explained. So so sad. He was so sure that Brayan wouldnt hurt him. I am so sorry, Hanev, I said softly. I am so, so sorry. He gave a small sigh, and looked out the window at the horse grazing. I just thought that maybe I wouldnt be alone. That maybe he forgave me. Hanev, I gently answered. Youre never alone. He smiled, blinked, and a tear fell. Thank you, he whispered, sincerely. Thank you so much. That was enough thanks for me. More than enough. Much more. -Saphyra
