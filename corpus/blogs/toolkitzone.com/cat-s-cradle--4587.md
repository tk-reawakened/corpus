# Cat's Cradle

by Darwin on July 6, 2006
My block cut off hot water. So to wash myself, I headed to my father's, how to call it, apartment, I guess, today. I mean yet another place of living in an old and ugly block of flats. Though I actually like the look of my father's block, especially the balconies, each higher one is drawn back towards the central line of the building, giving it a look of a ziggurat, but what comes to my mind is "cathedral", though I have no idea why. After washing myself, I went pass the interior driving exam, which doesn't mean anything, but allows me to start driving. Theoretically. The guy I'm supposed to drive with is supposed to call me as for when I should meet him, but he never did, and I have no idea where I have his cell phone number. In the meantime, I need to get photos and the photographer for future student license and the driving one; and pay the university admission fees at the universities. What I think of that way events happen is 'stasis', as I may have already mentioned. Statis occurs when I have no obligations, but I know some will come, or will have to be inevitably done, with time, but this time is beyond my reach at the time. While getting back from my father's house, I took some books with me. Namely, "The Blind Watchmaker" by Richard Dawkins (may Athe bless him), "Naked Ape" by Morris, who seems like a good read but I can't tell, and "Cat's Cradle" by Vonnegut. For a lack of better reference, Vonegut's works are to literature are like LSD visions to visible light spectrum. I've already read "Slaughterhouse Five" before. As for Cat's Cradle, the book is amazing. It doesn't make any goddamn sense. While reading it, one has to adapt a new system of logic, I guess. The main theme is a religion of bokononism. From the retrospection, we learn that the main hero adapted bokononism and various arts of book mention quotes from the Book of Bokonon and Bokonon's calypsos. Then it turns out Bokonon made up his religion completely to comfort people, and went as far as to ensure survival of the religion through persecution by being impaled on a hook. Loosely (it's a bit more complex, but, meh). What's interesting in Vonegut's works is that they're made of short, half-page to one-page "chapters", with names ranging from one word being a central theme and quotes to funny sentences commenting on what's going on, almost every one ending in what seems to be a punchline. The timeline is completely reversed, or doesn't exist at all (it didn't in Slaughterhouse Five). Books merge completely random elements, ike outright vulgarity where the use of "fuck" is humorous ("I fucking asked if he has any fucking connection with him. He apparently has. He's his fucking son!") to religious philosophies or mentioning of genocides. Add to that the outright realistic depiction of world's hatred attitude towards the US (as envisioned perfectly in one chapter) and historical facts, and Kurt Vonegut becomes the one author I wish all these fat obese American teenagers read in school. Instead of some, blegh, Washing-my-ass-tons and their pledges of slavery.
