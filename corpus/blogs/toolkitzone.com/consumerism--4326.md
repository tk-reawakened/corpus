# Consumerism

by Darwin on May 6, 2006
I went with my mother to drive the dog. My dog is technologically progressive, meaning she prefers to be driven around to a forest than walked out. Either way, as we turned to drive into a path near the forest and the fields, pretty far from my town, we've noticed a dog at the side of the street. What was particularly strange, the dog was a puppy. There were no cars standing around, at least none that I could notice, and while still in the car, my dog reacted naturally by barking at the creature. Logic would dictate that the dog was left there, kicked out of the car or something. Then again, logic would ask why the hell would someone get rid of a pup. Generally, if they didn't want a dog, they'd rather put it for euthanasia just after birth, not wait til it grows a few weeks. Either way, the logic ended at that point for me and was overtaken by empathy, the most self-destructive feeling I can possibly get. Empathy, in the strict sense, has nothing to do with social behaviour or altruism, it's a pure biological response, akin to fear or anger. The easiest biological explanation would be group selection, apart from the fact that group selection is a myth, as proven by Dawkins. Perhaps a form of behaviour stemming from parental instinct, due to an error or genetical limitations extended over non-related organisms. Empathy, quite obviously, is neither taught nor chosen. It ts completely involuntary and devoid of all logic. If one was to worry about state of all other creatures in the world, basic sense would indicate each hour, thosands, if not millions, suffer and die in agony. A person that would feel sympathy and empathy tswards all of these beings volountarily would probably just want to blow this fucking disgusting world to smithereens. But empathy is selective, is induced by direct contact with the situation, and maybe be gone just like any other instinctual feeling, along with memory. When we ended the walk and drove back, the dog was nowhere in sight. No idea what could probably happen to it, other than a sad and a long death - so it goes. Tralfamadorians would say a person dead in the now is just fine in other times of their life, but that hardly applies when children die. And they die virtually all the time, be it animals or people in some regions of the world, a small fraction of infants survives to the point of maturity - inevitably. But why is the title "consumerism" again? Ah, yes. Capitalism. Empathy is not an integral part of capitalism. No. It's actually the reverse; empathy is shunned and all situations in which it can be faced are kept to the minimum. The base of capitalism is choice - choice about yourself. Choice between A, B and C. Choice organised and set within boundaries, within phrases, categories, polls and bumper stickers. Do you give a fuck? (y)es, (n)o A choice that bears no responsibility for others if not enforced. As American puritan-capitalistic vision dictates, if someone fails, it's their fault. You can or can not help them, either way, it's mere choice between 2 alternatives. "Everyone should count on themselves" bumper sticker or "I help others" bumper sticker. What is particularly scary is that I glorify certain instinctuao behaviours and condemn others. I hate when people act based on genetical relation towards family member, or when they claim it's bad to hit females while it's just fine to beat up weaker men if they piss them off, but at the same time I in a way base my thoughts on empathy. Though empathy as an instinct fails when it comes to action. A bomber that pushes the button doesn't hear the screams and see severed limbs. He flies back home to play with kids and be a good father. That is human nature - there is nothing more human that that. More real. There were separate realities. Inevitably. There were realities in which it weren't the strong who survived, and all of those realities ended in chaos and doom. Inevitably.
