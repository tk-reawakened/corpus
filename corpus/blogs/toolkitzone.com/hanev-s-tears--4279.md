# Hanev's Tears

by Lisha on April 30, 2006
Whoa, long, sad entry. I like this one though. But it's by far the saddest one yet. Warning you, that's all. *** April 30 (Later) My God. My God. I have a lot to explain, so I will go straight to what happened. When I heard the scream I dropped this book and rushed to the edge of my cage. I peered through the bars and tried to see up the stairs, but I couldnt see a thing. I whirled my head around to look at Hanev. What was that? I demanded. Who screamed? He looked up, his expression annoyed. What the hell are you talking about now? I heard someone scream, I said. A girl, it sounded like. How could you not have heard it? When he heard me say this, his eyes widened and he jumped up from his seat. Saphyra, he said nervously as he frantically began to unlock the door. Saphyra, concentrate on- Suddenly, he was gone. Everything was gone. I wasnt in my cage anymore; instead I was in total blackness. Then a room began to fade in around me. It was a luxurious room; the room of someone belonging to a very rich family. The curtains were silk, the bedposts were silver, and the window frames were lined with gold. There was a young girl - she looked about fourteen - sitting at a small table brushing her hair in front of a mirror. She wasnt pretty, so to speak, but just from the look of her she didnt seem like one who would care anyway. I found myself moving backwards. I had no control over it, and I wasnt moving as if I was walking. It was a single, fluid, uninterrupted motion, unseperated by the jostling of footsteps. When I moved back I was able to see a boy, who looked about sixteen, standing and watching the girl. He looked so familiar, so so familiar, but I couldnt remember who it was that he looked like. I couldnt . It was like I was being forced to watch, and only watch, and do nothing else. The girl put down her brush and began toying with her hair, pulling it one way, then shaking it out and pulling it another way. After a few moments she gave it a yank of frustration. My hair is ridiculous! she cried, but with a tone of joking to her voice. Its positively insufferable. No, your hair is beautiful, the boy said, smiling. It just has a mind of its own, just like someone else I happen to know. And youre beginning to sound like that friend of Mothers, talking like that. I have more than enough of that woman when I have to see her. So stop it. The girl turned and smiled at him, then turned back to the mirror and resumed fidgetting with her hair. Wheres Brayan? Downstairs, I think, answered the boy. He was beginning to look a little uneasy and pale. Finishing some drawing of his Oh, alright! the girl said, laughing lightly. I was afraid hed run off again. I think hes finally gotten a girlfriend, considering how much hes been gone lately. You think so? But the boy wasnt listening. He was leaning on his knees and had his eyes drawn together like curtains forced too tightly. His elbows trembled, his claw clenched. His shoulders heaved up and down with gasps of breath. You think so? the girl said again when he didnt answer, and she turned around. Hanev? Are you alright? Hanev. Yes. Hanev. Hanev showed no sign of hearing her. Hanev? The girls voice sounded more frantic now, and she got up and began to run towards him. She didnt make it before Hanev jerked upright, so suddenly that he nearly fell over backwards. It was as if he had been in a tug-of-war with himself, and he had been pulling so hard that when there was nothing to pull against anymore he lost his balance. He straightened himself as the girl ran up to him. Are you alright? she asked, sounding worried. For a moment you were Her voice trailed off as she looked into Hanevs face and saw that something was wrong. He was staring at her with the most intense glare Ive ever seen in my life, matched only by Hanevs own. They stood there like that, Hanev glaring at her, her blinking up at him, for a few moments. Then he slowly lifted a hand and ran one finger down her cheek. Where his finger touched, it cut through her skin as easily as a knife would. It left a streak of red behind it. It was then that the girl screamed. The same scream I had heard. The same scream that I could hear, but Hanev could not. A scream that must have been screamed about ten years ago. Then she ran. Hand pressed to her cheek, she ran, and Hanev chased. I followed. She ran out of the room, down the long, elegantly curved staircase. Hanev caught her easily. He was taller than her, faster. He grabbed her wrist and held her back. Wherever he touched her skin, he left blood behind. She struggled, but she was hardly able to even waver him. Something was wrong with him. Any normal human would have at least moved a little bit. The girl was crying. Hanev, what are you doing, what are you doing? she shrieked, her frantic voice cutting the air. Hanev, Im your ! You love me! Hanev doesnt love anybody anymore, he whispered. Then he ran his finger along her other cheek, and then from under her chin down her throat. She cried harder, but her sobs were now laced with gasps and coughs. Then Hanev stopped. His hand dropped, and he let the girl go. She tumbled down a few stairs, then reached out feebly and stopped herself from falling further. Then he looked down at her, and saw the blood coming down her cheeks and throat, and he ran to her. Keya, Keya, he said as he pulled her into his arms. Keya, did I do this to you? Did I do this? Keya make a weak attempt to push Hanev away from her. Dont hurt me anymore, she murmured, voice wavering. Hanevs expression was tortured. He began to say something to her, but he didnt notice an older boy, a man, and a woman running up the stairs. Get away from her Hanev! the older boy yelled. Get away from her! Hanev held her tighter. You dont understand! he cried, tears rolling down his cheeks. The older boy grabbed him and tore him away from Keya. Get out! Get out of here! Get out of my sight! He was also crying. Everyone was crying. If a room could smell of tears, this one did. No! No please! screamed Hanev. Please listen! You dont understand! Shut ! the older boy cried. Dont tell me I dont understand! I understand what I saw! He began to drag Hanev down the stairs, away from Keya and the couple who held her and cried over her. Brayan! Hanev screeched. Please! Please! Let me stay with her! He struggled recklessly against Brayans hold, but the hold was strong, and he was dragged farther and farther away. You dont ! he cried again, hysterically. I understand fine! Brayan sneered. All I dont understand is how you could do such a thing! How could you! As everybody cried and screamed and struggled, I moved again. I moved next to Hanev, so close to him that I could reach up and wipe the tears from his face, and I wanted to. I wanted to so badly, but I could not. I could not. Hanev was pulled closer and closer to the door, and just before he was thrust outside I saw Keyas eyes draw towards him. Through the tears and blood on her face, true, sad, grief lay. Her lips formed one word, one silent word that I could somehow still hear: Why? And then I was back in my cage. And Hanev was there, the older Hanev, his hand on my shoulder, tears running down his face. You repeated every word, he said, and then he turned away and cried.
