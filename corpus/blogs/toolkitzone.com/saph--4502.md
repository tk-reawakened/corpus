# Saph

by Lisha on June 10, 2006
And we enter Part 3 with the Saph era! It's kind of fun to speak with a new voice, but it's a little hard. Saphyra's voice was really my voice, so writing through Saph's younger, more hyper ish voice is hard. I think I did okay, though. Short entry, but I can't help that think that Saph would have a shorter attention span than Saphyra anyway. At least, at this stage. *shrug* ***** ------------------- 20 Years Later ------------------- June 10 Hello. Well. This is new to me. Daehn gave me this journal this morning, and I figured I might as well write in it. So, here I am. Writing, even though I dont know what to write about. I guess I should start by saying who I am. My name is Saph Ferleke. My real name is Saphyra, after my aunt and the real owner of this journal, but I thought it was too long when I was little and just told people to call me Saph instead, and they did. I am eleven years old, but Im almost twelve. But, as Daehn would say, Almost only counts in horseshoes and hand grenades. I have no idea what that means - what is a hand grenade, anyway? And what do horseshoes have anything to do with it? - but I think its a fun saying. I suppose while Im on the subject Ill talk about Daehn. He is the reason Im writing in this, after all. He is my almost-uncle, because he was engaged to Saphyra - the first one - for a very short time before she died, even though they were dating for a very long time before that, and had known each other all their lives. He loved her very much. I can still tell, even though it must be twenty years later. I remember the day I told him that I wanted to be called Saph, not Saphyra. The look on his face couldnt be explained. He looked as if I had slapped him. But then it was quickly erased, and replaced with a forced smile, and he said simply, Of course. But he still doesnt seem to want to say my name unless he has to. He looks sad, too. Hes very friendly, of course. One of the friendliest people Ive ever met. But theres something about him he sometimes looks as if he is carrying some extra burden that the rest of us dont carry, an extra pound of sadness. Mother has it too, but only very faintly, as if she is only carrying a fourth of a pound of sadness. This morning Daehn had been talking with my parents again. He does that a lot these days. I think it must have something to do with the war. He came outside to see me, though, in the later morning. Hay, you! he called. Thats another one his strange words: Hay. He uses it in unusual places. The first time my friend Diala heard him say that, she asked me if he was asking me to bring him some hay for his horse. I told her that he always calls me that way. Instead of calling me by my name, he calls, Hay, you! Strange. Even thought he was smiling at first, by the time we meet his face is serious. I want to talk to you. I nodded silently and followed him to a lonely bench in the middle of a wide, wide, wide field. I wrote it three times because this field is three times as wide as any other wide field Ive seen. It stretches forever! Its very pretty, though, like a sea of grass. When we were both sitting, he turned to me and said, How much do you know about your aunt? I was slightly taken aback. It seemed like a strange subject for him to ask me about. Not much. Mother doesnt talk about her very often, only says that she was a martyr. She was a martyr, there isnt any doubting that, he answered. She was a hero. Shed been through very much, so many things that I honestly dont think anyone else could make it through. She had lost things His voice trailed off and he shook his head. I cant even explain it. It was horrible, but she still was able to smile when she got back. If it were me I would have been dead. I would have killed myself. Maybe she wanted to, sometimes. But she never did, and that alone is admirable. I didnt ask what it was exactly that had happened, because, even though Im curious, I dont know if I really want to know. He says that it was horrible, and Im willing to take his word for it. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, flat, black box. It was old, that was evident, but it still seemed elegant somehow. On the night I asked Saphyra to marry me, I gave her this necklace, he said quietly, voice shaking just a little. He opened the box and inside was a beautiful silver necklace. The charm was a heart with a sword in front of it, and around the sword wrapped pretty little flowers on a vine. It was so beautiful. You would never be able to tell that it was twenty years old, the way it still shone. He took it out of the box and handed it to me. At first I didnt know what to do. I didnt think he was actually giving it to me until he said, Well, take it. I did, and held it awkwardly in my hand. Why are you giving me this? I asked. He sighed and looked away, then looked back at me. You might have to do something very dangerous. Your mother doesnt want to admit it, and your father doesnt want to admit it, and I dont even want to admit it, but its true. I gave Saphyra this necklace in particular because she always protected what she loved, and I hope that youll protect what you love too. I hope you can be as strong as she was. And I really think that you can. He smiled for a moment, then he became solemn again. Just He stopped, and I prodded, Yes? He grimaced, and finished, Just dont get yourself killed at the end of it all. Oh. That was awkward. Very awkward. I put on the necklace, and then we were both silent for a few minutes. Then I said, to break the silence, Could you tell me more about her? He did. Very much. He told me everything, even how she tried to put her hair in a braid the night of her death and it had come out to be the messiest, most crooked thing to grace the hair of a girl. He said that he didnt have the heart to tell her so, though even though he also said, with the weight of sadness returning to his eyes, that he pulled it out after she died. His last gesture. Then he took me back to my house, and it was there that he gave me this journal. He said that he had never read it, but he felt like Saphyra wouldnt mind if I had it. He said that I should continue her story. So I am. I still dont know what he meant by saying that I might have to do something very dangerous. Its frightening, yes. Especially since I think it must have something to do with the war, and the war is something that no one seems to want to get wrapped up in except for the bravest people, like Daehn and my parents, and the rest of the people who help them. Im not a brave person. At least, I dont think I am. I havent really had the chance to be brave, and, truthfully, I dont want the chance to be brave. Id rather stay nice and safe, thank you very much. But Im only a child. Whatever it is - if it is anything - it cant be too bad. I try to think that. -Saph **** On a totally unrelated side note, Daehn has a far more interesting past in this part. *looks around and whistles nonchalantly*
