# Sauber

by Darwin on April 6, 2006
It's 2 days without antidepressants and I get suicidal thoughts again. Damn. How can I achieve power, how can I create my own morals, hell, how can I do math if I just want to shoot myself in the head or start walking in a random direction and never turn back? This reality, as I have realised, makes no fundamental sense. The absurd is that "sense" as humans define it is something certainly from within this reality. As there is no other. This reality is the only one. Determinism is obvious. Why is it then that processes like evolution are complete random and require a natural selection to fix it up? Can't life see where it's going? The boundaries are obviously that of this physical reality, and since it's the only one existant, I can't see why it is the one in which certain boundaries are set. Since no one obviously understands what I am talking about, let me present an alternate conception: Thus reality is going to hell. It's going to get eaten up by entropy, life is going to end, either way, 80% of creatures born end up dead before they manage to reproduce so the genetic mechanisms of evolution aren't that perfect either. One way or another, thin reality is going to end senselessly, expire, die. There are 3 cases in which such a born-to-die reality without judgement would make sense: 1. The reality reproduces itself. Before it dies, it manages to create a copy of itself that lives its own separate way and probably even evolves, that is, some copies it preduces are better than others and these create a giant reality pool that goes on forever. The above sounds silly, doesn't it? Mechanics of evolution can't probably apply to reality, thus, the goal of self-reproduction can't be the aim of a reality. 2. The reality has a physical impact on any other physical realities that exist or something out-of-reality. It leaves a physical trail before it dies. That trail may be some impact on outer realities, or maybe an insight given to the reality-creator which would influence the further realities he's going to produce. The above cenception requires that there are more limits put on physical realities than the planes in which they exist, and/or their creators/creator. Either way, it doesn't make much sense either as realities aren't likely to influence other realities. Dawkin's argument of complexity also violates the aforementioned requirement and the whole idea of a reality-creator. 3. Realities don't bear any physical impact on one another, but either separate realities exist or reality is replaced/recycled when expired, so that in the end there is never a case of nenexistence of any reality, that is, a case where no realities exist. The above isn't likely either. Entropy wouldn't allow such a case, in the end, all realities would be permanently put to a state of nonexistance or non-change (lack of all processes, the stopping of time). if I were to choose, the first conception would make most philosophical sense... but least real sense. There is no way in which a reality can reproduce or influence one another. Reality is something highest. Why in the world would it exist if it were to die? Wouldn't it make much more sense for a reality to non-exist?
