January 20 2006, 7:53 am
I guess you could use dupli-color, I would sand the trim if its not plastic, if its rubber you dont really have to sand it, but going over with a 600-800 grit sand paper never hurts. For best results, your best bet is to get some semi-gloss plastic paint, or any other paint for plastics. Rust-Oleum is pretty good, it can be found in most hardware stores, i know my local Sears Hardware carrys them. For the scratch, is it really deep all the way to the metal? I would go to a local body or collision shop and ask them if they can buff it out, many times you can. The thing is, some people may tell you you need to paint the car just to get you to pay for their service. Try a couple places, I have never used those pens or whatever, I've seen all the commercials for the liquid stuff that fills in your scratches but I've never tried it. But again, if the scratch isnt too deep you can probably buff it out which will be a lot cheaper than repainting. With painting, if its a good place they'll blend one area to each side of the piece with the scratch. Like if you have a scratched door, the quarter panel and the fender would be painted as well, its one of the best ways to blend. One last thing, when painting the trim, make sure you mask everything off properly, you dont want any excess paint all over the place. hope I was of some use/help =)

January 20 2006, 10:21 am
get some 3m masking tape from any auto place, make sure its 3m or any other "expensive" tape. Its about $3-4 a roll. Other cheaper tape peels off very fast. I wouldnt recommend using newspapers, I'm sure they should have masking paper in CarQuest or something. You dont reall need to tape off much, just about 6-6 inches wide around the area you're going to spray. Try to get the tape as close to the modling/trim as possible, sometimes you can slide part of it underneath the modling/trim. Its hard to put it into words. One thing i forgot to mention, take some lint free paper towel, put osme rubbing alcohol on it and wipe the surface before spraying. Makes the surface nice and clean, and removes all dirt, oil etc.
