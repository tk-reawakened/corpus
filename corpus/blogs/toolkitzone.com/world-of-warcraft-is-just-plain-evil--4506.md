# World of Warcraft is just plain evil.

by Isolocis on June 11, 2006
Well, I made another blog just minutes after my last blog. To start this blog with a quick summary, WoW and Blizzard Entertainment are just plain evil. So I just wanted to make an account. Though at one part, they asked for my phone number. I am the type of person who does not want to give out much of his personal information. I tried submitting a fake phone number and... just wow; submitting fake phone numbers did not work apparently. So I gave my cellphone number to them. Now it asks what kind of credit card I use and then later asked for my credit card information. I did not have a credit card, and I thought this was suppose to be a free trial. Well, even when I uninstalled the client, it did not bring back the hours that I have wasted just to download this client. So they lied about how players can play for ten free months. Instead, we have to give out my paying method just to play the trial. It works like this: You give them your credit card number so that when your "free" trial is up, they immediately charge the credit card so that you can continue. But during that free trial, your character is crippled by limitations. The character was suppose to be stuck at Level 20 and he or she cannot carry more than 10 gold. Some chat channels are also restricted. "but lolz, its 10 free days b4 u pay." Well I only intended to play the free trial and I did not want to play or pay for the full version. How can anyone like this game is beyond me. Now they are making a expansion pack of this game and I assume it will cost money to get extra features. So I conclude that Blizzard is only trying to make money out of their fans, including the people who wanted to play the "free" trial. Sad.

There are many games these days that have multiple expansion packs, even games that arn't MMORPG's. Hell, just look at The Sims. Expansions provide something new for those that have already progressed far enough in the game for it to start becoming repetitive, not for people that have just bought the game. It ensures that people will still play the game on a regular basis, and continue paying the monthly fee, it's hardly meant to rip you off. Now if those games were stripped down to begin with, then you might have a point, but the main game of WoW is hardly a stripped down game, and you could easily play for well over a year without ever having to buy an expansion. Now since those expansions cost Blizzard money to make them, would they not be obligated to charge for them as well? And being a company whose goal is to make a profit, wouldn't they also want to do that on the expansions also, just as they did on the original game? Expansions are hardly a new thing, and they've always cost money, so I'm not really sure what your complaining about. You'd have a far better arguement if you were to complain about companies now charging multiple dollars just for a single item (Horse Armor in TES: Oblivion comes to mind...). Now granted, there's always the arguement of "If you don't want it, don't buy it", but $2 for a single decoration that doesn't affect gameplay in the slightest? Now that's ridiculous. BTW, I think it's kind of funny that Blizzard only offers a mere 10 free days to play. I got an entire free month with FFXI... I'm pretty sure that's what most other MMORPG's offer as well...
