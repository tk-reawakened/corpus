# 1. Grass

Browse
**1. Grass**
Simple Yet Effective Tiles
This is my first tutorial. Yay! So, I might as well make it a good one. One thing I know people struggle with a lot is good graphics. And, considering some people on Youtube have given me the label "A Master at MS Paint", I might as well tell you how to make excellent graphics for your boards in only a couple minutes a piece. No offense, but most people on this site (including me) probably don't have the patience to take an hour or more to make one tile.
Ok, so, let's get started. I know most of you know what to start out with-- go to the tile editor. The editor is pretty simple. It's basically a take on MS Paint that allows you to make 32x32 tiles. Most of you probably already know what the tile editor looks like, but just in case anyone is new to this and has not opened it yet, below is a screenshot.
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1319.JPG)
So, let's get started making this tile. You see down by the color bar at the bottom there's a button that says "Select Color"? Click on that and there should be a screen that pops up that looks like this:
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1320.JPG)
You'll see that I've circled that nice green color in about the center, right? Well, I hope it's obvious what tile we'll be making - a grass tile! Since almost every RPG game has at least one, that might as well be the first stop for us. Click on the green color and then hit the "OK" button. You now will be able to put this color into your tile. Instead of just penciling the color in, let's use the "fill" function on the right. By the end, you should have a filled green tile. Now it's time to actually put some texture to it, make it feel more...well...grassy. Go back to the select color tool and click on the button that says "Define Custom Colors".
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1321.JPG)
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1322.JPG)
You'll notice that the window has extended as you can see above. You'll see all the numbers and colors of the hue, sat, lum, RGB, etc. Don't worry about those for now. First, click on that green that you filled the tile with. You'll now see that the color on the right has changed. I've circled the far right bar on the second pic, right? This is what we artists call a shader. It helps determine values to change lighting effects in a picture. You'll notice that the bar gets gradually brighter as you go up it, and darker as you go down. See that arrow next to the bar? That will help you determine where your current color stands on the bar. Click on the bar just slightly above that arrow. Ah! The color has changed! It's now brighter! Yes, it's brighter. Now hit the "Add to Custom Colors" button below all the settings (circled in the below picture) so you can come back to this color later.
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1323.JPG)
Now you'll see a new color in the custom colors palette. If you want, make another color just barely brighter than the last one. You can create as many as you want, but for two reasons you should limit it a little. Firstly, it will get more complex with more colors and secondly you probably don't need that many, so it's a waste of time. Now, do the same thing as you did with the brighter colors, only do it with darker shades. *Note: Try not to go too bright or too dark on any of the colors, as it won't look right in the final tile.*
Now grab one of those custom colors and hit "OK". Take the pencil tool in the tile editor and move it up and down the tile while clicking every so often to put the color in "random" places. Do this with all the colors you made. By the end, you should have a similar tile to the one shown below.
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1324.JPG)
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1325.JPG)
You could possibly end things here and it wouldn't be too bad of a grass tile, but there is a way to make it even better. You'll see in the second picture above that I've circled the "Effects" menu item. Click on that, and there should be an option that says "Blur". Click on that, and, huh? The colors merge together! That's exactly what we wanted! Click it a few more times until you're happy with the effect. You should get something similar to the tile below;
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1326.JPG)
See that I've circled the "Current Color/Tile" setting? Well, this is what your tile will look like in the board itself, once you put it in. Ok, now time to test this baby out! Save the tile under the name you want (mine was test.tst, but yours can be anything you want) and go to the board editor. Create a new board, and set the dimensions you want. Then fill in the board with your grass tile. This is what my board looked like at the end...
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1327.JPG)
Pretty sweet, huh? If you're not pleased with the first one, try again. Sometimes the blur with the random coloring doesn't work too well, and you'll have to recreate it. But at least you made a rather good looking tile within only a few minutes! This method of making tiles can go for a TON of different tiles. Water, grass, snow, dirt, sand, even the white wall of a house. Though not every tile can be made doing this (like, for example, the lines on wood can't be made by random clicking), most of the basic ones can be made through this process. I hope this tutorial has helped some of you who really can't do great art, or just don't want to spend hours making each tile perfect. I also hope you've enjoyed this tutorial and I look forward to seeing some very good tilesets in the future!
