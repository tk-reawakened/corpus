**1. Lotusware**
** **Juice Talks With...**
Lotusware
1. Lets get right to what we all want to know. Why, exactly, did Eraflux get put on hold for 2 years?
Okay...well firstly, the main reason is that it really requires me to put all my time into the project in order to build up momentum, and then let the inertia keep the project going. What with work now...and then college in the fall, I pretty much have little to no free time. Second of all, its a big project, and the ammount of work to be done both graphically and musically by one individual would make the workload too heavy a burden, therefore I'm training a student to help me do the graphical aspects of the game while I put a main focus on driving the project forward, planning things out and doing music. I of course will be doing most of the graphics, with my student doing minor and less needed aspects, freeing up time for me to keep going at a constant rate. In addition to all this, the new look of EraFlux will surpase the old, seeing as how in two years time, I'll have finished all schooling and can devote more time to it with my aquired training.
2. What are Lotusware's (the company) plans for the future? Plan on expanding with a bigger team and more projects?
What I hope to accomplish is provide direction and training to several individuals and build this group up into a major force and hopefully move into a more commercial market, rather than pool all the talent in the hobby genre. As of now the team consists of 4 members, myself being the middle man, so to speak, that ties all the parts together. I hope to have (aside from myself) two programmers, an additional composer/sound effects specialist, and at least two more artists. Right now we have enough to get a bit of work done, but nowhere near the scale that I hope to acheive.
3. What do you plan to do with Reflection?
Reflection started as a concept more so than a story. I wanted to create a game that didn't really matter how you play through, because its the playing through that makes the game no longer a game. I wanted to involve the player in every aspect and not chop the game up with fighting monsters over and over to gain experience before you can keep going. So, Reflection itself isn't going to have alot of gameplay hours, but its simplicity and involvement should be appealing. As for unique features, the player isn't bound by equiment, hit points and magic points, leveling or even completing quests...so I hope to break the boundries of story-telling. What I'm getting out of it myself is practice with graphics, doing all my own coding, and music...not to mention actually wrapping it all together. So Reflection is basically a game I'm making independantly to help me become a better designer/developer, and give everyone something fun and enjoyable in the process.
4. How did you think people respond to the stopping of Eraflux, and the immediate construction of Reflection?
I don't really refer to it as a stopping, its more of a sleeping if anything. I hope that some people are anxious as to when EraFlux will come back and with how much force, and I know that alot of people have expressed their rage with our descision, which is nice too, because I didn't realise that some people had that much interest. As for Reflection, It has gained crazy amounts of recognition so quickly, that is thanks in part to the publicity of EraFlux, which I am thankful for, but Reflection had meant to be fairly enigmatic, but it appears that it enjoys the limelight as I do. I'm pretty sure the fans of EraFlux are the fans of Reflection and are anticipating its release, which is set for this winter.
5. When can we expect a finished Lotusware website? And what can we expect to see on it when it does?
Well...the initial design and layout has been made and is roughly coded...but it definately needs to be reworked to keep everything looking clean and consistant. Why have website when one has so little to showcase? I think that the completion of Reflection will be coupled with a freshly baked Lotusware site. I'm planning to have a screenshot reel, and hopefully an online audio streaming radio, seeing as how there is alot of songs and such. Basically, everything a small company should have...contact, new reel, services...all the while keeping it Lotus-esque.
6. Your alias on tkz is Lotusware, why not Dana or something else? Did you originally come to tkz intending to recruit?
I originally came to TKZ as Dana_Harris, if I can recall correctly, about 5 years ago. I wanted to make a game where you could travel to different games in the Nintendo franchise (Legend of Zelda, Mario, Kirby, Metroid, etc.) as a oringal character...and you'd have to play through the game that way. This obviously didn't follow through, and I left the community for a year, and then came back as Dana Tagalon Harris where I was better respected because I actually wanted to create original games (Immortal's Fall), and not fan games. That project was fronted by a user, Gato Gaming, whom I am still very good friends with. That projected ended when our entire profile got deleted by a certain webmaster, completely on accident, but it ruined out spirits. I lurked around for a few months and left for another few years. I came back and changed my name to Lotuware. I got this because I wanted a cool name, and was originally to be called LotuSoft, but that look queer. Why the Lotus? Because its a simple design and is a pretty sweet flower. I started work on Cursed Myth and then people wanted me to make my own game...so I did. And that's where EraFlux came from. I had no intentions of recruiting, seeing as how I have credentials...the only recruiting I did was by making friends along the way.
7. With Reflection coming in as a Phaeton affiliation, can we expect Phaeton and Lotusware to team-up for future productions?
Hm. That might be a possibility, but you have to remember, Reflection is the creation of Dana Harris, whereas everything else is Lotusware. But I can see where Lotusware and Phaeton could have a possibility. Only time will tell.
8. How long has Lotusware existed?
Pretty much as long as there was paper and a pencil handy. There was always ideas to write down, music to play and things to draw. Lotusware really began to exist about two years ago or so, although the build up started around 1996 with me and a friend writing a short story heavily based off of The Hobbit, which later changed, got scrapped, dug up, revamped, shifted, dropped and reborn into the story to be used in EraFlux.
9. What was the first serious project Lotusware undertook?
Embarrasing as it is...our first completed game was a Star Wars shoot-em-up for PC. I burned a few copies and gave them to my friends and they got addicted by the easy navigation and simple controls, and addicting gameplay. Unfortunatly, those few CDs have long since perished...and thus too has Star Wars by Lotusware. On a more serious note, EraFlux is likely our most serious project, and hopefully it become written in stone when its done. The sad thing about it is I hate to have to see the game's completion, because it likely means that anything else afterward will be subpar...and I know for sure its way too early to be thinking about sequels.
10. What programs do you use for your works besides tk (non-game too)?
I use paint alot, its an amazingly simple and accessable program, but for more indepth graphic work I use Macromedia Fireworks, some crappy old 2000 edition or something. I am heavy into music, so I use FL Studios to arrange all my recorded tracks the way I want. I try to depend on the computer as little as possible, so I have binders filled with drawings and plannings and such...and there are always instruments around the house so I mess around a bit, and if I stumble accross a combination of random notes/arpegios that sounds pretty okay, I write them down for later. Everything I do is pretty low-tech compared to what some people have access to, but I guess its not the means, but the ends that matter.
11. What is/was your most anticipated game/demo release of ALL time?
Vii is probably on the list. I don't really like demos because I always find that they end too soon, or limit what you can actually do. I don't really anticipate games as much games as I anticipate new media. I'd be happy to look at screenshots and listen to music. As for a shameless plug, Bestiary is likely going to be pretty good when its done/demo released.
12. The hot topic now is ripped graphics. What is your say on this? And would you like to see any changes in the voting system?
Ugh...ripped graphics is crippling emerging designer/developers. The trend seems to be that if the graphics look good, who cares if they're not yours? I for one am sick of seeing so little effort being put into games. I know if I was new at the whole game-making process, I wouldn't dare show my face until I had something I could dish out. Graphics seem to be the quickest way to earn respect and therefore not a whole or respect is coming from me. As for the TKZ voting system...I'd rework it so that upon uploading, the user had to pick from "Original/Not Original Graphicsl". By picking "Not Original", a rating cap would be put on the image, a maximum of three for instance. That way at least they're getting votes for layout and design, but not nearly as high as if they created their own graphics. This would be an incentive to strive for original graphics, and not just making some lame attempt at mediocrity and having their friends 5/5 them all.
13. What type of project would you like to see come out of the tk in the near future?
I'd like to see a monster taming/catching/breeding game come out thats not a Pokemon fangame. I was always into those kind of games, so if someone did that, I'd likely support them...and if nobody does, Lotusware might go ahead and do up a project like that. I'd also like to see a huge collab game, where all the leading artists/musicians/programmers get together and make a game. MMORPGs are another thing. If someone can get one up and functioning to the best of their ability, that would be something to look forward to. I know the toolkit isn't meant for that, but it would be fun nonetheless. To put it plainly, I'd like to see some deviant games, games that push the boundries of everyone's standards and push the toolkit to the limit.
