**1. Overview**
Realistic Board Design
Written by Seafroggys
**Overview**
As a reviewer of Toolkit games, I have come across many problems among the design of each of these games. There is one problem that is repeated through numerous games, a problem that annoys me and impels me to break the monotony with my own games. This plague is the design of boards in games, a plague I want to cure with this tutorial.
When people design and create boards, usually they have an objective to reach, and those boards led to the eventual objective. Yes, boards need an objective, but they also require a purpose. There are countless boards that led to an objective, but have no soul, no purpose, in the game. My first objective is to show you a board that you should not make. Can you guess the flaws of this board?
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1129.jpg)
Lets set some basic principles down. Assume, with 32x64 characters, that each tile represents roughly 4 square feet to 1 square meter. The above example is the inside of a home. Lets count the floor space of the room (including the bed, table, chair, and woodbox). This numbers up to 180 tiles, which is at least 720 square feet in size! Not only does the size of this room compare to an entryway to a mansion, this room has nothing in it! Who realistically would spend the time building a rather big house (for the medieval time period) and only put a bed, table, and woodbox in it? I'm not saying 'no' to making such a house, but such a house with nothing in it? Lets break this down and create a board large enough for one person to comfortably live with a bed, table, and woodbox, but small enough so that the house is not exceedingly huge.
![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1130.jpg) With the same universal rules in mind, the floor size amounts to 20 square tiles, or at least 80 square feet. Quite small, yes, and tiny compared to the poorly designed colassal board in the previous example. One person can construct a house relatively inexpensively, put his belongings in it (a table, bed, and woodbox), and still comfortably manuever inside the house. Compare the two; what do you think is a better designed board? Now, moving on, I'll just show you an example from my game, Ashfall.
This is the main character's house that he shares with![](http://toolkitzone.com/vault/documents/1131.jpg) his sister, thus some of the parameters must be changed to accomodate for the second person. As you can observe, the objects inside the house are all along the perimeter, leaving a rather decent sized gap in the middle of the house. That is fine, since the character and is sister are not in poverty, and can afford such spacious areas. There are two beds, a chest of drawers, a workdesk, a table with two chairs, a woodbox, a fireplace, and food storage, situated in approximetly 192 square feet of space. Those can realistically sustain the two persons for an amount of time. Additional items are located in the attic, which is visited during the game. A hint: if you forget about a certain item in your board design, you can always include it 'in that attic'. Please be careful about exterior design if you do just that.
