# David Thany: How did most people enter the game industry?

Marc: Mostly by graduating from a degree granting art institution, either taking fine arts, animation or illustration - sometimes from a tech related mulitimedia institution taking game art, or digital art. The other path is from the mod community. Marc: No matter if it is from school, or mods - getting a game art is entirely a matter of having a strong portfolio of work. You need a demo reel or cd with 10-15 game quality examples. Ideally artists will have 3D modelling in max or maya, some texture mapping skills, and some illustration work geared at environmental concepts or character design. It's best to present your work as simply as possible - no fancy interfaces or animation - just clear close up shots of the work. Marc: Love of games, patience, good presentation of your work, attention to technical detail, Teamwork - (which usualy means accepting and givng criticism well, and sharing techniques), learning new stuff on the job, always getting better. Marc: probably attention to technical detail and good teamwork. It doesn't matter if you have strong artistic skills if your work is not sutiable for the game - naming schemes, file sizes, UV layout, all that is critical - assets have to work in the game engine, not just in max. Marc: Lots of people find the deadlines difficult - its usefull to learn to get 80% of the work done FAST, and polish later - so if you run out of time, the game can still go forward. It's also important to take direction well - make changes to suit the art director or producer - even if you disagree at first. That's another important part of teamwork. Marc: The NORMAL path for artists would be: Intern: working part time doing simple fixes and clean up (UV seams, re coloring texture variations), JR Artist: doing props and placing decor in levels (furniture, crates, barrels) Modeller: doing levels or characters----you can stay here forever, just getting better: or - Team Lead: organizing a group of artists and figuring out technical problems Art Director: planning how to implement a game design and managing the entire art department Creative Director: actualy designing the game and keeping it on track - working on developing future concepts, possibly managing other art directors----you can stay here forever, just getting better: or - Associate Producer, Producer, bla bla Marc: Draw fast! - to be able to do cool designs on time you have to do enough to throw out the bad, and keep the good. Be able to draw different subject matter (creatures, machines, people, environments). Draw the figure well from your head. Draw sexy characters. Draw expressions and capture personality. Design costumes. Have to be able to draw digitally and traditionaly. MUST! understand the game engine - draw what can be built, not just cool designs - have to learn what animates well, and what is easy to model and texture. There are very few full time concept art jobs - so you often need to be able to do UI, Promotional illustration or Texturing as well as concept art. Marc: Concept Artist, Ui Artist, Modeller, Animator, Lead/Director. Marc: Better graphic power for every game, larger teams with more communication problems, shorter dev cycles. Marc: Basicly the demand for high quality is going to keep going up until only the best are left in the biz, and everyone else has jobs at outsourcing houses and budget web and mobile game companies. Marc: Movies and games are going to be made simoultaneously, making deadlines tighter. Photo real tech means qualiity expectations are really high. Marc: Actualy, it think this is really open ended - there will be games in every genre of film - from cartoon to drama from comedy to horrror - I see nothign but bigger growth. Mobile is huge, cellphone and handheld - mulitplayer is huge - so it's all getting bigger. More mainstream, kids, girl oriented, that kind of thing for sure, since teen male games are pretty much covered. Marc: Just the competition, meaning dealines and demands for quality...that's not unusual - has been same in movies for ages. Marc: No print, all online - Gamasutra, AIAS, IGN, Gamespot, Penny Arcade.... Marc: Well, this depends a LOT on how hard you work - but averages would be 20-30 entry, 50-60 mid level, 70-90 senior, 90-110 director. That could happen over 5 years if you're lucky and good, might take 10. Marc: Oh, too much to answer - but the basics are manage the team, answer questions, solve tech problems, keep the schedule on track during the day, and draw concepts at night. I typically work 60-80 hrs a week. Marc: All kinds. Books, movies, the net,other games. Marc: -Ok, good luck ! Interview with Marc Taro
