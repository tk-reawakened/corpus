# Easy Terrains with Terragen

1. Introduction Welcome to my tutorial on using Terragen. Most likely, if you are reading this tutorial, you dont know what Terragen is. To be blunt, it is a terrain generator. Not only this, but it is free and very easy to use. Not to mention the realism of the landscapes created with this heavily function-laden program. Obviously, step 1 of this tutorial would be to download Terragen. Simply visit this website: http://www.planetside.co.uk/terragen/ After you download the program, install it on your computer. You are officially ready to go!
Open up Terragen for the first time and you will get a screen with a gray background and a few windows popped up all over it. It may look a little confusing at first, but it will all become familiar soon. Along the left side of the screen you will see 8 buttons. Here I will explain what they do.
2. Getting Started

3. The First Terrain
![](library/images/docs/823.png)
If it does not, click on the Rendering Control and Landscape buttons on the left toolbar. It looks somewhat complicated, but it is quite easy. To generate a terrain, click on the GENERATE TERRAIN button in the landscape window. It will bring up yet another window. From the drag down menu, pick one of the terrain options. When you have decided on one, click Generate Terrain in the lower left hand corner of the new window. In the two black boxes on the screen, you will see some white clouds. Press the Close button and then click on Render Preview in the Rendering Control panel. It will render a very rough preview in the gray window above the button. Now, drag the detail toolbar up to the maximum and click Render Image. It may take a while, considering that it is trying to render a realistic mountain range. When it is done, you will have your first terrain! YAY! This is the easy way. Now, let us go custom
To start this next part, go up to World File on the upper toolbar and create a New World. The windows should pop up again. This time, we are going to make our own terrain instead of having the computer generate one for us.
4. Custom Terrains
Click on the View/Sculpt button in the Landscape window. Another window will pop up containing an overhead toolbar, a black screen, and 3 white lines extending from a single point. This is your camera. You can adjust the camera by dragging it around the black screen. This would not make much sense now, however, because you are just looking at your completely flat landscape.
![](library/images/docs/824.png)
Click on the button with the mountain and tractor in the upper toolbar. The mode you were in was the camera positioning mode. After you click the tractor button, you are in the landscape sculpting mode. Click on the black terrain with your circular cursor. A white to gray circle should appear. This is a raised area of your terrain. You can continue to click on it in an effort to create a spire, or leave it at a bump. In the toolbar, there are also two slider bars which allow you to adjust the tool.
One is for size and the other is for tool strength. This means that the higher the strength the more impact it will have upon the earth and the more it will raise/lower the earth with a single click. Sculpt the terrain as you like and then render it when you are done.
It looks okaybut not THAT outstanding. So let us add some water. Click on the Water icon in the left toolbar to bring up the water control panel. Adjust the water settings to your liking. Now, go back to the sculpting tool. Right click on the part of the terrain where you want to add a lake and drag the circle around while holding the right mouse button down still. It should tear a hole in the ground and create a blue spot: water. If this does not work for you, increase the strength of your tool and try again.
Move the camera near the depression and render to see your beautiful little lake. If you would like the sun to reflect at a different angle, go to the Sun window and change the settings there. Click on the red line in the light angle adjust box and drag it to the angle in which you want the sun to rest.
We are closing in on the end of this tutorial, but you may want to know how to change the terrain texture. Maybe you want snow, light rock, sand, who knows? Simply click on the Surface Map text in the Landscape window and click the Edit button. A screen will come up containing two window sets. On the Surface Map, you cannot change distribution so just change the bumpiness and color in the top window. Remember. This color/texture will be distributed EVERYWHERE so make sure it is a color that fits the terrain.
If you would like to add something on top of your main surface, like snow, exit this window and select the Add Child button. Click the New Surface text that comes up and then Edit. A whole spew of buttons will appear allowing you to change multiple things. This feature is very self explanatory. The distribution is just how this top layer is distributed over the other layer. You can also change the color and texture of this the same way you did to the Surface Map.
If you would like to, you can also change the cloudscape. Just adjust these settings like you would any other set. Just open it up and follow the instructions.
There are still many more features to be utilized in this program that I did not cover. This is strictly the BOTTOM LINE basics. Explore the camera angles, terrain specials, water settings, reflection, refraction and everything else this great program has to offer. At the time of publishing, Terragen Version 0.9 was the downloadable version from the site.
