# "Filmmaking"

A Weekly Word
from Cyrus Martin
**"Filmmaking"**
**Part 1**
> Have you ever dreamed of making a movie?
>
> Ever wondered what it would be like to write a script, cast, direct, edit and release an actual motion picture?
>
> Well... I used to.
>
> I used to think about it all the time but I was really never confident enough in myself to try it... until a couple of years ago. Having completed an independent feature film and released it worldwide, I have probably done more than I ever really thought I was capable of... but... the way I did it was even more surprising.
>
> This is how it happened...
>
> First of all, I need to explain that I never intended to make a full length feature film.
>
> Say what?!!!
>
> Yes... that's right. That was not a typo.
> You see... back in early 2003... I started talking with a friend of mine (who happens to be in the entertainment industry) about writing and ideas for stories. The more we talked, the more interested we both became in the idea of actually doing something about it. We had some cool concepts and with the lowered cost of digital equipment... we actually had hope.
>
> I decided to jot down an idea for a story that I had been kicking around for a few years. I showed the scribbled pages to my friend (Bryan Kail) and he thought it was really great. He got me pretty hyped up about it and I decided to give this movie thing a shot. We figured we would throw an open audition and just put the word out on a couple of internet groups for local actors. We would probably get a few responses and just shoot a little indy film from the hip with no real script or anything... ya know... just for the experience of it.
>
> Ehem...
>
> Ok...
>
> So I sent out the message about the movie and threw up a web site.
> I went to sleep that morning (I was working nights at the time) and figured it might take a while to get any kind of response.
>
> I think that it may have taken a couple of days... maybe... before the audition requests that came in broke the mark. ***200***
>
> By the end of the week, I had over requests for auditions... and not from stray kids and passers by... from legitimate actors with head shots and resumes. These people had agents... these people had business cards... these people... were freaking serious. ***300***
>
> I was scared out of my mind.
>
> I got on the phone with Bryan and basically tripped out completely. He calmed me down as best he could and then we took a long deep breath as reality set in. I was going to have to write a script.
>
> We held the big auditions on a Saturday morning. I had worked all night the Friday before... so I was expectedly exhausted. We held the auditions at a local civic center which cost me only $150 to rent for 8 hours. I thought it would be big enough... but as people began arriving and pouring in... we had to start rushing through the auditions as quickly as we could. As the day progressed, I continued slipping down into a spiral of confusion and stress.
>
> Actors kept coming.
>
> They came in droves... by the dozens... literally hundreds of them.
> Some lived nearby... some drove from other cities... some from other states.
>
> These people were taking this seriously. **VERY**
>
> I told them up front that I could not pay them. I figured that would dismay most of the really good ones. Nope. They were in this for the same reason I was... for the experience.
> I hadn't counted on that. I hadn't expected commitment.
>
> Ok... so I finally had to call off the auditions at around 3 PM. I was nearly dead. As I lay on a couch in the lobby, I could hear my friends turning away actors right and left. Some had driven for hours just to come there. Needless to say, I felt like crap about that. We took their head shots and resumes though and gave them the usual line about "We haven't made any decisions yet". What else could we tell them? **TOTAL**
>
> ...ok... so I write the script.
> From start to finish, it takes me 3 weeks.
> I've read that it took Tarrantino 3 weeks to write Reservoir Dogs. Heh...
> I guess that little fact brings me some sense of comfort.
>
> Having never written a movie script before, I have no clue about formatting or even how long a script is supposed to be. I've since learned that a script is supposed to be somewhere in the neighborhood of 100 pages. Mine was over 200.
>
> No time to reformat it... no time to revise...
> No time...
>
> I still don't have a decent camera though.
>
> Ok so I sort of "borrow" money from my own bank account... meaning... I overdraft it as much as I possibly can. I win an ebay auction on a really sweet Sony VX2000 camera and the guy is very understanding and patient. I Western Union him a payment of $1900 and then a week later the camera arrives.
>
> We're in business...
>
> *gulp*
>
> **To Be Continued**
>
