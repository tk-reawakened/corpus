# "Filmmaking"

A Weekly Word
from Cyrus Martin
**"Filmmaking"**
**Part 2**
> Ok so this is part 2. I'm a little late in getting this written because of the release of my book (Deceit and Conquest Volume 1: SEOR). I apologize for being tardy but I guess there's just never enough time for everything.
>
> Anyhoo... On with the story...
>
> Ok... so I have the camera now. Cool. We can get started.
> Well... actually it's not that simple. For one thing, I need to have a place to shoot at. As it stands I have no real locations set up.
>
> I do have an apartment though...
>
> I look over the script and nail down what scenes I think I can do inside my apartment. I (of course) have to be a little creative. I put out the word that I need locations and ask for specific locales.
>
> ...but for now... on with the shoot.
>
> I decide to do a scene in my place that looks like it will be fun. It involves transforming my bedroom into a strange sort of interrogation room with all white walls and a 2 way mirror on one side. It's a flashback scene to the lead female's past so that means I can shoot it in black and white. This is good because it will make the room less recognizable during the other scenes in the film where I use it for something else. Just to add a little more to this effect, I open the iris a little too much... which causes the whites to overwhelm the blacks a bit... and adds a nice harsh contrast.
>
> ok cool.
>
> In order to make the room look like a government interrogation room instead of my bedroom I have to do several things.
>
> 1. Remove my bed, tables, shelves and all other furniture.
> 2. Cover the window with cardboard and a white sheet and tape the edges to the wall.
> 3. cover all the electrical outlets with tape and paint them white
> 4. methodically vacuum the carpet to remove all signs of dirt or dust.
> 5. Tape tube lights to the tops of two walls to present a nice fully lit room.
>
> I also have to tape microphone cables to the ceiling in such a way that they are not visible and also provide good sound for all the actors present. There will be 5 actors in this scene and they will pretty much all be talking.
>
> ...sounds easier than it is.
>
> Alrighty... So I do this... and then the actors begin showing up as scheduled.
> Everybody makes it on time except for one guy. The clock ticks by... everyone else is in makeup and ready to go... yet the guy is still missing.
>
> I decide to begin anyway and shoot a few scenes with just the kids.
> This takes WAY longer than I expected.
> I was anticipating that I would pretty much move from scene to scene quickly and only do 1 or 2 takes per angle... maybe 4 angles per scene.
>
> Ehem...
>
> For a scene that runs 2 minutes long from "Action" to "Cut" this ends up taking about 45 minutes per scene... and with no air conditioning... in a Texas summer... this is WAAAY too long.
>
> (The air conditioning creates a lot of background noise so it is good to shoot without it to avoid audio distortion and noise issues)
>
> So we're all sweating like freaking pigs... and still the missing actor is not here. I've left several messages on his phone and even emailed him but I have had no reply of any kind.
>
> We keep shooting.
>
> After another hour or so, the actors are starting to get kind of pissed off... and I am having one of those weird panic attack things where I can't breathe or think.
>
> FINALLY he calls.
> He cant make it. He thought we were shooting on a different day.
>
> Okeydoke...
>
> Right about now I am about to tell my actors that on the first day of shooting... after all the work I did to redesign my apartment... that I'm gonna have to reschedule. This causes me to feel something similar to nausea... but in my head instead of my stomach... if that makes sense...
>
> As I am explaining it to them... by some miracle... one of the actors suggests that a buddy of his might be able to fill in. I call said buddy. He arrives in about 30 minutes.
>
> The rest of the day is saved and we end up shooting for another 3 hours or so.
> It all goes very well and after a grueling day with no air and a lot of freaking hot lights right over my head... I call a wrap to the first day of shooting on my first movie.
>
> To be Continued...
>
**Sorry to end this here, but I'll continue next week with more.**
**Thanks so much for reading.**
