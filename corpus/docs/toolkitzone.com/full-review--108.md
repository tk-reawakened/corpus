# Full Review

Developer: Maelstrom - Release Date: 2001 (out of 10) Chimeran Empire is best described as a really good Godiva:Alpha. Though it uses ripped/default for all aspects of the game, this game is the perfect example of how a TK game can depend almost solely on gameplay to make it entertaining. Though the demo ends right when the plot begins really unfolding and more gameplay features arrive, the demo still delivers a great gaming experience that anyone in their right mind can enjoy. Though the game does use all ripped tiles, the impressive board design and excellent choice of tiles doesnt let ripped tiles hurt the games overall appeal much at all. Also, Maelstrom did a great job animating the ripped tiles, and Chimeran Empire definitely has the best animated tiles in any TK games Ive played yet. The flowers move, the water splashes against the coast in waves the animated tiles really add life to the boards. Even though the large board sizes can make the areas feel slightly empty, it also creates a good sense of exploration to the game. Thanks to its great gameplay, Chimeran Empire is simply fun - a feat that not many games have accomplished even after several years into TK's existence. The game features great balance, and the various amount of monsters you encounter keeps the battles from feeling repetitive or boring. Also, in Chimeran Empire, even with the default battle system, special moves are still worth using. The special moves actually do enough to be worth the three seconds you waste selecting them. Even though its just a demo, I got a good hour of fun gaming out of this game, which is more than expected from a demo. Although Im not sure if there will be anymore releases of Chimeran Empire, the demo is great fun alone. The music does seemed ripped in the game, but nothing seemed familiar to me, although I cant say Im much of a music expert. However, I do know enough to be certain that the music matches the various moods of the game very well. Though the story did contain its unique twists, there was nothing very innovative to it. It was your regular TK story, and was a bit corny and far-fetched, just like we classic RPGers seem to like em :P The story didnt quite pick up until near the end of the demo, which is a shame, but this game was more gameplay-focused than story-focused, so I gave it a break. Probably on my top five TK games list now, and one of my favorite TK games yet. Its no artistic revolution for the TK that many saw Dustball as, nor has an epic story like Deceit and Conquest, but it has some of the best gameplay Ive ever seen in a TK game, even with the default battle system. **Chimeran Empire**
