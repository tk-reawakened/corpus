# Full Review

Developer: White Divine (Mickey Crocker) - Release Date: 2000-2001 (out of 10) Mickey Crocker, better known as White Divine and the creator of Never-Ending-Scroll, is like any other respected TK developer, he has his own strength. Cyrus knows how to tell a story damn well, Xorlak can take us back to the golden years of Final Fantasy, and Sophia can make a game fun just by using her amazing style to create a fun atmosphere graphically. Mickey knows how to make games fun, and even though Traidon isnt quite Never-Ending-Scroll, its very similar, and hey  it doesnt have a bug that unbalances the entire game. Even though Traidon doesnt feature any original systems, you have to remember that this game is old. I know Traidon has been around for as long as I can remember, and it was actually one of the first TK games I played, which was more than four years ago. So even though it has to depend on the default battle system to deliver entertaining combat, it does infact deliver some fun. It uses the default battle system as effectively as other games like Chimeran Empire, Godiva:Alpha, and Dark Age. Traidon is similar to a Dragon Warrior and Never-Ending-Scroll, so the game has to be balanced because it depends mostly on fighting to entertain the player. It also needs a few other things like various enemies and a nice collection of items to buy from each town, and it does. The one thing the game lacks is special moves  even though it has a few, and they are worth using in boss battles, it only has a couple. Traidon features all original graphics, which is still considered a great accomplishment even these days. The game aims for a classic feel, and for the most part, achieves it. It also features great enemy graphics and some nice story cutscenes similar to Deceit and Conquests. The board design is very similar to classics like Dragon Warrior, meaning they are large-scaled and relatively plain. Unlike Dragon Warrior and Never-Ending-Scroll, Traidon tries to focus a little more on its story. The story is quite cliché, but its told nicely with the use of graphical cutscenes, personality-filled characters, and nicely written dialogue and cutscenes. The music in Traidon seems to all be original. They are also inspired by old-school Dragon Warrior-like games, so theyre a little plain, but they fit the theme of the game and the various atmospheres of the game great. There are sound effects for most of the fighting in the game. The game is around 45 minutes long with not much room for side-questing or what not. Being only a 3.3 MB download, the game is definitely worth the download, no matter what your connection might be. It is a demo of a game that we will never see another release to, which could affect the game a little bit for some people. Overall, its a fun little game, and it happens to end at just about the point where you start getting slightly bored of it, so you can play and enjoy it all in one sitting. To sum it up, Traidon is a classic TK game that, in my eyes, everyone should play. If anything, youll appreciate how well this aged game stacks up to recent releases. Its also one of those games that really make you want to work on your game when you play it, and for some of us thats a huge perk. **Traidon**
