# Getting Details Out of Your Works

Detailing a piece is important, although I admit that all the details in the world could never hide anatomical problems in drawing, but anatomy is relatively harder to study than detailing. We'll take a break from my programming tutorial(mainly because I couldn't find time to continue it) and I'll give you a short step-by-step suggestions on detailing your works. They could be applied to sprites as well, although depending on the size of the sprite, details can be minimal. Here, I'll show you how I make a tree style.... 1.) Draw it. I use two colors and draw what would look like a tree. Normally 5 year-old kids can do this 2.) Next, we add details. But how? Where? What? Ok, let's identify where to add. Focus on the trunk and on your left hand side, focus on the lower part. Now let's identify what we want to add--roots. We are going to add roots. Needless to say, tree trunks are fibrous in nature, so you should set yourself for a lot of work fibering it out. It's quite easy, you'll see. Next is..how to add it. Well, think of the possible shape that the root has...of course it's cylindrical, right? All you gotta do is click on the burn tool(or select a darker color than the brown) and start drawing arcs and curves across the root, like the one I did. Also be ready to erase parts a lot of times. We must trim down the colors to get the shape we want so when adding details you don't necessarily have to take up all the brown area. You should make a convincing root shape instead and the rest can be erased. Compare piece 2 and piece 1..you'll noticed that the root has protruded because of some trimming. That's detail already. 'd say that's level 1 detail..but normally I am not contented with this, although in some occasions detail such as this one is the proper choice. 3.)Level 2 detail. Constantly applying the burn tool(I used the Mid tone mode BTW) I can produce darker areas and therefore more details. Is this enough? I don't think so 4.)Level 3 detail. Added light sourcing. Actually I already had a decided light source that can be seen on step 3. You can see that the darker areas appear on your right hand side, so naturally light comes from your left hand side . I repeatedly say this..never forget the contour of the object. Always follow the shape of an object when shading. I used the dodge tool here BTW, switching from Mid tone and Highlight modes. 5.)The principle had been laid out, so I applied it to finish the trunk: How do I know what to place? Of course I wouldn't know if I didn't study or observe other works! The key to learning is not just practice, but observation as well. I think it's evident enough the tree looks like something from Legend of Mana or other similar artworks. I studied the artworks for months and up until now just to get its feel. I'm glad I'm getting it right Ok, more fibers on the roots. You can see that all roots look basically the same: an arc at regular interval shapes up the roots and the fibers across it. However, the same could not be said for the trunk itself. The lines of the trunks are somewhat vertical. That's what I wanted so that's what I did. Imagine if i did lines across the trunk like the one i did on the roots, I'd end up with a ringed trunk...which is not bad...could be cool, but that's not what I'm aiming for at the moment. At this point, the trunk is considerably done, but on final pieces with sceneries, more shadow-casting and lighting is inevitable. For the purpose of this tutorial, it's done..although I might get carried away and add more details XD So how do I know when it's done and not? It's just a matter of personal preference. Others might say it still lacks something, etc. but that's why you want to ask. That's why they're there--to tell you something that you can't see. Others may agree with you that it's done though. It's really up to your level of satisfaction. Now that the trunk is done, let's move on with the leaves. First thing: leaves are NOT fibrous. At least not like trunks. Remember that. So how do we add details to leaves? Well obviously NOT fibers...but noise. We are going to add small details that would represent leaves. 1.) Add leaves. Leaf size depend on the type of tree, but you must be consistent with a leaf size. You can't just get lazy and make an oversized one just to quicken your work Very tiny leaves, eh? I used a 2px brush here and the usual burn tool. I used the Shadow mode. Depending on the type of tree, you may choose to draw the leaves as a whole or by chunks. This particular one will be done by chunks. We'll finish detailing this part of the leaves... 2.)Detail level2 Do I always start by coloring the dark areas first? Well that's just me, but it doesn't matter whether you start applying lighter or darker shades first. What matters is you where to place those shades. More burning to emphasize on dark shades. Be consistent with the leaf shape. Be consistent with your light source. 3.)Detail level3 Let there be light "Khin, no fair! You jumped too far." Hehe, not really. You see, after level 2 I just applied more burning(Shadow mode, opacity 15%,5px brush) and applied it all over the area that will receive light. Then i switched to dodge tool(Highlight mode, 20% opacity,3px brush size) and started applying it on areas that would get some light. The result is as you see... 4.)Far from finish, needs more shading, but let's finish the whole tree first... Well you'd imagined by now this isn't an easy task to begin with. We still have the rest of the tree to put detail on, but I think I have expressed the idea well enough for you to start making your own. Finished the tree!
