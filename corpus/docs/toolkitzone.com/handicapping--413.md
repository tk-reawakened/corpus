# Handicapping

Browse
What Did You Say?
We recently awarded the Newcomer of the Year award ( ) to a user who went on a reviewing rampage, had his say on over fifty games, and in the process whipped up one hell of a debate about what makes a good review and what a fair score is. [Day 26](http://toolkitzone.com/library.php?doc=409) As shown on [Day 22](http://toolkitzone.com/library.php?doc=405) , this year we've had a record number of reviews written - 201 says on 74 games by 69 users. Not only that, but after Have Your Say was integrated into the forums we also saw a record breaking 310 comments discussing the reviews.
So in our final non-award article of the Zone Awards we're taking a general look at some of the questions raised over the past year and hopefully you guys can discuss it and share your views. Ultimately, I hope to improve the system further. As a result, several issues, not least where Field Marshal Shockwave was concerned, were raised by users in regards to the quality of reviews and the review system.
**Handicapping**
The old chestnut - should we rate a full game on the same scale as a demo game? There is a one scale fits all rating system, there is no distinction made for the type of game. Therefore, is an hour long release deserving of a near perfect score in the length category just because it's a demo and not a full game? Many users openly justify the scores they give games by conceding it was because "it's a demo", therefore handicapping the score to a higher rating. Is this an acceptable review strategy? I plan to seperate demo and full games eventually, but what about mini-games? They are a full game in the technical sense. Where do we draw the line?
**Scoring**
Is 60% a bad score? Recently a well known developer bemoaned a score he received because the 60ish score wasn't befitting, in his eyes, of the description of a "fun" game. The problem isn't that 60% is a bad score, it's that we naturally relate percentages to things like school exams where a score around 50% is considered pretty much a failure. Is it so hard for people to remember that the Have Your Say system clearly defines the meaningfulness of the scores? That 60-69% score is actually read as "Decent to Good". Does the rating scale need to change? Should we abandon percentages and just use the descriptive alternatives to stop people moaning about numbers?
**Bumping**
This is something that I hate with a passion. While the other issues described in this article are understandable and ultimately part a genuine review, this particular issue is the work of your classic noob or smartass who wants to abuse the system and undermine the hard work of the community. Bumping is when a user deliberately gives a bumped score despite it not being in keeping with their actual views of game because they feel the average score the game has received from other reviewers is not sufficient enough. There is no debate for this issue; it's an idiotic thing to do and that's that.
**Reasoning**
Of course, what makes a good review is reason and justification. It doesn't matter what score you give it, it's meaningless unless you explain why. It seems like common sense, but the majority of reviews are just two or three lines that say "it was fun" or "not stable" but don't explain why. How can games improve if developers are not told the flaws? What's the point reading a review, as a player, if it doesn't tell you what to expect from the game? How can we promote more articulate reviews? Do we have some kind of balancing system that ignores or lessens the effect of reviews with negative "helpfulness" voting? Personally, I like the following idea... should we introduce a secondry "review" tool that simply acts as a 1-10 "rate me" slider for people who want to quickly pass opinion on a game? If we do that, does it count to the rankings or do only "real" reviews carry weight? Does the slider act as a final "tilt" on the average reviews?
*Please do share your opinions. That fact we've had so many game reviews this year is amazing, not one in my ten years here have I seen so much feedback - it can only be good. But no system is perfect so let's strive to improve it further in 2009.*

Rating a game, and the creator's evaluation of that rating, is pretty much 100% about perception. If we rated out of 10, 60, or 100 makes no diffrence, rather if it's percieved as being fair or not fair. I really think a few words in the system would work to clarify this. Giving a percent score some text that describes the grade region it falls into would certainly soften the blow (or curb over-elation) of a score; also it would prove a tool to rationalize ratings.
