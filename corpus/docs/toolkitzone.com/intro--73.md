# ------------------ Intro ------------------

The year is 2084...the world is not what it once was. In the Americas, domestic upheaval rocks the area's once stable government. Overseas, political differences only grow more intense. Because of these complications, the European Union has essentially been dissolved; many of the smaller nations that made up its once thriving populace are being left vulnerable to military invasions and intelligence theft. Those larger countries that have survived this separation have recompacted to form what is now called the Coalition of Political Seperationism (more commonly known as the CoPS). In a scene reminiscent to the Holocaust of Hitler's rule, the CoPS are conducting underground genocides of the hundreds of small political and ethnic groups that oppose the Coalition's formation. Europe as a whole has become the centerpoint of the world's black market and many small terrorist organizations.
Civil wars in the Middle East have destroyed the oil industry. Young American companies hastily put the finishing touches on experiments with alternative energy sources. The most well-known of these, Globotech Industries, has just announced an ethanol-like fuel composed mainly of prairie grasses. The American government, headed by current President Paul Wayneright, has bought the formula from Globotech in an attempt to get a stranglehold on the world's fuel supply. Obviously, this has created rising tensions with America's allies, which have become few and far between by this point. Small "rebel" militias of American extremists have tried without avail to procure this formula. The skirmishes resulting from these attempts have drawn international attention to the United States and the way they deal with their citizens.
Within the States, mounting numbers of troops are being shipped overseas in an attempt to cease violences, but create only more. The largest of such deployments are taking place in France, where CoPS activity seems to be the thickest. Upon arrival, mass graves of anti-CoPS activists are unearthed and POW camps filled with still more protestors are discovered. Bloody outbreaks of violence begin to take place, and what the whole world feared most is beginning to unfold right before its eyes... ---------------------- Chapter 1 ----------------------
The tension and anxiety inside the Chicago airport that morning were thick enough to choke on. The young families of camoflauge-clad men stuck to them as if it were the last time they would ever see them again. In all reality, it very well could be. The youngest of the soldiers sat in the corner chairs, staring intently at the dreary tile floor. There was no one there to comfort them. They had nothing to lose.
Earlier that morning, banner waving protestors had lined the streets, yelling profanely at the riot control officers. Some had retaliated when the police tried to convince them to return to their homes. A few well-placed rocks from the crowd nearly set off a wave of violence, but the officers skillfully apprehended their attackers, prompting the remaining thrawl of people to relocate. That had become the norm among these troop deployments. Nobody wanted them to happen, really, or at least that's how it seemed. The troops themselves were demoralized from the heavy losses that were taking place overseas. President Wayneright and Congress had promised on many occasions that the violence would soon be over, but it was becoming increasingly evident that no such armistice would be reached in the near future.
The assembled troopers had less than a couple hours left until they would systematically board the personell transport plane and be air-lifted into a rural area of France. Of course, the soldiers had not been told this. All military activity was becoming increasingly top secret in an attempt to keep the element of suprise. In this time of technology, though, the "information superhighway" was becoming more and more accessible to almost anyone who had the patience enough to decipher it. In a country that had once seemed so safe and secure, everyone was being watched, listened to. They had no idea.
Outside the teeming airport, a young soldier leaned against the brick structure, cool gray eyes focusing on nothing in particular. He snuffed out his cigarette butt with the toe of his boot as he allowed his lungs to fill with a breath of morning air. The metropolitan air had become no better than a mouthful of tobacco smoke, worse if anything. Smog and acid rain had reduced the skyline to one of constant grayness. Day and night seemed almost undistinguishable at times. He ran his fingers through his close-cut hair, gazing down at the patch on his camoflauge jaket. "Sgt. Hope". It seemed nowdays that wearing a label like this was the only way to get any recognition. The chevrons on his arms and the ribbons on his chest were his identity.
This would be the first time that Sergeant Christopher Hope would see any real combat. Despite his rank and the many awards he had earned in the military, there were many men getting on the plane with him that morning that had seen more action than he ever could have dreamed. He had been tought to shoot his assault rifle, to survive in hostile environments without food or water, but nothing could prepare him for what he was being sent into. The media tried to butter everything up, make the war seem less than it really was. Although the draft had come back into effect, the majority of the soldiers had signed up voluntarily. Times were rough, and the military offered a steady paycheck if you were one of the few lucky enough to make it out alive.
All this played in Hope's mind as he watched strangers carry on with their day-to-day routine across the street. Occasionally, their gaze would meet his and they would look away quickly, ashamed for him, ashamed that he was there in his camo and heavy black boots and they weren't. He chuckled silently as he watched them pass.
Christopher Hope was not from Chicago, nor was he from Illinois. He came from a small town in Nebraska, where things were different from the rest of the country. The midwest, it seemed, was the only part of the world not influenced by all the politics and the war. He was not a farmer's son, but a normal boy who had grown up in a class of about 30 other children, had graduated, and who had planned to go to college. During the summer after his senior year in high school, he had gone to a few seminars put on by the the US Army, and had looked into the possibility of going to college on a paid scholarship from the government. He had reluctantly signed up, not imagining that only 18 months later, the most horrific and wide-spread war in the world's history would be underway. Now they had him right where they wanted him; a 4-year contract with his signature on it saying that he would get to go to college for free...if he lived that long.
As for a wife and kids, Chris had none. He was 20 years old now, easily old enough for the responsiblity, but he had left his high-school lover behind when he was relocated to North Carolina for basic training. Her name was Cindy Fisher. She was your typical blond-haired, blue eyed, 4.0 GPA valadictorian. The two of them had been close since their freshman year, and Cindy had wept for days when she found out that Chris was leaving. He had not heard from her since. It made more sense to leave her without saying anything. Chris knew that he would have looked into her puppy-dog eyes and she would have made him promise that he'd be back. That was a promise he wasn't confident in making.
He was startled when the voice over the PA in the airport broke his silence. "All military units of the Chicago 314th Infantry, begin boarding on bay 7. Takeoff is in 15 minutes." Taking one last look up into the sky, Sergeant Christopher Hope sighed and walked into the airport, preparing for what he knew would be the most disturbing chapter of his life.
