# Khin on Heraldry: Freija

UltimaWizard Presents... Well, Toolkit community, you may have seen an interview or two with Khin before on his conquests in the Expos that happen every summer and winter...but I have a true treat for you, with Khin's permission--an interview with him all about his project , currently one of if not the best-looking and best-rated games for the Toolkit. Many TK Gamers practically drool over every newly released screenshot, every new addition to the already lush profile...and for the TK Community in general, I'm going to be asking Khin some questions about what started Heraldry off, what's currently in the works...and what's still in store. For those who do not already know, is another game currently being created with the TK3.x by the Pigment Arts Company, who are Khin's devoted team of fellow game makers. Features incredibly detailed backgrounds, lush music, a clock for each area you're in that tells what time of the day it is in-game, and a Real-Time Battle system that though it resembles Street Fighter in some respects is purely RPG when it comes to it. Further, the pages created by Khin barely begin to describe the amount of depth to the world of Heraldry, though they tell much about the many races that inhabit the world young Freija Moonault and her companions explore on the course of their travels. Now that you're up to speed with the current status of Heraldry, it's time for us to talk to the big man himself, Khin. Let's get this interview started! UltimaWizard: Ok, let's start at the beginning. What inspired you to create ? Khin: Basically great games I've played in the past...like Legend of Mana, and FFTactics to name the most prominent. ...but let me tell you something I've never told anyone before about the origin of Heraldry: It was in 2002(or I might have remembered wrong) when I stumble upon this PSX game called RPGMaker...I was always the "maker" type. Whenever I play strategy games the first feature I look for is the "Map Editor". I dunno why, ut it was just my nature to customize and create. So there it was, RPGMaker for the Playstation 1. I was so thrilled and wanted to start making games already...coincidentally, my younger brother Frei is writing this small novel about his highschool friends in this imaginary world based on any fantasy-based story, especially since he's been into that "Harry Potter" series. I then introduced Frei to RPGMaker and he liked it. We conceptualized a game based on the novel he was making. Of course I could have just chosen my own novels(comic books that I made back then come in numbers), but they're not much of the fantasy type; they're more of the anime/manga and not very good as a RPG. The game was entitled Journey To the Crescent Moon, which was the original title of his novel...I then added "Heraldry" to the title because I thought it sounded good and to emphasize the fact that I'll/we'll be adding follow-ups to this chapter. I got the term "Heraldry" from "Star Ocean 2" where Celine(and any other black magic users) were called "Heralds". The "David Star" symbol that has become the official symbol of the game was from RPGmaker(PSX). There were boards with the symbol etched on the floor, as well as a preset title screen. Frei then went his way to study the symbol and it turned out to have some sort of magical significance so we went for it. Suffice to say, Journey To The Crescent Moon was being developed with rich storyline and characters..not to mention a rich world with history and such. It got finished and we enjoyed it..but sooner or later PSX was phased out plus no one was really able to play the game we made but ourselves. Then I went on to search for a PC version. Ashe stumbled upon this RPGToolkit. It was version 2 back then. Once I found out about it I started tinkering with it and in no time I was remaking Journey To The crescent Moon in it. Frei was still with me but due to the fact that we don't have our own PC back then we had to rent..and Frei couldn't always go with me to walk some miles to the nearest PC shop. JTTCM was never finished, sadly, as I had found lots bugs that discouraged me from using it further. Then TK3 was released. Some time in 2003-2004 I returned to the place where I got TK2 and found out there's a version 3 available. I was in my On The Job Training then so I had all the internet access a 12-hour shift could give. I downloaded it and found out you can now use bitmaps instead of tiles for use as a board...I used to hate making tiles back then. When I got home I told Frei about it..and in good time too because we already had our own PC then. It was now time to go make Heraldry once again. But it wasn't JTTCM that we made. During the course of the game's on-and-off development, more storylines were created, history was refined and redefined, and one of the most prominent figure in the story was "Freija". Freija was hailed as a historian, carthographer, poet, scientist, and a great Herald. Every documents/discoveries that the heroes would make is becuase of the help of her past works. It was in this reason that we felt we should start the series with this Freija character first so we could introduce the world, then we'll just delve into branching stories afterwards. This is the main reason why was conceived. But still, the graphics and gameplay of Heraldry:Freija was based on Legend of Mana, FFTactics, Star Ocean 2. UltimaWizard: That's incredibly interesting. Moving along, though, you just mentioned the art of Heraldry, another extremely strong point of the series. I don't think there's a TKGamer who's played your demo who hasn't actually drooled at the graphics, they're easily industry-grade. It might seem silly considering what you've just said about the origins of Heraldry, but where did you get the ideas for the graphical style you used in general for this project? Khin: Let me jut enumerate all RPGS I loved playing: Final Fantasy 8, Star Ocean 2, Valkyrie Profile, Final Fantasy Tactics, Legend of Mana, and Tales Of Destiny 2. Those are the RPGs I finished. The others I got tired of for many reasons. So then let me ask you: among those games which graphics is the best for you? We may have different opinions, plus many would pick FF8, but all in all Legend of Mana appealed to me the most. I've made the decision to make H:F look at least like Legend Of Mana..colorful, pastel-like, twisted architecture, plus its more feasible than FF8. I noticed almost everyone wants my(our) graphics but let me tell you the truth: I was frustrated. Why? Well just get a snapshot of LoM and put it side by side with one of H:F's (old) boards. I don't think they'll even be a match. There's something in LoM that I couldn't quite capture and having no formal degree at Art I lack that training about color study, etc.. I'm learning everything by myself. This is why I redrew some boards and will redraw more once the BS and MS are done. I'm quit happy with the turn out of the new boards though, but they're not the breath-taking shots; just basic interior. UltimaWizard: Well, it's quite funny that you mentioned that [lack of formal training], actually, considering that this interview is aimed at newbies and intermediate developers who are using the TK. I've noticed plenty of games including and that couldn't match Heraldry['s graphics] and still are rated quite high--Heraldry may be one of the best looking TK games, but it's still a somewhat traditional RPG in some senses. What I think a lot of the community would love to know is what will diffrentiate Heraldry's gameplay experience from other TK games, and how do you plan to go about achieving making Heraldry a truly unique and enjoyable experience? Khin: Systems. I am yet to play a professional RPG that lets the gamer switch modes of Battle. Arcade lovers will prefer the "Active Battle" mode as it plays much like Street Fighter crossed with Legend of Mana, hile fans of the traditional time-based/turn-based will prefer the "Wait Battle" mode where they can employ more strategies. The game also hopes to see the light of cooking, smithing, composing, and various creation system that we'll call under the common name "Forge". And of course, minigames. We already have one(the Sungka) and we plan on adding more. Minigames are basically simpler games anyway so they could come one at a time and probably would take me 1 week tops for each minigame. One of the most promising minigames however is the Heraldry Tarot Cards. We know many gamers like the battle card and Frei's fascination for real tarots gave me an idea. Now we'll just figure out how to make the AI "think" as we've already laid out basic . But all that jazz will go to waste if the current engine isn't stable..so let me just say beforehand that if anyone is curious as to when we'll release a full game here's our one and only answer: "The game will not be released until there's a complete Toolkit to run on." We don't want to promise too much things, but hey, dreaming is free and we're dreaming at PAC. UltimaWizard: Well, well, well. I'm afraid I'm drooling yet again, especially over the Composing aspect because I'm silly that way...which delivers me to my next question. Your music really inspired me for composition with my game, , it's pretty decent. Are you just using generic MIDI music, or is your music, like the rest of Heraldry, something with significance and complexity that will also amaze the TK community? Khin: Well there's a downer right there. We're being imprisoned with filesize consideration so we don't know yet if we should go midi, mp3, or wav. As far as tools are concerned though, we have Sibelius and SoundForge so we'll never be short. Sibelius allows creation of scores that would later be converted to MIDI format by starting from scratch, as if you were really using a musical manuscript. Soundforge allows mixing of sounds and can output to many music files except midi. I also play guitar so I have musical sense, Ashe and Frei knows piano and Frei knows flute. Right now we're not yet delving much into the audio department as it requires longer time and deeper inspiration to be done, but in the future most usic will be home-made with the exception of specials from respected community members. UltimaWizard: That's ok--as you've said, you've still got a long ways to go. And while we're on the subject...well, what exactly *do* you have in mind next for besides the Systems, more lush art, and hopefully more music in the future? Khin: Hmmm..what else would I want... Well after all that I wish the game be finished before it gets reeaallly outdated. I wanna try out different graphical approaches already but I can't. I must stick to one or else I'll never finish(take note newbies!) UltimaWizard: Well, before we go ahead and end this, there's one question I know is not strictly limited to H:F, but is worth asking anyways--when H:F *is* complete...what's next for Pigment Arts? Khin: Heraldry: Journey To The Crescent Moon (TK) , Heraldry: Great Divide (Java, C++ or any platform that would cater)...basically we're not going to stop making games. Well, TK community, there we have it about all kinds of things involving Pigment Arts Company and . With some advice to newbies--don't shift visual styles in the middle of your game--and with more information about Heraldry than I think anyone dreamt of getting, well, we'll be anticipating the BS and MS demos of Heraldry: Freija. Get your drool containers ready!
