# - MrB

You can sum Dustball up in one word  fun, and thats what counts. DustBall has always been one of my favorite TK games. Even being the gameplay fan that I am, I cant help but love this game. Dustball was developed by Sophia, and like she proves in Rudolphs Adventures  she knows how to make fun, stylish games. Sophia has now graduated college to become a professional artist, with her forte being animations. This game doesnt have much other than graphics and style, and just like with gameplay for games like Chimeran Empire, Godiva:Alpha, and Traidon, graphics and style almost alone can make a game fun also. Sophia taught me the ropes of TK, and we used to chat pretty much daily, and Id always ask her how her cat game was going. Back then I wasnt too excited about the idea or concept of the game, being the thirteen year old kid trying to make a Final Fantasy-wannabe that I was, but when Dustball finally arrived, I was blown away. Just like Deceit and Conquest grabs you with its intense story, DustBall grabs you with its amazing style, atmosphere, and humor. So how does Sophia achieve this? Mostly the little things. A few examples theres a house in the town where a weird man lives, and the house is a mess including spider webs scattered across the furniture, walls, and floor. Theres quite a few little things like this scattered around the demo that most TK games dont feature because its a lot of effort for the player just to see once. Also, there seems to be dozens of different message window profiles for the main characters in the game, each perfectly portraying a different emotion. A few other examples that are a bit larger scale are the graphical cutscene images used in the major story sequence of the demo, and how each character has its own individual personality that aids and supports the story. Unfortunately the game depends on the default battle system and menu system when needed. The game does a relatively good job using the default systems to the best of their abilities, but the game is generally too easy and lacks special moves. DustBall learns two special moves, both which are effective and useful, but James doesnt have any at all. This is understandable since there just isnt much fighting in the game, but it would have been nice to see one or two more. Musically, the game does feature all ripped songs. All the songs fit the game great, but a few are just a little too familiar for me. When I can name the songs game and am flooded with memories of the song's original game, it distracts you from DustBall. Fortunately, that doesnt happen more than once or twice in this game, at least for me. Theres also a fairly nice collection of sound effects for the games battle system and story sequences. Like I mentioned before, the story of Dustball is told amazingly, matching if not passing the quality of even Dark Age 2. With an amazing intro, a large collection of message window character portraits, great individual personalities for each character, and a story that we can relate to that isnt based on saving the world, this game delivers an experience that is unarguably fun. The game tells the story of a trouble-making, talking cat, and it does a great job doing so. I suggest Dustball to anyone that is looking to have an entertaining and relaxing time, no matter the connection speed. Its a relatively small download size, and the game will give you a good thirty minutes of fun, so go for it and enjoy.
