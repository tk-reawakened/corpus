# - MrB

Perhaps barely, but somehow this game pulls off what most TK games don't... it's fun. If you ask the average TKer how many games they've played, they'll probably give a few of the most popular games to be created, like Deceit and Conquest, Sophia's games, and the Dark Age's. Why? Because these games are fun - something that is much harder to accomplish than it seems at first. Deceit and Conquest's story that is fit for a commercial game, Sophia's style in both artistic quality and humor, and Xorlak's ability to give us another old school Final Fantasy, something most of us can never get enough of. And just like those games all had their own unique method of creating an entertaining experience, Just Another Day, though in a smaller quantity and quality, pulled it off uniquely. The title tells you the basics on the game - it's just another day. You're a happily married man that unfortunately looses his wedding ring one day infront of the bathroom sink. Since you seem to have one of those wives that would get extremely upset over something like this, you actually decide to take some action and drive down to the sewer plant to wander aimlessly around the underground sewer system in a desperate search for the ring. You do end up finding it pretty easily - and that's about the game. Doesn't sound fun? Well, read on. There's no fighting in this game at all... a concept that isn't that new to TK games, but this game pretty much pulls it off. Instead of fighting, you can shop for Tick-Tat-Toe and Etch-a-Sketch's and fool around with those (Tick-Tat-Toe I couldn't get to work because of a bug in the game), or you can bet on horse races. Though not extremely exciting, the simple fact that these possibilities are new to TK games keeps you on, maybe not the edge of your seat, but at least not slumped over like you're sitting in math class. Maybe the mysterious concept of the game helped it to actually let me have fun playing it. There's countless of games over at MS, and when you download one, you really don't know what to expect. Usually not much if you've never heard of the game, but this game really grabbed me from the first scene in the shower to the ending credits. This game is actually very well-done graphically. The game features all original graphics, and they're surprisingly well-done. The simplicity of the modern-themed tiles really fits the game well. Like I said before, there's no fighting in the game, so there was no battle system to speak of, but the custom menu system, though a little too simple, had a nice look to it. Most of the music is ripped, and even though not from familiar commercial RPGs, there are little classic tunes you'll hear that you wouldn't predict to see in an RPG, but they had a humorous sense to the game. The credits do say "Thing" composed some music, and I'm guessing he must have composed the sewer song and/or the start menu theme, which were actually very nicely composed. There are some parts of the game that do lack any music at all however. For a game full of simple mini-games, the game doesn't do bad at all. The Etch-a-Sketch was well done, and even though it's a pretty simple concept to accomplish, it felt exactly like drawing with the classic preschool toy. The horse races, also a simple concept, was also effective, and was fun to excitingly stare at the screen hoping for your horse to cross the finish line first. "Thing", the developer of the game, was smart - he picked easy mini-games to do, and made sure they would be fun. The lesson learned from this game is that you don't need to create something like FF8's ingenious and addictive card game to make your game fun, you just gotta be smart. The game is extremely short, and even though you can sit around and bet on horse races happily for awhile, there isn't much to do or see. There's a relatively large portion of a town to explore, but nearly all the doors were disappointingly locked. The download is small though, and any bored DSL/Cable TKers out there should definitely fool around with this game. Even some 56kers would probably think the download wait was worth it, though I have a feeling you just have to be in the right mood to play this game. It really has no meaning or interesting story, but overall... it's somewhat fun, and that's what ultimately matters.
