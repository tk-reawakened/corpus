# - MrB

As long as you're not in a battle, you should be somewhat enjoying yourself as you watch Angel Tears' well-told story unfold. For how much TK developers like to think over and share their stories before the game is actually done, you'd think we'd see more solid storytelling in TK games. I've only played a handful or so games that have successsfully gotten their story across so that it could be enjoyed, and I'm happy to add Angel Tears to the list. The dialogue is some of the best you'll see in any game, and the rather unique story is enough to keep you interested until the game ends after a good 20 minutes of playing. And there is actually a logical ending to the game! Even though it ends on a bit of a cliffhanger, a solid ending and nicely done ending credits feels like you've actually played, completed, and enjoyed a role-playing gaming experience. It's a nice change of pace. Angel Tears uses the spiffy new RPGMaker XP tilesets and characters, and add in some good board design, and you have a rather good-looking game. Visually, the cutscenes were messy with cut-off heads and random sprites appearing onscreen every once and awhile. And the game runs in TK2 unfortunately, so the battles are also messy visually and the scrolling and character movement is about as smooth as asphalt. As I said before, the story all comes together nicely to deliver a good gaming experience. You'll feel like you're actually playing a complete game where things make sense and characters have personalities. It's a nice length and will leave you with a smile on your face probably. The dialogue is virtually perfect grammatically and tells the story nicely. Some of the cutscenes are pretty poorly programmed, then stick it in TK2, and you have some ugly moments, but the story still manages to shine through. Gameplay is definitely Angel Tears' worst aspect. The only good thing to say is that there's not much fighting in the game and the battles aren't impossible. The special moves are confusing and prove to be basically useless. You virtually just tap ENTER the entire game, maybe glancing down at your party's HP once or twice. And it's TK2's battle system, so you'll find a fair share of just stupid things happening randomly. The menu system is the whole" custom main menu, then pops up the separate default system areas" deal. It works, and you won't find yourself having to use the menu much at all anyways. The one drawback is that there didn't seem to be a way to look at your special moves for explanations of what they did. The game uses various songs from different music packs out there. None of it was really recognizable, it all sounded good, and it all fitted the game's moods nicely. The battle sound effects could get obnoxious and were incredibly loud, so just having nothing play would probably have been better. To sum it up, Angel Tears is one of the few games out there where you can play it, finish it, and feel like you had a real gaming experience. The story makes sense, entertains, and ends nicely. If you can get past the dull and buggy fighting and the messy cutscenes, then you should enjoy Angel Tears.

I do not compare TK games to professional games - that'd be ridiculous. Creating a TK game is a hobby of usually a single person. I compare TK games to other TK games. I compare a game's gameplay to games like DAEX and Mortalis, I compare a game's graphics to games like Dustball, DA2, H:F, Quintessence, Illicit Soul, and TK Land. I compare a game's sound to games like DAEX, DA2, Alcatraz, and D&C. I compare a game's value to games like DAEX, Never-Ending-Scroll, and D&C. I compare a game's story (and how well it's told) to games like DAEX, DA2, and D&C. A 10 in gameplay would be a game with an original system, balanced fighting, a nice collection of items and spells for the characters, a variety in the fighting, etc. I should have the review for Dark Age EX up by tonight. It received a gameplay score of 10. A 10 in graphics would be of exceptional quality, and I take games on the horizon into consideration (H:F, Quintessence, Illicit Soul). I have not given a 10 in graphics to any game yet, but 9s went to Dustball and DA2 demo. I image 10s will go to H:F, Quintessence, and perhaps Illicit Soul and TK Land when/if they are released if the visual quality is consistent with the screenshots that have been released of the games. A 10 in sound would of course original compositions that fit the mood of the game when they play. Also, battle sound effects play a large role. DA2 demo received a 10 in sound. A 10 in story would be a well-told story that is intriguing and original. Also, the quality of the cutscenes come into play here. I don't recall ever giving a 10 in story to a game, but I have not reviewed any D&C games. But from what I've played of them (which is a good bit), they would receive a score of 10. The dialogue is well-written, the cutscenes are exceptional with great illustrations to aid at some points, and the story is very interesting and unique. A 10 in value would be either an appropiately long game, a game with good replay value, and/or a game with many option mini-games and whatnot. DAEX has received a 10 in value. The game is 7-9 hours long with several option sidequests involved.
