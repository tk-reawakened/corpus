# NOT

**
Creative Writing
Session 2:
The Power of Patience
The intended purpose of these articles is to explain some of the elements involved in the creative writing process. These elements range from the overall concept of story telling to the most specific details necessary to create a written tale. These articles will each focus on one individual consideration that the writer must master to successfully share a message, ideas or feelings with the audience. This means that it is my intent to help provide you with the wisdom I have gained studying the writing process. It is my intention to make any one feel unintelligent or untalented. My opinions and perspectives are not intended to replace your own nor do I expect anyone to change their minds after reading my words. I love writing and my intent is to help you love it too. I hope you will find this information useful and I hope you will share what you have learned with others who are looking for some insight as a writer. Thank you.**
Purpose:
**NOT**
Ok, so youve finished your Rough-cut and youve been talking with your bud about the story. Your friend is convinced that you are about to write a story the likes of which even God has never seen. You feel confident and are ready to get started writing
But
As you start filling the first page, you try to imagine the events you are describing. Its easy at first, but the longer you write you have more and more trouble visualizing things happening. What is going on here? Well, you have probably made one of the great classic mistakes that we writers fall victim toimpatience. Youve put a lot of thought into what you are about to write and most of it is worked out pretty well in your head. You even have a fairly decent first draft that outlines the basic ideas and events of your story. The problem is, you are having a hard time relating to your own story. How is this possible? How can someone come up with a story that cant be visualized even by the writer? As I said, the answer is impatience.
Patience is VERY important to a writer in many ways. A writer without patience is a writer with no real skill. You may wonder what having patience has to do with being a good writer. You may think that patience is the same as hesitation, but I assure you there is a difference and I will explain this now.
**
Hesitation
Someone who hesitates is someone who is unsure of what to do. If you are not sure how to proceed as a writer, then you may find yourself questioning your own ability or the purpose of your story. You may also hesitate during the actual writing process by avoiding issues you have not resolved within the plot or you may just put off an event because you arent sure where it should go in the time line on the events you are writing. Hesitation is a problem common to those who do not adequately prepare writing the final draft. Hesitation only happens if you reach a point you have either not prepared for enough or not prepared for at all. The only exception to this is when a writer changes his mind. If you change your mind during the final draft, you had better be sure you are doing so with a great deal of forethought. If not, then you are tipping a domino that will eventually leave you in quite a mess. **before**
If you find yourself hesitating occasionally, do not worry. This happens to us all. If you are often troubled about what you are writing though, then you should stop and take a long look at your rough-cut. If you arent confident about what you write then no one else will be. Prepare as best you can. Imagine as many details as possible and you will begin to realize your story in a whole new way. Hesitation is a reflection of how we feel about our writing. Writers have enough going against them so dont make things any harder than they already are. Get as much of the heavy lifting done during the first draft as possible so that you will be free to see your world as you write the final draft. I promise youll have a much better time.
Patience
Patience is something we learn because its smart. What does that mean? Well, we learn to be patient because of all the trouble we get into without it. This is true in life, as it is true in writing. If you eat your ice cream too fast, you get an awful headache. If you run by the swimming pool, you slip and fall. The same thing happens to writers. Ok, so youre probably wondering what I mean by that. Its simple really. An impatient writer will rush though the parts of a story that arent of as much interest in order to get to the "good" part. An impatient writer will write descriptions that readers cant visualize. An impatient writer will start the story in the wrong place or forget to tell the reader something vital or even give up out of frustration. We are all impatient. We all do things that we later regret. Usually we try and make up for our impetuous behavior as best we can and we learn patience as we go. As a writer, you will not start with a wealth of patience. You may never develop the patience and discipline to write a story of epic length, but you may not want to. You will learn to be patient enough to accomplish what you wish. In time, this knowledge will help you to write how you wish to and what you wish to.
A writer who can learn to wait is a master. When you are able to write your story with full knowledge of the events and choose when and where to reveal them, then you will have stepped across a line that only a skilled craftsman can tread. A master of this craft is not someone who is smarter or more talented than every one else. A master is someone who has learned how to tell a good story not just think one up. The key to doing so is patience. Learn to wait. Learn to weave your tale. Control what you write with preparation and forethought and you will amaze even yourself. Writing is called a craft for a reason. Much as a painter must study the elements and skills of that art form; so must a writer learn his. Open your mind to a deeper understanding of what it means to tell a story. You will be astonished at what you will become.
How you write and what you write are up to you.
T hese are but words of advice
Thank you.
