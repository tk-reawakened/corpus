Creative Writing
Session 4:
Maintaining Continuity
The intended purpose of these articles is to explain some of the elements involved in the creative writing process. These elements range from the overall concept of story telling to the most specific details necessary to create a written tale. These articles will each focus on one individual consideration that the writer must master to successfully share a message, ideas or feelings with the audience. This means that it is my intent to help provide you with the wisdom I have gained studying the writing process. It is my intention to make any one feel unintelligent or untalented. My opinions and perspectives are not intended to replace your own nor do I expect anyone to change their minds after reading my words. I love writing and my intent is to help you love it too. I hope you will find this information useful and I hope you will share what you have learned with others who are looking for some insight as a writer. Thank you.**

Recognizing some of the more profound instances where continuity can come into question can help monumentally and minimize future problems. **Continuity** **maintain a standard of consistency**
Past, Present and Future
During the drafting process you will have to decide how to structure your story. This structure is the foundation that will support all of the details and events of your tale. How you present this to the reader is vitally important and a major part of that will be the that you choose to write in. There are really only 3 options for this: past, present and future. Writing in the past tense will tend to create a sense of events that have already occurred and are now being revealed. Writing in the present will tend to make the events seem as though they are happening right now. The future tense is not typically used for writing entire narratives, but can be very useful as writing in this tense tends to have very powerful emotions attached to it. You will probably use all 3 tenses during the drafting of your work, but only one of them will take precedence in your structure. You may change between these tenses often, but unless you do so for a specific reason or at a specific juncture in your story you will only confuse your audience and risk loosing their . Changing the tense of your writing style erratically is a terrible mistake and a flagrant loss of continuity. Some rules can be broken, and some exist because to help you and the reader. Dialog does not apply to these rules because characters can speak any way they wish. You are the narrator. Think of writing as though you are telling a story to someone and all of this will make more sense to you and the audience. **tense** **Suspension of Disbelief (see Article 5)**
Perspectives
Some times a character is actually telling the narrative. Sometimes the writer is telling the story. Some times this can change during the story. The perspective you choose to write in is entirely at your discretion however if you change the perspective without informing the reader that you are doing so, you will destroy all of your focus and completely draw the audience out of the work. Someone reading your story will get either confused or irritated by such a perplexing shift. Changing the perspective will completely skew the way in which your story is described and explained. Even worse would be to change from the perspective of one character to that of another. Surely you can see how this would affect the reader. Maintaining a consistent tunnel through which the events of the story are described will keep the readers attention and help keep you from making mistakes. Shifting the perspective is something you can undertake in certain circumstances but it should never be done on a whim and should not be done without establishing this change with your reader. If the reader doesnt know or understand what is happening then you have only yourself to blame. Consider the reader at all times. Youre not trying to win a prize for dramatic structural shifts, and nobody thinks that confusing the audience is a sign of a writers talent. You might be smart, but if you think that you are clever because you can toy with a reader then you are wrong. A writer does not try to "out-smart" his reader. A writer is a storyteller and it is your job to make the story enjoyable and entertaining. Anyone who intentionally focuses on manipulating the audience rather than entertaining them has completely missed the point. **dead**
Truth
Establishing truths in a story is very important. Concepts like gravity, the presence of light or darkness; good and evil are just examples of a few truths that a writer must establish. As a writer, it is your job to explain to the reader what the truths of your tale are. Some stories can accomplish this by referencing actual events or ideas from the real world where as some must be explained entirely from scratch. Depending on the length and detail of your work, the amount of truths may vary. These truths must remain constant though unless you make a conscious effort to change them. An example of this would be the Star Wars films. George Lucas made a conscious decision during the creation of the first film that sound could be heard during the space battles in these movies. While in reality no sound can be heard in space, Lucas decided that it would make the story more exciting and interesting to change this rule. The decision he made paid off for him. Most people who enjoy those films agree that this was a good decision. The audience appreciated his choice because it helped draw them into the story and by consistently staying true to this, he has kept a sense of consistency throughout all of his movies. If he were to change this though, you can imagine the confusion it would create. The same is true with any of the truths that a writer must establish. Once you set the rules, they can not be broken unless you have a really good reason and have prepared the audience for the change. Remember, if you loose the audience it is your fault. You are the writer and there is no one else to blame. Be aware that a person will read your story and try to keep the feelings of that reader in mind.
Continuity is the responsibility of the author. It is up to you to keep your story consistent and coherent. Keep your reader in mind at all times. Stick to your decisions. Do not try to outsmart anyone. The audience is smarter than you think. The public is often dismissed as stupid or uneducated but this is simply not true. You are not writing to a bunch of idiots and your ability to create does not make you any better than anyone. I assure you that if you proceed with an inflated ego, you will find the audience you want to write to will have nothing to do with you. Use your ability and skill to entertain or inspire and you will learn and grow with your audience. There is much that you can accomplish
How you write and what you write are up to you.
