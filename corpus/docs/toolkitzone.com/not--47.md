# NOT

From here on out, the concepts we discuss are going to get more complicated and will require you to think beyond the obvious forms of storytelling that a general education can provide. Once you really begin advancing as a writer, you will become aware of the true complexity of what it means to be an effective storyteller. There are formulas based on psychology that were developed by geniuses like Shakespeare, Hemmingway, and Frost. There is a reasoning behind elements that more than likely exist in your story which you have never been aware of. You wrote out an idea that you thought sounded "cool" or "interesting" and you may not have even known why it roused your interest. A good writer can get by feelings. A great writer is a student feelings. Psychology is perhaps the most important field a writer can study. The range of emotions people experience is wide and expansive. The reasons why people have these feelings and what can cause such emotions are the fascination of any truly great writer. Analyzing those around you will help you understand the root of their actions. Talk to people about the same topic over and over and pay attention to their behavior, body language, and tone of their voice and try to put yourself inside their minds. Ask questions and then watch. Pay close attention. Many times in conversation we are simply waiting through the other persons words for our chance to speak. Not only is that ineffective communication, it is a waste of a great opportunity to study linguistic psychology. By that, I mean the study of the pattern of behavior present in the speech of a person. This is a concept that might be a bit tricky to understand, but if you want to write about people then you must study them. The only persons mind that you can say with certainty you understand is your ownand maybe not even that so it is up to you to make the study of human reasoning and human feeling the primary fascination you hold. I bring psychology up because to go any further, you must understand that it is psychology that will make or break your characters. If you base them on flawed or shallow principles of feeling or reasoning then you will doom your story to dismissal by the reader. Remember that the reader is trying to imagine this story as though it were real. If you do not fashion your characters in believable ways then they will seem silly, absurd, awkward or just flat-out stupid. Baring that in mind we can now continue Narrative Tone is a term that refers to the way a story "sounds" to a reader. More specifically, it is how the reader feels or responds to the general collection of feelings present in the words of your story. Some simple examples of this are used all the time when people ask one another about a story, film or other such medium for entertainment. Lets just stick with stories for now. If someone calls a story "Chilling" or "Dark" or even "Depressing" this does not mean that he did not enjoy the tale. This only means that the general emotions that the story evoked could be summarized in a statement. This is effective writing and a strong Narrative Tone. The overall feeling that your story generates within the reader is the way the story "sounds" to them. If they can figure out what kind of story you have written, then you have achieved the art of expressing an overall feeling with your words. This is how the reader will classify and remember your story and it is up to you to build a story that will manifest emotions instead of state them. You can tell a reader that your story is sad but that doesnt make it so. You can write a comedy but if no one laughs then it is not funny. Learn what words draw out feelings in people. Listen to the way people speak. A master of the writing craft is always observing and learning. You always have more to learn. It is that dynamic element that is at the heart of every writer and every great story. Inevitably, as you craft your words you will begin speaking to your audience as a narrator. This will happen in some form or fashion. Depending on the context of your story, the audience you have selected, your own background and the intended Tone you have chosen to write in you will find yourself targeting a specific intellectual vernacular that may or may not be your own. The vocabulary you use and the terminology you choose will determine the specific audience that will read your words. An example of this is the way in which the following are written. Pay close attention to the words that have been chosen and how you interpret the feelings and meanings present

Crafting Your Words
The intended purpose of these articles is to explain some of the elements involved in the creative writing process. These elements range from the overall concept of story telling to the most specific details necessary to create a written tale. These articles will each focus on one individual consideration that the writer must master to successfully share a message, ideas or feelings with the audience. This means that it is my intent to help provide you with the wisdom I have gained studying the writing process. It is my intention to make any one feel unintelligent or untalented. My opinions and perspectives are not intended to replace your own nor do I expect anyone to change their minds after reading my words. I love writing and my intent is to help you love it too. I hope you will find this information useful and I hope you will share what you have learned with others who are looking for some insight as a writer. Thank you.**
Purpose:
**NOT**
The drafting process is will require a writer to call upon all of the hard work that was poured into the rough-cut. This process will demand that the writer focus his skill, ambition and energy into something new and amazing. It is here that you must lay your work at the feet of the audience and hope that it was good enough and interesting enough to be noticed. In order to put your imagination down onto paper, you need only to write it. That summarizes the first drafting process you undertook. Spilling your thoughts onto pages is exactly what you are supposed to do in the beginning. That must now change. Instead, you must now begin to see your work in a real way and even more importantly you must show the reader what you alone have seen thus far. You must show the reader your imagination. This is a task that you will have to achieve in order to successfully convey your message or entertain anyone. This is a task you have chosen and to do so you must choose the words you will use with a careful precision. You must focus your skill within the language of your story. How you choose to present this will determine the emotions that your readers attach to your work and even the specific type of audience that will be drawn to it. Skill will be tested here. You are a craftsman and an artist. This is where you will prove that. Your story is a honed blade carefully polished meticulously analyzed and perfected and indeed it will be if you choose that path. As you can see, the dramatic elements of writing can be present even without the obvious presence of drama. Even the words above convey this. You are the writer. You create the drama. It is up to you to choose the way you will tell your tale and it is up to you to paint a picture in the mind of the person reading this. How can such a thing be done? How can anyone know how to create drama out of words? How can a writer know how someone else will imagine the story? Below are some of the elements that you must keep in mind when crafting your final draft. Though it may not actually be the last version that you write, you must proceed as though it is. You must build your structure now or else not at all. The choices you make will shape the story into words and into others minds.

Intellectual Targeting
**Example 1**
* That kid on the corner was always bothering me. I hated him. All I ever wanted was for him to leave me alone. Every day after school he would follow me and chase me. He would yell at me and throw rocks at my head as I ran. Id have to keep running until either he caught me or I got home. Sometimes he would catch me and when he did, he would spit in my face and punch me in the stomach until I passed out. I hated him. I wanted to ride the bus but my mom said that the school wouldnt let me because we lived too close. I just wanted him to leave me alone. Thats why I did it! Thats why! You feel sorry for him because hes dead but nobody cares about me! Nobody ever has Nobody ever will
**Example 2**
* We were children he and I I recall this street corner with all the vibrancy of yesterdays memory. It was here that he waited crouched in shadow seething at the thought of my passing presence. Every malcontented instant this scourge pursued me. In my heart, the hope for solidarity pervaded above all else but the detestation of my bane. Upon the days last hour at school, my frantic flight would begin as I rushed across a lonely path towards home but he was there always in my wake. The horrific pursuit would lead me to only two fates either I would find grace in speed and escape the hands of my attacker or (as was more often the case) my inefficacious efforts would be for naught. If it was to be that he would seize me, then upon my face he would cast a web of debase expectorant as his fists found satisfaction crushing my abdomen. Finality never came for when I would regain consciousness he had already left. I would often muse on the passion of rage my soul cried out and always for him. I pleaded with my mother to speak on my behalf to the school administration in the hope that perhaps my travel home could be taken up inside the serenity of the yellow-painted iron of a school bus. My mother loved me but she had no power here for the school facility was too close to our residence and therefore disqualified me from this single saving chance for peace. All my thoughts dwelled on peace peace from my unrelenting adversary. It was for this reason that my actions occurred This is why my hand was forced! Do not pity the predator! Think of its prey! I had no recourse! For my life, I ended his. Will you not consider this? Will sympathy not cast its longing gaze upon me? No Not this day nor any
Notice the extreme difference in the vocabulary used in these two paragraphs. They are both effective, but depending on the education, maturity and intellect of your reader they will each be seen in quite different ways. Some people might read the first one and think that it is clearer and easier to pay attention to. Some might read the second and think that it is more expressive and more artistic. Of course some might call the first paragraph simplistic or sloppy whereas some might call the second one arrogant or wordy. These are simply different interpretations of the exact same story. The difference is the mentality of the writer at the time of the actual writing. By targeting a specific level of intellect, you will find yourself writing in a very consistent vocabulary. This is essential if you wish to reach a specific audience. Once again, continuity comes into play. Remember to choose your structure in this way and stick to your guns. Classics are written like that.
Structuring your words with thought, feeling and interest in the reader will keep your story on track. If you wish to paint a picture in the mind of your reader then you must do so with a careful consideration for the many factors involved in creating and developing a concise and entertaining tale. Pay attention to people. You can learn something from everyone. The more you learn, the better writer you will become. If you thirst for knowledge then you will stay sharp and aware of the people and world around you. If you set aside ego for passion then you will progress beyond being someone who writes into being a writer. This is a careful craft. Respect the magnitude of research and study that has already been done for you and listen to those who would help. No one genuinely offers help with bad intentions. Learn always learn and some day you will find yourself teaching but for now just try not to let the knowledge you acquire go to your head. Respect everyone and everything for something for all things have something to teach us. Master this concept and you will write things no one has ever imagined.**
