# Progress Update - February

Browse
**Progress Update - February**
Development over the last two months has focussed on bug-fixing and documentation writing. Limited support for animated gifs has been implemented, and a change of the Toolkit's licence has been made. Animated gifs can now be used in place of Toolkit animations (.anm) wherever they may be used. Primarily this is for sprite graphics, but also applies to the animation RPGCode functions. One thing to note is that such gifs must be placed within the Misc folder (or subfolders therein) with the other animations, in the Bitmap folder. Additionally, animated gifs used in regular image functions (such as setImage()) will animate; only the first frame will be displayed.

When the Toolkit became open source in 2004 it was released under a particular licence (the Awesome Computing Open Licence, "ACOL") that defined the conditions under which the Toolkit could be redistributed. During the course of the latest version's development it became apparent that certain additional terms were required to ensure the security and integrity of the Toolkit as truly open-source software. To this end, an consensus was reached among both present and past developers and contributors to upgrade the licence under which the next version will be released. The Toolkit will henceforth be licenced under the **Licence Change**
[GNU General Public License](http://www.gnu.org/copyleft/gpl.html) ; this licence applies to the editor (toolkit3.exe, actkrt3.dll) and the engine (trans3.exe) and the default menu and battle systems (tk3menu.dll, tk3fight.dll). Previous releases (3.0.2-3.0.6) will remain under the original licence. Versions of the next release made under the old licence will not be offered. The consequence for users is that they must comply with the licence when redistributing the software (e.g., when a game is released); users altering the source are required to release the source along with the binary files they release. The 3.0.4+ plugin SDKs, written by Colin, will be upgraded and rereleased under the GPL with the inclusion of an exception allowing users who do not release plugins made with the SDKs for public not to have to include the sources if including them would impractical--for example, if nobody would be interested in using them. We recommend including sources if it is practical. If a plugin is released for public use in other games, the source must be included.

One of the freedoms of software as defined by the GPL is to be able to sell it. Of course you can still sell your games, including the engine. We cannot legally compel you to do anything with scripts that are merely interpreted by the engine, but it's not like you can hide them anyway. If you could, I would strongly advise that you release the source, because, after all, you want to help the community, don't you? If you modify trans3, you must release your sources, even if you are not selling. Nothing changes in the GPL when you are selling, except that you must include the sources in the download, rather than merely linking to them.

Stop missing the point, ????. It doesn't matter whether you are selling the game; your obligations are the same. And your obligation is to make available the source of the Toolkit. That can be done via a link to the TKZ for noncommerical distribution, or by including the Toolkit sources in the download for commerical distribution. Refer to section three of the licence, which gives you three options when distributing the engine. You do one of them for distribution of the GPL-licensed code, regardless of whether you are selling it. I imagine most Toolkit uses will be using option (c), which is linking to TKZ and explaining that sources are available there (unless they have modified trans3, in which case they will want (a)). However, (c) is not available for commerical distribution, so you will have to do with (a) or (b). Read the GPL. You do not need to accept it in order to use the Toolkit, but when it comes time to distribute your game, you will want to understand your obligations.

Not according to the FSF (see ). A warranty is something that is offered by the distributor. The notice simply informs you that there is no warranty. You don't have to "agree" because it is just an informative statement, not something you have a choice on. We do need to show you the notice, though. In fact, one of the requirements of the GPL is that you make the fact that there is no warranty obvious (unless you offer a warranty). (The reason warranty disclaimers are always in CAPITALS apparently has something to do with making them obvious.) I don't know the subtlties, but the licence itself says you don't need to agree to it (section 5). [this faq](http://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#ClickThrough)
