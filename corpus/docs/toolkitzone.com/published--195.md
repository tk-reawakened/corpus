HOW TO MAKE AN EVIL GUY IN YOUR GAME 101: AVOIDING CLICHES! BY: YAMMY I must say, when making an evil villain in your game, sometimes cliches are helpful. Technically, it is a cliche for an evil guy to be, well, evil! So therefore that is a good cliche. However, some cliches are very good to use. Cliches are much overused. Why do people use them? I'm sure the Toolkit game would be far more fun if it was totally ripped off of Final Fantasy, but is that really what we want. I hate games where you play as a 10-year-old peasant who somehow acquires a long sword and wooden buckler and decides to kill the evil king who destroyed his town. And when he collects all that gold the wolves mysteriously swallowed, how does he carry it all. Not to mention, how can a little kid defeat the Giant Angel Final Boss Guy With Super 1337 Dark Mages Magical Powers? Well, that's what we avoid. Fortunately, though, I'll be focusing on villains, not heros. If you've read my other articles, making fun of cliches, but want to make a serious game without cliches, FORGET about everything you read in there. Actually, do the exact opposite. I get tired of evil kings or shadow knights who curse the land of Eris and try to kill people. BOR-ING! I hate the stupid mideval troops who stand around, get beaten by a 10-year-old kid, and then tell you all the bad guy's evil plans. Why would the bad guy give them his/her plans anyway. I hate the gigantic fiery demon who laughs a lot, then says "NOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!1111" after you beat him, and becomes a pile of ashes. Once again, BOR-ING! Now I'll finally tell you how to avoid cliches. One, don't make your evil boss guy to wear giant, elaborate red capes with black armor, and laughs evilly often. Don't make the elderly mages who talks like Yoda. You need an original character. Let's look at Blob Dude's Blob games. All the bad guys are squishy blobs of jello. You can't get anymore original than that! Best of all, they don't wear capes and armor! I hate games where the bad guys always wear capes an armor! Play some games yourself, and make a villain totally different from the game's you've played. Even if you made the Levitating Webcam, it would be more original that Thya'lonu, the Knight of King Fazizzlysmith from the 15th's court. If you have to make some game, don't make an evil king, mage, or knight! It's too overused! Okay. You probably want examples of evil guys that aren't overused. Alright! I'll tell you some... You may want to try something exotic, and maybe crazy! Like the evil snowman who wants to make the whole Earth frozen. Or maybe the evil scientist who invented the Artificial Black Hole and is declaring war against some country. Let your imagination go wild for this one! You'll need an original personality for this villain, Do not make a villain who will become good at the end, or fall in love with a main character. Believe this or not, one of the most non-cliched ideas is to have your villain...insanely evil! I've never played a game where whenever the evil guy with a purple aura walked around, he'd shoot the closest guard with his 100-shot military grade-A high powered Portable Rocket Launcher. Few villains now a days even use technology! Do you want your villain to be super-de-duper-de evil, or maybe your villain will be an oh-so-kind stuffed plushie. Don't make your villain some cruel old man who stares into a crystal ball all day, or no one will play your game! And what evil act does your antagonist do? Does he/she try to blow up the world from Spacestation-Z, does he/she make the hero wait a really long time to have his/her article approved, only to find it needed to be redrafted. No symbolism intended, though. Most plots with magic, have been thought up, but there is still a sliver of some you may use. How about the plot where someone turns the king into a Nintendo DS using magic. You are much more open to free thought in Sci-Fi games, and have supreme freedom in making a game with a random plot. No evil person should just sit there, and you pretend he's evil. A volatile scheme is imperative to all games! Now, just don't make the bad guy do anything too stupid, like strangely leave his/her secret plans on the table, or bring hos/her TV remote instad of the Death Ray (C) remote. It just isn't fun to see this crazy bad guy who poses as no threat for his/her bumbling-ness-ful-ly-ness-kinda... After all, If I were to buy Call of Duty 2, I don't expect the arch nemesis to be an inflated Panda Bear, now would I? I hate it how the evil bosses are always a zillion times harder to beat than their troops! Why employ a bunch of babbling idiots when you could walk into town and rule the world by kidnapping Prince Lorenzo, King of Icy Elves? Or use the special "Evilful Magic" hidden under a giant Webcam by all the land's black wizards in 7777? I think I hit the jackpot... Nevertheless, make the villain a challenge and all, but don't make it seem illogical he/she'll hire a bunch of weak knights to defend his/her castle when he/her could just cast a spell and sheild it, if you know what I mean! A good way to come up with original bad guys for your game is to look at real-world references! Do you have a boss who piles you up on projects, or a math teacher who makes you write a half a page of steps even though the problems are so simplistic and I can do them all on my own head-wait! I'm going off on a rant, again. Ignore me if I do that, please. Thank you so very much. Back on topic, you can, I'm sure, find a way to mix your enemies into the game. Let's say you have this mean room-mate who keeps stealing all of your food. Maybe you can depict them as a giant pig, and then pleasure yourself with having the game end by that pig falling 760,567 miles into a steaming hot pit of liquidy acid! How fun! This is a great way to humor yourself, indeed? So in final, check out games, don't overuse an enemy, and take your imagination to the test! With those 3 skills donned, there is no way your enemy creation won't be deft. So, to sum this up, enjoy your Enemy Making! :D
