Happy Birthday
Although I'm writing this on the 9th, it'll be published on the 10th - my 24th birthday! Woo, party, etc etc. However, the title isn't actually referring to me but the fact that December is a rather important month for this website. Believe it or not but it has been a week short of five years since the Toolkit Zone made a comeback, all dynamic and PHPized. Add to that a week over two years since it got a massive re-code with fancy things like have your say, ability to comment on just about everything, integrated awards like these and lots of other tidbits. So for me, as webmaster, it's a significent anniversary. Yeah, this is a luvvy-duvvy article.
I was in my first year at University and had left the Toolkit community over a year before, but I still had all those memories of romantic walks on the beach with the RPG Toolkit and I couldn't shake it. I was starting to delve into PHP for the first time and decided a revival of TKZ could be the perfect hobby project. For a month or so I messed around and shared my progress with former staff member Dan on MSN, who duly did his duties as the selfless Mr.Motivator (every developer needs one!) We launched on December 19th, 2003 and within a month CBM was e-mailing me asking to become the new official Toolkit site. All jokes and jollies aside about evil webmasters and off-topic republics, personally that was a true "holy fuck that's awesome" moment for my geek-self. I've always been extemely proud of running the community and I take great care in doing my best for it, even if some of my decisions are unpopular. If you have selfish motives then you will ultimately fail, so I think I've stood the test of time. I've also had an amazing cast of people help me, whether staff members or moderators, who have done an immense amount of work. Many have come and gone, but I'd like to give a special shout out to Spyder and Oyerth who have been rocks over the years (until Spyder quit for that thing called life - the bastard! He'll be back, oh how he will be back...)
The community has changed and evolved over the last five years. Activity, despite some perceptions, is about the same but with a significent increase in game feedback (you were lucky to get one or two "reviews" back in the day and more than a dozen downloads). I've argued over and over that it's the software, not the format of forums or any other cosmetic thing, that ultimately dictates the popularity and use of the Toolkit. Our community is very good at blaming trivial things, it's all part of the art of procrastination I guess. In the end t he Toolkit suffered from a quickly, poorly coded TK3, a head-first jump into open source land and then a lengthy, but worthy, re-write. The Toolkit fell behind the competition in usability and stability compared to when I first joined in 1999 (and guess what, my 10th Anniversary as a member is in December too!) When the site launched TK2 was the big beast, it was a long fight to push TK3 in the community and for a while TK3 didn't deserve to be pushed in all honesty. I cannot stress how vital it is we had two users in Colin and Delano, whatever their shortcomings may or may not be, take up the flag of the Toolkit and carry it on. Otherwise TK3 would be dead as a dodo and TK2 would still be the best we have.
So why do I, and so many others, continue to devote so much time and effort to this community? It's probably always going to be small, always going to be a struggle to keep a flow of active content, so why do I bother writing these articles and awards when only a few dozen people will read them? Pretty simple really; you're awesome! No really, well... most of you. Maybe it's because we're relatively small, but we've always been a pretty grounded and friendly community. It's the same now as it was ten years ago, the usernames may have changed but the attitudes haven't. We have our occasional flame wars and blow-ups, but by in large we're a pretty articulate and well-balanced community. If someone comes and proves themselves through art, or music, or another creative field... we celebrate them. We don't get petty or jealous. If some prat comes and spams about, we push them away. Just take a look at the forums of similar communities and you'll see why people like the Zone so much. That's why people come crawling back, that's why some stay despite not touching the Toolkit. Yeah, we rock and stuff.
So what's the point of this article? Well... it's a filler, okay, I admit it... we can't give an award every day! It's also my way of tipping my cap (I initially wrote that as "cat"... what would tipping a cat entail? ... no pun intended) to a community that I enjoy running. But the Toolkit Zone isn't just about the webmaster, or the developers of the Toolkit, but the hundreds of users who have contributed the massive number of resources, topics, comments, reviews and so forth. It's a real collective effort. So...
Happy 5th birthday, TKZ!
Okay, I'm finished with the love-in, now get back to procrastinating over your games you lazy bastards!
Phil (aka Xavier).
