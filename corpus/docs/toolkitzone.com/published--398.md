The Not-So-RPG Toolkit
*Apologies for the delay in today's article, I've written it in a rush so it's very short and probably not the most coherent filler piece. Still I managed to go two weeks without missing a day until now, not bad by my usual lazy standards!*
Since the release of the RPG Toolkit 3.1 in late 2007, we have seen a shift in the type of projects undertaken by users. It's a testament to the flexibility of RPG Code, plus the minefield that is the completion of a traditional RPG project, that it's possible, never mind successful. Although diversions into other genres are not strictly new, just take a look at games like Bus Driver, they're certainly becoming not only more frequent but also more polished and complete. They're not just mini-game side-projects anymore, but full games in their own right. Standing shoulder to shoulder with the RPG big boys, they push the capabilities of the Toolkit even further. It's an interesting change in attitude by developers, although it's still met with strange skepticism by so-called "purists".

"Are they worthy of winning awards ahead of more traditional games?" A good game is a good game. Simple as that. If a non-RPG game is better than an RPG game made by the toolkit, then hell yes it deserves an award.
This article loses all credibility with me due to the glaring omission of Mini Cars.
[![TBD](thumb.php?src=forum/images/avatars/187483685549a4ea30cb30b.jpg&w=60&h=60&iar=false&bw=1)](forum/profile.php?mode=viewprofile&u=304)
Heck yeah they deserve mention and awards. This is a new renaissance for RPGToolkit and it's community. Look at the early years of the community in the early 2000's. There were alot of RPG projects because the software was new. Thus we created a library of many a game because the community was experiencing something new and exciting: the ability to make your own RPG game. This is the TK3's exciting thing. Today, you have more choices in game making software. Why get the TK when Game Maker can make action games and fighting games. RPG may be a popular genre but game maker doesn't sound so constraining. If the TK can be more flexible, then the TK, then that means we are proving the power of the TK. If you would compare GM and TK, GM would get more attention. But the learning curve may turn you off. If you never code something before, then trying to figure out how to control the frame rate of a sprite the way you want it will take too long to learn. But the toolkit is easy to learn because everything is as simple as pushing a button. Of course, like I said, the RPG part may sound restrainingm but if you show them Galaxy Fighter and show them it was made in RPGCode, add that with the ease of learning it, then the TK is looking better than Game Maker because of the learning curve and the power it displays. So by acknowledging non RPG TK games, you showing that the TK has evolved enough to deal with the more popular choices. That all hard work for trying to do what you want in the game is possible and that you wont be willing to give up and search elsewere because the TK is proving that it can do it. Who knows, maybe the next big thing will be online capabilities.
