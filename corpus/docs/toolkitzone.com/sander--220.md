# Sander

Lesson 1 : Intro/Basic Chords The guitar is one of the most popular instrument of modern day culture and society. It is heard in rock, jazz, classical, and other genres of music. That is why I am going to teach inexperienced musicians how to enjoy the true quality of guitar-playing. Before being able to play a guitar, you have to, of course, own one. You want a high quality guitar for a low price. For rock musicians, an electric or acoustic-electric guitar is necessary, and for other acoustic musicians, an acoustic or classical guitar. http://guitar.about.com has a guide to choosing a good guitar. So, anyway... To hold the guitar, acoustic guitarists set the guitar on the right knee while sitting down, generally, with the neck out to the left. The pick is held between the thumb and index finger of the right hand. The left hand goes under the neck with the fingers curved over the strings and the thumb lightly pressing against the bottom of the neck. Electric guitarists typically seem to stand up and have the neckstrap loosely hanging so the guitar is slightly above the waist. The hands work in the same way as the acoustic guitar. Each string on the guitar plays a certain note in standard tuning. From thickest to thinnest, the strings will play: LOW E A D G B E HIGH. Pushing down on each consecutive fret will raise the note by a half step. As you begin to play guitar, you'll start off by playing other musicians' music. It is written in notation called tablatures, or tabs. Tabs are written generally like this: And continued. Each number tells what fret to press down and on which string to press it down. An 'x' means that you either mute the string by setting an unused finger on it, or by skipping over the strings. As you play different chords, you will find out which way to skip these strings most effectively. The chords shown above are a G Major chord, a D Major chord, an E minor chord, and a C major chord. These are the four most commonly used chords in music. Practice these chord progressions over and over until you can play them easily. As you play them, make sure your fingers are curved and only push down the string it is playing. Each string that is being played should ring clearly. So, while you're practicing, you may as well try some songs. http://guitar.about.com has some easy to play songs, but for an ultimate database of guitar tabs, try http://www.ultimate-guitar.com. That's all for today. Till next time, --Gcn_Zelda
