# Saphyra's Journal - Entries 12 to 18

-Part One: -Part Two: May 3 I cant believe this. I cant. But it happened anyway, so I guess what I believe doesnt matter. Hanev came to me early, early in the morning. He seemed reluctant at everything; like he had to push himself to move. He carried a small silk cloth in his hand, and on it rested a few drops of magic. He unlocked the door to my cage, slowly and stiffly, and walked over to me. I was sitting on my bed, and he crouched down so he was at my level. Fear tightened around my throat. The words I heard yesterday, You will be the one to give it to the girl. Tomorrow., echoed in my head. I wanted to run. I have run. But I didnt, whether it because I had no hope of escaping or whether it because I was just paralyzed with fear. I didnt run. I looked into Hanevs face. His eyes were watering, threatening to overflow. The magic on the cloth trembled as his hands shook. Im so sorry, he whispered, and then he put his hand around the back of my neck, pulled me down, and pushed the cloth to my face. I think he muttered a spell of some kind, but it was hard to tell. My mind reeled. I felt dizzy and misplaced, almost as if I was fainting, but not quite. I felt like something was pushing me against the wall, shoving me out of the way, yet I looked down at myself and I was in the same place. Part of me felt as if I was moving; the other part felt completely still. Then I heard a voice. Get out of my way, girl, get out! it growled. But I didnt hear it from my ears; it seemed untargeted, coming from everywhere and nowhere at once. What? I tried to say, but the word was only half-formed. My lips were unwilling to move, it took concentration to even make them open. And it was then, I think, that I lost control completely. I blinked. But didnt. I didnt tell my eyelids to shut, didnt send the order. And then I rose to my feet, but again it wasnt who stood, only my body. I had no control over myself. My head turned, and through my eyes - eyes that I could not move on my own - I saw Hanev, who had moved farther away from me. My eyes passed by him, then quickly drew back. Hanev, my voice murmured. Hanev. Hanev visibly stiffened. So youre here now. His voice was only barely restrained, forced. Yes. I walked closer to him. Is that all you have to say? No His voice wavered, with fright or with anger, I do not know. Maybe both. I want you to get out of her. Now. And never come back. No. Then Ill force you out. I can. I will. He sounded unsure. Im sure you could. I heard my own voice slipped into a oily, subtle, mocking tone, one that I felt ashamed to have been made by me. Now. Before I have a good grip on her. It will only be about two days before Ill be here for good, and I can use her when I need her. But you wont force me out. I fought against the presence controlling me. I felt the impact of a blow to my face, yet my body showed no movement. How do you know? Through eyes that felt so far away now, I saw Hanev edge farther away from me. He was afraid. He was terrified. I tried to take control again, but was thrust away, again feeling the impact of a strike, even though my body didnt move. My head throbbing, and my nose - a nose that, somehow, was different than the one that Hanev was seeing, the one on my body - feeling slightly out of place, I recoiled. For the moment. They will kill you if you force me away, I said, my voice blunt. Cocky. Sure of myself. Whatever it was saying that, it was not me. Then Ill force you out later, Hanev retorted, but weakly. When they notice the mistake they made. My hand rose, waved nonchalantly. By the time those idiots realize how stupid theyve been, I wont be wished away. I may be insecure now, but not by then. You of all people, Hanev, should know this. My head turned, and my eyes fixed him with a malicious glare carelessly draped over by an expression of mock thoughtfulness. I suppose you must be grieving for you sister still it will do that to you, wont it Hanevs hand sharply went to his belt, then he dropped it slowly. He grimaced. He knew he made a mistake. My eyes moved to his belt, then, seeing nothing, moved back to his face. What do you have hidden, Hanev? my voice cooed with false, contemptuous concern. A knife? Magic? Just remember that if you force me away, it is you who will end up dead. If you try to stab me, then you will only kill Saphyra and I will still linger, just lost without a body. But Ill find one again, without a doubt. The way humans are now, you know, so power hungry I felt my throat contract into -ing sounds. Just get out. Get out now. He spoke through clenched teeth. Then I tried to take control again. I concentrated as hard as I could on making the muscles in my arm move, on at least lifting it. I strained myself to my limits, pulled and pulled. At first there was no response, but then I felt as if I had control again - at least of my arm - and I felt like I was slipped back into a sleeve. My arm lifted slightly. My head moved, looked down at my arm. Hanev looked at my arm as well, and out of the corner of my eye I thought I saw a small, almost-hopeful smile curl the edges of his mouth. But then I was knocked away again, harder this time, much harder, and pain shot through me - somehow me, but not my body. You are not mute, even if you have no voice. Try to speak, and you will be heard. I didnt see how that made any sense, but I was afraid of this creature, whatever it was. I spoke. No I whispered, unsurely. My voice, like the creatures, seemed to have no focus. It was just there. Now that wasnt so hard, was it? growled the voice. And I didnt think so. So I wouldnt get in the way, if I were you, Saphyra. I said nothing, and the voice didnt either. Instead, it took control of my body again. She is not so strong minded anymore, it said, with my voice. And even if she still is, she wont be for much longer. And with that, I felt its grip on my body relax, and my body fell around me again. Do you understand me or must I repeat it slower for you? I didnt answer, for fear of my anger escaping me. That could be dangerous, and I had no doubt at all that it would do what it threatened to do. Neither of us spoke until Rire and Hanev came to the cage about twenty minutes later. Saphyra? Rire demanded. Or ? Reshaye. Old Yaelen, before all the countries switched to the same language, for monster. Reshaye, I heard my voice answer. Rire gave a short nod, a single jerk down, and then unlocked the door. Hanev stepped in, holding a rope in his hands, which he began to wind around my wrists. Reshaye pulled my wrists away and glared at Hanev defensively. What are you doing? Hanev glared back. Binding your hands. What did you think? I felt my throat vibrate as it unleashed a sound that sounded like a mix between a groan and a hiss. Why? I dont know, answered Hanev. Think about the things youve done in the past, and then you can tell me. It made the groaning, hissing sound again and then allowed its wrists to be tied. I felt Hanev give the rope a sharp jerk when he tied it, so it wrapped too-tightly around my wrists. And then I was led up the stairs, through the building, and then outside. For a moment all I could think about was how glad I was to breathe fresh air again, to smell it, until I realized that it was not who was breathing the air, and that the smell was a stench of blood and death. And I looked through my eyes, eyes controlled by Reshaye, and saw that bodies were scattered all over the ground, and that in the distance, approaching fast, were soldiers from my own country, my home country. The country I Reshaye both of us were about to fight. I nearly threw up in the sudden burst of realization, but I couldnt, my body wouldnt move for me. Forced to contain my horror, I cringed and watched. Rire ran a knife over the edge of the rope binding my hands, and it fell away easily. The knife must have been sharp, or else reinforced with some kind of spell. They come fast, he said to me - to Reshaye. You fight them. I have no doubt that you can kill them all with no problems, and no help, but I do have others if you need them. I will need no help. My lips moved slowly, forming the words thoughtfully as my eyes lay fixed on the quickly-approaching enemy. Friends. I dont know which they are anymore. Rire smiled a black, toothy grin. I knew that you would say that! he cried, unleashing a rough, roaring laugh. Then he walked away, as he went saying to Hanev, Did I not tell you that it was a good idea? You worry to much, Hanev I thought, my fear rising. He worries just enough. A stabbing pain came and went on each of my fingertips, so fast I barely noticed it was there. Then it waited. It was only a few minutes before the first of the soldiers - all magicians, it seemed - were upon me. Us. Reshaye reacted fast. Just as soon as the first soldier in range, it brushed my fingertips across his throat and he fell to the ground, blood trickling from a slit in his throat. After that, everything just seemed to fall apart. Wherever my hands touched, they cut as fast, as deep, as the sharpest of knives. Just like Hanev did, to his sister, all those years ago. Hanev didnt kill his sister; Reshaye did. Just like he was killing all these people now. They were surrounding me, hundreds of them, and whenever one of them hit my body with a spell or a dagger, it was I as well as Reshaye that felt the pain. But it was fast. It did not let many people hit it. It killed fast and quick, and bodies surrounded me, another added by the minutesanother.and another. Time blurred. I cringed away from the sight, of people I recognized from my time at Quefa Military Base falling lifeless to the ground. Because of me; my hands, now red and dripping with the blood of dozens of other people, mixed with just a little of my own. It seemed unbalanced; unfair. Through my eyes I saw none other than Akee, Akee who I thought was dead, Akee who had apparently escaped with her life and a long scar running across her forehead, down her cheek. Dont kill her, I said, frantically, to Reshaye.
