# Saphyra's Journal - Entries 19 to 23

-Part Two May 14 I got a letter today. letter. From Hanev. I found it sitting on my bed when I came into my room. Sent by magic, I guessed. As soon as I saw it I knew what it was, and even though I was overjoyed I was surprised it came so early - surprised that it even came at all. But I was too happy to care much, at the time. But then I tore the envelope open and saw the unusually shaky, twisted writing, with letters deformed and crooked. My heart began to sink and my smile disappeared. A bad feeling began to settle in my stomach. Tentatively, I began to read. The letter said: There is a long space here, as if he was thinking about what to write - I was shocked, and not in a pleasant way. Im not sure how long I just stared down at that letter, horrified. I thought it would be better knowing, even if it meant that I knew he was dead, but I was so wrong. Now that hope that I once had was gone, and I could no longer imagine that hed never gone back into the building. And all I could think about was how he would die alone. That thought kept poking my mind, and it caused a surge of guilt to find its way into me. I kept thinking. Then I told myself that he wrote that he was relieved, and happy. And another part of me retorted that I would be a plain-and-simple idiot for believing that, because no one can be happy and relieved lying alone on their deathbed, without anyone there to even try to save them. And so, after letting these thoughts run around my brain for some time, as I still stared down at the letter, I decided that he wouldnt die alone, because I would be there with him. The decision wavered for a few more moments, threatening to fall back to the other side of the fence, until I reached out and grabbed it, pulling it back to my side. I was set on this. I didnt care about what my family would say, or how hard it would be to find him, or any of that. I all cared about was that Hanev had gone through much worse, for my sake, and that I was so, so grateful for that. The first thing I did was use a spell - a rather complicated one, at that - to try and find him. This was easier than I thought it would be. It only took me about an hour. As I expected, he did have misdirection spells and deception spells around him. To hide himself from the Yaelens, Im guessing. The spells were old and very weak though, very very weak. Even though I was glad it didnt take much for me to find him, it made me more worried that it wouldnt take much for the Yaelens to find him, either, if they wanted to. He was at his house; the same one I had seen in my vision about Keyas death, I think, though I cant be sure. It was located around the border between the Yaelen lands and the Quefa lands, though just barely on Quefa lands. It could pass as either, though. And then I had to tell my family. Easy? No. Complete opposite. After I had explained, rather hurriedly to try and get it over with, there was a shocked silence. Lianne was the first to speak. Are you ? she asked bluntly. I was just wondering the same thing, Daehn agreed. Neither Mother nor Father said anything, but I could guess that they both thought the same thing. All of them hold a grudge against Hanev, and I dont see how I can expect anything else, really. I cant blame them. They didnt . Well I said carefully, not sure of how to go about this, you might think that, but- Saphyra, Mother interrupted. I think we all know what youre about to say. Youve told us this too many times to count over the past few days. How Hanev risked his life for you, and how he just made some mistakes, and how it wasnt his faultwe know all of that. But still and youve only been home for a few days you can expect us to just let you- We dont trust him, Daehn cut in. No use trying to cover it up with long explanations. We just dont trust him. When he saw the look on my face - a look that Im guessing must have been a mix of disappointment, frustration, and maybe just a little bit of anger, he said, Dont give me that look. The man kidnapped you, threw you in a cell, got you possessed by a monster of some sort He grimaced and shook his head. He almost you, Saphyra. you. Through what looked like feirce determination on his face, there was something underneath it. Fear, I think. Fear for me. We cant let you do this. Youre just too , that is what your problem is, muttered Lianne. Ahem. My father cleared his throat. He speaks rarely, but when he does his words are wise. We all fell silent. The truth of the matter is, Saphyra isnt a child anymore. We cant stop her from doing this if she wants to. But He turned his eyes to look at me. Saphyra we are all just worried about you. If I could keep you from going, I would. I think any of us would. I cant trust this Hanev, just as Daehn said. Id been through ten times what he had, and yet was the one who needed protection?! What? I sputtered. If you think I need protection And that started a whole new round of arguments. To make a very long, very boring story much shorter, I am going to see Hanev. Daehn is coming with me. I dont mind that so much as I mind that he thought he to come with me. Frankly, its amazing how a girl can go through hell and back, make it out alive, and still need to be protected. I shouldnt have written that. I would be worried too, if I was in their place. Hanev did, after all, nearly kill me. He was the one that put me in hell in the first place. And they never how he was, never saw that vision. Were leaving tomorrow, early in the morning so well be able to make it there by nightfall. Its very late at night now. I should probably be going to sleep soon - I should actually probably be asleep . I just wanted to write all this down. Its kind of strange, when you think about it, how I record everything in such detail, even things I would rather forget. I cant tell why, but I can tell that I couldnt stop even if I wanted to. Its something I need to do, an outlet for me. I wonder if I would have even gotten out of the Yaelens hands with my sanity without this book. Hanevs house - which, when I first saw it, I suspected was the same house that my vision about Keya took place in - was huge. Calling it a house would be a huge understatement; this could only be described as a mansion. It must have been beautiful once, but now it is old and grayed. Its paint peels, but you cant even tell what color it once was anymore anyway. Some windows are shattered. Around a few of the windows there are what must have been statues at once point. Now, though, they are just chunks of crumbling rubble. Even as we sat there in our cart, gaping at the massive building, a piece of one crumbled and fell. The house is an image of abandonment; it looks almost as lonely as Hanev himself. After we gawk at the house - mansion - for a few moments, Daehn flicked the reins and the horse trotted on, up to the door. The mansion looked even lonelier up close. As soon as we stop, I jumped down and go to the door as Daehn went to find a place to tie up the horse. I knocked. Inside, I could hear my knock echo through the house, but I heard nothing more. I knocked again, and this time I strained my ears to try and hear an answer. A call, a voice, even just a footstep. But I didnt hear anything. Daehn joined me. No answer? he asked, his voice gentle but with an underlying hint of disapproval. An I-told-you-so tone. I turned and glared at him. Dont be that way! Hes - the word dying refused to come to me - not well. You cant expect him to My voice trailed off, because I was unsure of what to say. Instead I turned back to the door. Just be more forgiving for once, I muttered. I could hear him sigh, but he didnt say anything more. I knocked one more time, and when there wasnt an answer, I carefully opened the door and stepped into the house tentatively. It seemed even from the inside. This was when I realize for sure that this was were my vision about Keya took place. A wave of remembrance passes over me. There was the same elegantly curving staircase. The same loft-like balcony at the top of the staircase. With a sudden jolt, I realized that I could even see through a half-open door into Keyas room. I could see the corner of her bed. Hanev had sat on the bed, watching Keya, ten years ago. My skin prickled as I wondered if the bloodstains were still on the rug. Are you alright? I was jolted out of thought by Daehns voice. I looked up at him. What? Oh. Yes. Im fine, I answered, even though I wasnt sure if I felt fine. I changed the subject. Where would you think he would be? I didnt wait for his answer before I started moving towards the staircase, the first place I saw that made sense to me. I made my way up them with my hand tight on the rail, knuckles white, almost as if I was afraid of falling. I was anxious. Very anxious. I was a little more than halfway up the stairs when I saw something out of the corner of my eye. Slowly, I backed up a step and looked down at it, curious to what it was. It was blood. Old blood, very old blood, browned, but still evident on the gray-marble step. Shocked, I looked around and realized that this was the step where Hanev had cut Keyas throat. The blood there, on the step it was hers. It was then that I realized how quickly it had happened. How quickly his family had fallen apart. No one had taken the time to even clean her blood from the steps. There any time. It was at that moment that everything was over. When the top piece of the arched bridge broke, and the bridge crumbled. My fingers tightened even more on the rail. I didnt even know that was possible. But this time, I was actually afraid of falling over. I felt as if I was about to vomit, I felt suffocated. I practically ran up the rest of the staircase. I needed to get away from that blood. I dont know what it was, but it made me sick to my stomach. Something about it something about it just made me so sick. Daehn caught up to me at the top of the staircase. Saphyra, he asked, concern in his eyes. Are you theres nothing wrong? Youre just acting so I finished for him in my head, although his voice sunk. And I was acting strange, and I knew it. But I felt strange, too. Theres nothing wrong, I answered. The only thing thats wrong is that were wasting time. I began to turn away and leave, but he grabbed my shoulder and pulled me back. Saphyra, he said again, evenly. Tell me whats wrong. I already you, I answered, defiantly, there isnt anything wrong. Were just wasting time. I pulled away, and before he could say anything else, I set off down the hall. I was so frustrated with him, but I think most of my frustration was rooted at my fear and worry. I cant doubt, even a little bit, that I wasnt terrified; I was downright shaking. Part of it was fear for Hanev; another part of it was just fear of this place, of the events that took place in it. He was lying in a bed at the center of the room, and was perfectly still. On his face were several huge gash-like things, but they looked like they were under a layer of skin unlike anything I had ever seen before. His face itself was thin, yellowed-looking skin stretched tight over his skull. His left arm was bent where it shouldnt be, and at a disturbingly small angle. He was so pale, so still, so lifeless that my heart twisted as I wondered if I was too late. I moved closer, but hesitantly, slowly, afraid of what I would find. Relief flooded through me when he heard me, and opened his eyes. They wandered around to room briefly before seeing me and stopping. Hello, he said quietly. His voice sounded hollow. Empty. I dont even know how I got here. I couldnt think of any way to respond to that. Have you been eating anything? I demanded instead, again trying to hide the growing knot in my stomach, lump in my throat. No. He looked nauseated just at the thought. Youll starve to death. Im dying anyway.
