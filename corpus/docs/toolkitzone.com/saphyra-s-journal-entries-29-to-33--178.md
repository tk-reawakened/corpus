# Saphyra's Journal - Entries 29 to 33

-Part Three: July 2, Two days since Ive last written. Quite frankly, Ive had no time to write. Kaleana came for me just as the sun was rising on the thirtieth. Since then it seems like we have not stopped moving except to get the bare minimum of rest. With the exception of one short stop yesterday, which I will go into later. I understand now what Kaleana means when she says that shes changing, too. There are some times when shell tell me that she has to go be by herself, for her safety and mine. Those times, she leaves my mind and flies off ahead of me. I just follow behind her, keeping my distance. She comes back, of course, eventually. Sometimes its after twenty minutes, sometimes its after two hours, but she always comes back. As we walked, Kaleana would enter my mind sometimes and we tried to think of a plan. Nothing is coming to us, and its very frustrating. Every time one of us points out a loophole in a potential option, Kaleana would get more and more frustrated. For some reason, it doesnt bother me much. I do not know why. I think I havent realized that this is real yet. It feels almost as if Im walking through a dream, something that hasnt quite hit reality yet. I hope I wake up soon. If I do not, it could be dangerous. Actually, it be dangerous. Now, it is around ten at night. I cant see what Im writing. I just hope that the words are forming properly. I still feel like I am in my isolated world, with dreams that wrap around me and protect me like a cocoon. In a way I like it, but I know that its so dangerous because I wont be able to fly away if a predator comes for me. I dont understand why I am even in this cocoon, anyway. I dont want to be. Maybe it is because I dreaded this journey, I dreaded needing to uproot myself again, and subconsciously I made myself believe that it wasnt real. Almost like Hanev had. Hanev has been on my mind more and more the last few days, for two reasons I think. One is because of what Kaleana said a few days ago, about him being in love with me. I dont want to think about that, but it only makes me think about it more. I find myself asking myself if I loved him back, but I do not think I want to answer that question. I have Daehn. Im supposed to love him. It seems like it has always been that way, ever since we were just children. Daehn and I were a pair. Whenever the town children played a game with teams or pairs, they always said, Saphyra and Daehn can be on that team or Saphyra and Daehn will the last pair. It was never just Saphyra, never just Daehn. We were used to it. It was just always like that. It was taken for granted that Daehn and I would grow up, get married, and live happily ever after. I dont know why. It just was. Neither of us minded, truthfully. After all, we got along very well and falling in love - especially with three-fourths of the town pushing us together - seemed inevitable anyway. And so, when we were sixteen, we did fall in love and have been together ever since. Which is why falling in love with Hanev is completely out of the question. What am I saying? Hanev is dead. Somehow I need to get that through my head. Anyway. The other reason. Yes. The one stop that we made this entire journey happened today. We were just crossing over the Quefan/Yaelen border and I realized that we were near the Farlione house. Where Hanev was buried, in the family graveyard. At first I told myself that it didnt matter, but then I began to think about it more and more until I finally asked Kaleana, in her hawk body at the time, Hanevs house is near here. Could we? I didnt need to finish the question. Kaleana looked at me with bright yellow eyes. She seemed to consider it, then she bobbed her head up and down once in a prim nod. I hadnt expected that, since Kaleana seems so focused on this, so hurried, but I was happy about it nonetheless. It wont be long, I said quietly as a reassurance to her, and she nodded again. We really werent far from the house - even closer than I originally thought. It took nearly no time at all to reach the gates to the house, or maybe it only seemed that way since I was lost in thought on the way there. For some reason - and I still dont know why - as we began to walk up the long, twisted walkway to the house a strange, rather unpleasant feeling kept creeping up my throat. I was happy to be there, to be able to visit Hanevs grave again, but then there was that feeling a mix of sadness and grief, maybe a little bit of anger, too, I think. It slowed my steps as I walked up the path, aroused doubts in my mind. But I kept going. The graveyard was fairly small, with only about a dozen gravestones, and it was about three-fourths up the path. Before I entered it, I stopped and just stared for a moment, leaning on the small black gate the enclosed it. Then I stood straight again, sighed for a reason that I didnt know, opened the gate, and stepped inside. Hanevs grave stood out. It was the only one with just the beginnings of grass growing over it. The only one that didnt seem quite healed yet, like a scar that hasnt yet faded. And that, in a way, is what it was. I still cannot decide if I want the scar to fade or if I want to keep it forever. I knelt over the grave. It must have rained there recently, because the ground was wet and soft. A small amount of water squeezed up from the ground when I knelt, and I could feel it soaking through the fabric of my skirt. I did not care. I was silent at first. I found myself reading the words on his gravestone. Typical gravestone words. Nearly every other gravestone I have ever seen read the same way. But the next thing were the words that separated Hanevs grave from the rest, and what shot such a wave of grief up my body that I could almost feel the tears coming back again. I read that quote over and over again, eyes traveling to the end of it only to turn back to the beginning and start again. I was in the middle of the constant, endless loop - something that, in a way, I needed - when I heard something zoom past my ear, almost touching it. I blinked, and when my eyes opened I saw an arrow stuck into the wooden wall of a shed at the front of the graveyard, still trembling. I thought clearly in my head. I stood, but I was partially afraid to turn. Was it meant to be a threat? Was it meant to hit me? If it was, it had come dangerously close Then I heard a voice - a voice that was coming closer and closer - call out, with subtle sarcasm, Amazing! None other than Miss Saphyra, who doesnt have a last name. I knew that voice. I turned to see Brayan, of all people, walking towards me. He had a bow grasped in one hand and a quiver slung sloppily over his shoulder. My first reaction was to cry, What are you doing here? He laughed, humorlessly, as he took one more step and was beside me. You forget, Saphyra, he said, dropping the quiver to the ground but still grasping the bow, that this is my house. And because of that, shouldnt it be me asking you the same question? I looked down at Hanevs grave. I only came to see Hanev. Oh yes. About that. He was smiling, but his voice and eyes were furious. Why was I not told that my beloved brother had died? I dont know what it is, but there is something about Brayan that bring out my coldest, snippiest responses. Because you tried to kill him already, and I thought you wouldnt care. My voice had an edge to it. Then my eyes glanced over at the arrow stuck in the wooden door of the shed and I added, That arrow came too close for comfort, too. I would appreciate it if you never do that again. Brayan stared at me for a moment, smile gone, then turned and walked to the shed. As he went he said, Hanev wasnt the only one in the military, you know. I was too. He pulled the arrow out of the wall with one sharp movement. I was an archer. Most accurate one in the entire division. He set the arrow on the bow, pulled back his arm, and let the arrow fly. It passed me - thankfully, by a larger margin this time - and kept going, going, going, until it hit a tree right at the center of one of its knots. Brayan gave a half-smile, but it disappeared as he looked back at me. Youre thinking that I wanted to kill you, arent you? Saphyra, if I wanted to hit you with that arrow, I would have. No doubts about it. He made his way to me again. As he did, I asked, out of curiosity, Why did you quit? He looked confused. What, archery? No. The military. Oh. That. He looked down. I didnt quit. I was thrown out. Oh. I didnt bother to ask why. I wasnt quite sure I wanted to know. Silence. I looked back down at Hanevs grave again. Read that last quote one more time. So you came to see him. Brayans voice was cold, but somehow a little bit - only a little bit - forgiving, too. Almost regretful, in an angry sort of way. I nodded, not looking up. He sighed. From that sigh I could tell that he didnt understand it at all. He wasnt angry anymore; only baffled. He didnt understand how I could still care about Hanev knowing the things that hes done. Truthfully, I am not sure if I understand it either. You do truly miss him, dont you? I nodded. Bit my lip. He made a sound, as if he wanted to say something, then stopped himself and we were both silent. Finally he said, scornfully, voicing my thoughts, I really do not understand you. I had no way to answer to that, so I didnt say anything. Then something suddenly occurred to me. I looked up. If you didnt know that Hanev was dead, then why did you come back? For a split second I hoped that maybe he had come back to see Hanev. To apologize. It was far fetched, but it was possible. Brayan did not answer right away. I dont know, he finally said. At my confused look - how can anyone not know why they go somewhere? - he added, I dont stay in one place for a long time. I just go everywhere. I dont know why I decide to go where I do, I just do it. Coming back here, of all places, too I dont even have the slightest idea why I would to come back. It doesnt even make any sense to me. He looked up at the house. I hate this place. Hanev hated it too. He cleared his throat and looked at me again. Well. Most likely Ill only be staying for a little while, then Ill be moving again. I dont- Something else suddenly struck me. Wait! I cried, interrupting him. He stopped and stared at me in a not-quite-annoyed way. Slightly embarrassed, I lowered my voice to normal volume when I said, You said you were in the military. Were you with the Yaelens or Quefans? Yaelens, he said slowly. Why? I felt just a shard of hope. Do you think you could get us into the Yaelen military base? He gaped. No! he exploded, indignantly. Of course not! Do you have any idea how hard it is to get into the place even when you been specifically banned from the grounds? Which, I might add, I have. And besides, even if that wasnt the case, why would I help you? You, of all people? I dont see how you could even , after last time! The shard slipped through my fingers. Oh, I said, disappointment setting in. He bent down and picked up the quiver with a single, jerking movement. Stay as long as you need to, he said coldly. Then he opened the gate and began to walk away. Brayan? I called after him. He stopped and looked over his shoulder. If you change your mind in the next few days, I said, write to me. He just shook his head and continued to walk away. I sighed and turned back to the grave. Slowly I knelt again, my eyes drawn to the muddy ground. Suddenly I felt so hopeless, so helpless. And afraid. I felt very afraid. Right then, I jerked out of my dream world for just a moment, just a few minutes, and I realized that we was about to try and break into Yaelen military base, the place Hanev had almost given his life to get me out of, with no plan, no help, and a high chance of getting killed. From where I stood, things looked impossibly bleak. I leaned my forehead on the cold, hard, lifeless stone of the grave. What am I going to do, Hanev? I murmured. I dont know what I expected. Some sign? A note falling from the sky? A voice? Something entirely silly like that? I dont know, but for some reason when nothing happened disappointment wrenched through me. All I could think was, In my head I cursed Kaleana, for not stopping Railyn and for asking me to help, and Rire, for taking me captive, but most of all I cursed Reshaye. The reason why any of this happened. I wonder what Railyn would have done if he knew how many people that one stupid spell would affect. I sat on Hanevs grave for some time. I let myself just vent silently, and even though I never said anything aloud I had a feeling that I was being listened to. It was comforting. When I finally rose and left, I felt better about the situation. I still didnt - dont - feel wonderful about it, but better. So that is why Hanev has been on my mind, especially tonight. Meeting Brayan again was an experience, that I am sure of. I dont think that hes a bad person. Irritating, perhaps, but not as short-tempered as he makes himself seem. I think hes just lost, like Hanev was. The question Im wondering now, though, is whether or not he will be able to find his way back again. And the next question: will he write to us and change his mind or not? I hope he does. He knows the military base better than either Kaleana or I, and he would be helpful. Although if he did, it would mean that we would have to try and explain to him what Reshaye is, which means explaining what happened with Hanev. And I dont know how well he would take any of it. It could be disastrous, or it could be helpful. I dont think he will, though. -Saphyra July 4 Brayan leaned back in his chair and sighed. The sound didnt echo, but he felt like it should have. In a house as empty, as dead, as this. But then again, there were already too many echoes in this house. Echoes of events, conversations He slammed the door on the thoughts, memories, that that triggered. He wasnt ready to think about that again. He would never be ready to think about that again. His mind drifted again, and the first thing it caught onto was Saphyra. Ever since she came, since she asked him to help, for some reason he had been thinking about it. Actually considering it, against all of his expectations. He didnt know why he felt like he had to do it. It almost felt as if he was guilty and wanted to repent? But he had nothing to be guilty of, after all. He really was the only person in his family who was able to pick up the pieces and move on. Live a life. The only one who was able to be happy again. Right? He happy, wasnt he? Strange how he doubted his now of all times. He had only reflected - - this way twice before: once just after Keyas death, and once after he had last met Saphyra, the first time. What was it about Saphyra that made him this way? Maybe it was because of her relationship with Hanev. The first time he had met her, she had threatened him with a knife when he tried to hurt Hanev. And it was no empty move. He still remembered, almost too clearly, the anger in her face, in her eyes, in her voice, in the way she gripped the handle of the knife and the way she jabbed it at him. And then two days ago, when he met her again. She was there to see Hanev. To mourn for him. And, as clearly as he could see the anger in her the time before, that time he could see the grief. He could not put it into words, exactly how he saw it, but it left no doubt in his mind that she really, truly missed him and that she had really, truly cried when he died, and that when he was alive she had really, truly cared about him. All of that left a little voice in his mind that asked uneasily, If someone tried to hurt me, would there be anyone to stand up for me like that? If I died, would anyone cry for me that way? And then that would lead to more questions, asking if he was really happy this way, if that was what he was missing. And he would think, And then, more crucially, He could get lost in these questions. The time after Keyas death was the worst. It was slightly different than this, though. More What am I going to do? questions. Nonetheless, he did not surface from them for months. Even after he did, he was unstable and it took him time to steady himself again. Thankfully, these werent as bad as that. Still, they werent pleasant. They made him feel unsure of himself, exposed to a new form of doubt that he hadnt built up an immunity to yet. He just wanted it to go away, but somehow he couldnt stop himself from letting the questions into his head. And he couldnt stop himself from trying to answer. He would never know how long he sat there pondering all this, but after some time of it he stood up rigidly, making the chair fall back and hit the marble floor with a he thought, angrily. He decided, just then, that he was leaving. He hated this place anyway. There were better places to go. Better people to meet. As he went to gather the few items he had brought, he thought scornfully, But then he couldnt stop wondering if it shouldnt be the other way around. Saphyra and Kaleana stood only a few hundred feet away from the Yaelen military base. They both gazed up at the building with a mix of fear and anticipation. Fright because they were both sure that whatever awaited them in that building wouldnt be good, and anticipation because that still werent sure exactly what that was. Saphyra squinted into the distance at the entrance, where two gaurds stood. No doubt there were also many more soldiers inside. Her stomach turned at the thought of attempting to get past all of them. She looked down at Kaleana. What are we going to do? she asked, almost desperately. Kaleana stared back at her for a moment, then wrote in the sand, Then she bobbed her head in a jerking nod, and flew off towards the building. Saphyra watched silently as she glided around the corner of the buildings. She waited, unwillingly tense, for some sign, some clue as to what Kaleana was doing. A few moments later, she heard it. It came as a sharp, high pitched screaming wail, so loud that Saphyras hands instantly flew to her ears Even from her distance, Saphyra could see the two guards jump. They exchanged words, then one of them - only one, to Saphyras dissappointment - ran off around the corner of the building.   The screams came again, even louder now. It was followed by another scream - the scream of the guard. Loud but still partially drowned out by the screeches of who Saphyra guessed must have been Kaleana. The guard still standing by the door jumped and whipped his head around to stare at the corner. He hesitated, then drew his blade and ran around the corner. She took this as her cue. By now it had alerted many more soldiers, and the sounds of their shouts were only barely audible over the sound that Kaleana was making. Saphyra set off down the hall with hurried, but sure, steps. She knew exactly where she was going.. She needed to find Reshaye. By now, it would probably have another host, and there was almost no doubt in her mind that that host would be kept in the same cell that she had been. Even though she had barely been out of her cell while she had been captive, somehow she still seemed to know the way without even needing to think about it. It was useful at that moment, and she was glad that she knew which ways to go. But then, in a way, it scared her. It let her know that whether she liked it or not, this place had left an imprint in her head, and a deep one too. She heard voices and footsteps, and with a quick shock of panic that was gone as soon at it had come, she ducked around a corner, pressed herself against the wall, and prayed that they didnt notice her. She was almost afraid to breathe as the footsteps passed. There were a lot of them, and all were headed towards the exit. Towards the sound of Kaleanas screeches. They passed, and Saphyra exhaled. She slid out from behind the corner and began walking again. Almost there. Just down this hall, around this corner... She soon found herself staring at the door, the one that opened to a staircase, down which was the room that held the cage. cage. It swung back slowly, and she stared down the staircase leading in that dark, musty room... She felt herself wavering again. She hated - - to admit it to herself, but she was afraid to go back down there. Even though she knew that it was impossible, somewhere in the back of her mind her subconscious couldnt help thinking that if she went down there again, she would be trapped just like before, all over again... At the very least, she would be taken prisoner, though it was more likely that she would be killed.
