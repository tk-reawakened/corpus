When Mr. Achere retired, probably in a year or two from now, it was Daehn who would take over the shop. Saphyra thought as she entered the shop, and again she was struck by how fast time moved. Only for a moment, though, before her mind quickly turned back to what was happening now. Even though she wished she didnt have to think about it. Daehn was sitting at the counter with a book - a ledger, she guessed - open in front of him, which he had been writing in. He heard her walk in and looked up, and his face was surprised when he saw it was her. Saphyra. Well, I... His voice trailed off when he saw her expression. Whats wrong with you? She swallowed, then said in a slightly trembling voice, Im leaving. His expression turned to a mix of surprise and horror. What? Im leaving, she said again. And... I dont think Im going to come back this time.  ?! he exclaimed, jumping up from his chair. Why?! I dont know, she answered, hopelessly. I just... need to. He began to walk out from behind the counter and come closer to her. That isnt a reason. She didnt know what to say, so she didnt say anything. He caught her wrist, as if he was holding her back. You cant leave. You have no reason to. She was beginning to think that it would have been so much easier if she didnt say goodbye. Im sorry, she said, feeling that guilt again. Im sorry. But I have to. Why? She couldnt answer that. She pulled her wrist out of his hand and said, as she moved towards the door, I just came to say goodbye. I need to go now. Ill miss you. Ill miss you so much. And Ill write. Like before, she sounded like she was just making excuses. But as fake as her words sounded, they were true, only stiffened by sadness and guilt. But I dont understand why- Please dont make this any harder than it already is, she interrupted in a quiet voice. And then she turned around and slipped through the door. She didnt look back, and she walked as fast as she could, on the edge of a jog. She heard the door open and close again after she left, and she knew that Daehn had come outside to try and stop her. But she didnt stop. She prayed that saying goodbye to Hanev would be easier. She hoped that he wouldnt try to stop her, would just understand and let it be. He always understood better than anyone else did. But something told her that even though he might understand, that wouldnt stop him from trying to make her stay. Hanev tended to wander around the town, doing odd-jobs for whoever needed his help. When he wasnt working, he was at the field. This left almost nowhere where he be, and so she was expecting it to be quite a task just to find him. She was very surprised when it was him that found her. She was walking down a street, and as she rounded a corner she heard, Finally! Ive been looking for you for awhile now, Saphyra. She turned to see Hanev walking towards her, looking happier than he usually did. She guessed that he hadnt run into her parents or Daehn. Before she had a chance to speak, he said, I have something to tell you. I know it took me awhile to decide, but Ive finally made a choice. He paused, then said in a sure voice, Im going to stay here. she thought, but it was something that was pushed to the back of her mind. Because standing there in the road, looking at him seem so happy because he had finally found a home just as she was deciding to leave hers, made all of the sadness trapped inside of her awaken. The next thing she knew, she was crying. He didnt ask what was wrong. Not right there in the street. Instead he put his hand on her back gently and said, Come to the field. *** She tried to stop crying, but she couldnt. When they first got to the field he tried to ask her what was wrong, but she couldnt stop crying long enough to answer and eventually he just let her cry. They both sat on the ground that way for awhile - she crying, and he just silently watching her. Then, when her tears finally began to slow, he asked, Now could you explain whats wrong? She sniffed and said in a cracked voice two words that she felt like she had to say far too many times that day: Im leaving. He looked shocked, but confused. For how long? ? He sounded distraught. I - I dont know, she said. I dont know how long. I dont know why. Of course you do. He stared at her evenly, in a gentle but firm way. She looked up at him and breathed a trembling sigh. I feel, she said slowly, like I need to leave. Like I just need to be somewhere other than here. Anywhere. Just this... to just . She sighed again, this time of slight frustration. I dont make any sense. I cant explain it. It feels worse even than before, doesnt it? he asked, in a way that said that he already knew the answer. She nodded miserably. This is what happens, he said slowly, afterwards. I dont know why it happens, but it does. It happened to me. That was why I was so depressed when you first came to see me. I never felt like I needed to run away, but... He paused thoughtfully. I think its different for everyone. But you shouldnt leave. She knew this would happen. But this feeling, she said, is so intense. Even learning that the war was over... I cant it, Hanev! I think Ill go insane if I stay here any longer, even though I dont really want to leave. I just stay here! Her voice rose sharply at the end, coming out in a spurt of frustrated anger. I think that if you leave, youll never be happy in one place again. Like Brayan. After Keya died, I think he was struck with the same thing as you. He left, and ever since hes been moving somewhere new every week, if that. Is that what you want to happen to you? He looked at her in a way that was almost angry, but not quite. Protective? You wont be happy that way, Saphyra. She looked down to the ground and considered his words. There isnt anything left for me here, she finally whispered. How can you even say that? he asked, appalled. There are so many people here that love you. This is your . You could live so happily here. His voice held just a hint of envy, but then his voice softened and the envy disappeared. And if you left, I know that it would make a lot of people very unhappy. She twisted a blade of grass between her fingers and didnt answer. I cant tell you what to do, he went on, but I hope more than anything that you at least try to stay. I can almost promise you that this will pass. I dont know how long it will take, but Im sure that it will pass. It might really be just me being selfish, but I think that it would be best for you if you tried. Saphyra thought, and replied after a few seconds of thought, Ill try. I cant make any promises, but Ill try. He smiled a very real, very happy smile. Thank you, he said. And then he kissed her. At first she was taken aback, but only for a moment. She didnt resist, but instead she kissed him back and felt a way that she hadnt felt in a long time. And, in a surprisingly calm way, she thought, When they pulled away from each other, neither of them said anything. They just stared. Saphyra was waiting for Hanev to say something - to apologize, or to mention Daehn, or say something like that, so Hanev-like. But he didnt, which is why Saphyra wondered if Daehn had talked to him yesterday, and told him that he and Saphyra were no longer a couple. The idea made her slightly embarrassed, but she didnt ask. Neither of them knew what to say, so it was silent. After a few minutes, Hanev said quietly, I probably shouldnt have done that. She blinked. Why not? I shouldnt have just kissed you like that. If you didnt want to. She couldnt help laughing. She knew he was going to say something like that. What were you going to say, Saphyra, may I please kiss you?? He realized how silly that sounded - she could tell by his face. But, he asked tentatively, you didnt mind? She shook her head slowly, as if she herself was only just realizing that she didnt mind. Then she said quietly, thoughtfully, No. I didnt mind at all. He considered this, looking down over the town. The field was high and flat, like a plateau, almost, and almost all of town could be seen from that point. There was so much activity going on; people and horses and other animals all poured through the streets, talking and laughing and shouting, selling and buying and haggling. It reeked strongly of something that Saphyra could only think to describe as , just moving and feeling, and reacting to the huge, marvelous world around you. Im glad youre going to try to stay, he finally said in a soft voice. She stared out over the town for a moment longer, and then she felt something slide into place, like a book being put onto a shelf, or a horse being put into a stall. Or - and she realized that this was the perfect way to describe it - a new chapter beginning in a book. Yes, that was it. She finally felt her end that had already ended move out of sight, and she finally - - felt the first word of that new beginning, the one she had been waiting for, write itself onto a new, clean page. I am too, she said decisively. I am too. And she knew what that first word was, she knew right away, before she even needed to think about it: Well, there you have it.
