# Saphyra's Journal - Intro and Entries 1 to 11

-Part One: Well, if you're anything like me, you'll probably skip this. But I guess some people might read it, so I figure it's worth writing anyway - I can at least explain the inconsistancy of this story, anyway. So here goes nothing. Saphyra's Journal was originally something I just wanted to do for fun. I started it, posting each entry as a blog entry, on April 21 (I think), back when I really stunk at writing - especially when I wasnt even trying, which I wasnt. I decided that I wasnt going to break my back over this; it would just be something quick that Id scribble down every day to keep whatever part of your brain that handles writing loose. Then, somewhere around entry three or four, I think, I actually began taking it more seriously. I actually began to try to make the entries as best as I could. That was my best work at the time. Yes, I know. Sad. But actually now Im pretty happy with my writing skills, and I think Saphyras Journal was the cause of the huge increase in how well I write. So thats why, throughout the journal, the writing might seem inconsistent - because I was improving a lot as a writer while I was writing it. So just hang in there when youre in the deep, dark, scary places of bad writing, okay? But anyway, even though Im very proud of the book as a whole, theres a lot Im not happy with. Its far from perfect - VERY far - which is why Im probably going to rewrite the entire thing from scratch. I dont intend to modify this version, however, any crits on the plot are much appreciated. There are bound to be a lot of plot holes, since I made the entire thing up as I went along, and Id like to fix what I can of the plot rewriting the book. So anyway, that was my little blurb. Congratulations if you actually read it. If not, I dont blame you, because I wouldnt have either. Dont worry, Ill make this short, but I suggest you dont skip it. Some important stuff is in here. - In this story, magic isnt represented as energy, or life force, or whatever magic is usually represented as. In this story, magic is a liquid. Magic can be manipulated by anyone, but it takes a lot of practice and discipline to be able to use it well. Not many people actually get around to learning it. Also, some people are more talented with it than others. Its sort of like drawing, for lack of a better analogy - you can teach yourself to draw, but in order to get really good at it youve got to have some talent. - This book focuses on two peoples: The Yaelens and the Quefans. Both lands speak the same language. However, the Yaelens used to have a language before it was decided that all of the lands would speak the same language. Saphyra is a Quefan, fighting in the looooooong war between the Yaelens and the Quefans. I never really considered what the war was over - in the long run, it doesnt much matter to any of the characters, or you, the reader. All you need to know is that the Quefans and the Yaelens dont get along. - Daehn isnt Saphyras brother, or cousin, or relative of any kind. Hes her boyfriend. Basically. Youd figure that out eventually, but people seemed to think that because in the beginning I wasnt very comfortable writing lovey-dovey stuff, so it sort of seemed like Daehn was an older brother or something. He isnt. - The characters were originally a couple of years older than they are now, so if some time spans dont quite add up, or if I forgot to change something, just ignore it. I think thats about it. Now get to reading! April 19 Well its final then. Ive been called to serve in the army. Damn. My family was distraught when they heard, but they were accepting enough. Lianne was practically clinging to me for the rest of the evening, until she dropped off to sleep. I feel so bad for her. Shell be the only one left now. My father was the most accepting, by far. As an old soldier himself, I suppose he realized that I had to do what I had to do, and he made this clear to myself, Lianne, and Mother. It helped us all. At least, it did a little bit. Mother still cried, and Lianne still clung, and Father still looked about as saddened as a man can be, but it helped. But Daehn when he heard, accepting was probably the last word that could be used to describe him. What? What were they thinking? he cried. Youre just a girl. Not even twenty yet! It should be me going to war, not you. I told him he was a sexist jerk for saying so - and besides, it wasnt as if he is much older than I am -, but I probably shouldnt have. He was just worried about me. He scowled at that. What were they thinking? he said again, mostly to himself this time. They were thinking that they needed some good magicians. Thats what they were thinking, I answered. And its not up to you to decide if Im going or not. He ignored me and went on to say that I just shouldnt go, and they probably called so many other people that they wouldnt even notice if I didnt show up. I told him that yes, they would, thank you very much, and then they would come and chop all of our heads off. Besides, I said, I want to go. Which, between you and me, diary, was not true at all, and still isnt true. I dont want to go, not in the slightest. I dont know if I was trying to trick Daehn or myself when I said that I did, but either way it was a rock hard lie. He looked at me in a way that showed that he knew I was lying. Riiiiiiight, he said. And then he leapt into his previous arguments about how I was too young and how the military is insane for calling a girl like you and that I should just forget about the whole damn thing and not go. After awhile I just interrupted him and said, Daehn, Im going. And thats that. So here I am, on the road to the Quefa army base. I still have five more hours of riding ahead of me. Too much time, in my opinion. I just want to get there and be done with it. Being out here alone on the road gives me too much time to think, and I really dont want to be thinking right now, since I will probably be thinking about what I left behind at home. Truth-be-told: I am terrified. I am the only magician in my area; I dont even know if Im considered good at it or not. I taught the trade mostly to myself. And going to war that is definitely the most frightening thing of all. I dont feel like writing about this anymore. It isnt exactly something Id rather think about at the moment. Ill write more tomorrow, if I can. -Saphyra April 20 Yesterday was horror. I arrived at the base around nine at night, and didnt even have a chance to see many other soldiers amongst all the checking in and checking out and signing this and signing that. It was about the most frustrating thing Ive ever done in my life! By the end of it all, I was ready to tear my hair out. I was the only one in my room, too. There were six beds, but I was the only one there. Id learn why later. Today we were all called to the training room early in the morning. I was half asleep when I arrived there, but I was shocked awake after a moment. I was the only girl there. Every other recruit there was a male. So that was why my room was empty. I was beginning to think that maybe I should have taken Daehns advice and stayed at home. After we had all assembled ourselves in a shoulder-to-shoulder line against the wall, a tall, thin, scowling man at the front of the room surveyed us. He did not look pleased. There was a much younger, dark-haired man who stood just behind him, and the older man turned and said something that I couldnt make out to him. Then he turned back to us. So youve all- he began, but was interrupted when the door flew open and a girl, maybe a year or two younger than me, breezed into the room. She looked around with a critical eye, then pushed a stray strand of black hair behind her ear and said, Im sorry for being late, she said, but in a way that said that she wasnt sorry at all. She stepped forward and handed some papers to the older man. He glanced at them, then glared down at the girl. Who do you think you are, just gliding in here a day late with that kind of attitude? She matched his glare. Akee Jenia, at your service, she answered, her voice outright defiant. I dont think Ive ever seen someone as angry looking as the man did at that moment. He was clearly someone who was not used to being stood up to, and apparently he wasnt too fond of the experience. It looked like he just might reach down and strangle Akee when the other man, the younger one who had just stood and watched up till now, stepped forward. Unless you want to be put out of here, Akee, he said in a calm, quiet voice. I suggest you act like the lady Im sure is in there somewhere. Akee shot him a glare worse than what she had given the older man. I was never treated as a lady growing up, she spat. Why should I act like one? And I can if I want to. You wont turn me out. Im not stupid. You need every good magician you can get for this war, and Im the best of the best. Now, if I may, I think Ill go and take my place. With that, she turned and walked - actually, it was more of a victorious sort of strut. She was right, and she knew it, but I think the more satisfying part of it to her was that they knew it, too. She spotted me, and came over. She made room for herself and stod next to me. Thank God youre here, she whispered to me. I would murder myself if I was the only lady - she said the word lady in a mocking voice- here. Ive been around too many males in my life. I couldnt think of how to respond, but thankfully the older man started talking again and I was saved from replying. He didnt say anything worth writing down, though it would be a little hard to write what he said down when I dont even remember it anyway! Mostly it was a lot of things about how wed have to practice good discipline (with a glare to Akee). This lasted for a few hours. Then we were all given a small jar of magic, so we could show what we could do. Looking at this magic, all I could think was that it was so different looking than what I was used to. This was shinier, and pure-er looking. The magic I usually worked with was dull and brownish, with pieces of dirt sometimes in it. It made me nervous, but I thought that it was just magic anyway, and that it didnt really matter. Ohhh, breathed Akee as she received her jar. This is high-powered magic. Its the best you can get. This made me slightly more nervous, but I still thought that it was used just the same as any other magic. So when it was my turn, I stepped forward, jar in hand. I wasnt quite sure what I was going to do, but I had some idea. I thought that I would do some shape-shifting of it, some levitation, and probably end in a grand explosion of some sort. I was willing to improvise if I needed to. I unscrewed the cap. Thinking the magic would need a good pull to get itself started, I put my hand near the magic, then drew it sharply back. The result? The most mortifying moment of my life. As luck would have it, this best-you-can-get magic did not need a good pull to get itself started. So when I gave it a good pull, it flew out of my control and sprayed across the room, causing numerous little explosions and nearly everyone in the room to try and control it. After things had calmed down, I slowly slunk back to my spot in line silently. The older man (whose named Id learned was Sanan Lew) looked at me in a way that was all but a glare, and the younger one (whose name Id learned was Sanan Hanev - by the way he acted, I never would have guessed that he was of equal rank to Sanan Lew) looked at me in a sort of pitying way, and everyone else looked at me in a way that said, What the hell was she thinking? God, what did you used to use? hissed Akee. You act like you used dirt or something. She was fairly close, actually. I used to find some in the woods or underground, I answered weakly. Im not used to magic that pure. I only got more mortified when Akee stepped up and did perfectly, as did the vast majority of everyone else. I was the only one who made a mistake that severe. I suppose having a respected reputation is out of the question now So Im already a failure on my first day in the army. Father would be so proud. -Saphyra April 23 Its amazing what can happen in three days. Just amazing. How a life can just end, just like that, in three days. On the twenty first we were already called to war. Already. Akee was right, apparently, when she said that the army needed us magicians badly, because they certainly didnt hesitate to send us out onto the battlefield. We were sent out with nothing but a quick word from Sanan Lew about how We have to take care of ourselves, because no one was going to save our asses in battle, a dagger (In case you run out of magic, Sanan Hanev said as he gave them to us), and a belt that had lots of different compartments in it, each containing some magic. And with that, we were sent out onto the battlefield. No plan. Not even a good overview of our enemy. Some of us didnt even know who were fighting. I knew that they were called the Yaelens, and that they were known for their magicians, but nothing else. And yet there I was, walking out the door, about to try and kill as many of them as I could. After that I just fought. Everything seemed so blurry and unorganized. I had to stop and think and make sure I wasnt attacking one of our own. There was blood everywhere. I thought, before, that with magic the deaths would be cleaner and less gory, but I was wrong. To many people will killing not to end life, but to inflict pain. And with magic, this is just too easy. The fight gradually slowed as the wounded and dead began to outway the living and fighting. I finally had the chance to stop for a moment and breathe. Looking out across what was once a sandy-colored, flat plain, I could see the difference. The ground was redish colored from the blood that had seeped into it, and the plain was no longer flat for all the dead bodies making hills over it. People still fought and slashed at each other, with their daggers and magic. And the ground grew redder. Then I caught sight of Sanan Hanev, crouched down behind a huge rock. He was with Akee and two other soldiers, who I recognized from before. He beckoned for me to join them, so I crept over to them. Were going to attack the remaining Yaelens, he whispered to me when I got there. He seemed nervous and edgy, even more than the rest of us were. No wonder. He opened up a tube of magic from his belt. He whispered something I couldnt hear, then waved the magic around in some sort of spell. Stealth spell, he explained. So theyll have a harder time hearing or seeing us. So we have the advantage. Lying traitor. Alright, he whispered, after waiting silently for a few moments. Ill go first. Then you follow. He began to creep around the edge of the rock. Akee pushed him aside. Ladies first, she said mockingly. Then, in spite of Sanan Hanevs hurried protests, she slid around the edge of the rock. We heard a shout - Akees shout - and then a thump as a nineteen-year-old girl - sized body fell to the ground and the shout was silenced. Hanev, you idiot, get out here! I heard a gruff, heavily accented mans voice call out. Hanev visibly winced as he rose to his feet and walked out from behind the rock. What? he snapped. I watched, horrified, and wondered if this was what it looked like. Where are they? Where are the others? the mans voice barked. Why did you send out the girl? Now we have one less! So what? Hanev answered, sounding rather sickened. Youre just going to kill all but one anyway. Though I must admit. He paused. She was one of the better ones. The other man made a grunting sound. Fine, Hanev. I do not care. Get the others out here. Fine. A moment later, I felt myself being pulled forward by some invisible hand, a spell, most likely. I resisted and strained with all that I had in me, but still I was dragged out from behind the rock along with the two others. My stomach turned as I caught sight of Akees mangled body lying lifeless on the ground. We were held there, still struggling, as a tall bulky man with a scraggly beard and thinning brown hair surveyed us. Which should we keep? he finally demanded, turning to Hanev. Which is the best? Hanev stared at us one by one, seeming torn by the sick decision he had to make. Part of me wished I would be killed, but in the other part of me the animal instinct to survive shone through. The girl, Hanev answered in a shaky voice, eyes turned to me. Keep her. She is talented. What?, I thought. So obviously a lie. Id never even seen pure magic before. Talented, right. Excuses, excuses, you stupid idiot, you stupid lying traitor. So that was how I ended up here, back in this musty old disgusting cell, alone. The only person I could come even moderately close to calling a friend is dead, along with the other two soldiers that were tricked by Hanev. All I have now are a race my country is at war with and a filthy traitor. When I was put here, he came to see me. He was a wreck. I dont know if he was guilty or afraid or what, but he was a mess. Either way it doesnt matter, he has still done to me and the others what has been done. I asked him why he chose me, when he came. I wanted to know. Because you really are the most talented, he said, matter-of-factly. I snickered at that. Honestly, you are, he protested. It would have been Akee if she hadnt His voice trailed off. Why am I even being kept here? I demanded. Why didnt you just kill me like the others? Why do they keep me here? Because, he answered, eyes carefully averting mine. Because they need a talented magician. What about you? I cried. You know more than I do. Than any of us do. Why dont they just use you? As far as they know, he said quietly, eyes averting mine even more. I only know enough magic to signal them when I was ready and to get you restrained. So that was it. The idiot pretended he didnt know magic, save his own skin and gain a little in the journey, and then sell me to the Yaelens. All for his own selfish reasons. So I sit here now, my paper dotted with tears and sweat and blood dripped from my body, and Im alone, and Im restrained, all because of that idiot bastard. I hate him, I hate him so much. He still hasnt left. He stands outside my CAGE now, just watching me like Im some kind of BIRD that he caught in a TRAP. Which is what he DID, that TRAITOR. That STUPID IDIOT TRAITOR. What is he doing there? Making sure I dont kill myself? Making sure the Yaelens still have their TALENTED magician so he doesnt get himself killed? Well Ill tell you one thing, my lovely little book, its just you and me now. And youre just only a book. A bunch of papers bound together with string. Youre no good to me. Useless. So I suppose Im just alone, plain and simple. Alone. -Saphyra April 25 I keep playing the scene of my capture over and over in my head. I cant help it. Its so hard to describe I feel like it was just happening, and then it was over, and now it seems so hard to remember. I keep trying to remember silly things, like the way Akees head cocked snootily when she defied Hanev, the exact way her voice rose and fell when she quipped, Ladies first. The way Hanevs eyes had shifted when he was choosing the captive, exactly where his foot stepped when he turned to tell the Yaelen his choice. The precise position of the hairs in the Yaelens beard, the defined sound of Akees body hitting the ground. I dont know why I do this. Maybe Im in shock. I dont know. All I know is that the anger that practically possessed me the first two days has now been forced underneath new sadness and homesickness and greif. Its still there, and just as strong as before too, but its covered up by other things, and the moment the other feelings begin to fade it will show itself again. Sometimes these feelings do fade, a little bit, and the anger emerges, and I look outside my bars at Hanev, who seems to have been assigned guard duty or something, and would like nothing more than to wring his neck, stab him, [I]anything[/I] just to get him away from this earth. Anything. What the Yaelens in charge of this place were thinking by putting him right outside my cage I cant tell. To me it just seems like a crude attempt to tease me, taunt me with what I desire to do right outside the bars of a cage the restrain me from doing it. Maybe its really just part of Hanevs sick deal with the Yaelens; he gets the magicians, and he watches over them. Maybe that was the deal. I wonder what he got out of this stupid deal. The curiosity of that question pokes and prods at me all the time. I would love so badly to know what must be so VERY breathtakingly amazing that it would be worth betraying us for. I think he does feel some guilt though. Just a little. The way he watches me when I cry makes me think he does. I dont care; it doesnt help what he did at all. All I have to say on the subject is that it serves him right. I hope he dies of guilt. I hope its a long, painful death. As if that would happen, though. He cant feel much guilt, or else he would break away from this stupid place. Or else he would let me go. I wish he would let me go But something tells me he wont. Not until they use me for whatever they want to use me for. I cant believe I write this, but part of me hopes that time will come soon. I want to go home. -Saphyra April 27 Today I finally asked Hanev what Ive wanted to ask him since I got here. I just walked over to the side of my cage, peered, through the bars, and asked, What did they pay you with? To get a magician? Hanev looked up. He looked extremely taken aback. I suppose I cant blame him for that; it was a bolt out of the blue. I havent even spoken to him since the first day, after all. Once his initial surprise was over, though, his eyes quickly moved away from mine, avoiding eye contact. Nothing you have to know about. It isnt any of your concern. Of course it is, I answered, my voice turning hostile as my anger rose. Id love to know what was worth trading a human life - no, four human lives, three dead, one alive - for. What was it? Whose money? Whose position? Whatever is was, - I glared at him - It must have been so worthwhile. His eyes snapped back to me. He seemed at a total loss for words. What? he finally asked, voice rising. You heard, I said, and waited expectantly. His eyes averted mine. Again. It was nothing youll care about, he said. His voice was quiet, nearly inaudible. Of course Ill care, I answered. I wouldnt have asked if I didnt. So tell me. He looked away. No. Now leave. I cant! Im in a ! I shouted, slamming my hand against the bar. Regretting putting me here now? I KNOW you cant LEAVE, he said evenly, the tension just barely restrained in his voice, but just stop talking and leave me be! So you want to hide it, dont you? Feeling guilty, is that it? No, he murmured. I am not feeling guilty. But his voice said otherwise. WELL YOU SHOULD! I screeched. I felt angrier than Id ever felt it my life. Angrier than I felt when I was first taken here, even. Why this anger was coming out now? I dont know. I still dont know. Killing Akee! Killing those other two! Practically killing ! I went on. How could you? How can ANYONE do that?! By now he was on his feet, and was clearly struggling to keep himself calm. Akee, he said with forced coolness, was not killed by me. She killed herself. I told her to stay away. I warned her. But she didnt listen. And YOU, - he glowered at me - dont even know what happened to the others. You dont even know if they were killed. Dont be so haughty, I spat. I heard them scream from down here. Im not the stupid girl you think I am. He sneered. If I thought you were , then I wouldnt have chosen you as the magician. You also said that you wouldnt have chosen me if I wasnt a  - I said the words talented magician in a mocking tone - but we both know thats a lie too, dont we! I nearly destroyed the base because I hadnt so much as seen pure magic before! And despite of that, Im so very , arent I? Youre the one that said you didnt think I was stupid. He shot me a piercing glare, then turned and sat back down. Id rather not discuss this anymore, he said, in an arrogant way that made it sound like he thought that just because he said it, it would be that way. But despite that tone, his voice was slightly shaky. I had upset him. He was wavering. So I persisted. Our argument went on, in circles, it seemed, for what felt like hours. Finally, I dropped back, emotionally drained, frustrated, and angry. I failed to learn anything, except that Hanev must be very guilty about what he did and that he can yell very loudly if he wants to. I think the first one I can use to my advantage if I want; the latter, not so much. I just read over that last paragraph. I cant believe I wrote that. That I think the first one I can use to my advantage if I want comment. That sounds horrible. I really am changing, arent I? Turning into what I can only think of as a much crueler, more manipulative version of Akee. Although Akee struck me as a manipulative person to begin with. But then, I have a right to take advantage of his guilt, dont I? He took advantage of us. We trusted his role as Sanen, and he replied by killing all but one of us and making the third a prisoner. It isnt as bad, me taking advantage of him to get myself out of this situation. It isnt. Another thing I dont want to think about right now. That list seems to get longer and longer. April 28 Early this morning - though I cant be sure, since I have no windows and no clock - I was woken by someone grabbing my arm and pulling me up. Roughly. After I blinked the sleep out of my eyes I looked up to see it was the man that had shown up at the battlefield, the tall one with the thin hair and scraggly beard. He didnt say a word to me; instead he thrust open the door to my cage and dragged me - literally - outside of it and down a narrow hall. As he dragged me, I wondered what was going on. I was tired, and it still seems a little unclear. But as my head cleared, I began to resist - just from instinct - and the man stopped walking and whirled around to face me. You will struggle, girl, he growled, sounding menacing with his accent. Not if you know what is good for you. After the I went with him without a struggle, but only because I was practically half dead with fear. I told myself that Hanev said that the Yaelens needed a magician, that they wouldnt kill me if I did what they asked. But there were two things wrong with this: 1. Hanev is not the most truthful person in the world. 2. I was also terrified of what they would ask me to do. 3. Why would the Yaelens need a magician so badly if they were known for their magic themselves? Needless to say, none of this was terribly comforting. The man pulled me up several flights of stairs and then more hallways in between them. He walked fast, almost running, and I tripped over my own feet trying to keep up with him and then tripped again when I tried to stand. He didnt seem to care, if he noticed at all. He just dragged me on, like a little girl drags her favorite doll. We stopped at the top of a staircase, and the man banged open a square door above us. Light flooded in, and I covered my eyes. I hadnt seen daylight in what felt like ages, and the light hurt more than I would have expected it to. It still seeped through my eyelids and made my eyes ache. We climbed up through the door, and I opened my eyes just enough to see where we were. The roof. It was flat, and circular, with a wall surrounding its edge. It reminded me of the towers on old fashioned castles, in a way, though I think this must have been larger. A few other people stood around it, leaning against the wall nonchalantly. The man threw me down, and I hit the ground hard. I broke the fall with my hands, and when I lifted them blood spotted my palms where the gritty stone had bitten into my skin. Get up. The mans gruff, barking voice sounded agitated for no reason. Get up! I did.
