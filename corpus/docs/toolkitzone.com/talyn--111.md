# Talyn

In the subject of Philosophy, no topic is more hotly debated than that of the existence of God. The arguments for and against His existence have raged back and forth for centuries now, from the 16th Century philosopher/scientist Descartes, all the way up to contemporary films and literature. Yet still no answer has been found to this very significant question and there is considerable belief that none ever will be found.
Programming and God
However, that doesnt stop people from trying! Everyone it seems has his or her own slant on what God is and what His significance in life is. Some believe it exactly as the bible states, some believe in the Hindi myriad of Gods, others say that God is a force or energy, while some say that he never existed in the first place. Whatever your personal belief is, you are sure to have one.
Now Im not going to try and answer the question of whether God exists or not, but what I want to do is share something which Ive thought up, which could be another slant on what God is.
Before I begin to explain my theory, Ill just mention a few conventions Ill use during this article. Firstly, Ill refer to God as he; this is not a statement that God is male, simply the common way of referring to him. Secondly, Ill be talking from the point of view that there is one God (however you should be able to see how the multiple Gods theory can also tie in). Also, the programming language I write the example in is based on RPGCode, as alomst everyone here should know that language!
Right, lets get on and philosophise!
As you can probably tell by the title, my slant on God is going to be linked to the art of programming. Not a new idea, but one that has grabbed much attention in recent years due to a certain trilogy of films being released. But the idea dates back far further than when the Wachowski brothers got their dirty little mitts on it.
Remember that philosopher I mentioned earlier, Descartes? He came up with the idea that we could be living in a world where our senses were being permanently deceived, so as to cover up what was real. In this case though, it wasnt machines, it was simply an Evil Demon (the evil demon instead of God, since Descartes reasoned that an all-loving God would not want us to be deceived). This idea has grown and changed over the centuries, until it has finally become this notion of this world being a computer program, by aliens, AI machines etc. I however want to move away from this rigidity, by drawing parallels between programming and the world we live in.
God is a programmer
This is the bare bones of my theory, and its eventual conclusion. Allow me to put my case and then you can decide for yourself.
Think of this world as a whole. Marvellous isnt it? How everything interacts with everything else to create equilibrium. But when you start to look closer, it looks a little too perfect. Processes such as Osmosis (the transfer of water in plants) are well designed to have come about simply by chance. This is the classic argument for the existence of a designing God. When you start to wonder how it was all put together, then it becomes overwhelming.
However, put it into a programming context, and things become clearer. God is the program architect. He designed this world and created in it a set of variables; us. Here we all are as variables: But these variables are not just any variable. Theyre actually arrays of several dimensions so as to encompass all of our traits: With this variable information, a rendering program can create an image of us in a computer simulation using code such as this:
** Alex! = 1 David! = 1 Marion! = 1 Finn! = 1
** Alex[1]! = 190 *Alexs Height (190 cm) Alex[2]! = 62 *Alexs weight (62kg) Alex[3]$ = Green *Alexs eye colour.
** If(Alex[3]$ = Green) {
SetImage(greeneyes.jpg) } If(Alex[3]$ = Blue) {
SetImage(blueeyes.jpg) } Etc.
My point is, is that when you think of us all as variables, or sets of numbers, things start to become easier to understand. I am aware however of the obvious flaw in this. Conscience. We are thinking, feeling animals that surely cant be expressed as a variable. Or can it? If you think about multi-dimensional variables, then it is possible you could store all the character aspects of a human. However the idea of free will and choice makes this line or thought shaky, since you surely cannot code these elements?
So thats got the basis of my theory established. Now to move on to something else which I think this theory can explain, and thats the problem of evil and suffering.
When talking about the possibility of God, this is the argument that comes up the most frequently. If God is all-powerful and all loving, why does he allow pain and suffering? Youve probably used it yourself or had it used against you. This argument can be defeated though, when you take away an assumption.
Lets for a moment say that God is NOT all-powerful. When thinking in programming terms, this isnt so hard to believe. Sure, the programmer has the ability to create or do whatever they want with their program, however they are still not all-powerful, even though it would seem that way. A programmer still has to work within the confines of the system that he is using. For example, you cannot write a computer program that will make your laptop jump into the air and do the Highland Fling on your desk for the amusement of you and your mates at work. Whenever you write a program, you are limited by the confines of the system or language or pure physical impossibilities.
Now put this idea of confines back into the theory of God. Yes he is all-powerful, and he can do whatever he likes in the universe, however there are some rules which simply cannot be broken. Evil could be one of these. It can be argued that times of suffering and danger bring people closer together, and so evil isnt really so evil after all, and thats its use in the system. Whatever its role, if evil is a rule of the programming language that God writes the world in, it surely cannot be broken.
Another take on this could be that evil is a glitch. God really designed the world to be perfect, but he hasnt quite got it right yet! Evil might be one of the items on his debugging list along with natural disasters and deja vu. This ideas a little far-fetched, but its another point of view at least!
To wrap this all up then, God is a programmer. However Im now going to move away from the idea of God sitting at his brand new Dell computer with Visual Studio.NET 2005 and move it back into the realms of reality. God doesnt program in the way we think of it (nicely coloured words on a screen), he programs in the laws of nature, physics and mathematics, using a language that we cannot even begin to imagine. We are his little variables, running around a system of rules and glitches.
The idea of this world being a computer simulation will come and go, just as Descartes theory of the Evil Demon passed as the ideas of religion became weaker. When computers have had their day and some new technology comes about, we will more than likely speculate about our world being something to do with that. However I think, that to find what this world is really made up of, you need to look at the fundamentals instead of the technologies.
Physics, Mathematics, Nature. The oldest programming languages in the universe, and the building blocks of everything that we see, taste, hear, smell and touch. If there is a God, these are the languages that he speaks in.
*Talyn*
