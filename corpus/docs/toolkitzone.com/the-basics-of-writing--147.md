# The Basics of Writing

The first thing I want to say in this article is this: I dont know if Im even good enough a writer to be giving other people advice on how to write. You can choose to take my advice or not, and this is something Im doing to try to help who I can, if I can, and the things I say in this might not be the proper way of writing. (And there, my friends, was a run-on sentence. That is something that you do not want in your writing unless youre going for dramatic effect. *heh*) You dont have to follow my advice, so whether its proper or not shouldnt matter. All that aside, let me explain, first of all, what this article is going to be about. To be incredibly obvious and basic, its about writing. Im sure you already know that. To be more specific, now, its mostly going to be about to basics of writing. Getting started, thinking of a story, that sort of thing. Ill try to give the best advice I can, and although that still might not be incredibly great advice, I hope it will help you nonetheless! So, I dont see any reason why not to just get started. This is probably the hardest part of writing overall. At least, I think so. Its not something that you can force, which sometimes can really stink. You can, however, help move it along by doing things that inspire you (as clichéd as it sounds). For example, I personally listen to Joe Hisaishis music to try and get myself to think of new ideas. There is a problem with this method, though. Lets just say you watched a movie or TV show or read a book that inspired you. All well and good, until you start writing and wait, this plot sounds strangely familiar! Congratulations, youve fallen into the most common trap amongst writers. Sometimes things inspire you a little too much, so youve always got to be willing to step back and look at your plot from a distance to see if youre being a copy cat. Hopefully, you arent. But then again, a lot of times you will be. Always be ready to trash what youve got and start again. Another method I find works well the get the ink flowing is just sitting down and writing. Doesnt matter what it is, just write. Sometimes youll end up with a bunch of gobble-de-gook that means absolutely nothing, but sometimes you end up with the beginning to a decent story. Lastly but not leastly, we have dreams. From my experience - which, albeit, isnt all that much - I think that dreams are the very best source of a good story but unfortunately the most rare. Some dreams make great stories, others well, not everyone wants to read about the pink dancing bunnies (ha ha). The thing with dreams, though: 1. As I said before, its only once in awhile that you get one worth writing for. 2. They are incredibly hard to forget. One has already been talked about. Two: the only thing you can really do about this is have a piece of paper and a pencil handy near you bed so right when you wake up you can scribble down something quick about the dream. Just so you dont forget it. Sometimes you can really get a good story idea from dreams, as long as you can remember it! Okay, Im going to go out on a limb here in this section. In the vast majority of articles you see on writing, you read make a rough draft of your entire plot first or be sure to make an outline of the whole book before you start to write. Well, Im not going to say that. All I can say is that the methods above work for some people, and they dont work for others. Me? I cannot STAND working with an outline. It just totally drains the fun out of writing for me. Other people cant work without one. I honestly think that you could go either way. The only thing I suggest is that even if you dont work with an outline or rough draft, at least have an idea about where the plot is going in your head. Just a sketchy outline in your mind. Something like that. Trust me, its very hard to build an entire story on nothing. Also, be ready to edit. Theres likely to be a lot more plot holes if you dont work with an outline than if you do, obviously. So be prepared. Remember at the beginning of the article when I said that the hardest part of writing was thinking of a plot? Well, I lied. I think that for most people being able to finish a story is a weak point. Its a weak point in me, thats for sure. I find it kind of ironic that Im giving advice on how to stick to a story when I myself have a lot of trouble doing just that, but lately Ive been a lot better so Ill try to help if I can. In the past, whenever I got bored writing a story I would think, Well, if its boring for me to write, it must be boring for someone to read, so I should stop. I was so totally, completely wrong that it isnt even funny. The problem wasnt even that I was bored with the story - it was just that I didnt feel like writing, or that I had hit a slow spot. The trick is just knowing how to get past these things. First things first. When you write, get rid of all distractions. If youre the kind of person that slips online whenever, disconnect from the internet so you cant. If you have a cat that wont leave you alone, than shut him out of your room. If you tend to look out the window and just zone out, shut the shades. Any distractions that you can get rid of, get rid of. Half the battles done already after you do that. So youve done all of that, and youre STILL bored writing? Well, okay, one of two things could be happening here: A. Writing just isnt your thing. B. Youve hit a slow spot in the story. Im not even going to get into A, because there isnt anything anyone can do about that anyway. So youve hit a slow spot in the story. This is a tough thing to get past. There are two things that I think really help this situation. One: Just force yourself to keep going. Yeah, this can backfire. But sometimes its all there is left, and besides, sometimes it works fine. But try the next thing first, just in case... Two: Get really into your story. Get to know your characters so well that you love to write them. Youd be surprised how much this helps. Near the beginning of Saphyras Journal, I really had to force myself to write sometimes. But, as I began to like Saphyra and Hanev more and more, it got unbelievably easier to write, even in the boring-est of the boring parts. Just write short stories with your characters until you know them as well as youd know a person in real life. I probably sound crazy right now, but it works, I swear. Eventually theyll begin to grow on you, and youll know exactly what they say in different situations. This makes writing slow parts a heckuva lot easier because youre having fun just writing the character. I wrote more than I expected to in this article. Still, I know I didnt cover a lot - a LOT - about writing. Even so, I hope I helped. Again, these are just the things that work for me. Everyones different. These things might not work for you, or maybe they will. Either way, writing is something thats really fun, and you should enjoy it! Dont try to force yourself into something that doesnt feel right. If youre going long periods of time of forcing yourself to write - not just a slow spot in the story - then its time to stop and start again. Hopefully it wont come to that, though!
