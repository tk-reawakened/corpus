# The story so far

At the Winter Expo held late last year, few games attracted as much attention as the community based game: TKLand. In this exclusive article, Ill be giving you the low-down of the game so far, talk to the designers themselves and also reveal some new screenshots of the guys from Indreams Studios excellent work. TKLand, short for Toolkit Land, is by no means the first stab at a community based game. However, the early attempts all ended up in disaster, with no demo ever being released. But as the Toolkit's popularity and community grew, it was inevitable that someone would have another go. Up stepped the guys from Indreams Studios to take on this mighty feat. The cast of TKLand includes some of the most loved members of the community, past and present, and even relative newbies should recognise names like Spyder, R043482, Asimir and the legendary MrG. Those of you who havent been in the community that long may not recognise some of the other cast members, but there are enough of the present community members to keep everyone satisfied. TKLand itself has only had one release to date, and that was at the Winter Expo last year. The release was only a graphical demo, but it still captured the imagination of the community, so much so that it won multiple awards in the Expo itself. The artwork of David Thany (Indy) and the melodies from Norman Vreuls(DarkwinterDreams), Chaos and Pezzer wowing the audience right from the word go. The intro sequence to this game is breathtaking from the start, with a superb title screen and impressive story sequence. After that youre free to wander the TKLand as far as you like and interact with many of the characters who appear from place to place. By far one of my favourite conversations was with Asimir near Thanian Nation HQ. This is a very humorous piece of scripting, showing the writing talent behind the project as well as the obvious musical and graphical talent. So far, TKLand looks to be a very promising title and one of the few community games to really get a good footing on the development ladder. Lets hope that the game will continue to go from strength to strength, and we hope to see much more at the Summer Expo this year. (This is completely new, so go and take a look!) The very kind Sander (aka Woozy) got in touch recently offering some new images to be put in the article, as well as the exclusive interviews with the guys. I recently sent off a set of questions for the IDS guys to have a go at answering. Here's some of the best bits: (Unfortunately, Indy is having some problems with his internet connection and so couldn't contribute) - : Indy started in the summer of 2003 and asked me to do music for it. After a while the project was put on hold and later, somewhere around spring 2004, Chaos and Woozy joined the team. Right now almost everyone in IDS is working on it. - : Yup, what DwD said. For the most, this is how everything goes: Indy: Graphics/Story, Sander: Programming/Board Design, DwD: Music/Sounds, Chaos: Music/Sounds/Script, Pezzer: Music/Sounds, OBCT: Graphics Mike is also planning on helping out with programming later on (Of course, I don't need help but I don't wanna make him sad =P) - : The bad organization at the start. At first we didn't really know how to organize something this big properly(which is right now probably the biggest problem most people in the TK Community have while making games). Right now we are doing things much better, though. - : The fact that I hardly have time to spend on it =( - : We hope they'll enjoy it, of course =) - : Yeah, and that they will become interested in other IDS projects, which will be shown on the future IDS site. - : A profound sence of oneness with the universe...no... well, yes, but no. I just hope they will enjoy it and get involved with other IDS projects. - : Yeah, hopefully people will enjoy it which make it all worth while. - : No! Release dates suck, we had one for the Winter Expo 2004 and I know one thing: Never Again! - : We work as fast as we can, it's done when it's done. - : Time is scarce these days, we aren't going to rush anything. We want a clean professional release. - Well, Indy started out with TK Land alone. At that point he just wanted to make a nice, small game about the Tk Community. Right now almost everyone in IDS is working on it, and while we were building the graphics demo we started to realise the game was becoming much bigger than Indy had first planned (Blame Chaos for this =P). Anyway, after the demo was released we decided to totaly redo all the boards to fit with the new plans we had, the original town was simply too small & simple. - Well basically from the graphics Indy made. When he asked me to do the music for TKLand in the early days of the project i asked him to show me the boards first so i could get inspired by the environment of TKland. So it really came from the environment and graphics. Now we also get inspiration from the story too. - Very, very good. At first the project depended too much on Indy - he made the graphics along with all the boards, and since he doesn't has that much time progress wasn't going very fast. After the release of the demo I offered to make the boards for the game so progress could speed up a bit (And it did, heh). We also decided lately that OBCT will also be helping out with the boards, increasing progress even more. - Well much to my surprise it's easier than i thought. The reason why this team works well is probably because we respect each other and all are busy enough to let others in on a project and accept help and that is a great thing as when i am terribly busy Pezzer or Chaos may have free time to do more work and the other way round, so it's actually better than working alone. I must say working in my studio together with other people is quite hard though. But working in a team for a game where evryone composes a part of the music works very well. : It depends on the composing style, I work well with dwd because he composes in the same way - drawing out each individual note by hand. It's a very clean way of composing - very easy to pick up each others tracks and know what's going on. I would find it much harder to work with a keyboard composer because it's a completely different way of doing things. So, It's quite difficult for me to pick up one of pezzer's tracks, but very easy to pick up one of dwd's tracks. - I think I like the music for the Town of Thanian Nation the most so far because it's got a sunny feeling to it. But it's a song that is part of a piece of the game still in production. It's clear to see that TKLand has a huge amount of potential and talent. The feeling I got from the creators is that they really care about this game and want to see it do well. If you haven't got the Graphical Demo from the Winter Expo, then go and get it now from . 2005 should be a great year for the IDS team, with their other projects (such as Bounty) also demanding a large amount of attention. I'm confident they'll go from strength to strength. Thanks again to the IDS Guys for their partcipation and a special thanks to Sander who has really gone out of his way to make this article littered with new stuff.
