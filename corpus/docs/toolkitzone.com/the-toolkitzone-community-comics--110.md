# The ToolkitZone Community Comics

The people of Toolkit Land is very diversed. Ironically, this diversity brings them together closer, just as how a negatively charged particles attracts a positively charged one. From this community you can see a lot of representatives in the firleds of art, writing, music, game-making, programming, and so on. Today's article shall focus on one aspect of the aforementioned fields of expertise, which is coincidentally the most active. Let's talk about art.
More specifically, let's take a look at some of the craziest community tributes made-- the TK comic series. Since our community is such a good source of different people; from wacky to totally crazy to just plain mushy and simple. I believe these qualities make a perfect topic for a comic book, whether it be focused on comedy or action.
TK Rants
![](library/images/docs/99.gif)
I have to admit that this is the first community comic series for me. I do not know if other people have done such thing in the past, but TK Rants are the funniest ever. TK Game Boy surprisingly never runs out of humor for everyone in his comic book. Let's take a closer look at it.
![](library/images/docs/100.gif)
First of all, it is quite noticeable the artwork isn't your traditional anime nor western style. Who said comics should always look good? Anyway, if TK Game Boy made serious-looking characters his TK Rants wouldn't be as funny and entertaining. I must say he found the fitting style for his comic. They fit the panels very well, and they express the themes well too. Sometimes I am amazed that at such simple detail he can capture sarcastic facial expressions, which made it even humorous.The story has no relationship with the real TK member. everything is built on his fiction, which makes one interested on being ranted because they want to know how TK Game Boy would depict them. He is also consistent with every character's design and role.
![](library/images/docs/101.gif)
You can find TKRants usually on TK Game Boy's sig(forum), or you can go to his . [Archive Site](http://tkrants.toolkitzone.com/TKR_home.htm)
TKZ Wars
![](library/images/docs/102.jpg)
TKZ Wars by the_slasher07 brings us closer to the world with his cool designs and panel layouts. You can already tell the the_slasher07 has a bright future in comic book making. *Manga*
Conceptually, TKZ Wars follows the story of the beloved MrG as a superhero, and Horizon as a highschool girl who turned out to be a sidekick of MrG. More characters were already drawn and posted, but as of Issue 1, only a handful of appearances were witnessed. This gives readers things to expect and keep them interested; and I believe that you'll indeed be interested. TKZ Wars sports a more serious form of art, although still heavily comicated, it is clearly geared for action and comedy, again, just like a . It is quite hilarious too, what with all those side comments and laughable dialogues, not to mention soundeffects and great timing for fanfares and such. It is an interesting piece to read.![](library/images/docs/103.jpg) *Manga*
TKZ Wars was written for the sole purpose of enjoyment, and I think it achieved the goal. Both the author and the readers are enjoying the series. You get two things in one package--there's an interesting esque material and you get to see your cool-looking self in it, playing a role. I believe you can also request how you would look like and what you'll likely embody. *Manga-*
Effort is undoubtedly given to making it, and kudos to the_slasher07 for a job well done, but he could improve this series even more. First off, he could use pen for the writings and for giving emphasis to certain parts. Everything is equally registering to the eyes which make the characters and text merge and makes reading harder. He could also use some panel improvement. Panels should be distinctive from the rest. It is sometimes confusing where you should go nest, or where is the text,or it is simply distracting to see misaligned panel divisions and so on. The impact gives the comic a appeal. *dirty*
I'll assure everyone that as the_slasher07 works on his comic he'll get better and better, and eventually he'll learn to overcome the points that I mentioned plus he could see more shortcomings by himself and learn to solve them.
You can catch TKZ Wars on . Petition for a site and he just might make one. [the_slasher07's thread](http://toolkitzone.com/forums/viewtopic.php?t=12548&start=0)
Crime Does Not Pay, Much
![](library/images/docs/105.png)
This comic book made me do this article actually. The moment I saw his first issue, I had this idea of making a feature article for people who makes comic books, since I believe they deserve one for the effort they put into it.
by AlexOxander is another TK Community comic series geared for humor, and it is indeed humorous; but what got my attention is the style of art that this comic boasts. *Crime Does Not Pay, Much*
![](library/images/docs/106.png)
As you can see, it really resembles those comic scenes found on signs, newspapers, and other public reading materials. It could be because they look like silhouette, but whatever the detail is, it looks very good. A nice change of view too since I don't see this tyle very often around the community.
You can see that each panel is standardized, having thicker lines. This is a very good thing because they become well established. They are also drawn straight, which is a sign of professionalism. The stroke is also an evidence of the quality of the piece.
Each panel is neatly drawn, with no unnecessary lines and stray dots. Very neat, yet you do not sense an unwanted emptiness. Sure it wil eventually look empty but somehow they fit into the panel, wich makes a perfect picture for a given scene.
Characters are not drawn in detail, but important parts are present. Parts that needed expression are shown like the mouth. When someone is in distress you can see it from the mouth. Who said you need eyes to show emotions? Not in this comic. Once again I aplaud AlexOxander for capturing human emotions with minimal body parts. Fingers need not be detailed, but you can tell if a character is pointing, or clutching the fist, or just plain opening them. The darkened outlines adds quality to it too.
One thing also worth noting here is the convincing use of shading. This piece do not need colors to be beautiful. They look good already with the monochrome look. The proper stroke and pressure is applied to get varying shades appropriate for the character, and even objects.
Dialogue isn't plenty here, but there are a number of great punchlines very well-thought dialogues adds to the humor.
It is indeed very nice and very comedic, but don't expect it to be as funny as TK Rants. CDPM requires deeper thinking to get the joke out.
![](library/images/docs/107.png)
You can read all of the issues from this series by visiting its . Petition for a site if you want one. [official thread](http://toolkitzone.com/forums/viewtopic.php?t=12559)
Of the three comic books, TK Rants is the most active. Mainly because it's easier to make and it applies to any occasion, like the recent TK Winter Expo. Keep your fingers crossed or simply PM the authors to death to update their comics.
