# You left the community last year to concentrate on a web company called Aeronic, how did that pan out?

Well, it's not here any more, so obviously it didn't go as planned... I spent a lot of time building it up and developing our own systems, only to get a phone call the day I finished with an offer of a merger with huge incentives for me, the company and the staff. Invision Internet Services is an internet product and services company based in the USA and UK. We offer a wide range of services from business consultancy to web design and hosting. My role in the company is pretty huge and describing it could take all day, but in a nutshell I am the Senior Vice President, meaning I am in charge of the vast majority of hiring & firing and day to day operations. I am also in charge of product developer and act as the lead software development. It's been a crazy ride, it all started as my idea of making a Content Management System, I decided it would be best to sell it under a company name and Aeronic was formed within 3 days of the initial conception of the idea. Within a month I was working for IIS and in the past 5 months I have been promoted 4 times again. It's an odd feeling when you finally feel comfortable enough to hand in your notice to live and work in the job you created as a dream. Honestly? I think I succeeded because I have failed so many times before. I've always been a very determined person and I have always wanted to be able to run my own business. As you will likely remember, I have had several similar projects in the past, but have never really had the knowledge to succeed. This time I did and I got lucky. This sounds so cheesy, but being a part of the TK community changed my life a lot. Some people would say in a bad way, but I like to think it improved it greatly. My first programming knowledge came from learning all about RPG Code and as any fellow coders will know, what you learn in one language almost always helps in learning the next. The TK got me into learning Visual Basic, which in turn pushed me to learn HTML to make Toolkit X, I learned ASP to update that website more easily, followed by PHP to keep me up with the best and most common web language in use today. PHP quickly became my language of choice and learning more about that got me where I am today. Of course, aside from myself of course. I used the TK a lot in my earlier days, but due to my personal need to re-write everything I do to continually improve it, the only thing I ever got around to releasing was a rubbish demo I submitted to Mystic Software. The main reason I stayed so long was because I enjoyed the community atmosphere, especially in the days of the threaded forums. No, I most definately prefer IPB software, but I much preferred the community during my first year or two, it was much smaller and far easier to get to know the people within it. Some of my favourite people were around in those days, too (Who couldn't love comfox and Suro!). No, I am still surgically attached to the TK community. I check it regularly and even occaisionally manage to post. It's amazing how much the development team has changed the software in the time I have been gone, I just hope the changes haven't taken away any of the charm or the ease of use the TK is known ( at least to me ) for. Yeah, I think Ms. Duff is a good artist and is an attractive young girl... Though, I do find Master Tamer's obsession a little scary. Why are you appalled and shocked? Oh and yes - just as soon as you do some work. Lazy boy. I'd like to see myself in the first IIS Office in either the UK or the USA. I'll be in a spacious office with an iMac G7, a bowl of haribo, a coffee machine and a comfy chair. Do it. That is the best advice I could give. If you don't try or you try, fail and won't try again - you'll never get where you want to be. I've failed at these projects more times than Xavier has used his GDP scam. I would also recommend that you make sure you know what you are getting into and make sure you are good at what you do. Thanks Phil, I'll be sure to add your bonus to my to-do list.
