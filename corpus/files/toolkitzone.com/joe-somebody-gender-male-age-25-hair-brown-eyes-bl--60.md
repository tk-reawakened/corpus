# Joe Somebody Gender: Male Age: 25 Hair: Brown Eyes: Blue Race: Caucasian Height: 5'7" Weight: 130 lbs. Class: Thief

Or, How to make believable characters. **Characters and You**
By Brenton Dinsmore AKA Walker-san
**Table of Contents**
1. Introduction 2. Starting Off 3. The Basics 4. Character Class 5. Magic/Skills 6. History 7. Present 8. Future 9. Disclaimer
**1. Introduction or Hey check this out!**
Hello there. I once had made a character tut for TKZ loooong ago, and I'm going to assume Vampz lost it. So here we have a new, spiffier version to start you off on character creation. And this leads us to….
**2. Starting Off or So you wanna make a character but don't know where to begin…**
Everyone knows that you can't have a good RPG without characters. Everyone also knows that making up characters is the most annoying part of the whole creation process (well, okay…graphics are the most annoying part, but making characters is up on the ladder). So, in order to begin, let's make a quick character and take a look at each part separately.
Name: Joe Somebody Gender: Male Age: 25 Hair: Brown Eyes: Blue Race: Caucasian Height: 5'7" Weight: 130 lbs. Class: Thief
Let's stop there for now. Now then, you're probably wondering why I gave useless details like weight and height. I mean, after all, when we put Joe into an rpg, he's not going to have these details listed under his profile (you can if you want, but it just isn't needed.) Well…it is because we've now got a good picture of what Joe looks like. Let's pick it apart.
We can see that Joe is male. He is of average height, but is about 15 lbs underweight for his height and age. So we know he is quite slim. He is Caucasian, so his skin color is light. He has brown hair and blue eyes, which are very common. Our friend Joe is that guy you walked by this morning to work or that asshole in the car in front of you. He is the most ordinary looking fellow in the world, and because we have this description of him, we can tell that.
This is why we list out useless details. Using them, we can form a mental picture of our characters in our minds. If Joe was, say 230 lbs instead of 130 lbs, then we'd know he is quite overweight, and our mental image would shift to accommodate for it. The mental image is what helps create your sprite graphics, so it's always good to list these things out. It also helps us later on when we decide to flesh out his history and his current job/class. E.g. Joe is obviously thin, so he must have a history in either proper dieting or physical workouts.
You'll also notice that I gave him his job/class now. Why would I do that now instead of later during the chapter made for jobs/classes? Well, because most people will already have an idea on what they want their character to be. So I list it now instead of later.
Let's continue forward.
**3. The Basics or Hey! We just went over the basics!**
So you say. Yes, we did go over the basic of basics. Now we hit the next point. Stats. Yes, stats. Stats are basics and are required unless you are making a storybook adventure game…Which would be interesting as I've never seen one made with the RPGTK. Hmm…idea…
Anyways…so we've got Joe fleshed out in our mind, we're going to give him some stats. Note that I am using standard RPGTK stats. You can add or subtract what you want, but for sake of being as basic as possible, I'm gonna use the standard.
Name: Joe Somebody
HP: 50/50 SMP: 0/0 FP: 30 DP: 40 EXP: 0 LVL: 1
NOTE: These stats are what I consider well rounded stats. You should adjust for use in your own games.
Here we have Joe, and he's fleshed out a bit more. Before I get into too much detail, I'm going to quickly define each stat, for those who don't know what they mean.
HP (Hit Points): How many hits your character can take before "dying."
SMP (Special Move Points): Relate it to Magic Points in other games…how much power your character has left for special attacks and/or magic.
FP (Fight Power): How much damage your character can dish out. Final damage is calculated by subtracting the enemies DP from your character's FP and then subtracting the result from the enemies current HP.
DP (Defense Power): How much damage your character can take before HP drops. Final damage is calculated by subtracting your character's DP from the enemies FP and then subtracting the result from your character's current HP.
EXP (Experience): How much experience your character has earned from defeated monsters, doing tasks, or however your character can gain exp in your game.
LVL (Level): The current level of experience your character has achieved. Usually determined through EXP itself, higher and higher EXP is needed to gain higher and higher levels. Some games do not make use of a level system and use EXP to purchase new skills and power. Others don't use either (Zelda series).
Whew. Okay. So now that we've learned that. Let's break it down. Joe has a fair amount of HP for a LVL 1 character. He has no SMP. He is a thief. He steals things. There's no reason to give him SMP unless I plan on giving him some special abilities. This would require more programming than necessary for a beginner. I assume people who are actually USING this as a tutorial are beginners. He has less FP than DP. This is because, as we've seen, he is a slim person. He doesn't have much weight, therefore not much force behind his muscles. However, less weight means more dexterity. So we'll say that because he can move fast, he can dodge attacks and not get hit as much or not as hard….so we raise his DP up a few notches to simulate this in the game.
Joe has no EXP! Well, that's because he's LVL 1 and just starting the game. Many people tend to make their characters a few LVLs up, claiming that the characters have had experience in the past so shouldn't be just LVL 1 at the beginning. That's just fine. Just make sure you set the EXP up to whatever LVL the character is starting at to compensate. After all, if the character has had experience before the game starts, then their EXP should reflect that. Plus is sucks when to reach the next level you have to go through the first four levels of experience.
So let's summarize what we've learned. We've discovered what the stats mean, and how to make them work with each other. We've learned how FP and DP are related, and have seen an example of how to balance them appropriately. We've also seen Walker's opinion of average stats. Please note again that you can have them be whatever you want. I just think those are good stats for this character.
** 4. Character Class or What My Little Guy Does for a Living.
Another important factor in character creation. I said before that most people have already chosen their character class from the get go. So why have this section after the stat section? Well…some people, once they put stats down, say "Oh wait…I wanted him to have SMP and use magic." Then, they change the class to a Mage. So it's really for those people who want to change the class of their characters, but don't know what to change them to.
Yes my friends, this is mostly going to be definitions of character classes. Sorry.
Fighter/Warrior: Your standard sword swinging, hard headed war machine. Fighters are those guys who are big, with muscles the size of tree trunks. In many games, we call them Tanks and for a good reason. They tend to have high HP, FP, and DP. So they can deal massive damage while taking a beating and barely being scratched.
Thief: Joe is a thief. These are the crafty guys who use their cunning and stealth to steal valuables from people. There isn't really a steal command in the RPGTK, but I do know that someone has made a plug-in or a method or something and can be found over in The Shack on Mystic Software that allows for the steal command. Thieves usually have average HP, low or no SMP, average FP, and slightly above average DP. They're made to steal, not to damage and take damage.
Mage: Ahh…The power to destroy whole cities with but a word. Magi sit in the back spelling destruction for the enemies. Their HP tends to be on the low side, but damn do they have a lot of SMP. FP and DP are also quite low….but hey; if the Mage is good enough, it's not going to matter after one spell anyway.
Cleric: The common name for the healer. Also known as the White Mage. These guys are a must for any journey if you don't want to die. Usually casts healing magic, and, during battles, assisting magic. Clerics also have low HP, but it's a bit higher than the mages. They've also got the high SMP. FP is a little higher than the mage, but not much. DP is usually low, and doesn't get very high compared to other classes.
Ninja: In many ways an advanced version of the Thief. They are also capable of stealing, depending on if you want them to be able to. They also like to throw items from the party's inventory to inflict damage (has this ability been programmed yet? I don't know. If not, try it…shouldn't be any harder than the steal ability). Ninja have high HP, and some have SMP for their special attacks. Their FP is also high, being deadly assassins after all. DP tends to be average though. If all goes well, they shouldn't be attacked anyways.
I think I covered the basics. What? You say you wanted a modern/futuristic setting!? Oh for the love of…fine, fine…okay.
Punk/Jock: Like the fighter of medieval ages, these guys are tough. These guys are also big and dimwitted. Their stat ratio is about the same.
Thief: …A thief is a thief…get over it. Okay, so modern day thieves are better equipped. I give them higher HP and FP because modern thieves train more than their medieval counterparts. They may also get SMP for their little toys and stuff, but personally I would use the "not in inventory can't do it" system.
Chemist: Need something to blow up? Need to burn a hole through the floor? Look no further! These guys spend their time mixing chemicals and creating new potions and such. HP is average to low. SMP should be used in the making of mixes, so it can be high. FP is generally low, and DP is average to low. They spend all their day in a lab…can't expect them to be physically powerful.
Doctor: The modern version of a Cleric. These guys take longer to patch up injuries, but they can do it. Their stats are very similar to the Chemist's. SMP can be used or not…all depends on how you're going to have your doctor perform his healing techniques.
Sniper: Gun toting badass who sticks to the back and kills with deadly accuracy. HP and SMP tend to lack, but god they have FP. The right gun in this guy's hands brings that even higher. Because they don't expect to be hit before they kill, their DP is a bit on the low side.
Okay. No more. Think up some of your own! You moochers!
As a short summary, there are many different jobs/classes you can give your character. Unlimited, in fact. Take your pick. Remember that the stats you chose in the beginning should reflect what kind of class you choose, and vice versa.
** 5. Magic/Skills or I have SMP and I want to use it.
These are really up to you. But you should realize that a Fighter isn't going to be using Fire magic nor a Mage is going to be using Blade Rush. Think logically and you'll persevere. Let's go back to our friend Joe.
Name: Joe Somebody Class: Mage Magic: Fire Fire 2 Poison
"Huh? I thought Joe was a thief." Remember what I said about people wanting to change class after stats? I want Joe to be a Mage now.
Joe seems skilled at fire magic right now. He should remember this when fighting enemies. It would be silly to cast fire on an enemy made of fire. This is the key to offensive magic. Any magic cast on an enemy should ideally be opposite of it's natural element. The six standard RPG elements are Fire, Water, Earth, Air, Light, and Dark. Fire opposes Water, Earth opposes Air, and Light opposes Dark. ALWAYS REMEMBER THIS. If you make a magic spell that's called Fire 29384 and it does massive damage to a Flaming Brick, people will not take your game seriously. Fire doesn't damage fire. Water damages Fire. Fire evaporates Water. Air erodes Earth. Earth blocks Air. Light and Dark are constantly fighting for space.
Of course, you can always make up more elements. Like electricity, for example. Or even wood and metal. They all work. You just need to make sure one is weak to the other and vice versa.
Skills are a harder variety. This is where you'd actually give your Fighter SMP. Let's say I didn't like how Joe looked as a Mage. So I'm going to make him a Fighter (he's going to be a weak Fighter, mind you, because of the build we gave him. Always remember this).
Name: Joe Somebody Class: Fighter Skills:
Blade Rush Heavy Swing Defense
His skills all have physical traits to them. Nothing is magical about this. As such, we really don't rely on elements. The exception is when we have attacks that use the elements in them. Not many should.
Blade rush would be an attack that would most certainly hit the enemy, deal light damage, and take up very little SMP. Heavy Swing would hit the enemy and deal heavy heavy damage, using up a lot of SMP. Defense would use some SMP and raise Joe's own DP for the remainder of the fight or for a few rounds or whatever.
Let's look at Joe being a Ninja
Name: Joe Somebody Class: Ninja Skills:
Throw Aqua Veil Fire Veil
Here's where we get the elemental skills. Not magic, but elements attached to them. To be honest, it's just like magic…except that I'm not going to make it so that Joe has to use up SMP to use them. All the attacks to the same damage, FP wise…but they have different elements attached to them. So now Joe has to use strategy whilst attack with his skills. Throw I just put in there cause ninjas can throw stuff.
Okay. So, we've seen that magic requires elements and SMP to use. A fighters skills rely mainly on how much FP is dished out. There are other skills for other classes. It's really up to the creator on who gets what when and how.
** 6. History or He's been through a lot of stuff so he decided to become a thief and steal stuff and ended up in the back streets where he learned to sword fight and then…
So yea…I actually know someone who came up with that.
Histories are EXTREMELY important. You should write them down as soon as you've done the classes and skills. Why? Because many people forget that characters have a reason they are the way they are and start a game with no past history of the characters. Then, while struggling to come up with a game, they pull something like the above title out of their ass. Okay, first off, if he decided to become a thief, then he probably was already on the backstreets.
Start off by deciding how much family the character has.
Joe's Family:
Mother Father 2 Brothers Uncle
That's it! Stop! Okay…we've something to work with now! Now then….who's alive and who's dead?
Joe's Family:
Mother - deceased Father - deceased 2 Brothers - 1 deceased 1 alive and missing Uncle - alive
…By god…we've got something here. Okay okay wait…the uncle killed the two parents and stole the brother away, missing Joe and his other brother completely because he had hidden in the wardrobe. Joe and his brother became orphans, living in the streets, stealing what they could for money and food. One night, Joe's brother was caught trying to steal from the king's carriage, and was put to death. Joe has vowed revenge on his evil uncle and now vows it upon the king as well. He sets out on his journey from here.
Story. Something different? Okay…Joe has lived with his uncle all his life. He was told that his parents and brothers were killed by bandits. They are very poor, and have to steal to survive. One day, a man bearing the colors of the bandits shows up claiming to be his brother. Joe threatens the man's life, but, intrigued, sets out to find the bandits camp and see what is really going on.
Not all stories have to be about saving the world. Nay, most start off with the hero doing something completely unrelated.
So let's review this one. You should start with who the character's family is. Then decided who is alive, who is dead, and who is still in direct contact with the character. Finally, based on the character's age and class, figure out a back-story that sounds both convincing, realistic, and works with the character.
** 7. Present or Well first he's going to sleep at the inn…then buy a sword and save the world!
This is a continuation of the character's history. How are you going to shove him into the plot. Let's go back to the first history of Joe.
Joe's brother was just killed; his family is all dead except for one brother and one uncle. Joe is off to gather information on the whereabouts of his uncle. Joe remembers his uncle is a rich man, and may have met the king. Thinking this, he believes his brother's death was not accidental. He goes to a local tavern to see if anyone has heard of his uncle. There. That's the present. "Shouldn't it be…you know…longer?" Why? The game does that for us. We just set what he's doing NOW, at the beginning of the game. It doesn't have to be extravagant, just long enough to give a simple understanding of what the character just did or is just about to do.
