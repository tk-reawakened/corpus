# NOT

What is Timing? In the narrative thread, Timing is defined as hitting the marks. This means that you must carefully structure your story so that you control the specific emotional impact of events or words with the purpose of evoking predefined responses in a sequential order. Ok so that makes no sense right? Well, lets think about what timing means in a normal context. Suppose you are jump roping. You must time your jumping so that the rope swinging around you does not hit your feet. If you can maintain your timing, then you can even do a few tricks that look more impressive than just jumping. Another example of Timing would be how a baseball player must wait for just the right pitch before he swings his bat and then he must swing it at just the right speed to make proper contact with the ball. Timing in the writing field is quite similar however it is not as easy to recognize when, how or why to make your choices. The choices you will have to make regarding Timing will vary depending on the style of writing you have chosen, the Intellectual Targeting you have implemented and the audience you are writing to. **NOT**
At this point, we are beginning to wade into deeper waters. Perhaps you have been here before or this may be completely new to you. No matter the case, we must now delve deeper into the elements of creative writing. This article will focus on the concepts of Timing and Pacing. I will outline the basic purposes of these and discuss the methods by which they are commonly applied. These are not easy elements to master. In fact, these are probably the two most difficult concepts to effectively apply to your work. If you have any trouble understanding what is about to be laid out for you then do not worry. Everyone struggles with both of these. It is very important to be aware of the necessity of Timing and Pacing even if you are unable to totally understand how they work. Lets take this slowly and please read carefully as I attempt to explain the operations of these two story-telling devices.
**
Timing
**
- When to reveal information - When to shift interest  When to hit a punch line  When to draw out a scene  When to explain a mystery  When to set aside a story element  When to return to a story element  When to introduce and idea - When to introduce a character  When to focus the readers attention  When to broaden attention  When to surprise the reader  When to foreshadow events  When to end a characters run  When to end the story
These are some examples of how Timing can be recognized and applied to a writing work. Understanding how Timing applies to a story in this fashion is very important and is essential in order to have any control of how a reader will interpret your tale. These examples are one critical form of timing however there is another form that might not be so easy to recognize. Please read the following paragraph:
Janet began walking down the stairs slowly. Her heartbeat thundered though her ears in the absence of all sound around her. Her hands pressed firmly against the gray rock walls of the tomb as she slowly descended loosing a fine dust into the air. Her eyes widened as the light of the moon faded into the shadows of a damp room below. Terror seized her now. She could go no further. Every muscle in her body became frozen inside her as she stared into the deep emptiness before her. Every thought in her mind eroded into a single undeniable truth. Her eyes fixed in a wide gaze of complete horror as an icy chill slowly crept down her spine. She knew that he was waiting for her quietly silently waiting
There are many examples of how Timing can be implemented in the above words. Notice the way information is revealed in a controlled and specific pattern. Also, notice the delay of events as I describe her feelings. This creates the illusion of a pause before the story continues. I have timed the moment where the purpose of Janets descent into the tomb is revealed so that the reader learns it last. This is a typical device used to draw out interest and establish a defined subject of the story. By delaying the release of information in such a way, the writer can evoke many different kinds of feelings in the reader. The trick here is to study how words affect both you and others. If you can break down a story into individual words, you will see the use of timing present throughout any polished literary work. The key to timing is waiting. If you can recognize the value in patience and forethought, then you will be that much closer to mastering Timing
Pacing
Now that we have discussed Timing, lets take a look at a more complicated side of this element. Related to Timing is a device called Pacing. Pacing is essentially defined as the metering of a story or the pattern that a story is constructed in. What does that mean? Think of it like this A story begins and ends. In between the first and last word of a story there are many other words, sentences and paragraphs. All of these constitute the ideas and events of the work. If you can divide your story into a series of different events or ideas then you will see that it may be one story but it is constructed of many smaller parts. Establishing that is necessary in order to understand Pacing because in order to pace a story, the writer must understand the individual pieces and how they relate to one another. In order to pace a story effectively, the writer must know when or where to stretch or condense each element within the story. Those elements can be anything from descriptions contained with text to specific paragraphs or even dialog. Pacing your story will require you to see your story as individual parts. Once you have done that, you must see the relationships with the parts. This involves the timing mentioned above and also the collective feelings that are generated by reading each element of the story in a continuous stream. A film editor is responsible for the Pacing of a movie. He must decide where to shorten scenes and when to make edits. He must decide if one part would be better used in another place. This is exactly how a writer must see his story. You must see the separate parts and then decide how to edit them. A properly paced story will flow as it is read. A properly paced story will not confuse the reader or seem to drag on and on. Pacing is absolutely necessary if the audience is to be expected to enjoy your story. It is a difficult concept to understand because it is something that will vary from writer to writer and story to story. How you pace your story will be how your story is read. Its that simple. I often think of pacing in the context of writing as though I were jogging. Anyone who runs on a treadmill or works out regularly should understand this comparison. If you run to fast in the beginning, you will get tired and have to quit before you want to. If you dont run fast enough, then you will walk away later feeling like you didnt do enough. Learning how to run is like learning how to write. You have to do it wrong to know how to do it right. You will get there eventually as long as you keep trying.
Life is about learning. Dont let the complications of the process overwhelm you. The more you understand about what you are doing, the better youll be at it. Give it a shot make your mistakes and try again. Trial and error is how man discovered electricity, created the light bulb, landed on the moon and it is how you will learn to be a better writer. There is no shame in making mistakes. We all do it. Just listen, learn and move on.
How you write and what you write are up to you.
