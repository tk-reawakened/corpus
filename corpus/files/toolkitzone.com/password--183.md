** IAOP1 : Color
**(Important Aspect of Pixeling)**
** Written by Xontu (Brad Forbes) 1/2/04
**Introduction**
Hey guys, Xonny here. I'm no Sophia, Mark, or Chia, but after a few years of pixeling, it's hard not to pick up a few things. And in this article I'll be talking about one of those things - selecting colors for those pretty little tiles of yours. Pixeling is a challenge that takes practice, but if you ask me, anyone is capable of creating good tiles with enough practice and dedication. Like I said, I'm no expert, but I think a few tips to get you started even from someone like me can make the whole experience a little easier.
**Part 1 : Pick a style**
Pick your wanna-be, cause with how basic I'm talking with style, you can't really be unique. Are you gonna be a Pokemon wanna-be with cartoonish sprites and bright colors? Are you gonna be a D&C wanna-be with realistic sprites and dark, grayish colors? Obviously you'll have to refer to what your game is like. If you're gonna be catching fuzzy cute teddy bears, you gotta go with the bright style. I've always liked a brighter style since it is a bit easier to do if you ask me, and my games have just always been that kind of them.
**Part 2 : You're no expert cause you did a 300x300 enemy sprite in MSPaint**
There's no problem with MSPaint when it comes to the basic of pixeling. And for the basic outline of many objects, MSPaint is just as good if not better than Photoshop. But the color selection in MSPaint isn't quite as user-friendly as Photoshop if you ask me. One of these reasons is that it is harder to select a specific shade in MSPaint. Also, Photoshop has a nice "Un-contiguous" paint fill that's great for changing all of one color into another in one click. Just select the Color Fill from the Tools then in the top Toolbar you'll see a check box labeled "Contiguous". If you uncheck that, all the pixels with the color you click on with the fill will turn the color you have selected. You can also change the Tolerance level. If you want only that one shade you click on, set it to one. So for coloring purposes, I do suggest Photoshop.
**Part 3 : Think inside the box**
Hopefully you have Photoshop because I am going to refer to it's color selection for a little bit. Anyway, when you chose a color in Photoshop, the purest/brightest shade of a color is on the far right side of the big sqare to the left. As you can see, the farther you go left, the move gray goes into the color. A LITTLE GRAY IS GOOD!
For about six months I used the Tile Editor in TK to create my tiles. I never once added any gray to any of my colors. When I discovered Photoshop, my pixeling improved by at least three times. "A little gray is good" may be the best hint in any of my articles, and if you mess around with it a bit, you will see why. Moving your mouse to the left adds another several million different shades that you can use, and when color is as important as it is in pixeling, that's a very good thing. Even if you're going for a bright style, you'll need some gray in your colors once and awhile. Hell, even Animal Crossing has it's share of gray.
**Part 4: Put the gray crayon down now kid!**
All this talk about gray sure makes it seem important, huh? Good, because it is. However, the shade gray is important, not the color. Yeah, so stone is gray and you want to make a castle. For a realistic game, that may not be a huge problem, but especially in bright styled games, gray just ain't good. If you want to make a castle, I suggestion you either add a tint of blue or purple to it. Except for small, skinny swords/daggers, gray should never be used as a color. Look at SOM2, they never used black or dark gray even with dark shadows, they used purple.
**Part 5: Cheating with variation by using colors**
So you got fifty sprites to do? When you say fifty, I say five. Why? Thanks to our handy dandy.... Un-contiguous Paint Fill... it's easy! So this guy has a red shirt and green pants on? Well, now he has a green shirt and blue pants. You got a new sprite in about six clicks! Of course, you'll have to change his hair and maybe give him a couple more pounds or years on him every so often, but it really does work. You don't think the people that made Morrowind didn't do that when they were creating those 20,000 something NPCs? It may have taken them more than 6 clicks, but hey, they get paid. You can also use the paint fill with pretty much anything you need variation to. You can have two more colors of flowers in about a minute with the color fill. Obviously you don't want to overuse it, especially with the most tempted objects like sprites and flowers, but it really does help to give a bit of variation to your game. You don't need to be a Morrowind, but you also don't want to be a FF1 and find your audience commenting "you sure look like the three thiefs I killed in the last town and the five I killed before that town."
