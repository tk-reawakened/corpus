Creative Writing
Session 3:
Choosing Your Audience
The intended purpose of these articles is to explain some of the elements involved in the creative writing process. These elements range from the overall concept of story telling to the most specific details necessary to create a written tale. These articles will each focus on one individual consideration that the writer must master to successfully share a message, ideas or feelings with the audience. This means that it is my intent to help provide you with the wisdom I have gained studying the writing process. It is my intention to make any one feel unintelligent or untalented. My opinions and perspectives are not intended to replace your own nor do I expect anyone to change their minds after reading my words. I love writing and my intent is to help you love it too. I hope you will find this information useful and I hope you will share what you have learned with others who are looking for some insight as a writer. Thank you.**

**NOT**
Ok, so by now you are reasonably sure that you want to write and you feel pretty confident about doing so. Now, its time to make a decision about how you are going to write your story. If you just want to write a story for yourself, then you can ignore pretty much any rule or advice that I have to offer. These articles are being written with the idea in mind that you want to share your story. Almost everyone who engages in the creative writing craft (Other than some poets) intend for others to read their work. I am assuming you do as well. With that in mind, it is now up to you to decide to whom you wish to write. The type of person you choose as your "target audience" will greatly affect the words and structure of your written work. You are not necessarily trying to alienate any group of people, but if you can visualize what kind of person you want to read your story then you can minimize turning off . There are many factors to consider about potential readers when making this decision **everyone**
One Person
Try to imagine one person that you want to read your story. When you see your story finished and in the hands of a reader, what does that reader look like? This exercise may sound unnecessary or you may disagree with the general idea of narrowing your audience down. Trust me on this. If you can see that person you are writing to, then the whole process from here on out will be much easier and you will feel much more confident about doing so. How old is the person? Is it a male or a female? Both of these factors will vastly skew the wording and structure that you will need in order to effectively reach this person. You may wonder why you would choose one person to write to. You may also wonder what difference it makes how old or what sex the person is. The answers to those questions can be a bit complex. The reason you write to one person is because only one person is reading your story. Even if someone else is reading the story to another person, there is only one person imagining the events that your words describe. People do not share thoughts and each think individually. If you try to write to a group of people then you will lose focus. A story written for an audience is called a speech. If you want to learn how to write a speech, go read another article. If you want to write a narrative, then heed my advice. If you can write to only one person, you will find that you naturally expand on your descriptions and explanations. You will let the words become more intimate and focused. Essentially, this will allow your story to become easier to read and have more of an impact.
The reason you choose the age of the person is because people of different ages have different interests and educations. Depending on how old someone is, they may have completely different interpretations of your work. Someone who experienced The Vietnam War would have a much different interpretation of a war story than someone who has never seen a war. Someone who has never had a job may have a hard time relating to a story that takes place in the workplace. Someone who is in High School will have a hard time relating to a story about a mid-life crisis. These are just examples of some of the kinds of concepts that readers of different ages will look at differently. Deciding what age you will write to will help you reach the group of people that will appreciate and understand your story. It may seem like a contradiction to narrow down your audience to 1 person in order to reach a group but once you see the reason behind doing so, I hope you can see the necessity.
Gender
The Gender of the person you are writing to is equally important but far more complicated. Women and men each have different ways of thinking and understanding. We all recognize that this is true. Most male writers tend to write towards the male demographic (group) where as Female writers tends to write towards theirs. Experienced writers will learn to study the opposite sex and how they think. This will eventually make it easier to write to a unisex audience. Write to one of them yours. It is much easier and much more honest to write what you know. You know how you think. Do not try and figure out how the opposite sex will perceive what you are writing as you are writing it. If you wish to reach both sexes in your audience, then you will have to write a story that both sexes can appreciate. Determining that is something that will require a little psychology and a lot of luck. The best thing you can do in this case is to ignore the gender when writing and only consider it when you are thinking up the ideas or writing your Rough-Cut. If you wait until the drafting process to take sex into consideration, it will be obvious and possibly even condescending. This can be very tricky and unless you have enough experience, I would highly suggest that you do not factor in sex at all. As important is it is to get it right, it can be disastrous to get wrong. Please take my word on this.
Geography
The Internet has made it easier to reach a larger and more diverse group of people than ever before. We commonly communicate with others in different regions, countries and even continents. On a day to day basis, we may not have many problems expressing ourselves but often we misunderstand or misinterpret language usage, spelling, grammar or even slang. When writing a story of length, you must take this into consideration. If people can not understand your words, then they will not understand your story. Some of these situations can be avoided by explaining the meanings of the slang or type of words you are using. Some of this can be avoided by having your work proofed by someone in your target demographic. The key is to decide as best you can where the person reading your story lives and go from there. Try and be aware of different cultural, economic, sociological and linguistic barriers that you will need to face. Identifying problems before hand is another critical part of the drafting process. As people around the world learn more about one another, this may gradually become easierbut only to a skilled writer.
There are many other factors that you may wish to consider when writing your tale. Everything from the color of someones skin to the types of clothes people wear can be kept in mind when drafting a story. The specific choices you make when determining your audience are yours to make. Understand the reasons for choosing an audience and be cautious about how you decide whom to write to. This is a choice only you can make for better or worse.
How you write and what you write are up to you.
These are but words of advice
