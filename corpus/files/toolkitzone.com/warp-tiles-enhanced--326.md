# Warp Tiles Enhanced

Prolly took the award of the most spelling mistakes Ive ever seen in a tutorial. (I fixed em though) Great subject to talk about! and really I suggest not using the warp tile thing in the board editor at all because those warp####.prgs get very very confusing :). His ideas here work very well. but of course always room for improvement and spell check.
Use of Warps
Warp tiles are how you move your character from one board to another. Well one of two ways. It is often preferable to use the other method but most of the time a warp tile is necessary. A town, for instance, will not have all the doors to the houses on the very edge of the board surrounding one open area.
Placing a warp tile is easy. in the board editor screen select rpgcode. Then select set warp tile. You will be prompted to click on the board where you want the warp. Never put it on the same square you intend the player to enter the board on. Never put it in front of where the player enters the board. Simple enough.
When you click you are then prompted to input the destination of the warp. Use the browse button and select the proper board. Type in the coordinates that you want the player to appear on the other board. Never on or infront of the warp tile that takes you back to the board the editor is displaying.
Well now you have a warp tile connecting two boards. You must make a warp tile on the other board if you want the player to be able to return or go back and forth between the two boards.
Sure you have the warp tiles and they do their job but it seems to be missing something. Lets modify the program and see how we can improve it. Well lets say you were making a two story house. You have the boards and the warps but since you are suppose to walk into the warp one way and then walk the other way when on the next board you might notice a problem.
It is often necessary to carefully walk up to and onto the warp tile in this situation or else you end up walking onto the warp tile of the second board and returning to the first. This is a buffer problem. When the player presses a key like the movement buttons they often hold it. This can build up in the buffer. You may notice in the game you accidentally walk a little farther than you meant to.
Open the program editor and select the warp tile program. it'll be called warptile and have a number behind it. it'll look something like this.
*Warp tile generated by board editor #send("School2ndFloor.brd", 18, 8, 1)
This is what one of my unmodified warp tile programs look like. It is a simple send command. All I had to do to solve the buffer problem was add this to the end of the program
#ClearBuffer()
It now looks like this
*Warp tile generated by board editor #send("School2ndFloor.brd", 18, 8, 1) #ClearBuffer()
It is still possible to walk onto the warp tile on the other board if the player holds the movement key while they get warped. But at least now it happens much less.
Have you ever played a game that when you entered a area it's title was displayed on the screen. Thats simple.
*Warp tile generated by board editor #send("School2ndFloor.brd", 18, 8, 1) #ClearBuffer() #MWinCls() #Mwin("School 2nd Floor") #Wait(a$)
Now when the player enters a board they get that nice little message telling them where they are. Because of the wait command it is now even harder to accidentally walk onto the warp tile. Although sometimes the player may miss reading the message window. Not like they will be paying allot of attention to it anyway
Now keeping in mind doing this for every warp tile I have to date in my game nearly drove me insane. Games have a lot of warptiles. Editing them all may take time. Thank god for cut and paste. Then it simply becomes a matter of typing in the proper message for the mwin command. And as always I advise to you to go and use the information provided not to mention build upon it and improve it.
