### cuthalion · 2009-12-17 20:31 · #K487064

**the age old question...**

It's exactly like Blades of Exile, except completely different.
I recommend you download the version of the toolkit on the site and then jackyboy's bugfix. (Search "members" for jackyboy, go to one of his posts, and it's linked in his signature.) Backup the trans3.exe that came with the original download before replacing it with jackyboy's fix.
Basically, it works pretty well, but there are a few glitches with the default systems that jackyboy's fix... er... fixes.
You can work with isometric or classic 2d.
You can use pre-rendered 3d graphics, but the toolkit doesn't do 3d itself. We're not planning to add that anytime soon, because we think 3d is for n00bs. Razz
There have been some very nice-looking games made with the toolkit. The catch is, it's a hobbyist site, and as such, most people never finish their projects. But this is a problem with motivation and real life getting in the way, not with the software itself. Check out any games or graphics by Lotusware, seveneternal, or Grindalf. Sevene's Vii, in particular, is a good example of old-school graphics done really well. Lotus's stuff is happier, but also good. Grindalf is one of the better programmers, and his projects show the versatility of the toolkit.
Basically, toolkit is more versatile (last I heard) than RPGMaker, but you have to put a bit more effort into it. The default battle system is very simplified, so most people opt to make their own. However, you can use RPGCode to make practically anything you want.
As for Blades of Exile, the toolkit can do way more. Whereas in Blades, you're basically doing custom maps and scenarios, in Toolkit, you're free to change how the game itself works. If you really wanted to, you could make Blades with the Toolkit.
Baldur's Gate could hypothetically be made with the Toolkit, but would probably run a little slowly until the graphics system in the toolkit is redone. (Don't wait for this, as it could be a while. Development is on a volunteer basis.) Also, there are some graphical things in Baldur's gate (3d-ish spell animations, etc.) that would be tricky in the toolkit.
I've never used ika, infinity, crystal space, or torque. However, I can say that Toolkit is meant to make 2d (top-downish or isometric) RPG's like you would find on the SNES or GBA. Other stuff can be done (see Grindalf), but it's designed for these.
