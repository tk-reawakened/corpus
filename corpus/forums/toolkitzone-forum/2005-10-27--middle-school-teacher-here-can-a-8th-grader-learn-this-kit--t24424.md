### spitfire · 2005-11-12 00:39 · #K270658

To create an actual playable game that isn't the demonstation game, no. Your students would need coding abilities to some point. If they are willing to learn some simple object-orientated programming, start them here.... I guess. But my advice is to start them on actual game design. Depending on what you have planned for the scope and objectives of the class (and the time frame), I would start them out with programming, assuming they have adept enough computer skills. Start to teach them C++, then have them start working with DirectX. It'll get them farther in the world than anything the toolkit could ever do. I'm not trying to bring down this community in anyway, I love this community, I stumbled upon it, and it sparked an interest in technology for me. I've been here almost 4 years now, and a lot has changed. I've realized very few toolkit games get fully designed, generally to lack of motivation, or to lack of skill. Art isn't a needed skill to create a wonderful video game. Intelligence and willingness is. Programming isn't hard, it isn't difficult, it's just different. It's another way of speaking.
So my answer: No.
Good luck with your class; whatever you choose to do with it.
