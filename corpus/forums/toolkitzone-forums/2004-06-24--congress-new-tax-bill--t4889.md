# Congress' new tax bill

### Haraldur · 2004-06-24 14:25 · #B49636

President Bush has doled out record subsidies to farmers and imposed illegal tariffs and has generally distorted the free market, crippling poor farmers trying to compete, although, the EU is just as much to blame, but I am not praising it either.
Congress is about to pass a law that will distort and confuse the tax cose, increase the number of loopholes and dole out prok in onscene quantities.
This legislative monstrosity started out with a sensible goal. The goal was to repeal a $5 billion annual tax break for American exporters that the World Trade Organisation in 2002 ruled was an illegal subsidy. The WTO gave the EU the right to impose up to $4 billion of retaliatory tariffs until the tax break was removed. The EU put 5% tariffs on a range of American goods in March, and promised to raise them by one percentage point every month for a year, so it could become 17%. These tariffs forced Congress into action. They could have at the same time eliminated some more loopholes left over from the 1986 tax package. They could lower flat rates as well. Although firms benefiting from the tax subsidy would lose out, the overall economy would have been better off.
However, Congress looked for other ways to compensate exporting firms. With an election looming, it soon became a free-for-all, with politicians rushing to buy off voters and with lobbyists coming up with ever longer lists of endangered companies. The bill's backbbone became a cut in the corporate tax for manufacturing. The idea was to distort tha tax code and American firms' investment decisions. And thanks to all the lobbyists, "manufacturing" is now defined to include farmers, film makers, and oil-refiners among others. America's tax-collection agency has called the bill impossible to implement.
Then there are the special favours. The 1000-page Senate bill doles out tax breaks to such deserving causes as Oldsmobile dealers, foreign punters at American race-courses and NASCAR track owners. The House version includes tax relief for bank directors, alcohol distillers, producers of fishing tackle boxes and sonar fish finders. It also offers a $9.6 billion "buy out" to tobacco farmers and allows residents of states with no income tax to deduct their sales-tax payments from their federal taxes, at least for two years.
Both bills attempt to reduce the cost of these giveaways by declaring them to be only temporary. This is the same fiscal trick that President Bush used for some of his tax cuts. In Washington "temporary" tax breaks have a habit of becoming permanent. If that is the case, one budget watchdog organisation thinks that the house version of the corporate tax bill could cost more than $220 billion over the next decade.
President Bush must veto this insanity.
