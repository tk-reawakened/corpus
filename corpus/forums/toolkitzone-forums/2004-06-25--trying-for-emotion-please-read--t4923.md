# Trying for emotion...please read.

### Levi · 2004-06-25 06:05 · #B49967

Okay I wrote this in an attempt to try and place emotion into my writing. Making it very sad. However I need real critisism and someone to find what's wrong with it. Any and all help will be appreciated greatly. THank you. Also it's seperated into present and flashback areas. I tried to seperate them clearly. Tell me how I could fix this if I needed to.
Teen Angel
By
Matthew Johnson
Matt ran, no reason, he simply ran. The sky was dark and grey, the clouds threatening rain. But that didnt faze him, nothing did anymore. It was hard, but worth it, to hide his emotions especially from himself. He no longer knew how he felt. No longer cared, no longer wondered. It seemed that this mindless running was nothing more than a way to avoid his emotions even more. And so he ran, from town to town, along deserted highways and abandoned dirt roads. He ran through paths in the park and cold haunting walkways through the forests. Then he stopped.
It was a peaceful place, though he didnt recognize it as such. It was odd, but even without emotion this place seemed to effect him somehow. It was like he remembered it. The path was not all together extraordinary. There was nothing that might catch the eye, no beauty to speak of. In fact most of the trees appeared dead and decaying, leaves from years past littered the ground, some flittering across the path, as the wind blew across the ground ever so gently, like ghosts of a long past remembering a life that they had once known. The gnarled branches of the dead trees looked like the hands of death reaching for all forms of life to fill its void, unable to move yet stretching and reaching nonetheless.
It was eerie in every sense of the word, but still it was only the vague recollection of the place that caused Matt to react. His eyes swept the ground and surrounding area trying in vain to remember what was so familiar about this place. He had been running for days, passing friends houses, old towns and familiar buildings the whole time. But this was different, why?
Matt just shrugged his shoulders no longer caring, his emotions once again locked away and hidden behind an expressionless mask. Matt turned back to the direction in which he had been running and started again at a slow jog, and easily accelerated to a regular run in a few seconds. But the world hardly seemed to move, and the faster he went the slower the world moved about him. He seemed trapped, and helpless here, there was something here. He knew it, something that wouldnt let him leave until he figured out what it was. Then sweeping the landscape again with his eyes he noticed it. A slight glint of light being reflected off a piece of metal. The source of the light appeared wedged between two Siamese trees, which were connected at the base, stuck inside a small knot hole. The small object was rectangular, covered in metal, with a few small holes on one side all in a row, the tops of which were numbered one through ten.
Matts body began to shudder, as sobs of terror and sorrow wracked his body. No. He thought to himself quietly. It couldnt be. The small object brought memories surging back, and emotions he could no longer hold behind the already overflowing dam he had constructed to separate himself from the world. It was more than he could stand as the mental dam lost its integrity and broke, spilling all he had held back for these past ten years at once, with emotions suddenly overwhelming him as he fell to the ground clutching his heart and his head.
His eyes watered fiercely as the pain of years gone by hit him full force. This was the purpose of his running; this was the reason for the dam. Now it was all for nothing. He held the small object in his hands. Placed it to his lips and began to play a soft and mournful song as he began to remember.
Come on, come on Matt banged the dash board of his car desperately trying to get it to start. Come on, darn you. Piece of junk. He heard a loud whistle as he noticed a tall stack of smoke heading their way.
Matt whats wrong? Why wont it start? Mimi asked getting nervous.
I dont know. Its never done this before. Matt turned the key but the engine only sputtered a little before dying. Matts eyes grew wide with terror as he saw the train come around the bend, and they were stalled dead on the tracks. Giving up Matt kicked his door open.
Come on. Matt grabbed Mimis hand and pulled her across the seat and out his door. They ran quickly trying very hard to avoid any debris that may come flying when the train hit. Then Mimi stopped and held back. Matt looked at her in puzzlement begging her with his eyes to continue. But she only turned back and ran toward the car and the fast approaching train.
Mimi? MIMI!!!!
Matt couldnt stand to remember the sight, the train had hit too soon, she hadnt had enough time to return and got the brunt of blow as the end of the car knocked her backwards. He hadnt been able to save her. He should have stopped her, should have kept him moving. Why did she feel she had to go back? What did she go back for?
Mimi. Matt said quietly to himself in anguish and self loathing. As he went back to playing unanswered questions were running through his head.
A group of people ran up to the sight, Matt stood staring in shock unable to move. A few attended to him, making sure he was alright, others rushed over to Mimis body to inspect the damage in hopes that she would be alright. All the while Matt continued to repeat softly to himself Mimi.
Sorry son, Im afraid shes dead. Son? You alright? One of the bystanders asked, worry starting to show on his face as he watched Matt stare after Mimis body. June we should get him to a hospital, I dont think hes going to be able to handle this. He and one of the women who had stopped to help him, picked Matt up and carried him like a scared child to their car and turned around to get him to the nearest hospital in the town five miles away. Matt, hadnt moved, hadnt said anything but her name the entire way and through most of the examination. He wasnt even aware of what was going on around him. All he could see was Mimis face as she tried vainly to run back to him, and then how it had been too late. The train hit, the debris flying, the look of shock and terror and pain. He had to forget, he had to forget. Why did she go back? Why?
Any idea why he was spared? The doctor asked, the man that had tried to help, quietly.
From what I saw, they got stalled on the track and after getting far enough away she ran right back up the hill and went through the car looking for something.
Any idea what?
Not yet, but her fingers were wrapped pretty tightly around something. Im not sure what though.
Alright, well well figure that out when she gets here. The Doctor turned back to Matt who continued to stare at nothing. Poor kids, I bet he wishes he were dead with her now too. Its a shame. A real shame.
His ring, he couldnt believe that was what had caused her to be lost to him. His stupid meaningless ring. Why did she felt it was worth risking and even losing her life over? It made no sense. Matt dropped the harmonica, no longer able to play as the memories hit him harder than before. Dropping him to all fours as he fought to gain control of his pain wracked body. It hurt so much, he didnt know if he could stand it. If his heart could take it. It was too much, so much more than anyone should have had to suffer. Far more than anyone should have to endure. Why Mimi? WHY!? Matt called to the sky, believing she could hear him, and afraid that she would hate what she would see if she were to look at him now.
His mind flashed faster through the memories, and the torrent of emotions became easier and easier to bare and his mind began to free itself from the chaos of that day. It leaped to a few weeks later, the funeral. He couldnt stand to see her. Thats when he came here. Thats when he first ran from his problems, ran from the pain. Here, along this path. It was so hard, so hard.
The preacher stood before a multitude of people, among them were Mimis original friends from camp, those she had spent the greatest years of her life with, all except Matt. Tyson did his best to hold back most emotion though he was not above letting it hit his heart full force. Sara sat by him, the ring Tyson had placed on her finger not a few days before shone brightly as her tears fell on it reflecting the light in many directions. Alex, his laptop open, tried vainly to keep his mind on his work and to focus on the preachers words. Sam sat in the back, keeping to himself. Usually the crybaby of the group, unflinching, he wasnt crying he simply sat, seemingly uncaring. As were Thomas and Carrie. Most had been through too much to simply cry for her, all of them believing in something better after this life, but not missing her any less. And all wondering, where Matt could possibly be.
Matt sat on a small path in the woods just a few miles off from the grave yard where they held the ceremony. The day was bright and cheerful, the gentle and quiet sounds of the woods tried to comfort him as he stared at the most recent picture he had of the two of them. It hurt too much to think about her, but he never wanted to stop.
Matt quickly kissed the picture, wrapped it around his harmonica and stuffed it into a tree. This was their tree now, a lingering memory of each other. So different and yet bound at the base by their feelings for one another. For him this would be her grave, this would be where she would see him. This is where she would talk with him. Here he vowed never to forget, never to lose faith, and never to give up.
Matt jumped at the tree stump where his harmonica had been found and quickly dug around, desperately trying to find her picture again. Mimi he said softly to himself, motivating himself to try and find that picture. Mimi where you? He soon gave up unable to find it anywhere. He grew angry and beat his hands against the rough bark of the tree slicing his hands open all the while glorifying the pain it caused, because it was physical pain, pain that could be treated, pain that could be stopped. He soon lost his strength and simply cried as the skies opened up and a soft rainfall came down upon him; washing the blood from his hands and onto the ground.
When he opened his eyes he saw a tiny white corner of what appeared to be a piece of paper underneath a huge brush of leaves. Matt slowly bent down to pick it up, gently pushing the leaves out of the way and was soon greeted by Mimis smiling face. Matt smiled the pain in his heart easing. He quickly scooped up the picture and drew it close to his heart.
Im sorry Mimi. He prayed hoping she would hear him. I didnt mean to forget, I didnt want to forget. Forgive me. Forgive me. Matt laid his back against the tree still clutching to the picture, protecting it from the rain. It never occurred to him that the picture had remained untouched by nature, been left undamaged and whole. All he cared was that Mimi was still in his heart, and he was never going to lose her again. He only wished he could see her for real one last time. He just wished she could hear his voice as they stood face to face, he wanted to say goodbye.
Matt?
