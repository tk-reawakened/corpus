# War, hatred, aggresion - possible elimination, fate?

### Cesque · 2004-07-11 14:00 · #B57215

I just read a bit of a quite interesting book, "The so-called evil", by Conrad Lorenz. The book focuses mainly on one thing - "interspeciment aggresion", in short, aggresive behavior among animals, which obviously resembles human behavior.
As Cartesius said, Animal non agit, agitur - Animal doesn't act, it is caused to act. Everything in nature has cause, same is case with agression, which eventually leads to wars, racism and hatreds in human community.
Aggresion itself is natural in the world - each carnivoral animal has to learn to be aggresive to hunt. The problem is showing aggresion toward same type of animals. There is some natural "desire of aggresion" which is presented among even smallest animals, like fish, for example. This leads to battles to unleash the desire for fighting - without blood. Most human forms of sport are products of trying to fullfil desire for aggresion without spelling blood, to promote concurrency without bare combat.
Aggresion among wolves is mainly stopped by hierarchy. When every animal knows where it's place is, none fight. The problem is, most animals don't want to occupy their place in community, they want to lead, they want to increase their position, territory, respect - thus becoming aggresive toward others. You can't deny humans don't do the same.
Another problem which is easily viewed using animal examples is racism, aggresion toward the "others". Rats live in families. Within families, combats never actually occur when everything goes right. The problem is, there are other families of rats. Which rats after years of evolution of their community system learned to hate without real purpose. I don't mean fight over food, over territory - I mean hate. Desire to destroy without real gainings from it. Hospes, hostis - all aliens are enemies and must be destroyed (at this point rats resemble large insect communities, like bees or wasps).
An experiment has been made, when a rat from other "tribe" has been put into an isolated territory of other rat family. It first wandered around, unsure what to do, and others tolerated him, until one rat in community "checked" the intruder.
(rats detect members of their family by their smell, everything which doesn't smell like the rest of tribe is evil for them)
Rats got alerted, and thus, began the hunt. Over next days, rats turned aggresive, suspicious. They repeatedly "checked" each rat found by smell. When they found the intruder, they chased him, attempted to kill. Over time, they finished their goal, and community returned to normal.
(this all also reminds after human behavior, the witch hunts, racial purges, communist cleanses)
By observing animal behavior and the purpose of aggresion, author of the book believed it might be possible to eliminate factors causing aggresion and hatred or to minimize it... is it really?
After all, we have 20000 thousands years of civilisation and we have learned nothing. People have landed on the moon, but they have't learned to respect others. Doesn't it sound funny?
And, seeing through history... is it really possible humans could once estabilish an order in which there won't be hatred, wars and "interspecimental aggresion"?
(personally... I don't think so)
