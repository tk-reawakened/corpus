# Philosophy. Something I believe to be new and original.

### comfox · 2004-07-15 06:30 · #B59230

The beginning to a book I'm (slowly) writing. Copyright me, now. Enjoy, eh?
PART I
Section One: On Criation and Destruction
Throughout the years, science has assumed the truth of a rather simple statement which has never been contradicted by observation or expirement. This postulate is the idea that everything comes from something else and leads to something else*; nothing can be destroyed or criated**. While this is an essential (perhaps the essential) concept of science, it must be noted that it is, in fact, not a postulate. The idea is easily deduced from a definition of existence and simple logic.
In order to explan this idea, then, we first must come to a suitable definition of existence. The first step we must take upon such a path is that of noting similarities between existing objects. Everything considered to be in existence has, at some point in time, by necessity, been observed- otherwise its existence would remain unknown. It is reasonable to think, on the grounds that the preferred sense of the human being is sight, that many or all of the existants*** discussed wil be found to possess at least some measure of visibility. Indeed, it seems that many do (among which are included plants, animals, technologies, and writings).
There are, however, a great many counterexamples to this. Speech is considered an existant, as are wind, air, electromagnetic fields, etc. Speech is observable only through hearing, air only through feel, wind through both hearing and feel, and electromagnetic fields only through their effects. This last is interesting; it indicates that not all existants can be sensed directly. All of those that can be sensed, however, exert effects upon their surroundings (otherwise they could not be sensed in the first place), as do those that cannot be sensed directly. It seems, then, that all existants affect other existants. Anything that did not do this would be undetectable and utterly irrelevant to everything in existence. It seems to be a valid definition that exitence is "the set of all objects capable of effecting, directly or indirectly, all other objects included in the set". Naturally it is possible for many such sets to exist simultaniously, so we must clarify our existence as the set in which we are included.
Using this definition we shall engage ourselves in an indirect proof of the "postulate" that nothing can be criated or destroyed. Let us imagine that objects can be criated (brought into existence from nothing) and destroyed (removed from existence, leaving no part of themselves behind). A hypothetical object x does not exist:
Ex = 0
Then it is criated:
Ex = 1
Then destroyed:
Ex = 0
While x not existing does not denote the possibility of its existence in another set, it does denote the idea of anything in the other set causing x to enter existence- for that would be indirectly affecting the constituents of existence. By definition this would render the nonexistent items a part of existence. Therefore nothing in any set could have caused Ex to change its value to one, nothing could have caused x to enter existence. Existing, then, must be an intrinsic property of the object x itself; Ex will immediately return to one.
Ex = 0 (x does not exist)
Ex = 1 (x exists)
Ex = 0 (x does not exist)
Ex = 1 (x exists)
Because time is an existant, no time passes while Ex = 0. Therefore x, in effect, always exists. If we withdraw our assumption that objects can be criated and destroyed, we also say that any existing object will, by necessity, always exist. The only assumption we have made in this proof is our definition of existence, which is hardly an assumption, but rather a logical definition well-suited to the facts.
------------------
*This idea is codified as the Law of the Conservation of Energy
**Throughout this text the word "criation" shall be used to denote an actual coming into existence from nothingness- "creation" shall indicate a new system or object arising from ( a ) previous system( s ) or object ( s ).
***Existants: Things which exist.
------------------
That's section one (this sentence and those below are not part of the book, sorry mates Razz). The next few sections deal with applications of this theorum, which are greatly expanded beyond its applications as a postulate. As a postulate it only applied to physics and particles, but as a logic-deduced theorum it applies to any entity that exists as an object (a unit) rather than a system (multiple units in a specific patern) or an interaction (units effects on each other). The most obvious application of this is that, provided that human beings posess an existence of any sort beyond simply a complex pattern of objects (a system), an afterlife becomes a logically inescapable fact. Of course, the real question is if consciousness is some sort of "self", a "soul" (an object) or if it is simply an illusion created by the system of our brains. But my theorum greatly simplifies the question of whether or not people exist beyond death from its previous tangled knot into that simpler question of the identity of consciousness.
Perhaps that question can be answered in the same manner, but I've not yet found a way to prove it either way. If I do, I'll end up as one of the most famous people of all time Razz. Hehe... Anyway, comments?
