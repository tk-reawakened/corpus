### comfox · 2004-07-17 19:40 · #B60658

I must point out something.... The possibilities:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1) There is no objective evil and good.
THEREFORE
a) Christianity, and most religions, must be wrong, since they center around these two forces.
2) There IS an objective evil and good.
THEREFORE
a) The aforementioned religions may yet be correct.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Since number one is a dead end to our discussion, we'll assume number two to be correct, and move on with possibilities:
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1) There is no omnipotent, purely good creator.
THEREFORE
a) Christianity, and most religions, must be wrong, since they center around this being.
2) There is an omnipotent, purely good creator.
THEREFORE
a) The aforementioned religions may yet be correct.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Again, we assume number two to be true for the sake of argument.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1) Evil did not come from this omnipotent, purely good creator.
THEREFORE
a) It came from another being.
b) This other being must not have come from the omnipotent, purely good creator (hereafter called "God")- or it would be merely a step in Evil's creation, contradicting possibility 1.
c) This other being therefore exists independently of God- and therefore God must not be omnipotent (for how can something happen without his approval, and essentially cause, if he is omnipotent?)
d) Letter C contradicts the previous number two.
2) Evil did come from this omnipotent, purely good creator.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Since possibility 1 leads inevitably to self-contradictions, we must accept possibility 2 for the sake of argument.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
1) Evil was created as the only way to create or to maximize good or happiness.
THEREFORE
a) God could not maximize/create what he wanted to without creating Evil.
b) Letter A is a restraint on God's abilities- which contradicts his omnipotence. God cannot be constrained by even logic (such as the idea that without evil, there would be no good).
2) Evil was created, but not as the only way to create or to maximize good or happiness.
THEREFORE
a) God created Evil when he did not have to.
b) God WANTED to create evil.
c) This contradicts his nature of being purely good.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every possibility leads, eventually, to a contradiction. Those possibilities that we ignored for the sake of argument all were ignored because they said there could be no God- and we wanted to take the trail of possibilities as far as we could to see if it ended. And it did end. Any possibility you choose to believe leads to the inevitable conclusion that there cannot be an omnipotent, purely good creator.
Sorry mates, but I don't think there's an argument that can beat this. Pure, uncontestable logic- no loopholes.
