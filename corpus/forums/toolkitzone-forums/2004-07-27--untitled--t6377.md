### Tenala · 2004-07-27 18:41 · #B65955

Meh, since no one else has much to say about it I'll go ahead and give it a full critique.
The hair has volume against the head; it's not laying completely flat against the skull. This is wonderful, keep doing it. However, I think the line for the top of her head is too choppy and angular in places. If you could finess this area and smooth it out I think it would look much better.
Her bangs have no definition from the rest of her hair. That's really a stylistic preference, but I do honestly believe that adding a bit of separation here would give her forehead and crown more visual interest. Right now the whole area looks very flat and cartoonish. The separation doesn't have to be through lineart, just a bit of shading and highlights would be enough.
Also, her hair doesn't look like it's growing out of her head, more like it's just laying on top. I think the bangs are again the big culprit. The jagged ends don't look like hair at all and really take away from the depth. I can't give any specific tips here because I don't know what kind of hairstyle you're going for. Start researching your favorite portrait artists and examine how they draw hair and bangs; there's a lot of little tricks that can only be learned through practice and observation.
The face and features look a little crooked and off-center to me. Did you use guidelines to place everything, or is her head tilted? Also, she has no ears; I'm assuming that they are hidden by her hair.
I like the eyes you've drawn on her. They're nicely rounded, though the left eye is just a touch bigger than the right. I'd like to see a little more finessing of this area, perhaps thicken the eyelashes or add more shading to the iris and whites. I think the eyebrows could be more curvaceous rather than the straight lines you have now, but again that's a stylistic preference.
Anime noses have very little definition and are not good to learn from. Study real noses to learn the basic construction and simplify from there. The little wedge in there now works just fine, but keep pushing yourself to improve.
I think the mouth is a little low on the face. Scooting it up just a wee bit closer to the nose should do the trick, and pop her chin out a little more. I prefer to add lips to my characters, but the mouth really does look just fine here.
Like her hair, the lines for the sides of her face could use more finess. I just love nice, smooth lines vs. sketchy jaggies, it looks much more finished and professional. The extra sketchy hair lines around her face look messy, like she's in need of a good brushing. Study how your favorite artists draw hair and work from there.
The most glaring anatomical problem is the neck and shoulder area. Necks do not look like that at all, they tend to be thicker and far more straight. Try reversing the curves and pulling the ends down towards the collar bones. The shoulders and collar bones could use a bit of adjustment as well, but there's not enough there for me to pick out fixes.
Start studying human anatomy. The best book I would reccomend is Human Anatomy Made Amazingly Easy by Christopher Hart. It is written for young artists and cartoonists, and it covers all the basics of human anatomy and figure drawing.
It's a good start, you've obviously got a decent grasp on drawing basics and a developing anime-influenced style. Now take it to the next level. Right now it doesn't stand out from the crowd; it's just another cliched anime portrait of a girl. You're really going to have to work on developing your own unique style. I'm not saying give up anime entirely, but it would be best if you started branching out into other areas.
Oh, and it looks nice. ;)
