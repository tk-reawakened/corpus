# Sony adopts Blu-Ray for PS3

### Spyder · 2004-08-10 01:39 · #B72468

this may be old news to some of you, but here goes...
Following a technical discussion held in Tokyo earlier this week, Sony has confirmed that, as suspected, PS3 discs will take advantage of Blu-Ray technology. Blu-Ray discs have a storage capacity five times greater than that of standard DVDs, so games and high-definition movies would normally have 27 GB to play with. And thats just a single layered disc.
However, Sony has confirmed that PS3 will use its own standard of Blu-Ray discs, which are read-only and have a slightly reduced capacity of 23 GB. But that's no great loss. This system can also maintain full support for standard DVDs. That means that old fashioned DVD movies should run fine, and the option for PS2 backwards-compatibility can be kept open.
The first Blu-Ray video players are intended for release before March 2005, and once this technology has been launched Sony will proceed to confirm further PS3 technical specifications.

Actually most PS2 games probably don't come close to 4GB's. The only ones that even can are the ones on the silver discs, which are DVD's (4.7GB single layer). The purple discs are normal CD's, and hold a maximum of 700MB's. The 23GB capacity will obviously be used for extremely large games such as RPG's and games like GranTurismo and GTA. It will probably also be used for High Resolution (progressive scan) FMV's, high-resolution textures, and play-back of movies that are written on Blu-Ray discs. I also wouldn't be surprised if there are far more PS3 games on DVD than on Blu-Ray discs. Not only that, but Sony is a major supporter of Blu-Ray, which is the competition against the HD-DVD format. Considering how many PS3's will be in households world-wide, it pretty much guaruntees that Blu-Ray will dominate over the HD-DVD format.

In Japan, those dates "may" be correct, however they are not likely for US release dates. The PSP will have a Memory Stick port for use as a memory card, as well as MP3's, and probably other types of data, like possibly image and movie files, but games "will" be on the UMD. Sony has spent too much R&D on the UMD to ditch it. Not to mention, putting them on Memory Sticks would be the stupidest idea as far as piracy goes. Think how easy it is to make emulators and ROMs for cartridge games. It's far easier to get emulators and ROM's for, say, SNES, Genesis, and N64, than it is for PSX, PS2, and XBox. Emulators abound for them, but stability and compatibility is usually pretty low. Not only that, but PSX/PS2 and XBox games are on a universal format, CD-ROM and DVD-ROM, which means computers can read them quite easily. There is no UMD-ROM, which means piracy would probably be damn difficult. I wouldn't be surprised if the UMD's do "not" fit in the mini-CD slot in your standard DVD-ROM either.
