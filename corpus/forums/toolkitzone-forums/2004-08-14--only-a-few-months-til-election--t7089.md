### Haraldur · 2004-08-14 22:45 · #B75513

Supply and demand has everything to do with practically everything in economics.
Jobs are only created to increase the supply of goods or services. An increase of supply will not happen without an increase in demand (after all, why would companies make stuff noone bothers to buy?). Lighten the tax burden on poorer people, and demand for good and services, will increase. To make more money, companies will hire people so they can supply the demand, and make money.
If, however, you give the CEO of an American company (earning over 500 times that of the average worker) a huge tax break, the effect on the economy will be minimal. Most rich people have everything they need or want in material terms. Big house, nice car etc. etc. etc.. The rich person would have no incentive to buy anything more. Nor will the rich person have an incentive to hire more people. Why do that? No money would be gained, actually, more money would be lost, because of the extra wages for the employee, overproduction reducing prices etc.. Hiring when hiring is unnecessary does more harm than good to the rich person, so the rich person does not do it.
Trickle down, or supply side, economics is an illusion. It does not work. The people who are supposed to trickle the money down or increase supply have no incentive to do so.
In a situation where the poor have more money to spend, the rich employ more workers so they can make more money, they have an incentive. More workers means more common people with money, which means more demand which means more jobs which means more money.
Demand side economics works, and has been seen to work.
Besides, are you telling me that a person making 500 more than the average person should keep almost all of his relatively undeserved paycheck? Does a CEO actually work 500 times harder, better and more importantly than the people who actually assembled the car? Are you telling me that the person who made your shirt you are wearing (assuming the person is in the USA, which is unlikely) should earn 500 times less than the boss who manages finances, marketing etc. and goes to office parties. If you are, I shake my head and wonder why.
