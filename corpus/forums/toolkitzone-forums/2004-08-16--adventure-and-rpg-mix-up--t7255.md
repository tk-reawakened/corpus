# Adventure and RPG mix up

### khin · 2004-08-16 03:48 · #B76355

A friend of mine had his story to tell:
"One day, whilst I was making my way through the traffic of people at the mall, ever so avoiding those sticky hands of children filled with cotton candies, popcorns, etc...I saw this CD store. I checked it out right away for I am craving for fresh RPGs. I asked the saleslady 'Any new RPGs here?', she replied, 'Yes, sir. We've got Resident Evil 3.' I was shocked,appaled,outraged,dumbfounded by the ignorance of the salesperson. magine Resident Evil categorized as an RPG!? I walked away disgruntled. I should have called the manager for that incident but i didn't. What a bad day the rest turned out for me."
Well, he's exaggerated. It is forgiving that the lady doesn't know the categories...as most people tend to interchange the two genres. And it is quite confusing too because the word "Role Playing Game" is applicable to any game. Aren't you "playing the role of someone else" everytime you play? Take Gran Turismo..are you not playing the role of the car owner/driver/racer? What about Super Mario(NES)...are you not playing the role of Mario/Luigi? Take the context of an adventure game...any game where you get to venture huge places can be classified as adventure game, can't they?
But in my opinion, two genres are separated by specific elements which make them up.
RPGs do have the adventure element, but it's the character growth that makes a game an RPG. Whenever there's a level-up system, the game is already classsified RPG. Story doesn't count, as adventure games also have storylines. Battle systems don't count either, although most adventure games are realtime...and some doesn't have a formal battle system: you just bash the enemy by stomping it, hitting it with a club, etc. Both games tend to have puzzles too..although puzzle-solving is more interactive in adventure games because gamers have more freedom of movement(jump, crawl, throw an object,etc..).
Modern adventure games incorporate more and more rpg elements into them, but you'll know an adventure game when for example you are forced to search the entire map/level to find a key to be able to progress to the next level, and so on. It's in this aspect RE belongs to this category, as well as other similar games like Dino Crisis, etc. I believe adventure games are also called platformer..but this monicker is more commonly applied to games that look childish, or has a 2D-game-gone-3D appeal, like Sonic Adventure, Crash Bandicoot, Tomba, etc. In these platformers, you also get to collect floating/scattered objects like coins, gems, bananas, etc.
Now we have hybrid games that are hard to classify. Games like Vagrant Story is RPGish, but has elements of a platformer, like cube puzzles and collecting keys.
Also i would like to note that the concept of RPGs was taken out from card games such as Magic:The Gathering, Dungeons and Dragons, etc....

### Marz · 2004-08-18 03:18 · #B77463

The term RPG is given more to games that allow you to assume the role of a "known" character. In most FPS, ones where it's just pure shooting, there are no RPG elements. Sure you're playing the role of some guy with a gun, but that's all you know about him and that's all that you want to know about him. This is what appeals to most FPS fans that hate RPGs. For example, my Uncle. He absolutely hates RPGs, I don't know why though. He doesn't play racing games because he says there are too many RPG elements in them. He only plays "arcade-like" games. And really, there are only three genres in the gaming industry:
Arcade: Some racing games, most FPS games, etc
RPG: some other racing games, games where you customize your characters with levels, games that tell a story in the form of an adventure, etc
Strategy: turn based games, games that let you build a city/civilization/whatever, puzzle games, etc
Then there are other games that mix some of these 3 genres, and come up with a different "genre". I don't know why though, it's pretty pointless.
