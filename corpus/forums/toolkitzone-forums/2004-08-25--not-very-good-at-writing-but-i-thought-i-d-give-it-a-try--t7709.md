# Not very good at writing, but I thought I'd give it a try...

### Nih · 2004-08-25 23:21 · #B81623

It's kinda crappy... I tried to leave a lot up to the reader's imagination. Anyway, here it is. I call it "Guilt"

**Quote:**

> You are about to hear the story of a man and his tragic end. This man, your average slave to the modern society, was faced with quite an ordeal of the mind. Under the weight of utter guilt, this man would collapse. This once-average gentleman employed at your average monopolistic cooperation was faced with a quite manageable responsibility. Due to the shortcomings of a subordinate employee, the man who is the center of this story was given no choice but to fire the young man. There ensued a bitter verbal confrontation between the two. The younger man sulked off to his quite battered vehicle. His brakes screeched as he drove off.
> As the mans alarm clock went off, he was expecting another ordinary day. Dragging his weary body off of his living room couch, he found the days newspaper resting on the floor beside him. The mans pet dog had just brought it in. After much bathing to prepare for another work day he finally settled into his splintering wooden chair for a warm serving of coffee. He lay the newspaper down in front of him. Shown on the front page was a photograph of a wrecked car. The man looked closely and discovered, to his horror, that the vehicle was similar, if not identical to the car of the man he had fired last night. The man, having been quite shaken up by the photo, proceeded to read the article. To the mans even greater surprise, the man killed in the accident happened to be the exact age of the man who had been laid off only the night before. No name was given.
> The striking facts seemed impossible to be just coincidence. The man was now certain that because of his bad temper, another person was dead. He screamed in agony.
> The man, now soaked in tears, viewed the city streets below through his apartment window. On the street, a small group of young men played a game of basketball. The young men only reminded the man of his terror inside. It was too much for the man to bear. He rummaged through his musty closet for his most prized possession  a firearm given to him by a coworker as a gift many years ago.
> The man was dead long before he pulled the trigger.
> Not but a week later the mans funeral was held. Friends, family and coworkers all mourned the loss of their beloved friend. Attending the funeral out of an act of respect was the young man who, nights before, had been fired from his job by the man now laying dead in a coffin.
>

Again, I am by no means a good writer... Any ideas on how to improve the writing style would be greatly appreciated. Thanks.

### ForeverNoobish · 2004-08-26 02:24 · #B81750

Well, I like the statement "He was dead long before he pulled the trigger" a lot.
Decent imagery, but I would say add some more metaphorical stuff in there, give some different images that can be associated with what you are saying, perhaps. It just seems a bit too matter-of-factly.
Also, you aren't subtle enough at the start... you are letting us know at the very start that the man who fired the guy is going to die. Perhaps just talk about a guy employed at this place dying, but say nothing of his subordinate employee. Merely have that little paragraph be a lead-up intro, and then introduce the characters with names, and begin telling the story of how the guy got fired and so forth with names, so that the person can't directly know it was the boss, but instead assume it was the guy getting fired. That would make the ending that much better.

### Levi · 2004-08-26 06:00 · #B81905

I agree on a few points, the beginning is sort of blah. It reminds me of Friday talking in the background during the beginning of an old dragnet episode. Not a great attention getter but not bad enough to make me want to stop reading. I would suggest some sort of philisophical, metaphorical idea thrown in here.Actually no, I like how you introduce this. But rather than introducing the story, introduce the concept of the story. its not great but one idea would be like "Take your average man, a typical slave to modern society, and force him to face an unspeakable ordeal of the mind." And so on.
Next it is hard to determine who you are talking about after the man who was fired drives off. You continually say "The man." Well this isn't bad but it leaves us seperated from the character. He's simply another guy in the world. He is nothing more than a boss who fired someone. Now this wouldn't be bad if he were a secondary character but we need a little more info. I like how you left so much for us to consider, but you also kept us right on track. Guided us through what was going on. This is an excellent way to write a story. But don't leave everything up to us. Give the boss a name, at least a last name, give a reason for the young man being fired. Give section of the dialogue between these two as they start their verbal confrontation but don't get in too deep. Start it off and then end it with a short description of what the rest was about. And have the boss watch and see or hear the young man drive off. Also give a slight emotion to the boss on how he viewed the need to lay off an employee. Sometimes a "I hate firing people" attitude is good and sometimes a "Filthy bugger" Attitude is good. I'd suggest something in the middle, don't make him a scrooge but don't make him already emotionally unstable.
Next make his life seem more dull and scheduled when he wakes up. The idea of "just another day" was good, I liked that. Also when he's reading the paper make him doubt it, one of the first things that happen's to someone who found out someone they knew died is denial. It's simply psychology, make him react like he's far from just a character being guided in a story, make him more human.
Now "screamed in agony" is a bit strong for where the man appeared to be emotionally at first. And how he ended up at the window. Make the possibility slowly dawn on him, that he might have indirectly caused someone's death. Make him hyperventilate a little, a sort of shock to his system. That leads him to the window, then introduce the concept of him beliving he actually killed someone through his temper. Make him realize that the man who died use to be like those on the street. Don't explain all of this of course, just general descriptions and some very good imagery will lead teh reader to this conclusion on their own.
The ending was very well done. I liked that. And I agree "He was dead before he pulled the trigger." Is an excellent description of this man's emotional state and being. It just brings the world into focus.
Now wording...I noticed a few places where you used wording that appears redundant and uncessary. Let me give you a few examples.
"The man(by now we should know who he is by name), now soaked in tears, viewed the city streets below through his apartment window. On the street(that comma seemed uncessary) a small group of young men played a game of basketball. The(should be these,for plural to match "men") young men only reminded the man(the man is not necessary use either a name or 'Him' because he is the subject of the sentence by then anyway. Preferably a name though) of his terror(Not terror, maybe suffering or something to explain the depression and hatred towardhimself that he feels) inside."

You are a great writer, the plot line and story idea where very nice and the descriptions you used on a lot of it were very well done. I liked it and I hope to see more. Although death does make me queasy. LOL.
