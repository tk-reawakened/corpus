Okay I originally wrote this on neopets. If anyone knows neopets you can only post paragraphs up to 300 characters long so it was written over alot of posts. On a slow computer. It was meant to be a group project but they kept saying "Continue please" So I wrote it all myself. It hasn't been edited or updated. I want to finish it before I start working on a final draft. So here is my story, I originally called it "The Message of Narnia" Doesn't work because I went completely from the title to a different story. I see no relationship between the two. So if you have an idea for a title I'd like to hear that as well.
Here's the story.
The Message of Narnia
It wasn't suppose to happen like this. Not like this. The loud deafening sounds of those machines, the knowledge that one flicker of energy and he'd be gone forever. She couldn't bear to think of it. That's why she left. She sat in the cafeteria of the hospital. Except for a few nurses picking up food every now and again she was alone. Alone with her thoughts, with her memories.
He was her son, just ten years old. How could it have come to this. She took out a small book she had been working on, it held a multitude of pictures of him growing up.
"Oh Matthew." She said softly to herself as she caressed the picture of when he was first born, and in this very same hospital that threatened to take his life.
Turning the page she found herself laughing slightly as the pain ebbed from her heart. There he was not three years old, totally covered in mud with a handful of worms placed randomly on top.
"See mom? It's a mud pie, I made it for you. It says, 'I love mom.'" At that the tears began to flow, and the truth returned to her mind. So young, he is just so young.
Mom help! MOM!" The screams filled her head as she covered her ears. "Stop, stop." She couldn't stand to remember. The tires, the screatching tires. That sickening sound as he hit the pavement. She couldn't bare to remember. Flipping the page she forced herself to forget, to forget and remember.
This time he was five. Just before his first sleep over. There stood beside him three of his friends. Tony, Micheal, and Jennifer his "twin." She laughed as she looked at them. How remarkably similar they looked, and acted for that matter. It was such a joy to watch them talk about the coming party.
Hey Jen I thought this was a boy's party." Micheal said teasing her as usual. "Yeah, well David's mother said I could come." "Oh yeah go everywhere your boyfriend goes." Tony chimed in makign kissy faces. "Shut up." Matt pushed him and started one of their famous dog pile fights. She had to force them all to stay home that night, espcially after tehn antique plates got broken.
But it was worth it, to keep him home one more night. To hold on for just that one second longer. It's scary to think but she believed she knew this was going to happen, and she hated herself for not seeing it sooner.
"It's not your fault." The doctor had told her, "There's nothing you could have done, he jsut didn't look where he was goin."
It didn't help. Nothing they said would ever change the fact, she was loosing her only son. He was dying in that room alone, and here she sat refusing to believe it.
Eight.
Hey mom watch me hit this one out of the park." Matt said pointing toward left field like his favorite batter of old. Oh it made her laugh to see him. If but a little embarrassed, but he never failed in his promise. Each time, up and out it went. He was so full of energy, so full of life. This was how she would remember him.
Nine.
"CANNON BALL!" He shouted jumping into the air, his red hair flailing about his head as he came down hard out doing all of his friends in their annual splashing contest. "And the winner is....Matt" David announced from the side of the pool as everyone cheered. Matt was so proud, he refused to let anyone forget it that whole summer.
Ten.
Her breath caught in her throat. The picture was taken only three days earlier originally to waste film. Now it would be one of the last ways she would ever remember her son. He sat silently on the porch swing of their house reading a book. The Lion the Witch and The Wardrobe, his favorite. She rememberd thinking that he must have had it memorized by now. Sucha great message.
To give one's self up, to die and yet to live again. How sweet, how beautiful. How hopeless to wish for. Despite the machines her son was dead, he wasn't coming back to her. Not now not ever.
One of the nurses in charge of her son walked into the room and beckoned her back upstairs. Slowly she got up and walked stiffly back to her son's death bed. She knew what they wanted, but could she do it?
Could she kill her own son? No, she shook her head fiercely, her son was already dead. She wouldn't be doing anything except shutting off a few machines. He was already dead.
"Mrs. Johnson I should warn you. This is not an easy decision for most people to make. And no one choice will make it any easier." The nurse said calmly, trying to make her understand. But she understood all too well.
This was going to be the last time the world ever considered her son to be anything but dead.
She nodded her head and the nurse opened the door. Inside the doctor was waiting. He told her she had to make a choice, it didn't have to be then but she would have to make one soon. She had already made up her mind.
"I'm not going to leave him like this. Just do it."
The doctor handed her some paper work to be signed the left the room instructing her on which switches to pull to end his life. "I'm sorry." She said more to herself than to him. "I'm sorry." She flipped the switch and it was done.
