### khin · 2004-11-06 08:13 · #B121886

Here's another one.
A few days back, zzurdeadzz has PMed me if I could show another version with some vegetations. Well, sorry this isn't as much floral as you would've loved but next time i make one i'll show them Smile
This one is the Skyra Park..the board that you'll enter after passing through the Aryx Avenue...
Step 1 Same old style. Layout the perspective that you want. This time i chose a semi-top-down view.
![](http://www.mysticsoftware.net/piarts/parksnap1.gif)
Step 2 Refine them. Thick lines are helpful because this is just a draft. As you get more and more into detail you should change your brush to a more appropriate size. I just use thick lines because they are easier to see when i'm not zoomed in.
![](http://www.mysticsoftware.net/piarts/parksnap2.gif)
Step 3 It's starting to shape up real nicely. Start using more precise brushes this time. If you want your work to have an airbrushed appeal, use a paintbrush or an airbrush. If you want to get a pixelled appeal, like those low-res GBA games, use a pencil tool.
![](http://www.mysticsoftware.net/piarts/parksnap3.gif)
Step 4 Apply basic colors. This is important. It shall establish the ambience of your piece. If at this point you chose the wrong color combination..your work will suck even if you shade and add more details to it.
![](http://www.mysticsoftware.net/piarts/parksnap4.gif)
Step 5 Detail. Color every piece and imagine all lumps, bumps, and holes in it. Of course you dont have to deatil everything if you're going for a cartoon style...but if you are going for a realistic one(like Indy's)..then go shade every piece of rock/stone and do not neglect a single detail Smile I'm not yet an expert on that matter though...
Okay..so where's my lightsource this time? As you may have guessed, NORTHWEST. Therefore all shadows must be casted at the SOUTHEASTERN part. all shaded stones have their shadows at the
SE direction. The walkway, as with other stone textures have random streaks of dark and light color. This is to give them that "stoney, semi-rough" texture. Yes, all you gotta do is select the burn tool and draw randomly on the surface. Take note however that the direction in which you should draw must the the sam direction as the contour of the figure.
Take a look at the road..do you see any brush stroke that goes ACROSS the road? I see none.
The brush was stroked in line with the road itself..so that they will be somewhat parallel.
Now there's the tree. I have 4 trees, 3 of them has the leafy parts showing. All 3 trees are colored differently. Take a good look at the NE tree. I detailed each leaf by drawing every piece. Of course they must be arranged in a convincing manner to produce such results. After
drawing all leaves, i applied a darker shade to the part of each leaf that will be hindered from light. That way it wouldn't appear flat. The 2 other trees don't have this. This is for variety..and for experiment too Smile
Now the roots. Roots are shaded across, contrary to what i said about shading the road, why? because the roots are fibrous. In my game, tree roots usually has its fibers laid across it, so the shading should follow every fiber. Observe it carefully and see they look like elephant trunks Smile
![](http://www.mysticsoftware.net/piarts/parksnap5.gif)
Step 6 Do you think it's done? No. I don't think so. There's still no light. what do I mean no light? It's clear enough that the shading reflects the light source!Well take a look at this one:
![](http://www.mysticsoftware.net/piarts/parksnap6.gif)
That one is better, don't you agree? even with all that shading, a real
shadow-casting is still important. Each part has its individual shadows, but they are not in unison. The trees do not cast shadows on the ground..as well as any protruding objects...that's what's missing, so that's what i placed.
this one took me only 5 hours. It's smaller and has less objects in it. Plus this is my second try at making backgrounds with a mouse, so i'm likely to get used to it and be able to work faster.
If you haven't noticed, the well looks rather bland. truth is, i haven't decided what i'd do with it just yet...i wanna put a fountain..but that'll require it to be animated..so i guess i'll just make it as an item.
Feel free to use them as templates. I really hope you're learning from this. I'd like to see some background paintings from all of you and give them some in-dept criticism Smile
Delano: I use layers. It's hard to do these things in photoshop and ignore layers Smile Each snapshot is a new layer.
Tips on shading:
If you're using Photoshop, you're in luck. I have some tips for you when it comes to shading. Smile
Photoshop offers a wide array of drawing tools for us to use. Among them are the dodge tool, burn tool, airbrush tool, paintbrush tool, and pencil & eraser tool. It has cool features too like layers and opacity. If you are shading, you don't neccessarily have to pick a color each time you shade. You have 3 options:
-Use dodge/burn
-Use layers/opacity
-Use brush/opacity
Dodging a color means lightening it. If you want a highlight, use dodge, if you want darkening areas, use burn. The tool has 3 modes. Experiment with it and learn to use it. This is the easiest as it automatically shades the piece for you. It can get useless sometimes because somehow the outcome isn't what you want.
Layers are your friend. I don't know the limit of layers in Photoshop, but i've tried up to 50+ i think and i didn't have problems. Create a new layer. Pick a darker color..or you can pick black. Adjust the layer opacity to a certain percentage. Start drawing on that layer.
You will see that you are actually shading the layer/s below it. Good thing with this is that if you made a mistake, you can easily modify that layer w/o affecting the rest of the pic..asuming they're on other layers. Normally i use 20% layer opacity..but it depends on the intensity of light. The higher the intensity, the more opaque it should be.
The last one is for brushes. Pick a brush(Air/Paint/etc.), adjust the brush opacity at a point. when you start shading(with a black forecolor assuming), the parts you stroke will be darkened. When you let go of your mouse, and again strike the same area, it will get darker. The concept here is that you aply a certain amount of black at an area
therefore repeatedly applying it at the same area shall cause the area to get even darker.
the problem here is that you are working with one layer, so if you made a mistake the entire pic will be affected.
You can do combinations of the three Smile
