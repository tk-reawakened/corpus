Issues That Are Most Important to My Ideals for America
By ForeverNoobish
-----------------------
Economy
First of all, our economy is not on much of an upswing right now, despite what some may say. The fact remains that we are still in a huge deficit, and that there are no signs of that deficit shrinking anytime soon. The DOW and NASDAQ numbers are nothing to jump up and down about, either. However, the NASDAQ does show a little more promise than the DOW right now, so I will only focus on the DOW.
![](http://img.photobucket.com/albums/v426/forevernoobish/dowjonesnov2k32k4.png)
Now, one might say "well, in the last month, we've been on an upswing!" Truth is, it's only been in the last two weeks. Also, if they were to pay more attention, they would also see this two-week upswing trend in practically every single month. Granted, we are higher than we were at the end of 2003 as far as the DOW Jones Industrial ratings go. However, look at where it was in the middle of February, 2004. What could explain the drop that has taken place since then? Moving out of Iraq would probably be the first big step to helping the majority of the companies in the DOW but I will get into that later on
As far as this deficit, which was created mainly by the decisions of an administration that was handed the largest surplus in the history of our country, there are no signs of it being fixed in the next four years. President Bush said at a press conference the day after the polls closed, "I earned capital in the campaign, political capital, and now I intend to spend it. It is my style." (Yes, I nabbed that straight from the transcript) This is perhaps the main reason why our deficit will not become less severe during the next four years. Any money that President Bush earns, he spends it on something. If he cannot find something to spend it on, he creates something to spend it on. This is probably the worst aspect of his presidency. Also, the deficit has been exaggerated by such actions as having a tax cut (which was for rich people only, by the way) during the middle of a costly war. Such actions as these do not help an economy in any way.
Also, the tax cuts that Bush has had over the last few years have been inane at best. Now there are two ways basically that taxes can be distributed (or at least only two that have worked anywhere in the world over the last century): a flat tax rate (i.e. everyone has to pay, say, 50% of their income) or a utilitarian-based tax rate (those with low income pay, say, 10%, those with middle income 20%, and those with high income 30%, etc.). Now, although a flat tax rate has worked quite well in egalitarian countries such as Sweden and Denmark, it could not work here in America currently because we would first have to change several other aspects of our government system as a whole to do so. Alas, we must use the utilitarian-based method. In order for this method to work, the rich must be taxed a significantly greater amount than the impoverished and the middle class. However, with these tax cuts being targeted at the high end instead of the low end, the utilitarian system is being hurt because now the proportion of taxation is becoming too close for it to work.
So, what would I do to help the economy? Firstly, I would try to leave Iraq as soon as possible. We don't need to stay there and "ensure they have democracy." But that's in a different issue Anyway, I would not give any tax cuts while we are still in Iraq; and after we left Iraq, I would only give tax cuts to those who are of middle or working class. I would probably give the rich a tax cut too, but just not as much as I would to the working class. The biggest cut should come at the bottom, and the smallest at the top. Also, I wouldn't spend so much on things that don't need spending.
War on Terrorism and the War in Iraq
Obviously, at this point, everyone in the world except for President Bush and his dittoheads are accepting that Iraq was not a threat to America when we went to invade it. However, many of these same people that acknowledge that we should not have been in Iraq still say that we "should finish the job" and impose democracy on its people. Well, I entirely disagree with this, even knowing that if I were a politician and saying this, my chances of holding office would be slim to none. The situation in Iraq right now reeks of White Man's Burden. For those of you who are not familiar with this terminology, it started being used when the major European countries went to Africa and started imposing their own "cultural values" on the people there because they felt that their culture was superior and it was their obligation to get Africa "up to speed". The term has carried over to modern times, as America has in the last century tried to impose their culture on Latin American countries, namely. The same is happening in Iraq. Why do you suppose that there is all this fighting in Iraq? It's because we are there. If we leave, then the fighting, if not stops, goes down to a very low level. If they want democracy badly enough, they will find a way to impose it on themselves. They do not need another country controlling them (for their oil oops, did I say that? Well, it's true) in order to have a good culture and government system. If we are so worried about imposing democracy on them for the good of their own country, why don't we care about the rest of the Middle East or the world, for that matter? I don't see us anywhere else in the world imposing democracy.
Now, regarding the war on terrorism obviously we should be focusing our efforts on those terrorists who prove to be the greatest threat. Well, the two biggest threats are right around where we are currently (Iraq). Unlike Iraq, Iran has a proven alliance with Al Qaeda, as well as at least one weapon of mass destruction. They also aren't shy about it; they are essentially jumping up and down and waving their arms in the air telling us this information. Same with North Korea. Kim Jong-Il is ranting and raving about his numerous WMDs and how he has the capability to use them on us. The situations socially there are much worse than in Iraq. North Korea has a rising cannibalism rate due to massive food shortages. If we want to actually change a country for the better of the entire world, we should go there and get out of Iraq A.S.A.P.
Education
I am going to focus on the worst education-targeted act in the history of the United States: The No Child Left Behind Act, or as I like to call it, the Every Child Left Behind Act. The general basis of this act is that every child should be at a standard, and that they all should meet a certain expectation, which is determined based on how a group of students does on tests. Now, this act requires ALL students to take these tests and meet the national standards. That means that special ed kids that are in schools are expected to meet the standard that the other kids are supposed to meet, and that ESL kids are supposed to be at the same standard in English as everyone else, et al. If a school does not have enough kids meeting the expectation, they are to be shut down. Because of this act, 1300 schools are expected to be closed in the next 8 months in my home state of California. Now, think about this: how many kids will be left without a school in 8 months because of this act? Now, how many of those kids are "Left Behind"? The act is ludicrous, and if I were currently the President, that is one of the first things I would attempt to ditch.
"Morality Issues" (Gay Marriage/Abortion)
I personally am not gay, but I also do not see how being gay affects the rights of others. It certainly doesn't affect me. I go by the basis that someone should be allowed to do something unless they are infringing on the rights of others. If gay people were to be allowed to have civil unions, who would it hurt? Does it really affect Christians at all if Jewish people get married in a synagogue? If not, then why should it affect them at all if gay people are married in a town hall or a court? The nation's Constitution doesn't mention anything on the definition of marriage. Just because the Bible (a.k.a. "that book that told you specifically not to do that thing you did") may define it as a union between a man and woman doesn't mean that the law in this country has to say the same. If there is no dispute over whether the constitution hold restrictions on someone's sexuality, then why is there a dispute on whether people of a certain sexuality should be allowed to get married? It really boggles my mind. At a time, people considered interracial marriage to be "unholy," but the majority of us have come to accept it now. Hopefully the same thing will end up happening with gays.
On abortion, if I were involved in a relationship with a pregnant girl who wanted an abortion, I would probably try to talk her out of it due to health complications that could arise. However, I do not view a zygote or a fetis as being a human being; it is living, yes, but not a human being. The technology is there to perform this operation, and it doesn't kill any humans or violate the rights of any humans. The zygotes or fetises can be used for stem cell research as well, which, as a type I diabetic, I fully support. If you are so worried about killing a non-human entity, then you should probably be more concerned about the environment, which, in its current condition, is resulting in the deaths of many non-human entities.
Environment
I could go on all day about this, but I'll just keep it short and sweet. First, the threats that we as humans pose to particular biomes in the world, and how America is somewhat involved in all of it:
Coral Reefs: Perhaps the most threatened biome in the world today (well, not biome, but "aquatic life zone"). Coral reefs are being killed off at such a great rate due to pollution in the ocean and the methods of deep sea fishing that there might not be any coral reefs left in the world by 2050. Coral reefs are an important part of our world because they help quickly throw away CO2 from the atmosphere. In short, coral reefs help slow down the inevitable state of global warming. If you wonder why global warming has become exaggerated recently (which is why they were so quickly able to determine that it is actually happening, and the debate on its existence died), then just look at the dying coral reefs. What exactly kills them? Well, many companies who go overseas or are on coasts throw their waste into the ocean; not smart. Also, other major companies fish via giant nets that capture anything in their paths on the ocean floor; this results in the killing of many organisms in the reefs that are essential to its well-being.
How we fix it: Impose regulations on the fishermen, which we have yet to do, and companies that fish. Designate certain areas of the ocean for them to fish in, just like there are certain areas in forests that lumber companies are allowed to cut in. As for the pollution, well, we just have to find a better way to store waste. We do some nice sanitation systems, but unfortunately, they send the waste to rivers, lakes and oceans.
Estuaries: The greatest American biome threatened, the estuaries are the areas where rivers/lakes meet the ocean. This results in great biodiversity usually, as the conditions are ideal for many types of organisms. This biome can be found in the Bayou areas of the South, as well as in places such as Northern California and the rest of the Pacific Northwest. The greatest threat to the biodiversity here is, again, pollution, namely from factories that dump stuff into the rivers, which all lead to estuaries. The regulations on these companies now are minimal, as the corporations seem to be favored over the environment greatly. The corporations vs. EPA conflict in America is perhaps one of the most important conflicts in the world today, because what American corporations do all over the world affects the entire world's environment. I will talk about this conflict later on.
To fix: Determine what is necessary for corporations to make a reasonable profit, and then allow them to do no more than that, so that pollution is minimal.
Boreal Forest: This forest is the largest biome in the world, and it stretches all across Canada and parts of Alaska, as well as most of Northern Asia and Europe. Essentially, the entire northern hemisphere is either this forest or tundra land. The boreal forest is home to many trees that we use for furniture, as well as minerals we mine for various purposes. The logging companies are going at such a rapid rate of destruction in this forest that the Food and Agriculture Administration predicts that 70% of this forest will be gone by 2020. After all, 63% of what it used to be was destroyed over the last 40 years already. The cutting of trees here without having any knowledge of what it does to organisms is ridiculous. While it may be true that humans are at the top of many food chains, and therefore we don't need to have regard for our prey, these are not animals we eat or even interact with. These are animals that we just take homes away from or food away from. Also, the mining has led to much pollution in the forest, and has radioactively infected many plants, which then of course are eaten by animals who then also become infected. Only 2% of this biome is protected by law, so that leaves 98% of it to be depleted by logging and mining companies.
To fix: First, protect more than 2% of the biome. Maybe 10% would be better. Also, limit the amount of mining that can be done. Determine what a reasonable profit is for a company, then determine how much should be mined based on that. And, unlike other EPA laws, enforce it!
Tropical Rainforest: Everyone knows how endangered THIS biome is. Logging companies go crazy here because there are so many trees, and of different types! However, the most disturbing part about this biome is that we don't know anything about half of the organisms living there. Let me elaborate: the conditions in this biome for living are beyond ideal, which is why there is so much biodiversity. Now, the conditions are SO ideal that a species of an animal perhaps could have started its life in one tree, and then found it to be so great that they never had to leave that tree. When that tree is cut down, that animal becomes extinct. You might say "so what?" but frankly, you shouldn't. There are at least 20 types of species that originated from the Rainforest that we have extracted chemicals from to make groundbreaking medicine. Now, who's to say there aren't any more species we could do this with? We could have the cure to some giant disease in there! But the logging companies go and cut the trees without knowing what lives in it; therefore, we could have possibly killed off such medicines already, and we'll never know it.
To fix: Require trees to be inspected and researched before cutting. Or, protect the entire biome. Either or. Razz
In conclusion, what revolves around most of our issues is the conflict between the EPA and the corporations, who currently have the government on their side. What should be done is negotiations to determine what a reasonable profit is for a company. Then, it should be determined what is necessary to reach that. After that, there should be limits placed allowing no more than that to be done. This limits pollution and keeps resources available to more than just American humans (I'm thinking of animals here). This is the best way to figure out how to keep pollution low and keep biodiversity high. About global warming though, it is probably inevitable. If we wanted to slow it down though, then we should protect coral reefs better.
Oh, and since oil is going to be gone before the end of the century, I think energy companies should start focusing on creating an economy in methanol and ethanol temporarily until they can have full, cost-efficient access to hydrogen or electricity, at which point they will have a new economy to run off of in that.
