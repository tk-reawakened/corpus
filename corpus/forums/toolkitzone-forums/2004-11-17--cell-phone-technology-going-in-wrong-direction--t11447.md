# Cell phone technology going in wrong direction?

### Willy Wonka · 2004-11-17 14:44 · #B126314

I could make it in debate forum... but I guess it's not too serious.
Anyway, the problem is, cell phones are advancing. In good direction? Some think not - they lose on simplicity, and gain on, well, gadgets.
Look at the old cells: they were a size of hand, had monocolor display, small menu, one big central button for accept/recieve call and two up/down arrows.
And now?
The cell phone size is getting smaller, which makes it harder to fit in the hand. It's unnatural. Best size is the size of hand, it's the most logical one.
The button for accepting and recieving calls has been divided into 2 different buttons, of which neither is in centre, in fact, the . So if a cell rings, you can't simply press single button with the thumb, the new position of "recieve" button is usually to the left of the pad used to scroll menu, usually out of reach of thumb - to press it, you have to place the phone before your eyes, hold with one hand, press button with another, then put phone near your eat and talk.
In old phones, you could simply press the button intuitionaly when someone ringed you and not even look at it to recieve call.
As I said, the pad with arrows, all buttons for moving through menu merged into one. Is it simple? No. Logical? No. Natural? No. What the hell is this all for, therefore?
What do you think of this all? Cells aren't turning easier to use. They're turning into microcomputers. But phone should be phone, not a computer.

### Spyder · 2004-11-17 14:58 · #B126317

My phone is extremely small, probably one of the smallest on the market ( [Samsung e105](http://212.213.255.72/keepthls/pics/samsung/sae105_00.jpg)) and I have pretty large hands being 6'6", and I can operate it just fine. As for accepting calls, I just open the phone when someone calls and I'm instantly connected. When it's time to hang up, I just close the phone. It's not as if cell phones are going to eventually be the size of pennies. There are plenty of phones that are quite large, and with new things being added to them all the time (MP3, video, TV, GPS, internet) they're sure to stay that way. I'm personally all for the "microcomputer" aspect of it all. The more things my phone can do, the better. It's not as if it's requiring you to pay more. If you don't want a phone with lots of features, you don't have to buy one. If it has features you don't want, don't use them. It's simple.
