### Levi · 2004-11-24 19:45 · #B129634

Ah, man I'm so sorry. But I'm having a lot of problems getting past that first line.

**Quote:**

> A large, vast, lush-green forest sorrounded the radius of about four miles, and centered in the forest was a large lake.
>

Ah, that is such a frustrating thing. Don't tell us how many miles the radius of the forest was. Give us a name, give us a location if you want or just make it a forest. But don't tell the radius. That just takes away from it. The first part was good, large vast lush-green. Very good imagery. But you tried to make that a little to exact. Please try to avoid this. I'll get to the rest of it soon. I promise. I just had to say something about that line though.
Oh and the lake, unless you are bringing it up later, or it happens to be where this main character is. Or something along those lines, there is no need to include it. Its good to help us picture the satellite view of the place but not necessary towards the story in any way. Usually some description that means nothing other than to describe the surroundings is good. But in this case you bring up the lake, and drop it. No real point. You could place him next to the lake and have a cool breeze blowing up from the water to make him slightly chilled. That would work.

### Levi · 2004-12-04 08:35 · #B134487

Okay, I have finished reading. I finally forced myself to just stop what I was doing and fulfull my promise to read.
Just a few small comments.
Okay if the lake is important than naming the forest or naming the lake even would be good. The way you've moved the story on, if you were to bring up the lake again, there would be no way to correlate the lake in that forest and a lake that you bring up later, other than telling us right out that its the same lake. And that would just ruin the story.
Okay I agree that imagery is wonderful, you do this so well but sometimes it seems you're focusing on certain descriptions that don't really have a lot to do with the story and leaving those that do out. I can't remember where exactly this problem came up but watch for it. Sometimes too much scenery description takes away from scene description or the action and facts of the story.
Next, the transitions from present, to dream, to memories aren't real clear. Its like you start talkign about one thing then digress to another with very little to seperate the two facts. When you went to describe the Elder of the village and what he had said had happened, it came right after describing the knives that Kai had placed onto the table. Sometimes this sort of transition is made in the changing of fonts to like Italics and doing more dialog than narrative or making the narrative seem like a present day thing. The simple changing of the font changes the focus of the story. Or you could say he rememberd this, or he heard the old man's words again like a bad dream that refused to leave ones mind long after he'd woken up. That sort of thing, there has to be a transition between just thought and actually going back to the past.
Also the details of the battle and how the village had come to be destroyed are a little sketchy, try to clean this up a bit. In fact make it like a living memory. Like Nova said "Action Action Action." This would add a battle scene that the Elder rememberd. And help to move the plot along a little faster as well as to keep the interest of the reader.
There are also a few dialoges that are a bit awkward in how they are put together. Like "Sakuya, get something work while on!" That's just a bit odd. I'm not sure if this was on puprose but it looks like it should be "Sakuya, just get something to work on for a while!"
Well I hope this helps a little, and remember these are personal critisisms. Not necessarily to be taken as needs. All in all its a good story and I hope to read more later. Thanks for posting. Later days.
