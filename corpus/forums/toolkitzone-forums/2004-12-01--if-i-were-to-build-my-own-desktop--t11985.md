### Spyder · 2004-12-01 11:04 · #B132796

I'm only on break at work right now, so I can't go into too much detail until I get home in a couple hours, which is when I'll do some research. I reccomend using newegg.com for your stuff though. Although they might not have the absolute cheapest parts, they definately have cheap prices, extremely fast shipping, and you can get everything you need there at one low cost, rather than buying everything from 10 different websites.
Fans - Most cases will come with at least one 80cm fan, many of them will come fully stocked with all the fans you need, so wait until you pick your case before actually picking out fans.
Networking Card - A lot of motherboards now days come with on-board 10/100 networking, and some even come with gigabit LAN. Again, wait until you've chosen your motherboard before shopping for this.
Sound Card - You can scratch this off your list. All motherboards come with on-board sound, 90% of them have 5.1, and some of the higher end boards will come with 6.1 or 7.1 sound cards. Buying a sound card these days is worthless unless your an audiophile, and has $150 or more to burn.
Graphics card - This one really depends one what you plan on doing with your computer. You already have a Radeon 9000, which is decent enough for web browsing and light gaming. To prepare for future games though, you'll need a DirectX9 compatible card, and preferably one that's going to run games for the next 2 years if your much of a gamer.
Floppy Drive - Do you really need this? I don't know about you, but I don't think I've used a floppy drive since I graduated high school; but I guess since your still in school, it may be useful for class assignments.
CD or DVD RW - I would say a CD-RW is a minimum. You can buy the fastest CD-RW available for around $30, so there's no reason you shouldn't have one. DVD burners are another story. Unfortunately I haven't had much experience with them, so someone else can probably help you a bit more; but it's my understanding that single layer DVD-burners can not duplicate most DVD's. Most DVD's are dual-layer, and you would need a dual-layer burner, as well as dual-layer DVD+/-R's to duplicate them, among other things, like software. If your just looking at DVD+/-R's for a storage medium, I'd look at what you plan on storing first. For most purposes, CD burners should do the trick. If you honestly think you'll need a DVD burner, get a dual-layer drive, preferably one that writes single layer DVD+R's at 16x speeds. Then just buy single layer DVD+R media. I've seen it as low as 19 cents per DVD, far cheaper than just a year ago.
HeatSink and CPU fan(are there proccessor packages with them) - If you get a retail version of a processor, they usually come with a stock heatsink, however, depending on if your going to overclock or not, you may want to get an OEM (bare) processor, and buy your own heatsink. There are usually better ones out there than the stock ones.
THe card for Flash cards and the likees - Card for flash cards? Do you mean a card reader? Like a 9-in-1 card reader?
Edit: Ok, I'm at home now. But before I can begin, how much do you plan on spending to build your computer? Your going to need at least $300 for anything worth building, and obviously the better the parts you buy, the more it will cost. You can build a decent gaming rig for ~$600, and a damn nice comp for $1000. This is without taking into account buying a legal copy of Windows, if that's a factor. If you need/want a legal copy of WindowsXP Home, add $90. XP Pro will set you back ~$150.
Also, much of the stuff for your new comp can just be taken out of your old one, such as your hard drive, etc; depending on how old everything is.

### Spyder · 2004-12-02 08:48 · #B133520

Yes, you can easily afford all of that for less than $2000. Here is a basic list of what you should be looking at...
Motherboard - [ABIT "AV8" K8T800 Pro" Socket 939](http://www.newegg.com/app/ViewProductDesc.asp?description=13-127-181&depa=1) - $113
Processor - [AMD Athlon 64 3500+ Socket 939](http://www.newegg.com/app/viewproductdesc.asp?description=19-103-463&DEPA=1) - $267
Memory - [Corsair Value Select Dual Channel Kit 1GB (2x512) PC3200](http://www.newegg.com/app/ViewProductDesc.asp?description=20-145-480&depa=1) - $160
Hard Drive - [Hitachi 250GB 7200RPM SATA Hard Drive](http://www.newegg.com/app/ViewProductDesc.asp?description=22-145-071&depa=1) - $148
Video Card - [Sapphire ATI Radeon X800 Pro 256MB DVI/VIVO](http://www.ewiz.com/detail.php?p=AT-X800VVS&c=pw) - $411 (The VIVO will let you attach a video camera to your computer, so you can edit your videos easily. Video cards can get a "lot" cheaper without VIVO, so if you already have a method of capturing video (ie firewire or video capture card) you can save a lot by looking for something without VIVO.)
Case - This is pretty much up to you. A decent case will run you anywhere from $50-150, and will come with a power supply.
Total - $1099 - $1249
The total cost will obviously vary depending on what you buy, and where; but as you can see, you can build a damn nice computer for quite cheap.
