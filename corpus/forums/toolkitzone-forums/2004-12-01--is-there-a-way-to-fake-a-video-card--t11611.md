Heh. It's x86. That means you have an x86 processor, which is just about every processor known to man. I'm sure you've heard of processors like the 286, 386, 486. The Pentium and AMD K5 were 586's, the PII,III and K6 were 686's, The PIV and Athlon are 786's. That's why it's called an x86 processor, because the x changes, and the 86 stays the same. All x86 processors are backwards compatible with each other.

As for your friends computer. The only reason I can come up with as to why it says x86 where his graphics processor is supposed to be listed is because he's using an on-board chipset. Essentially his graphics processor "is" the main processor of the computer, which is an x86 compatible processor. With your Radeon 9000, it uses a seperate processor (aka GPU) than what is plugged into your motherboard (CPU).
