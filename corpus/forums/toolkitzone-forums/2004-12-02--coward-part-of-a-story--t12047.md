# Coward...Part of a story

### Levi · 2004-12-02 08:37 · #B133519

With the exception of the horrible typos I know I have there, and my poor use of grammar. Tell me what you think of this. I want to build off of it, but I'm not sure.
I stare down the long path to my home almost everyday. It never changes, except with the seasons. The trees that line its edges do change and shed their leaves in the fall, the snow does cover the path in a bright wintry glow, and in the spring blossoms to sprout from the gnarled old branches. Yet still the path remains, ever changed yet never changing. Its countenance still and serene, its life older than time itself to my mind. This path to me was always there, there was no beginning to it, nor will there be an end. It has always been here, it survived wars and plauges. It stretches forrth like a timeline of history. Here, a small rock lies. This is when the civil war brought many soldiers to this small town. And here, pointing to a fallen twig, this was the end of that war. I measure the distance with my hands, and they nearly touch. How short life is, and how short their's was. I'm no warrior, I'm a coward. I'll never deny that, its a truth I live with every day. And as I watch my road I see the effects of nature against it. I see it standing firm and still. Watered with the blood of soldieres, this path is strong indeed. Three leaves skitter across the path in the wind like remnants, ghosts, of those long dead soldiers, more time has past and more lives are lost. Yet here I stand, at the road. Unwilling to pass.

### DistantCitySilence · 2004-12-03 14:54 · #B134128

I rarely review so I'm not sure if I'll do this properly. It is an interesting piece that to me appears to be packed with detail. You have some history of the time the character is in, some perspective (the way in which he views things is good for character development) and a little of a poetic side. I see symbolism and philosophy also. I like it, but I think it can be improved. Perhaps you aren't sure about doing more because that passage opens up, it deals with these things that are quite big, but then you close it. The character is a coward so he won't walk the path. Maybe you could solve this by having a reason for going home like a death, or the home is destroyed, or something else that may not be so depressive. I think you have potential, just a case of practice and study.
