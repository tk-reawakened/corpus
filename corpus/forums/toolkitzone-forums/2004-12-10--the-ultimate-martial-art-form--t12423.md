# The Ultimate Martial Art Form.

### QuickCat · 2004-12-10 23:19 · #B137706

There is no style that can fit every need. There is no pre-written formula that will match every situation. Many have argued that there is one style that can rule all other styles, but in truth, all styles and forms of martial art have limitations; all have weaknesses.
So why remain content in a style? Martial Art is just that, an art. Art is self expression; communication of feelings. Can you really express yourself with a style someone else has made, thousands of years ago?
Bruce Lee, unarguably the best martial artist in the world, said "An intelligent mind is one that is always learning, never concluding. Styles and patterns have come to a conclusion and therefore have ceased to be intelligent. This is an undeniable truth.
Be like water. Water can become anything, it can take any shape. Put water into a cup, it becomes the cup, put it into a teacup, it becomes the teacup. Now water can be soft, or it can be the hardest substance in the world; it can go through anything: rock, granite, you name it. Be like water, my friend.
-Bruce Lee
So, onto what is the ultimate practice of martial art. It is not a way as the name implies. A way sets you onto an unchangeable path. When there is a way there is a center, when there is a center there is a circumference, and therein lays the limitation. You are stuck on the way, stuck within the circumference, you are trapped, left there to rot and wither away. Now, suppose that Crane Style was invented by a cripple, someone who couldnt use their left leg. Now, three thousand years later, everyone is a cripple. It is the same in all ways of marital art.
Jeetkunedo means Way of the Intercepting Fist, and it was not invented, but discovered by Bruce Lee, the first man to see through styles and forms, and to see through to what the governing dynamics of martial arts were. Do means way, but this is only a name, and not to be confused with what the actual martial art is, for this isnt a way; not a set of rules all must abide by. It is a flexible way to train yourself.
There is always a best way to perform a kick or punch or throw, but when people try to derive when to use the proper technique, you become sloppy. Many forms have several steps to do something; do this and then this and then and why you are remembering all the and thens, the other guy is killing you. Almost all martial art forms try to make a certain method that will work in all situations, and that is wat creates their weaknesses.
So I ask everyone here who practices the martial arts, to defend why their style is the best. Maybe there is an ultimate form who knows?
