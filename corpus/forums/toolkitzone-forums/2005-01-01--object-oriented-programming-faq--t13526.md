# Object Oriented Programming FAQ

### Colin · 2005-01-01 23:11 · #B149475

OOP in RPGToolkit, Version 3 :: Frequently Asked Questions
In light of the new object oriented features found in the RPGToolkit, Version 3.05 and likely upwards, the forum on which you are reading this has been created. Object oriented programming, or just OOP, is a very powerful tool for designing your game's scripts, especially larger projects like battle systems. However, with power--often hand in hand--is often found complexity. When first introduced to OOP, many people (including myself!) find the topic to be just...beyond anything you could comprehend. It all seems very foreign.
Fortunately, I--and hopefully you, after working with 3.05--no longer believe OOP to be, in any way, complicated. Once the basic principles are understood, there's really nothing we'll be able to throw in your way that you won't instantly grasp. A concept, incidentally, at the heart of object oriented programming.
"I have no clue where to start"
As mentioned above, the whole idea of how things work (even systemically) can be--and often is--found very, very convoluted. Before asking this question, consider what, exactly, you don't understand. If you're finding everything overwhelming and simply have no clue to start, I'd suggest reading Matt's [Introduction to Objects](http://toolkitzone.com/library.php?mode=read&doc=127). If the syntax is tripping you up, consider reading my [RPGCode OOP Overview](http://toolkitzone.com/library.php?mode=read&doc=64). Provided you fully understand how classes work but just don't see why you'd want to utilize a mechanism that'll cause extra work, contemplate reading my article on the [Seque](http://toolkitzone.com/library.php?mode=read&doc=62). Granted, if your situation is none of the above, you may want to consider requesting further information.
"I have an object as a member of a class and when I attempt to call its methods or access its vars, I recieve error messages"
Fixed in 3.0.6.
