# Here's the first chapter, draft 2, of a story I abandoned...

### Ryukenshen · 2005-04-05 21:24 · #B190731

Here's a story (my second draft, heh) that I wrote awhile ago. I like the basic idea and how it was written, but it was only the first chapter and I began to bore of it. Draft 3, which I won't post here, was even better.
Ch. 1: ???? (draft 2)
James gritted his teeth together furiously, casting about for something to destroy  something he could sink his teeth into, something he could maul, something he could contort so badly that it wouldnt look even remotely like it had originally. His chestnut-brown eyes scanned the backyard; most everything had already been severely damaged from Jamess daily rampages. Rain lashed downward, unrelenting, while thunder sounded in the distance. James felt a sudden feeling of shame as he crawled into the small doghouse that had been built for him. Rain streamed down his thick, shaggy black hair and mingled with tears of fury and frustration. He hated living here so fervently that he destroyed anything within his reach to relieve himself of his pent-up anger, although he knew it was foolishly stupid. Covered in mud, he sat somberly in the mud, staring out into the rain. Frigid blasts of wind bombarded him cruelly. Think of something else, he told himself fervently. Take your mind off him. For a moment he amused himself by thinking of a terrified animal  a cat, maybe  being hit by a bolt of lightning and crumbling into a fine, black powder, but then, he thought, if a cat was really hit by lightning, the result would be a lot messier and not very funny.
It wasnt long before, inevitably, an image of a man, dressed in a black silk robe, his dark hair slicked back and tied into a ponytail, leapt into his mind. An odd feeling filled him  it was like a combination of anger and sadness that he had never felt before. Normally, he would have released the feeling (or at least the anger) by tearing something to shreds. Now, however, he was just too drained.
A sudden urge to disobey consumed James completely. Before he could even have time to consider what he was doing, he found himself crawling out into the backyard. His masters backyard. William Mayrick was a very rich man who owned all the land for miles around. He disappeared everyday to go to work, leaving James in the shadow of his large, well-kept mansion. James had no idea what was inside, or where Will went every day  but he never asked. Will had a short temper. James paused suddenly. What was he doing? He had just been kicked about like an animal and thrown out into the rain for disobeying, and now he was having another go at it.
It didnt stem the flow of his hatred towards William, however; on the contrary, it made him even angrier, and he plowed on towards the back door. Mud dripped off his body in a steady stream as he stood up and turned the door handle. He took one step when he realized to his horror that mud was dripping off his foot and onto the ground. If he went in, a trail of sludge would lead right to him. And yethe had come here to anger Will in the first place, hadnt he? If he were to, say, knock over his collection of books and rip them to shreds, Will would surely give him a horrid beating anyway. With a great spur of confidence, James leaped inside and ran off towards Wills library.
At least, he hoped he was heading that way. He had only been to the library once, where he had stolen some books to amuse himself out in his doghouse. He had had to learn to read all on his own, and it wasnt easy. Luckily, it was very late  undoubtedly William was lurking somewhere in his room, so James wouldnt have to worry about bumping into him. The hollow sound of his bare feet slapping the ground echoed off the walls, but other than that, everything was eerily silent, and when James stood still it covered him completely. There was also no light, but James was accustomed to darkness; Will was often depriving him of food and he would have to bound about Wills yard (which was uncannily large) at night, looking for things to eat, though there were few. He had already used up everything in the vegetable garden.
Finally! James thought. He looked around and saw, well, books. Books of all kinds, shapes and sizes  most of which Will never read. It was a mystery as to why he even had them, but James wasnt thinking about why Will had them, only how to cause as much damage as possible. He began by taking a particularly large book and tearing numerous pages out of it with relish. It wasnt long before books were lying everywhere, utterly destroyed. Up until that moment, James had really felt quite scared  breaking into Wills house in the dead of night and destroying his library, while leaving a trail clear as daylight to the scene of the crime took an extremely large amount of guts, which James just so happened to posses. Now, however, he felt a burning satisfaction that almost felt like an odd sort of happiness. He savored the thought of seeing Wills face, contorted with rage, as he tore apart Crimes and People Who Commit Them.
James sat down and looked around him; pages, bookmarks, and other things of the sort were lying everywhere, and he just couldnt deny himself a broad smile. He felt instantly refreshed and cheery, even though he knew it would cost him greatly. Maybe Ill try to run away again tonight, he thought, settling down on a large cushiony sofa. Ill never have to see Wills face again, so long as I live Filled with these thoughts, James didnt even realize how tired he was as he drifted off into a deep, contented sleep that he hoped would never end.
It wasnt before five in the morning that James suddenly woke up in horror. The feeling of grim satisfaction from the previous night left him immediately as he leapt to his feet. His heart was beating against his chest; he wouldnt be at all surprised if he broke a rib or two as he ran to the door out of the library. Sweat streamed down his face and tears stung his eyes when suddenly-
Bang!
-His shoulder collided painfully with the side of an empty bookshelf that splintered the polished mahogany. Pain coursed throughout his body, and stars exploded before his eyes. The bookshelf was rocking back and forth; he had to move out of the way or else hed get flattened; but then, Will would probably murder him anyway when he found out what happened
As the bookshelf fell, Jamess mouth fell open with horror  he rolled out of the way not a second too late as boards flew everywhere, along with stray splinters. If Will had lived in a neighborhood, everyone in it would surely be awake right now. James got shakily to his feet, his mouth was so dry it probably felt something like sandpaper. I have to hide myself, he thought fervently. He could already hear footsteps, Will was coming down the hall; the first thing James saw was the sofa  he seized his opportunity and leapt under it just as William burst into the room. It was in complete and total ruins  along with books laying everywhere, bent and ripped, there were also boards and splinters, walls with deep gouges in them, and rips in anything that could possibly be ripped. Time seemed to stop as Will stared from the doorway, and James from under the sofa, shaking with fear. And then
Come on out, James, Will said in a deceptively calm voice. At first James felt an insane urge to do as Will asked. Will surveyed the room once more. The marble tiles were covered in mud. I know youre in here, Will said, walking closer to the sofa, his lip pulling back into what was unmistakably a snarl. James squeezed his eyes shut. Please dont find me, he pleaded. Please dont!
But his pleas were for nothing. Will suddenly kicked back the red sofa, and James, who had been right under it, stared upward, mouth agape, unable to speak, or, for that matter, scream. Will smiled triumphantly. So, you decided you wanted revenge? he asked, gray eyes shining malevolently. I think Ill have some myself. James felt like he had turned to stone as Will grabbed him and pulled him to his feet. Just the sight of William Mayrick, standing there in his swirling black cloak, shirt, pants and boots made him feel sick. Will grabbed a large board from the floor, and before James could react, broke it over his head. The last thing he saw before he passed out was the evil look of triumph on Wills face as his victim crumpled in pain to the floor.
James woke up to the familiar smells of the great outdoors. To his surprise, he was out in the backyard once more. Standing slowly to his feet, he looked back at Wills mansion. The back door was bolted, so there was no chance of going through to the front of the house and escaping, and the great stone wall that surrounded the backyard was, of course, far too tall to climb. For a moment James just stood there, thinking of how lucky he was. Last time he had dared disobey Will, he had received ten whiplashes and a large cut above his eye, which were still fresh from a couple nights ago. Would Will punish him again when he returned home from work? Terrified, James crawled into his doghouse. Ill wait and see, he decided, preparing to sleep, disturbed though he was. He just hoped that Will wouldnt do something illegallike killing him, for instance
James didnt know if it was a relief or a disappointment when Will didnt show up later that evening. A bit perturbed, he settled back down into his doghouse and went back to sleep, a sleep riddled with nightmares.
The next day was just like the last, and Will didnt show up. James had become worried  he had always wondered why William had taken him in, and if his master had finally decided to just leave him there to rot. He had never given his parents a great deal of thought; he remembered them educating him, but for the most part everything was very foggy. He remembered some very odd things, though; like chasing cats, and sleeping outdoors
By the time Wednesday arrived, James was positively frightened. He was looking exceptionally gaunt and worn, having to eat raw eggs and strange roots. Hatred blazed like a raging fire in his eyes; the mansion was always overlooking him like some monstrous beast. Come out, James challenged Will silently. Fight like a man!
But it seemed that James had finally crossed the line. Exactly a week from his raid on the mansion, a knife was thrown at him from the highest window in his waking hours. It was as if someone had put out the fire in his eyes  he felt no more hatred, just blank emptiness. He felt very nostalgic, and often had dreams of his past. His parents faces flashed before his eyes, and those of two great, black dogs he must have had when he was younger.
He was hungry now, he was very hungry. James had spent the last couple of days lying out in the open. Just let me die, hed think. Have it end quickly Or at least, thats what he wanted to think, but he was too resistant  hed never give in willingly to death. It was almost like he could feel that it was a matter of hours before he was lost; he could feel his life ebbing away slowly. It was the most terrifying thing he had ever felt in his short life, which he was sure would end quickly. James rolled over, and there was a small thud! by his side. He rolled over again, and saw, to his amazement, a single apple lying on the ground. He made a move to grab it, but another something landed hard on his head. It was another apple. James was weak with happiness. He looked up at the wall surrounding him, waited a couple seconds
Thud!
Another apple landed by his side. James grinned broadly, but regretted it  his lips were so cracked and dry that blood was soon dotting the parched ground.
James felt like he had never felt in his life. Just sitting in the warm sun, eating apples, was pure bliss. He attacked all three with a will, even crunching down on the core. When he was finished, he sat back down and resumed staring into the sky. The warm sun slowly lulled him to sleep, of which he had been denied for many days
James was running. He was running faster than he ever dreamed he could. Everything was a blur of black and white; two shaggy black dogs were running beside him, one a male, the other a female. James looked at them and grinned; his teeth were elongated somehow, and his tongue flapped freely as he chased after a gray and white rabbit. His body was soaked in sweat, and somehow the sun seemed twice as hot as he thought it should.
For some reason, he didnt mind.
The rabbit was growing tired; a sense of victory filled James as he shot forward like a speeding bullet. Trees, bushes, flowers and grass rushed by as he splashed through a small puddle. As he did so, he felt a strong longing to stop for a moment and drink from it, until he realized that the two other dogs were running ahead of him. He put on an extra burst of speed when suddenly everything flared white
and slowly turned to darkness.
James opened his eyes slowly. It had been such a nice dreambut then, it had only been a dream Slowly, James stood up. Moonlight poured down on him from above. Another pile of apples was waiting by his side, and he launched himself upon them, crunching ravenously until nothing was left, not even the stems.
