### Ersuis · 2005-08-08 17:46 · #B242963

The Russian technology is good stuff; it is built to work almost no matter what happens to it. It just keeps going, which is part of the reason the Russian have been using Soyuz capsules for something like thirty or forty years now.
We're still using the Space Shuttle because it's all we have. We don't have any other manned space vehicles anymore except in museums. NASA is [updating the technology](http://www.nytimes.com/2005/08/02/science/space/02nasa.html?ex=1280635200&en=c97b63d7bb47af82&ei=5088&partner=rssnyt&emc=rss), though, to get rid of flaws in the Space Shuttle's design (namely the low weight limit for Shuttle cargo and placing the manned vehicle on top of, not beside, the engines).
Unfortunately, space is a hard thing to sell. Politicians like to say they support it, but when it comes down to it NASA just doesn't have enough budget to do much. Unfortunately, Mr. Bush hasn't really decided to do much about it either, trying to sell himself as the man who restarted the space program with outlandish trips to Mars and back to the Moon while not giving the agency more realistic goals. NASA's yearly budget is nowhere near what we'll spend this year waging war on Iraq. We can afford NASA and more space programs, but the people we elect choose not to. The debt doesn't matter with regards to funding for any agency in the United States.
The ESA has a nice space agency, and they also did a great job with putting the Huygens probe on Titan, where we're learning a ton of neat things. They're also able to launch quite a few satellites using their Arianne rockets launched from, I think, Equatorial Guinea. China is working on getting into space, as is India, I believe. There's a few other countries with space agencies also.
ESA, NASA, and the Russians all have great technology, and it'd be great if there was even more cooperation between them. We need a new multiagency project that's not useless like the ISS.
