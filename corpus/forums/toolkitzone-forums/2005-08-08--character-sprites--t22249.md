### venstor · 2005-08-09 16:43 · #B243325

They do need shading, but that's not the main problem.
I hope you don't take offense to what I'm going to say here, but the characters themselves all look generic. They all look like the same guy, standing stiff like a soldier, wearing a long-sleeved shirt and long pants. There are minor adjustments in what color of shirt and pants they're wearing, but they still all look like the same guy. (Even the girl) The ent is different, but he still just looks like a pine tree with a face.
![](http://www.toolkitzone.com/stuff/files/1710/dudeman1.gif)
A template is a valuable tool to have, especially for drawing a whole lot of NPC's. But you should really put a little work into individualizing your main characters. The way they stand should tell you something about their personalities. You should be able to look at a sprite and say, "That looks like an interesting character! I wonder what his story is?"
The key is to cram in as much detail as you can. It helps to draw the character out on paper away from your template so you can start to define some of his personality triaits visually.
Here, I've attempted to imbue your character sprites with some personality. I have no idea what your characters actual personalities are like, so I just made guesses:
![](http://www.toolkitzone.com/stuff/files/1710/dudeman2.gif)
And there you have it. I think a bit of work designing your characters prior to spriting them could make a whole lot of difference.

Ha ha! I spend a certain amount of time pixeling something every day. I was burnt out on my game that day, so I just spent it playing with your sprites.
Your new sprites are a lot better. Regarding the side view, it looks more like they're at an isometric 3/4 view than a true profile. Side views are really hard to do. You normally want it so that if the character is standing, you only see one eye, one arm, and one leg. Then when they start walking, the limbs that are on the other side of the body will swing into view.
