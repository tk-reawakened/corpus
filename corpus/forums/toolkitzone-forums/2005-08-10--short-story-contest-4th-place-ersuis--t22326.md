# Short Story Contest - 4th Place - Ersuis

### Xavier · 2005-08-10 16:12 · #B243828

Although the story topics themselves are locked, feel free to discuss entries with other members on this forum.
An excerpt from the journal of Andrew Aldersen:
I fear that this entry may be my last. This horrible dream that has haunted my soul for the past six months appear to be reaching a climax, but what that climax is not I or God Himself could tell you. Daily I pray to Him for the guidance to see me through these visions and the strength to face whatever is to come. The guidance does not come, and I grow ever more fearful of the phantasms that grapple with my oneiric self.
The dream has always started innocently enough. When I enter, I find myself sitting comfortably under a lonely ash tree on a hot summer day. The canopy above me rustles with a light, cool breeze, and I feel like a child again, lost in the pleasant moment. In the distance I can see the figure. He rides towards me on his stallion, and crosses the gulf from his place at the edge of infinity to mine beneath the tree in an instant.
As a common courtesy, I stand to meet the stranger. Looking up towards him, I struggle to find his eyes but see nothing beneath his hood. The dark robe he wears shrouds his face from the midday sun and casts a foreboding shadow on my form. He is a giant of a man, and his steed is taller still. The stallion snorts at my mortal fragility in disgust.
The stranger, with a pale hand outstretched, beckons me closer. I try to resist, as I have tried to resist every night, but I ultimately fail and find myself through some trick of reality on the horse. From this distance I can feel the coldness of the stallion from beneath me and the numbing chill emanating from its rider. I wrap my arms around his body, and the feel of a million tiny pin pricks explodes through my outstretched limbs. With a subtle kick, he coerces the horse into movement and on towards our final destination.
As we ride through eternity, galloping through infinite darkness, I often contemplate on the nature of this man. I struggle to call him a man, as he seems so inhuman compared to the creatures with which I am familiar. Is he really a man, sent in search of me for some cruel lord's unlawful purposes? Could he be a demon, spawned from evil and taking me towards some awful fate? Is he Death incarnate, manifested in this equestrian rider to remove me from the world of the living? Maybe this creature is God, the true form that we finite beings can never hope to understand or comprehend. My mind prods at the concepts and I realize that I will never know the truth of what this man is.
We reach our destination, and inky darkness surrounds us. This place we come to, there is no light to provide solace and comfort; there is only the heavy blanket of black to weigh down upon my now ever diminishing feeling of helplessness. Why am I here? I shout to the void, but no response is given. I notice the rider leaving as the sound of the stallion's hooves whisper through the darkness. I call out to him for help, but he does not return. Waiting in the dark with only the thump of my beating heart, I can quickly start to feel the Presence.
No words can adequately describe what the Presence is. The abject fear gnaws at your heart, damaging your will to continue life. It consumes your soul, leaving you a helpless shell of a man alone in the dark. The being that creates this feeling is never seen, but I know it is there. I can hear its raspy breath in my mind, and feel its eyes boring through my body. It can see in this abyss, I am sure of it.
Only recently have I reached this point in the dream, but I am shaking with fear as I write this. The Presence of the creature haunts me even in the daylight, continually digesting my spirit while the sun tries in vain to wash away traces of the nightmare. I am afraid of the Presence, of what it has done to me.
I find myself restless and ill at ease. My concentration is diminished, and my thoughts are scattered when I can think of anything besides the awful touch of my nightmares. I cannot eat without my body rejecting the nutrition required to keep myself alive. I have subsisted this week on a meager diet of bread and sips of water. My body and soul are weak and numb. My thoughts stray towards suicide, but my hand shakes too much to hold a blade. It is only through strained effort on my part that this, my final annal, is written.
I wake from my dreams sweating and cold. Often I hallucinate that the statue I keep above my bed glows with a strange inner light. There is no color, but I can feel the fluorescence pulsing in my body. The hallucination eventually subsides, and I drift back into an uneasy, dreamless sleep, but before I do the irrational part of my mind toys with the idea of destroying the statue in order to gain control over this shadow world I seem bound to.
The statue itself is a mystery, a worn, weathered form of an obese creature carved from stone. It is a strange piece, and I have inquired several times with anthropologists at the local university as to the origin of the piece. They are as clueless as to its nature, and only offer hypotheses of shamanistic cults, strange gods, and its supposed role as a magical item of importance to the tribe.
The stone itself is even more mysterious, being seven billion years old and obviously not of this planet. How could such a stone ever arrive on a tiny mote of dust as insignificant as Earth? An alien stone, drifting through the reaches of outer space to arrive and torment the being of those who possess it.
Even now, my thoughts are running towards madness. The idea of pounding the figurine with a hammer fills me with glee, but I cannot do such a thing; my body is weak and my mind is tiring. Perhaps if I survive another day, I will attempt it, but my hope has vanished for survival beyond tonight's dreamland excursion.
My hand aches and I cannot write much more legibly. The fear of what will happen to me is upon me as heavy as when I started, but there is nothing more that I can do for myself. I shall be consumed by this fear and mania that wrestles with my psyche, or be destroyed by the creature that inhabits the state between rest and consciousness. I must rest my weary body and mind. Perhaps this time I will find peace.
