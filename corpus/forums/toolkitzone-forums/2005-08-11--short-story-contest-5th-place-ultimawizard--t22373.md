# Short Story Contest - 5th Place - UltimaWizard

### Xavier · 2005-08-11 10:54 · #B244317

Although the story topics themselves are locked, feel free to discuss entries with other members on this forum.
The evening heat was radiating from the stones of the Nolen Mountains where the riders were coming from. Their steeds, tough, oxen-like beast known as Gashnakil, were dripping sweat from their brown fur like water from trees after a rain. With a subtle signal, the leader on his tan-colored mount motioned the party to a halt. Clearly, there was some trouble ahead.
Andreal Nalthamian, a priestess and accomplished mage who was accompanying the envoy from the elven village of Althimbril to the north, beyond the Nolen Range, was alert. She was the lone human among the partys elves, and by both races standards, she was quite beautiful. She had long, blonde hair, emerald green eyes, and a slight, curved build belying her warriors training that priestesses were obliged to undergo. Her sky-blue gown rustled in the breeze as she began chanting a scouting ward to see what was the matter. The elves were doubtless disgusted at the seemingly unnecessary noise, but it was necessary, so they made no comment.
When the ward was finished, she felt it shudder, as if under a great physical impact like from a battering ram, and then shatter completely. Clearly whatever was now hunting them had magical powers equal to or even greater than her ownpossibly even greater than the partys collective magic power, which was something to balk at.
With silent elven sign language, the priestess signed to the rest of the party what had happened, which direction the attack had come from, and warned maximum caution. The partys warriors, all elves with long goldoon pikes took up defensive positions as trembling vibrations became noticeable, the Gashnakil snorting with their tusked mouths in terror.
Andreal hadnt been at all aware of the threat the party was facing until it burst past the rock formation to their eastthe opposite of the direction she had sensed it from. The warriors turned about and attacked fearlesslyand in vain. The green-and-gold armored elves in Goldoon vambraces stood no chance against the monster that beset them. It took three heartbeats to slaughter all 30 warriors, and the rest of the envoy.
Andreal had heard tales of Earth Elementals, and had learned Earth elemental magic during her mages training, but this was a foe that was a match for an army of mages, warriors, catapaults, and the resources of two nations together. Supposedly, an Earth Elementals command over terrain and flora and environment could turn anything into an enemy, could turn any advantage into a weakness, and could decimate any foeeven a Priestess of the Great Redeemer, who was trained in Elemental Magic as well as Divine Magic.
The priestess was therefore aware that her elemental spells were of little use against the behemoth, seemingly man-shape, but made of boulders with vines holding the limbs together, and iron, mythryl, and goldoon claws and spikes emanating from the shoulders, feet, toes, and head. Also, Divine Magic would do little to halt an attack, as she was still learning Divine Magic, and her most useful spell would be a physical defensive wardand the creature had already demonstrated its skill at dissolving enchantments.
Andreal pulled a small mythryl rod from her gown and with practiced fluidity and grace, flicked the rod. It swelled and grew into the weapon the females of her noble house had wielded for centuries, since the motherrealm had been established: Sheeons Fury, a gigantic spear, forged with elemental, archaic, Dark, and Divine magic. It was a truly impressive weapon with a Genjitite handleGenjitite being an alloy of Mythryl and Goldoon, two of the strongest elements in existencewith a thrice-forged Goldoon head. Along the shaft of the spear were runes with spells of strength and magical power, some being protective wards for the user, some even bolstering the users physical condition, along with seemingly decorative crenellations in the metal, carefully crafted spirals, giving the spear a sort of organic look. Of course, these crenellations were actually more wards, just crafted into gigantic size to reinforce their power and presence in the users mind.
Andreal shouted the Nathalmian familys war cry, and using some of her now-present magic augmentations, jumped straight at the Elemental. Clearly thinking the pesky human being another fly he could simply swat away, he did soonly to swat thin air.
The Priestess had used another ability granted by the spear to evade the attack she knew was coming: the Double Jump, the ability to jump in midair, as if from the ground. The Elemental was confused, but quickly sensed Andreals positiononly a second too late. The enchanted Goldoon blade sliced deep into the Elementals skull, a boulder of magnetic iron ore, and the great being released a cry of pain. Andreal tried to pull the magical implement, but she had buried it too deeply, and would have to find another way to get it out if she hoped to wield it again. She again double jumped onto a nearby foothill, where she began to cast an elemental spell with the necessary speed and precision, making smaller movements than was common for spellcasters to make so as to speed along the process.
The Elemental tried to rip the enchanted weapon from its skullthen gave up, and came on, undaunted. Clearly, it wasnt suffering from the spears bite, but was just annoyed and angry now. While intelligent and in tune with the environment around it, and deeply in tune with the mana currents flowing from the earth to the sky to it to the lone human who opposed it, its rage blinded it to the humans spell.
Andreal finished casting. A spark of fire appeared at the Julaq vine joining the beings right leg to its lower torso. The fire spread along the vine with astonishing pace. The creature, now realizing too late its rage had stopped it from using its elemental powers to halt the fire, tried to employ them, but the Priestess had finished casting a new spella great spike of ice, sharp as a sword split the left legs joining in two, and the monsters leg, now devoid of the magical force holding it to the behemoth crashed to the earth sending up a plume of soil and insects.
Thus far, The Great Redeemers Emissary on Earths plan was working flawlessly. Shed quickly cast two elemental spells, and had badly hurt the creature. Knowing she couldnt use the Double Jump technique without physical possession of the spear, she was forced to cast as she walked towards the downed creatures great head to retrieve the ancient weapon embedded in it. She began a new spell
As the creature regained its felled leg. The parts of the leg that were seemingly sundered from the body again rejoined together, then rejoined with the Earth Elementals body. Using more elemental force, the titan extinguished the fires on its damaged right leg with a plume of dirt which disrupted Andreals spell. The creature then used its elemental talents to clear a window through the dirt cloud so it could see its opponent and crush it.
Andreal was getting worried. Shed lost her most powerful weapon, and the only weapon that could hurt the abberation in front of her in the first round, shed distracted the titan, but couldnt weaken it further with an eroding blast of water, as shed hoped to do, and now, the Elemental was looking straight at her, no expression on its great head, doubtless charging a blast that would destroy the Earth beneath herand her with it. She quickly ranged over her options, and her mental list of her supplies. She had healing draughts, but none of those would help if she was dead. She had no more talismans, not thinking shed need them in lieu of Sheeons Fury, and was praying to the Great Redeemer to save her.
Sadly, those thoughts were the last she ever had.
