# Short Story Contest - 2nd Place - Moonews

"Voluta de los Magos" by Matthew Elias
It's hot and dry as hell. My horse is breathing heavy, every bit as
exhausted as I am lost. With my left hand I clench a bloody hole in my
chest that sucks in the dry, sandy air of the desert each time I loosen
my grip on it. My right hand is deep into the pocket of my duster,
clenching the very reason I'm in this mess. A couple of pieces of paper
rolled up, tied and labeled with a small paper tag reading "Voluta de
los Magos". My horse continues on his way, unphased by his fatigue and
our current situation, while I sit dumbstruck. I thought this was easy
money, after all who would miss a couple of pieces of paper? Besides, I
had to try it out just once. Being a real mercenary I mean.
Later in the week I had received my orders, I arrive at my destination
and my prior question answers itself in expert clarity as gunfire burst
out the door almost quicker then those firing. Their shots were frantic
and senseless, I was quick enough to draw and take out the first two
before I took one in the chest. The smaller one had run out of ammo
long before I blew the other two away, I must've caught him in the
shoulder by the way he was grabbing himself. I spent only a moment in
dread at what I had done before I started for the door when the little
one let out the most assailing howl I've ever heard. He lunged at me,
knife in hand all the while gushing like a geiser from his wound and
screaming some Mexican mess my way. I gave him a good knock on his
cheek but that didn't seem to settle him down at all. He just threw his
knife to the ground, grabbed his face and let out a horrible bloody
roar. I drew my gun and aimed but he just kept on screaming that
horrible scream, fully aware of the now present .45 pointing in his
direction. I was terror-stricken and breathless at what I was looking
at. He was cut down at that second, not fit to live the rest of his
life even if the chicken liver were to survive that little scratch I
gave him. I could tell you just by looking at him in his state and with
ace experience in life and death that he was finished at the very
second he realized that I'd be riding off with those papers. It had to
be those papers. Parts like these, nobody has any regard for any life
their own or otherwise. But this young man of maybe fifteen years had a
reason to live, and it sure as hell isn't to smell this lovely desert
air. I did what I had to do.
The longer I hold tight to these seemingly valueless pieces of paper
and think of the twenty two thousand waiting for me back in Amarillo
the worse I feel about what I have done and what I'm about to do. The
worse I feel, the tighter my grip becomes. Something about the civil
and secure grimace the man who is paying me to do this had when he gave
me the mission tells me that he knew I'd leave be leaving with those
papers. Something about how those those men I had just killed hours
before were on their toes and ready to die for these papers tells me
that they knew somebody was coming for it. And something about the
capacious barracks I had retrieved these papers from tells me that I
have at least a dozen men on my tail. Everything about this mission
stinks to high heaven and God's wrath is gonna be waiting on my hide
when he smells it.
Two days pass. Two days of sleepless nights and blazing sun baked
days. The more I think, the lighter my grip on the papers becomes and
the more paranoid my trigger finger gets. I find my self stopping my
horse in it's tracks and taking a nice long listen at nothing but
desert wind and buzzards. I could be back in Amarillo within the next
few days but my supplies are good for much longer and I need to be as
careful as possible. After what I've done I know there are men after
me, and I know I won't be able to sleep soundly for the rest of my
life, but these men aren't nearly as smart as they are steadfast. The
middle desert isn't an ideal place for a fair skinned fellow like
myself to spend more then two weeks going the complete opposite
direction of my destination, even if it is the best route for my
situation. Knowing that, they assumed I went the direct route thinking
they'd go the opposite. That aside, I'm still not enjoying my little
vacation one bit. While my wound puts me in no immediate danger I am
going to need to get professional help as soon as possible, and soon as
possible isn't soon enough. I'm in one hell of a bad position.
I grow weaker every day. My final thoughts remain on the man who's to
blame for this. I'm such an ass for trusting that stale smile of his.
The twenty two thousand dollars, wherever that may be coming from if it
even existed in the first place, seems about as distant as it is
meaningless in my final hour. I sag off my horse and onto the heated
sand and say my final goodbyes. I fire off the last of my bullets which
is more then enough to send my faint-hearted steed well on his way
otherwhere. I roll onto my back and stare into the sun. I feel like
some abominable villain welcoming the flames of hell. I still haven't
the slightest clue about what I'd done, but I do know it was horrible.
I also know that although I lay here where I will undoubtedly die, at
least be able see that old bastard one more time.
