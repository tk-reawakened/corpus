# Short Story Contest - 3rd Place - Ryukenshen

### Xavier · 2005-08-15 11:48 · #B245986

Although the story topics themselves are locked, feel free to discuss entries with other members on this forum.
Chapter I: The Pendant in the Plumbing
Kyle pushed a strand of dark hair out of his eyes and bent over his book, lips moving silently. His bed groaned under the weight of piles of old volumes and scrolls, some of them left open, pages fluttering in the breeze coming in from the open window. His eyes were darting back and forth in an ocean-blue blur as he read:
The Water Conjuring Spell is a form of magic simple enough to learn. The incantation is spoken within the depths of the users mind. If said with feeling and deliberation, it can be used to control the water within the air. But be warned: if pronounced incorrectly or said without spirit, the caster may find himself horribly dehydrated. (If dehydration occurs, turn to page 397 for the Replenishing Spell).
Incantation: Aquatius
Making a mental note to try out the spell the next chance he got, Kyle flicked through to the 397th page, flattened out the slightly crumpled parchment, and found the incantation Riquentis written beside the emboldened words, Replenishing Spell. Heaving a great sigh, Kyle closed the book carefully, gathered up the others, and shoved them unceremoniously into a large, wooden chest in the corner of his room before locking it with a little brass key, which he hid in the drawer of his desk. Stifling a yawn, he walked back over to his untidy bed, but not before glancing into the mirror hanging up beside it.
Perhaps it was simply because the mirror was so cracked and grimy, surrounded by peeling wallpaper, but his reflection looked strangely withered and depressed. Though he was only sixteen, flecks of gray streaked across his otherwise brown hair, and his complexion was somewhat darker than average. In fact, the only part of him that did not look as though it were wilting prematurely was his eyes. They seemed to glow palely against the rest of his face and were strangely bright. Shaking his head and sighing once more, Kyle flicked the light switch, hopped into bed, and pulled the covers all the way up to his nose. His breathing became deeper until it had sunk into soft snores. And then
The world around him in a blurry haze, Kyle darted out of his house and slicked back his devoid-of-gray hair before running down Sheep Road. He was sure his father had run down this waybut why, why had he looked so distressed? And  Kyle squinted  what were all those cars doing, all stopped in the middle of the road? Had there been an accident? Curiosity roused, Kyle walked toward the intersection, wondering what this was all about. It didnt look like an accidentthere were no damaged carsbut everyone was crowded around something  a person? Horrified, Kyle rushed forward; people were screaming, angry drivers were honking their horns irritably, unable to see what was going on In a dreamlike trance, Kyle approached the crowd and struggled through the mass of people.
A second later, he wished he hadnt.
There, lying on the ground, clouded eyes wide open, lay a beautiful woman, hair tangled, dress torn, though otherwise completely unscathedunless you counted the fact that she was dead. Kyle just stared, mouth hanging open, before he started to back away, shaking his head, trying not to believe, willing it not to be true
Mom? he whispered.
There was no answer.
Kyle couldnt, wouldnt believe it He refused to, hed never admit to it, she was just asleep, shed wake up tomorrow, hed find her down in the kitchen, cooking breakfast, talking with his dad, who would be sitting at the table, reading his newspaper
But then the truth began to sink in: she was dead. Elizabeth Perry, mother of Kyle Perry and wife of Marcus Perrywas gone.
It was as if his mind had switched off and some instinct had taken over. Kyle turned around and ran, knocking over frightened bystanders, struggling to get away  far away  somewhere his father couldnt find him  where he could cry without anyone seeing, where he could hide when he didnt want to be found Then  an elm tree! The branches were low down on the trunk  he could climb this easily
Leaping onto the first branch and taking hold of the next, Kyle began to hoist himself upbut thenwhat was that cracking noise?
In a moment Kyle was lying in a crumpled heap on the ground. In the split second before the large bough came crashing down upon his head, Kyle thought he saw a large, emerald-studded pendant lying on the ground next to him His mothers, the one she always 
Crash.
* * *
The next morning, Kyle woke up with a grunt, wiped a trickle of drool from the side of his mouth, flattened down his long, dark hair, and leapt out of bed. Not bothering to tidy his bed or pull on a fresh set of clothes, he kicked the door open and walked out of the room.
The dream was still haunting him. Almost every night he revisited the day his mother had died mysteriously, and, though he would kill himself to admit it, he was slightly glad of this. True, it made him downright depressed and, as his father said, it gave his eyes a deadened look, but it provided him with a relatively clear depiction of his mothers emerald pendant. Looking back ten years ago, when she was still alive, Kyle could not remember a single instance in which she wasnt wearing her pendant. He had since come to the conclusion that it had been taken from her by her killer and discarded for reasons which Kyle had to admit he did not know, but that brought up a couple other interesting questions: who had killed her and how had he done it?
The strangest thing about his mothers death was the fact that nobody had any idea how it had happened, though Kyle had a hunch. As soon as he had woken up after having been knocked out by the tree branch, he had ran back to his house and searched his mothers room, hoping fervently to find some sort of comfort there, or perhaps some kind of something that might reveal the source of her mysterious death. The only thing he found, though, was a large wooden chest, pushed into the corner of her closet. And when he opened it up with the little brass key he found in a crack between the floorboards, what should he find but a pile of spell books and scrolls.
Every day after that he pored over them by night, his decorative dresser shielding the big wooden chest from view, which he had pushed into the corner of his room. They were very interesting books, to say the least, and he was eager to try out many of the things he learned on various objects. What he did even more of, however, was wonder if his mother had been some sort of sage, and whether the pendant she wore was connected to that somehow. It seemed logical to think that she had known a healthy amount of magic herself, and also his father, Marcus, as well. They shared the same bedroom and closet, after all, and he doubted that she wouldnt tell her own husband about something so important as the ability to control the water in the air, though Kyle never asked Marcus about it. For one thing, he hated talking about his mother; it made him distinctly uncomfortable. For another, even though he didnt like to admit it to himself, he was a little afraid of his fathers reaction.
Kyle ran down the stairs three at a time, strode through the living room and entered the kitchen to find his father sitting at the table, eating a plate of fried eggs. Kyle gave him half a smile before sitting down opposite him and pouring some porridge into a clean bowl.
The kitchen was the only room that Kyle used as a substitute for the living room besides his own. The very air smelt of various spices and peppers, and the ticking of the clock in conjunction with the warm, smelly air made everyone present feel an immediate sense of pleasant drowsiness. Then there was the fact that it looked just so homely; every bit of furniture was homemade by Marcus and extremely comfortable. The windows were beautiful stained glass, and all the cupboards had carvings of pastures full of grazing cows and farmers.
Having finished eating, Marcus had put his feet up on a footstool and was peering intently at Kyle over the top of a newspaper. Though Kyle tried to eat his porridge and ignore the eyes boring into the top of his head, there was no excusing the loud, consistent throat clearing going on at the other side of the table.
What? he asked, a little more rudely than he had intended. Marcus chuckled dryly, folded the newspaper, and raised his eyebrows, stroking his moustache thoughtfully.
Wellits about that fight you had in the park with the boy that was trying to bully you. I went to talk to the boys parents.
Kyle tried to ignore the fact that his heart was beating twice as fact, or that his hands were beginning to shake Hoping fervently that Marcus hadnt noticed, Kyle bent over much lower than necessary and resumed eating his porridge with alarming speed.
Do you admit to grabbing this boy  Brutus  on his left arm?
Yeah, Kyle said quietly. But you would too if someone were going to punch you in the face, right? You cant blame me for that.
No, I cant, Marcus said with the shadow of a smile flitting across his face, But I can, however, blame you for the fact that there are five scorch marks on his arm.
Kyle laughed a loud, unconvincing laugh and tried to arrange his face into an amused expression, his head full of angry protests he was longing to shout. That wasnt my fault! I didnt mean to do it!
Scorch marks? Kyle asked, still smiling his fake, amused smile. If he dropped a match or something on himself, well, thats not my fault, right?
Marcus frowned, staring hard at Kyle, a steely glint in his eye.
Youve been using magic, havent you?
What? No  I mean, theres no such thing 
I want you to tell me, Marcus said loudly, drowning out Kyles feeble stuttering, Whether or not youve been using magic.
Kyle felt a hard lump in is throat; Marcus knew. He had known, all this time. Kyle was sure of that now.
I
Marcus raised his eyebrows expectantly.
N-no, I havent
But Kyle knew Marcus didnt believe it. Not for a second.
* * *
Hey, Kyle, over here!
Kyle looked around, grinned, and immediately ran down the sidewalk, stopping at a little wooden sign that read, Lion St. Standing next to this sign was another boy, short, pale and trying to force a smile onto his face, although he was looking a little morose.
Hiya, Kyle I havent seen you all week, whereve you been?
Oh, said Kyle, momentarily taken aback. Er, wellIve beenspelunking.
He didnt know why he had said itall he knew was that it wasnt true.
During the past week, he had been hiding away in his fathers old treehouse, a couple miles away from the house, hidden in the forest. In that small sanctuary he had been practicing various forms of magic, away from prying eyes. But, of course, he wasnt about to tell anyone.
You have? Really? Why?
Umhowve you been, Pete? Kyle asked quickly.
Oh, Im alright, Peter muttered, and a his cheeks turned a bright pink. But my moms a bitwellsick.
AhIm sorry to hear it.
Peter quickly looked in the other direction, and Kyle had the distinct impression that he was silently shedding tears. Kyle turned the other way as well.
It was really a gloomy day; the roads were still wet from the little rain shower in the middle of the night, and a thick fog was hovering over the ground. All the trees seemed as if they were melting; the leaves were soggy and drooping, branches dipped down nearly to the ground. This did nothing to improve Kyles mood.
Was itreally horrible when youre mom died, Kyle? asked Peter, voice barely audible, lips hardly moving. Kyle looked at him, startled.
I  well, yeah, of course, butI meanI got over it, right?
I guess I will too, then Peter murmured, looking thoroughly depressed.
Listen, Kyle said bracingly, you cant be sure shell die. These things happen when you least expect itand just remember that you can never be prepared for something you cant see coming.
As he said it, Kyle could feel his face grow warm, but he didnt care. Peter had never experienced such a loss, and, though he knew he would never be able to cheer him up, he could at least give him fair advice.
Thanks, Kyle, Peter said with half a smile, tears slipping down his face, not bothering looking away. Kyle just smiled back.
The gloomy weather improved greatly on Kyles walk back to his house. Though a wall of clouds shielded the sun, the density of the fog diminished somewhat and the temperature rose a fair amount of degrees. True, it was a little more humid than entirely desirable, but it was, at least, better than the bitter cold. Finally Kyle was standing in front of Number 12, Sheep Road. He walked up to the door, seized the handle, but then stopped suddenly, turned around, and walked to the back of the house. With a grunt, Kyle managed to hoist himself up onto the brick wall that circled the house. It was very old; vines were growing unchecked all over its surface and the mortar was crumbling in various places, but it held his weight. Muttering the words Volar Eximenti! under his breath, he took a springing leap and was just barely able to grasp the end of his windowsill with his fingertips. Then, with another grunt, he pushed open the window with one hand before scrambling through his window, into his room.
He could have used the front door, of course, but there was a lot less risk of being caught by his father and having to be interrogated about his fight with Brutus. He knew that it wouldnt be long until his dad returned to the attack; when Marcus wanted to find out something, he rarely gave up until he succeeded.
Retrieving his key from his desk drawer, Kyle unlocked the hidden chest and swept up as many books as he could carry as quietly as possible. He knew that he hadnt been very creative in hiding the spell books, but his father never came into his room; instead he let Kyle take care of it himself. True, he would occasionally poke his head in to check on how the state of things was, but the chest, hidden behind the dresser, was quite invisible to the naked eye from the doorway.
Tottering slightly with him arms full of books, Kyle managed to get his legs out of the window. Then, as carefully as possible, he began to slide outside.
Of course, climbing through a window fifteen feet above the ground, holding a pile of books and not even worrying about the brick wall below you isnt a smart thing to do. Kyles spell books went flying everywhere as he fell over sideways onto the brick wall before falling off that and landing on the hill on the other side, where he rolled a couple feet before stopping. Wincing, Kyle struggled to his feet and began to gather up his books. By the time they were all back in his arms, somehow feeling heavier than they had before, Kyle was already walking (with a bit of a limp) towards the forest that was only a couple miles away from his housetowards the tree house.
Kyle dropped all his books in a heap on the rough, wooden floor as soon as he had found the tree house. Then he just leaned up against the ladder and looked around sadly.
He couldnt ask for any place better. Not only did he have the cover of trees aplenty, but he also had the most pleasant atmosphere one could possibly ask for. The little river that ran behind the tree house was very useful; if he ever needed to use magic that involved fire, well, there was a never-ending supply of water for him. Often little woodland creatures would approach him, unafraid of the strange human that could make an acorn grow legs and walk away, and just watch plaintively. As for the trees, they were the best of all. As it has already been noted, they help to hide the tree house. The special thing about them was their leaves, and the fact that they were a dark blue. Kyle had no idea what kind they could possibly be, but when the light filtered through them it had a remarkable effect.
The problem was that he had suffered too much in life to not know that more misery would be coming soon. Marcus, his very own father, knew that Kyle was learning to spell cast. Kyle couldnt keep lying to him forever. And, of course, the day that he had found the spell books was a day that marked him as a person that would never lead a normal life, no matter how much he tried. Was it really worth all this?
No, Kyle admitted to himself, but, as he looked down at his books, he knew that it was too late to turn back now. Heaving a great sigh, Kyle picked up a large, red volume; the one he had been reading last night. Flipping to the right page and tracing a finger down the list of spells he came across Aquatius, but then bit his lip. If said with feeling and deliberation, it can be used to control the water within the air Yeah, but what then? How is the water controlled? If pronounced incorrectly or said without spirit, the caster may find himself horribly dehydrated. He could use with more details on that However, Kyle closed his eyes, spoke the incantation, and then opened his eyes, willing something to happenand it did.
Slowly little droplets of water formed in midair and began to join together. Kyle found, to his delight, that controlling it was all too easy. He could move it, make the water gather more quickly, even make it stop dead in midair. Finally, after ten minutes of fun, he turned his attention once again to his books. The water fell on the floor with a splash.
He had learned long ago that spell casting was not restricted to using one single spell for a particular purpose. Indeed, he had laughed many a time at the silly wizards and sages in childrens fiction books, who said words from a completely made-up language to open unopenable doors and turn dragons into teacups. The language that could make real magic was a simple branch of Latin, and the spells never had definite purposes. Kyle found that mixing spells and types of magic was a much more efficient practice. Volar, for example, was a spell that caused an object to be lifted. Eximenti, however, is used to push forward. Combine the two and, as observed above, and you can make anything you want fly forward through the air. Furthermore, had Kyle the desire to stay aloft, all he would have to do was add on a couple other spells, and he could easily mutter other words to change the direction of his movement.
Kyle took several books and transferred them to a pile of them that he had already read through, but he stopped, holding the books inches away from the used pile, staring downward. Lying innocently on the tree house floor was a little blue book with a tattered cover, with no title or writing of any kind on the front. Intrigued, Kyle flipped it over  still, there was nothing.
But, as he rifled through the first five pages, he discovered that it was just another spell book that he must not have noticed before in the big wooden chest. Sighing, he gathered up all the books once more and began to walk back through the forest to his house. Still, something was bothering himsomething that had nothing at all to do with the large purple bruise on his shoulder. So lost in thought was he that Kyle didnt even notice he had wandered into farmer McDors paddock, and was chased out of it by the farmers fierce bay horse, Syrup.
Finally, back at his house, Kyle walked up to the front door, once again, but still didnt open it. Instead he reached down and removed a rather large flagstone in front of the front steps. Underneath was a giant hollow, large enough for a grown man to sit down in comfortable. He quickly dumped his books into it, covered the hole and reached out once more to open the door, but, for the third time, did not open. He hesitated, then ran instead around to the back of his house and jumped onto the brick wall. Hed face his dad tomorrow
Kyle crossed the upstairs hall in a haze of sleepiness, thinking dimly that hed wash up before bed going to bed, regardless of whether or not he had eaten lunch or dinner. Entering the bathroom and trying his best to ignore his wilting reflection, Kyle turned on the hot water tap and held out his hands under it expectantly. The problem was that nothing was coming out. Not a single drop. Instead, a loud rumbling issued from the basin! Horrified, Kyle tried to turn it off, but the tap was stuck. The rumbling was growing in volume  it sounded like the entire sink was going to explode 
Then, with an almighty scraping squeal, the grill covering the drainpipe burst off and flew halfway across the room. Seconds after, water shot out of the sink  from the drainpipe.
There was nothing Kyle could do. He frantically tried to cover it with his hands, but to no avail  he even tried to get into the basin and sit on it, but all he got was a stinging rear-end. Soaked and rubbing his rump, Kyle was forced to back away and simply watch.
Then, without warning, a giant, golden something burst from the sink, landed with a loud thud at Kyles feet, and suddenly the water stopped. Mystified, Kyle picked it up, and saw, to his amazement, that he knew what this something was. He had seen it every single night for the past ten years
It was his mothers emerald set pendant. Though it was covered in grime and looked distinctly tarnished, there was no mistaking it. No mistaking it at all.
