Jeff Spicer
At least Brandle could think in the desert. The unicorn was almost dead, but he had Hope he could revive it. To complicate matters, the shishleaks were forming raiding parties--small skirmishes in the villages and ambushes along the forest roads. It was his flight from those furry humanoids that had brought him to the desert in the first place. He knew he was safe here, but only for awhile. The shishleak populace would gain momentum. And while he knew they would never venture into the desert themselves, he could clearly foresee some unscrupulous merchant discovering the horned steed in his care--or worse, Hope itself.
Brandle held Hope incarnate in a metal rose. And it was from this rose that every other force on his world flowed. If there was no love in the hearts of men, there was still Hope. If disaster befell known civilization, he stilled held Hope in his hand. But if the world knew that he possessed the awesome power of this relic--if he lost Hope... He shuddered to think of it.
He knew he had to do something, and the desert gave him space to think. But the desert is a steady-handed teacher, whose lessons do not change, whose contingencies are but one. Brandle knew what he had to do. And so, between the clarity of a crystal-blue sky and the shimmering plains of sand, he held Hope in one hand and with the other sacrificed his unicorn, Innocence, with its own horn.
Several days had passed--days of wandering, the metal thorns of Hope dripping blood from his hand into the desert floor. It was an anguishing journey, a journey of birthpangs, a journey of dillusion and mirage. And then there was the man in the desert, and the awful war. Was that himself? Did he fight with himself? There were thousands of them. They just kept coming--hordes and hordes--and each one of them looked like Brandle. He fought them with his full resolve and killed every last one of them. Did he know who he was anymore? But in the end, when the dust had settled, Brandle managed to pull from Hope, Faith--the war-tested horn of Innocence. And at that moment, War--the inevitable incarnation of Innocence--approached through the desert haze, a mighty blood-red steed.
Brandle mounted the beast and flew off to confront the shishleak menace. He knew that by then they had formed war parties of rank
and order. They were growing--and organizing--exponentially. No doubt, their commanders of Fear and Chaos had already transmorgraphied from within the ranks. He only hoped that Death had not yet come. But even if it had, he still had Hope.

Quint had always enjoyed a simple life, though not really by his own choosing. Throughout his otherwise normal boyhood spent on a vast, secluded plot of farmland with his field-working parents, Quint had never been able to indulge in the exuberant energy of youth - he had been born with lame legs. His home, the pleasant, spacious family heirloom where hed lived since birth, was to him a prison that taunted with stairs going up and down, and a clear view of their property through large, open windows.
Although it was, in the beginning, all wondrous to him, he had always envied more than anything the horses and their riders. The galloping, graceful giants had forever mesmerized and enchanted him, and on the uncommon occasion that his ageing parents could remove him from the house, Quint would spend all of the time talking to the horses, watching them frolic in the waving reeds and grasses of the rolling hillside, feeding them and brushing their glistening hair at night until they slept.
But years and years of days spent in longing to venture into that view beyond the window had slowly but surely soured Quints wanderlust into bitterness towards all things out of doors, and when the boy became a man, with no one to carry him up and down or help him to the outside, he remained a recluse.
Years passed, his home falling into more and more disrepair along with Quints psyche and body.
As Quints heart grew emptier and colder, the rustic, quaint exterior of the cozy little house grew more and more derelict, the white-painted wood beginning to rot; as his body became atrophied and weak, crushed under the weight of his despair, the once-proud aluminum of the roof above him began to buckle and twist. Fewer and fewer people dropped by as he made it clear that his interests lied only in his own misery.
One day, from the weakening wheelchair where hed spent many a lonely day and night staring out his window, he heard a sound that over many years of silence had become quite unfamiliar to his ears- the doorbell. Its chime awoke in him a flood of memories long forgotten, and for some reason, after a moments trepidation, he wheeled himself hesitantly toward the source of the sound. With a shaking hand and an uncertain heart, he eased the rickety, squealing door open, peering through the widening crack. He was sure he would remember what people looked like, and, upon seeing no one standing at his door, the half of him that was not alarmed was relieved.
Hello? HELLO? he called out into the rainy afternoon air. No answer came. Furious at this disturbance, Quint turned to leave when something on the ground caught what little light there was that day, and caught his eye.
What... what is this? he croaked aloud, his voice strained from years of neglect. Stockily, with a hand to brace his contorted back, he bent over to pick up the dark, metal semicircle of an object. It was heavy and cold in his palms as he contemplated it, but surprisingly not wet from the rain. Upon further inspection, he saw that it was just a plain-looking, nail-dented horseshoe.
A horseshoe? he barked with incredulity. Who leaves a horseshoe on some strangers porch? Just as I suspected, a waste of time it was to come out here.
He flung the horseshoe out of sight with distaste, not caring to watch where it landed. But when it did, the sound it made as metal looped around metal again and again caught his attention. The horseshoe had just landed around an impossibly small, broken fencepost.
Quint was astonished. Hed never had good aim, as he hadnt cared to develop an ability he could never use. But the accuracy was incredible - how had he done it?
He sat stunned a moment, before rationalizing that it didnt matter anyway. He wasnt about to go pick it up out of the rust-covered muck and filth that surrounded the house, and decided it would be best just to forget it and go inside.
But as he turned to head back into the house, a blinding white light from outside shot up like a beacon from the horseshoes landing spot and into the sky, piercing the clouds. Quint spun in his seat, shielding eyes wide with awe, as the light enveloped the house. He followed it to the top with his gaze to find that a blue-maned, white-haired horse was descending motionlessly from the sky onto the spot the horseshoe had landed.
It set down elegantly and noiselessly, mane settling perfectly back into place. Even Quint had to admit to himself, in his presence was a truly magnificent animal. The spotless body that shone despite the rain, the dark, gleaming eyes that seemed to speak kindly into his own, and the fine, flowing hairs of the ocean-blue mane were a sight to behold.
Quint stared in disbelief. Sliding his wheels through the soaking mud, he approached the creature cautiously, a beast afraid to insult its beauty with his appearance. It had been so long since hed seen a horse, he was afraid to spook it. It followed him with unprejudiced eyes, unmoving as he sluggishly made his way towards it.
Hello there feller... you sure are a nice-looking animal, arent you? he managed to say hoarsely but endearingly, his voice barely more than a whisper. Whatre you doing out here?
Unsurprisingly, the horse did not answer, but looked thoughtful for a moment before motioning towards the ground nearby, where the horseshoe sat ringed around the fencepost.
Quint ventured his conclusion. What? It was because I threw that?
The horse quietly nodded.
Well, what do you want? As you can see, Im a cripple. I cant ride horses. He sighed. What did he hope to gain by talking to a horse? He began to back away.
The horse was quick to follow, its muzzle slightly nudging an alarmed Quint, to whom it presently occurred that this horse may mean to attack. Hey, what do you think youre doing?! he grumbled, pushing it away.
But as the merest touch of this horse he began to feel stronger, the life long absent from his bones seeping back into them. He felt lighter, and realized that, much to his amazement, he was getting out of his chair. A moment later he was standing, wobbly on such unused legs. But for the first time in his life, they worked! He looked down at them, outstretched beneath him, pressing his hands to his newly straightened knees.
Wow! How did you... Quint fell silent. He was speechless.
The horse motioned towards its back. Quint was unsure whether he could mount it, let alone ride with his battered body. But he soon found that the pain had faded, and he was his former self again, the boy of boundless wanderlust.
He sat high atop the horse, laughing and grinning as it carried him through the rolling oat fields of old, and up towards the sky, to the great view far, far beyond his window.

Ambrose
A beautiful day had settled over the small town of Thumblethorn Glade. The small town was one of the many that made up the large and beautiful kingdom of Algernon. The cool fall air made its way over the warm apple pies that cooled on windowsills, down the street over the Crystal Water Creek and settled nicely in the meadow of golden hay that seemed to stretch out endlessly in all directions from town. Hanzi Fey, an elderly farmer, with salt and pepper hair, stood in the midst of the sea of yellow, cutting through it as gently as a knife through butter with his sickle. He wore his oldest red shirt and simple blue pants and vest. The Somerset Festival would be beginning soon and he had little time to prepare a decent location for the festivities this year.
Hanzi Fey was a simple man. He had two sons, Mathias and Samuel, both of whom had taken an interest in magic. For you see, in the land of Algernon, it was said and widely believed that any child could become anything they wished to be. From a great wizard to a powerful King. For the longest time he had promised them a trip to the old Sorcerer Ambrose but as of late he had been side tracked by the festival. His wife, Elena, had passed on years before and rested beneath the Cherry Blossom Tree in front of his home. Hanzi Fey had been chosen to host this years Somerset Festival by the King, Ladrac of Algernon, three weeks prior. This was indeed a great honor since only the finest nobles and the King were allowed. Hanzi Fey was the first farmer ever to be given this honor.
He paused now to blink the sweat from his eyes and dab his forehead with his handkerchief. He leaned on the old long sickle and watched the sun start to set in the distance.
Not long now, He thought to himself, kneeling down and setting the task of tying the hay into small squares. He knew, that this way he would have both a seat for his guests and would be able to stack it neatly into his stables for his horses. Truly, the Fey stables were something of a whispered legend amongst the townsfolk. Everyone knew that the a Fey steed was guaranteed to race like the wind, some even wondered if the horses even touched the ground. The fastest of all the horses in the stables was Ruby. Ruby had joined the Fey family shortly after the death of Hanzis beloved wife and on more than one occasion had served as a guardian angel for the boys, saving Mathias from a nasty fall off Cliffs Edge, and Samuel from a snake bite in Bone Hollow. Hanzi knew the children of the town would wish to ride Ruby during the Somerset Festival so he could also use the cleared space in the field for a small track.
He finished tying the last bundle with a grunt. He stayed kneeled down for a moment, content to enjoy the breeze of the day when he heard the approaching hooves of two of his horses. He looked towards the road and saw Mathias and Samuel locked in what was surely a race to see who could reach home first. Rather than be angry at them trying to beat one another home, he smiled and watched, curious as to whom had become the more skilled horseman of the two. Mathias was the elder of the two by two years. The 13 year old boy has shoulder length jet black hair, like his father, and had the deepest blue eyes that anyone in the country had seen. Now they were set in firm concentration as he focused on the gate that led to the house. His horse, Shadowfire, was an beautiful specimen with a white mane and tail, that was slightly ahead of Lucky. Lucky was a creamy tan color with a chocolate brown mane and an attitude of passiveness that matched her owner Samuel. Samuel, a skin and bones 11 year old with short blonde almost white hair and forest green eyes, had a smile on his face of sheer enjoyment.
This is where you give up and I win, Sammy. Mathias jeered as he leaned forward further hoping to push Shadowfire along.
Is it? Samuel smiled, gently nudging Lucky, who leapt ahead of Mathias and through the gate.
Hey! Mathias complained, stopping Shadowfire and frowning. What was that? You were holding back?
Was I? Samuel laughed, sliding off his horse and taking it by the reins. Guess I just got lucky.
Yeah, yeah. Mathias sighed, waving his hand over his head as if fending off a fly. I let you win, because I hate hearing you complain about losing all the time.
Oh ok. Samuel said, turning away to hide his smile, but unable to hide it from his voice. Mathias smiled after a moment and followed after him headed for the stables. The boys waved to their father who was laughing heartily from the field. Mathias opened the wooden gate to Shadowfires stall and gently removed his black saddle as Samuel led Lucky into his own. Hey Mathias?
Yeah? Mathias answered turning his head slightly, already knowing what his brother was going to say.
Should we tell father... Samuel began, but was cut off quickly by Mathias laugh.
No, of course not! Mathias scolded light heartedly. Well show it to him...at the festival. Hows that?
I dont know, Samuel sputtered, trying hard not to sound too nervous. Is it right to keep things from father?
Sammy, Mathias said with an expression of overplayed sadness. Were not hiding anything from him. Its going to be a surprise. Like last year when father gave you that sword you wanted. Remember?
Yeah, Samuel sighed, kicking the dirt with his feet. I didnt even want that dumb thing...youre changing the subject! Samuel started as Mathias laughed again.
What subject are you changing? Hanzi Fey asked, stepping into the stables. He knew immediately that he had caught them up to something because right away Mathias stopped laughing and Samuel moved back a step. Boys?
Its a surprise for tomorrow dad. Mathias spoke up quickly before Samuel could say anything. Right, Samuel?
Right. Samuel said after a moment of deliberation.
All right boys. Help me place these bundles in the barn so we can have place for our guests to sit then well head inside for supper, all right?
Okay dad. Both boys said and hurried with their father into the fields to finish the work. All the while Hanzi Fey never noticed the small brown pouch hanging from Samuels belt.
The time flew quickly and before anyone had blinked it was time for the Somerset festival to begin. Everyone in town, from Marcaus the Bard, to Karen the Taylor, even Elwood the Wise from Sunset Marsh put in an appearance. As things had begun to wind down, Mathias knew that the King would be making his speech soon. He quickly went to his brother Samuel.
Weve got to hurry, little brother. Mathias said quietly, being careful that no one heard. Go and get it from your room.
All right, Samuel shakily breathed, rushing inside the house to retrieve the little brown pouch.
No sooner had he left than the King, Ladrac, wished to make an announcement. The King, who was dressed in his royal gold armor, looked stunning in the evenings light. He wore only gold and red. His long red cloak hung down to the floor, with the royal insigniay imprinted on it, however, his clock never seemed to get dirty no matter how long the day went on. He had his long black hair tied back in a ponytail and his handsome features were more easily presented this way. Everyone knew that the King was slightly vain, but no one said anything bad about the King because his good deeds far out weighed the bad. Everyone near began to grow silent as Ladrac took the center of the barn floor, which was large enough to hold half the town, while the rest listened eagerly from outside.
Dear friends, the King began, looking at each in turn with a warm smile. It is my greatest honor to bring to a close our celebration of the seasons! A mighty cheer rose up throughout the crowd. Mathias cheered also but looked back to the house to see if Samuel had showed up yet. There was no sign of him. I believe that we should all take this time to thank our gracious host, one Hanzi Fey for his kind hospitality and his fine accommodations. Ladrac raised his goblet into the air in a toast that was followed by everyone else. To your health, sir, and I hope that I am only able to match this fine evening next year. Laughter rolled throughout the townsfolk and everyone drained their glasses. Ladrac offered the floor to Hanzi Fey who took it.
Ladies and Gentlemen, friends and neighbors. This has truly been the happiest day of my life. Never before had I thought this great honor would be given to me. I... Suddenly, Samuel bursts through the crowd, clutching that small pouch in his hands.
Not yet! Mathias hissed, trying to grab his brother. But, momentarily distracted and too excited to notice perhaps, Samuel tripped over the cane of Elwood the Wise and fell to the floor. He small pouch opened and a flash of silver skipped across the dirty floor like a rock across a Crystal Water Creek.
And who might this be? King Ladrac questioned, smiling at the young man. The king had apparently not seen the silver item which had conveniently landed at Hanzi Feys feet.
My youngest son, Samuel... Hanzi Fey answered, kneeling down and picking up the item. He looked at the silver locket and brushed off the dirt from the black stone it held in its center. What might this be... He was suddenly cut off by the shocked gasp of Elwood the Wise.
That be the enchanted locket of Sir Zachariah the Bold, it be! Elwood hobbled forward, supporting himself on his walking stick, to get a better look at the item. This led to the sudden surge of townsfolk to see the locket. After a few moments of confusion, the King called for order and called the festival to a close. He instructed all but Elwood the wise and the Fey family to retire for the night. Reluctantly, everyone did so leaving the five in private.
Now, could someone please explain to me what this is? King Ladrac inquired, all the patience gone from his voice.
The locket of Sir Zachariah the Bold, your majesty! Elwood the Wise cackled again. The very same locket that his love, the loveliest and most powerful sorceress in all of Algernon, Raven, had created for him before the war.
The enchanted locket... King Ladrac said thoughtfully. What exactly is it enchanted to do?
Anything that wearer wishes for, my lord. Elwood the Wise wheezed, spitting on the dirt floor. He wore it into every battle, wishing only to last the battle through and return to the arms of his love unharmed.
And he did? King Ladrac asked again, tearing his eyes away from the black stone.
Excuse me for saying so, my lord, but where have you been the last hundred years? Living in a cave somewhere? Elwoods laugh was enough to make a deaf man flinch. Yes he did, but a changed man. You see, the locket allowed Sir Zachariah anything he wished for and so his heart become tainted with greed. He was no longer the man that Raven had fallen in love with and so she died of a broken heart. With the lose of his one true love, Sir Zachariah spent the rest of his days tending to her grave and never using the locket for anything for the rest of his days. It is said that only the purest in heart would be able to attain the enchanted locket and that person may grant rest to these two sad souls.
The boys glanced at their father throughout Elwood the Wises sad tale, and noticed that their father seemed unaffected by the sad words. Rather he became angrier with each thing Elwood said. The boys had a horrible feeling that their father would take them on a trip to the woodshed before the day was done.
And how did two boys manage to find this priceless item? King Ladrac mused with a look at the boys. I must admit, Im interested.
If I may, your majesty, Hanzi Fey spoke up suddenly, for the first time in their conversation. My boys tend to be quite shy about such things. If I may have the night to discover how they came across this item then I would be more than happy to...
No its all right father, Mathias said, seemingly hurt by his fathers attempt to remove him from the importance of their discovery. I want to talk about it.
After a few minutes inside Hanzi Feys home, the boys revealed their very odd experience. Mathias had actually been the one to see the strange colors coming from the Forsaken Forest.
Dark purple, yellow green and blood red lights all seemed to be coming from the woods. Mathias explained, his face betraying the excitement and...the greed he felt when he saw those lights. I thought it was an entire forest filled with jewels. At this Elwood the Wise shook his head slightly.
Thats not right, Samuel sighed, also shaking his head. It was white and gold lights that came from the forest. Elwood the Wise smiled at this while Hanzi Fey and King Ladrac made it quite clear that the more important thing was how the boys had obtained the necklace.
Like I was saying there were lights coming from the woods. So we rode over on our horses through Buttercup Meadow until we reached the entrance to the woods. We both rode in, me first because Samuel was afraid and kept hanging back. Mathias snickered looking at his brother. Samuel said nothing because he wanted the story to quickly be over, but in truth he had hung back because Mathias had asked him to watch him back for him while the entered the forest.
Get on with the story child, it will be dark soon and we have much to do. King Ladrac persuaded, becoming more and more uncomfortable with the enchanted locket that lay on the table.
Sorry, your majesty, upon entering the woods we saw the largest tree in the entire forest. It had to be at least several hundred years old and it was the source of the lights. I walked up to the tree and reached out towards it... Mathias stopped, clearly trying to decide how best to precede: truth or lie. After a moment of patient silence from the room the sighed and went on. And nothing happened. I was ready to go, but Samuel wanted to see what would happen. He stepped forward and the tree opened to him. Inside was the locket and he took it.
Extraordinary! Hanzi Fey exclaimed jumping to his feet. My own sons have found the enchanted locket of Sir Zachariah! He clapped his hands together happily, a smile that looked as if it would never leave his face firmly in place, he turned to the king. Your majesty, may we keep this precious item?
No, Elwood the Wise answered for him. King Ladrac was furious, he had never been interrupted before.
Excuse me, sir... he began but Elwood the Wise turned on him.
No, sire, this locket, he lifted it from the table with his staff. Is much more powerful than any of us may know. Only the childs heart is pure enough to keep this. He will keep it till day break tomorrow then I must insist that you travel to the Sorcerer Ambrose. He could tell you far more about this item than I could ever dream.
Are you sure this is the best course of action? King Ladrac asked, clearly disappointed with Elwood the Wises decision.
Aye, your majesty, it is.
Then so be it, tomorrow, we will travel to the Sorcerer Ambrose.
No, your majesty. The two boys found the stone, they must travel this way alone. Elwood the Wise said, and instantly Hanzi Feys smile vanished.
Please, may I not travel with them, Elwood the Wise. They are my sons and...
And you miss the thrill of adventure, is it? Elwood said with a grin. Hanzi Fey suddenly seemed to be embarassed. It is nothing to be ashamed of, my good Hanzi Fey. In every mans heart lurks the desire to become more than what the Gods made him. But, this adventure, belongs to your sons...
And so the next day Mathias and Samuel saddled themselves into their horses. Mathias on Shadowfire and Samuel...
But why cant I take Lucky, father? Samuel protested, looking at his father.
Because, dear child, Ruby will protect you and guide you safely through the dangers that lie ahead. Hanzi Fey smiled and hugged both his sons. Be safe, my sons...
Well see you soon father. Samuel comforted with a smile and then both boys were off. Hanzi Fey watched them vanish into the distance, never knowing that the greatest adventure in the history of Algernon was beginning at that very moment...

The sky was dark and solmen, casting shadows over the moon lit hills. In the
distance a long, lone shadow slowly crept over the land. It's owner followed
in a slow pace with an almost melancholy aura to it. Before long the
shadow's owner stepped into the soft glow of the moonlight.
For seven straight years he traveled across the land; from snow covered
mountains, to dried wastelands. Time and the trials he's withstood showed on
his rugged, weather beaten face. Stubble long ago was replaced by a beard;
his eyes once bright were now sunken and faded. Everything about him looked
battered and worn out long ago. Even his clothes were covered in patches and
darkened from frequent washings in streams and rivers.
His horse who was his only companion on this long journey, has seen better
days. The poor black steed, almost skeletal in appearnce, looked long for
another world. The man reassured the horse that this quest would soon be
over with acouple quick pats to the neck. With great effor the horse shook
it's head from side to side as if he too knew that the days of hardship
would soon be over.
Day would break soon, as a faint glow of light could be seen on the
horrizion. "Tomorrow. I'll finnally be there," the man thought with a sigh
as he gazed off into the distance. "It's been so long, I'm not sure what
I'll even do when I get there."
He reached into his saddle bag and withdraw an item that glimmered softly in
the glow of the stars. The gold necklace was beautiful and clean, not a
speck of dirt on it. In the center was an elegant blue saphire about an inch
in diameter, which was surrounded by a beautful and articulate white leafing
in the shape of roses. The man carefully examined it, making sure it was
clean and without fault. After all, this was the reason he spent so much
time and risked his life on this journey.
Seven years earlier, he was working for a local farmer in a small out of the
way village. It was a quite, uneventful place where nothing happend, and
everyone's lives were happy and content. Except for that one day, this man's
life was predictable and uneventful.
He was tilling the farm one day when a huge carriage rolled down the road.
It was huge, bigger then anything he ever saw in his life. He sat that there
in awe on that fateful day's burning light and watched as six massive horses
pulled the carriage along. Suddenly a loud crack filled the air as one of
the carriage's wheels broke. The whole carriage shook and tipped to the
side, and before he knew what he was doing he ran towards the carriage at a
frenzied pace.
The driver of the carriage jumped down and stood up just as the farm worker
got to the carriage. The driver was a huge man, standing much taller then
the worker. "Quick," the driver shouted and pointed to the missing wheel,
"make sure it doesn't tip over!"
Both men ran to the broken wheel and pushed against the carriage. The door
to the carriage squeaked open slowly, and seeing this the driver yelled with
a grunt, "Mi'lady, don't concern yourself. It's just a broken wheel. I'll
have it fixed in just a few minutes!"
Appearntly not listening, the passanger stepped out in into the dirt road.
She too was tall, but beyond elegant and beautiful. A long light blue gown
flowed down to her ankles, and her black hair fell over her shoulders almost
haphazzardly. She wore a soft smile on her face, and looked at the two men
propping the carriage up. "Now quick, Grahm!" She said in a songstresses
voice, "let go before you strain yourself!"
Both men let go of the carriage as if on que and stepped quickly out of the
way, letting the carriage rest on it's lopsided corner.
"But, Mi'lady," the driver Grahm said, "you shouldn't bother yourself with
these matters. If the master finds out you left the carriage..."
"And if I didn't get out and you strained yourself, I would be without a
driver. What then would the master say to that?" she quickly retorted. "I
could careless what happens to the carriage as it can be fixed. If you were
hurt because of it, then how do you suppose that'd make me feel? You
shouldn't have to go through so much for my sake. We are both people, Grahm.
You are no worse off then I."
"Mi'lady..." Grahm said, bowing his head. To the farm boy it looked like he
almost blushed.
"Oh," the young lady said as she turned to the farm hand. "I'm sorry, we
