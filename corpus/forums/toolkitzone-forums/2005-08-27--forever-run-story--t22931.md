Forever Run: In Time, In Dreams
Nakia Zamura flashed on the ID as it was swiped in the card reader. The computer in the hydraulic door asked for a retinal scan. A tall and awkwardthough awkward is perhaps not proper; the man was considered quite beautiful by Northian standards--man dressed in a white, flowing uniform lowered his head to the retinal scanner. After brushing aside an emerald green bang of hair, a red flash issued from the scanner. There was a reassuring tone from the door; the computer asked for voice verification. My name is Nakia Zamura. Said the man.
With a hiss and a click, the hydraulic doors flew open, revealing a long platform that seemingly stood on the edge of nothingness. Nakia walked across the platform, the bottom of his uniform swaying gently. The darkness lit up with stars and those stars exploded into geometric shapes. The whole room seemed to be moving forward at an incredible speed. It all slowed and cooled into an array of white squares and hexagons. The largest one, which rested in the center, held the image of a young Northian woman. She was very similar to Nakia in turns of physical appearancegender held little meaning to the Northian people. They both held the tell tale signs of a Northian birthright; the green eyes and hair; the tall, slender figure and pointed ears. There was a slight difference in the shape of the bony structures protruding from their elbows and the back of their earswhich was to be expected from two different individuals.
Nakia reached the end of the platform, puckered his mouth, and put his hands on hips. Well, whats the situation on Hofess? asked Nakia.
The woman smiled and replied, Its been resolved, Nakia. We arrested the smugglers hours ago, so cool your jets.
Nakia waved a finger at the floating hexagon and said frankly, See Cecil, you should have put on security patrol. I just knew something like this was going to happen!
Cecil sighed and said, Youre already on guarding the actual shipment, Nakia. Lets not forget that the smugglers attacked on of the dummy convoys.
One of the hexagons illuminated, showing the image of a man being handcuffed. Nakias hand went back to his side. Well thats good I suppose. The number one thing here is getting that Galaxy Stone. If I really wanted action, then Id be dealing with Ruther Nells family. Oh my god, have you seen those people? They are crazy right now. And I cant much blame them. Theyre not exactly happy that weve confiscated that mans possessions. Most of it was gona be left to them! Said Nakia, jutting his mouth and motioning with hands.
Well were going to give it all back, Nakia. All we want is the stone and it would look suspicious if we took just one of Ruthers possessions. Anyway, I just wanted to continue your briefing, before you make your way to Hofess.
Nakias hands gravitated from his sides and flopped slighted as turned to look around a bit. Well lets get on with it.
Its come to my attention that youve already read the entire mission outline, lengthy though it may be. Said Cecil.
Just being prepared old girl. Said Nakia with a bright smile.
Haha, well I just want you know that once this mission is started, youre basically cut off from HQ. The heads dont want to take any heat from this, should anything go wrong. Considering the circumstances, its quite understandable.
Nakia continued to look around, not particularly interested what Cecil had to say. Mmhmm, just covering their own behinds I see. Said Nakia.
Be that as it may, you are no longer under my jurisdiction. The Local District manager calls the shots from here on out. And of course this is only tempo Cecil tried to say.
Suddenly Nakia turned to Cecil and put his hands on his hips. It darn well better be! Thats just ridiculous! Me out of your jurisdiction! Well I tell you Cecil, theres just a certain order to things. Youve got the deputies under the marshals, those are under the sheriffs, those are under the district manager. Those are under Nakia was cut off by Cecil.
Im aware of our hierarchy, Nakia. This is only temporary. After this you can go back to being a freelance officer. Said Cecil.
After this my Sponser will randomly reassigned Cecil! You I work best with youits just the plain true! continued Nakia, turning his gaze all about the room and motioning with his hands.
Nakia, listen to me. Nakia! yelled Cecil.
Nakia arrested himself. His hands fell to his sides and his expression grew plain. I know how you feel. I know whats bothering you. Said Cecil.
Nakia remained silent. Look this might be for the best. Quite frankly, you just havent been yourself lately. You lost two illegal residents along the galactic border.
Nakia lowered his head to the floor. In a class 7 Griffle ship. Its also come to my attention that you unnecessarily terminated the culprit of a double homicide. You received heavy sanctions from HQ for that breach of code. And in fact, youve had fourteen conduct citations since that time. Im sick of hearing about you, Nakia. I know youre hurting from the break-up. But this is-- asked Cecil.
Nakia smirked You dont know-- He tried to say.
I know it hurts. I suppose our relationship was only about you? asked Cecil.
The room grew silent for a full minute. Cecil signed and rubbed her forehead. Code mandate that I either fire you or recommend you for a psyche evaluation. Youre lucky you even have an assignment this important, Nakia! The Local District has no idea of the importance of this mission, but they are aware of your record. You are currently classified as Special Agent Plus. Everyone remembers how you dissolved the situation with Hakar Tyza. Youre a good cop, Nakia. But what we had is over. Its time to move on. Why do you have to drag this up now? Said Cecil.
We both made a mistake.Cecil. said Nakia.
Youre on your own Nakia. Dont worry, you always find a way. Said Cecil.
With that the room went pitch black once more. Nakia left the communication room completely absolved of the matter. It was the way of his race to be controlled and mathematical. Nakia briefly thought that Cecil should have fired him, then he pushed it down to the back of his mind, moving on to more pressing matters. He entered the docking bay and boarding his Griffle ship and left the space station at the speed of light.
Hofess was a mosaic of jungle, desert, and civilization. The hot desert air blew Nakias hair. The far away cry of the wind signaled the start of the funeral procession. Ruther Nells family had insisted on turning the transport of Ruthers possessions into a kind of ceremony; this was mostly owing to the fact that Ruthers body was being transported from his mansion as well.
The rich Icacoo had constructed him mansion in the middle of the desert. Perhaps this was because he was a recluse, but more than likely it was to keep away bandits. The mansion was a psuedo-fortress with battle armaments stationed around the main gate. There was even a small militia that guarded the mansion and its treasures. Ruther and his family had lived there for many years and now that he was dead, the rest of inhabitants of Hofess felt a certain relief in the airing of dirty laundry. Very few people had actually ever gone into the Ruther mansion, and there was a general mystique, which pervaded the whole place.
It was an ancient place. But now the roar of Space Police cruisers could be heard above the mansion--the proud paean of technology had been sounded. Nakia had ordered his ship, whom he called Milo, to patrol the area, while Nakia rode with the ground tanks. Now who are they trying to fool with all this blasted security, Nakia wondered to himself. Everybody knows somethin going on, thought Nakia, people arent stupid! The Northian let out a yelp as the tank he was riding on wobbled slightly from a bump in the sand.
Meanwhile, 7000 meters away, behind a large sand dune, a group of Post were watching the whole scene. They were not there for the funeral ceremony; they had more sinister intentions. Behind them, ingeniously camouflaged in a thin layer of sand, were ten Post battle ships, along with the Post Flagship herself. The Post each scuttled about anxiously on a set of four, spider-like legs. The Post was a mysterious race that abhorred three things above all else: laziness, complacency, and stagnation. And as such, most of the Post took the profession of bounty hunters. They all wore obsidian bio-suits, painted with red markings that denoted any rank or special merits. The group chattered and argued in their native languagea careful series of clicks and hisses. Suddenly there was a long screeching noise, followed by several beeping tones. A tall, lanky figure stepped up on top of the dune they were hiding behind.
Distinctive of this creature was the fact that it was bipedal and wore a modified Post bio-suit. It spoke not in their language, but in Comlistatthe computerized language. The figure watched through a pair of binoculars as the transport convoy began to move out, the ceremony finally over. Lowering the binoculars and motioning with his right arm, the figure said: [Masha, Rash, take point position. Re-direct all external tech-mines to signal interference.]
The Post ceased their chatter and began to move out. They scuttled into the sands depths and entered their ships. One of them stopped and asked: [Phi of Zero, what of the Space Police? Destroy?]
Phi Zero answered: [Flagship will destroy. Intergalactic Space Police know not to disturb my operations. They will pull out when under fire.]
The Post nodded and went to his ship. Suddenly Phi was alone on the sand dune. Suddenly the air was still as the wind went to the next part of its cycle. Phi raised his hand quickly tapped several buttons on his wrist. A series of tones issued forth.
Nakia looked up and to his horror, a black ship, the shape of a giant stingray, decloaked above the convoy. No sooner had it become visible, than a shot of green napalm rocketed from it, and onto two of the support tanks. There was an explosion and then the sound of an alarm going off. Nakia rushed inside his tank and grabbed the radio from one of his men. This is Nakia! Opposing target has been sighted; all available units open fire! The instant a line becomes available, I wan Nakia tried to say, before his radio turned to static.
Sir, weve got signal interference! Im trying to put up an oscillating field, but its not working! The whole area must have jamming mines! yelled one of the technicians.
Nakia rushed over to the control panel and asked, Can you at least get me a line to my ship? If I can get to Milo, I can contact the District Space Station. They need to know whats going on down here!
I-Ill try sir! said the technician.
Outside an intense battle was going on. Phi had ordered his ship to attack the very center of the convoys defenses, thus creating a distraction for his men. After drawing the ground tanks and a battle cruiser, Phi ordered his men to attack. The Post ships burst from the sand and let loose their fury. Hot beads of silicon splattered through the air, as laser fire tore across the desert. At first the Space Police were disoriented and disorganized by the surprise attack. But they greatly outnumbered the Post, so it was it was not long before the tide of battle changed. Nakia took command as best he could with the signal interferance; luckily the officers of Space Police had been trained for such situations and could coordinate themselves into small, independent groups.
As Phi slowly walked across the sands, he noticed that someone must be trying to direct the Space Police forces. Suddenly a Griffle ship zoomed above him; Phi recognized the ship and instantly knew who it was. Phi raised his wrist to the base of his helmet and spoke: [Andra, Niko, Masha, finish mission. Obtain the objective so that mission directive can be accomplished. Flagship plus Commander will destroy Space Police command center. All remaining units ignore ground unitsdestroy cruiser units.]
With that, the Post ships formation changed drastically. Two Space Police ships crashed into one another, sending a ripple of hot air through the sky. The black stingray flew over Phi; seconds later he vanished in a yellow haze. The stingray reversed its course. It was headed for a lone tank with a struggling radio system.
The transport vehicles that held Ruthers possessions were completely defenseless. One by one, the defending tanks were picked off by laser fire. Two of the Post ships managed to snatch one of the two transports with a tractor beam array. The other transport was hit by a stray blast and caught fire. Abandon ship! yelled one of the men on the transport, as a fireball erupted behind him.
Meanwhile, back inside the command tank, Nakias men were still struggling to contact Milo. Dang it! You mean you cant get any line out to him? asked Nakia.
The technician shook his head. Nakia grunted. He closed his eyes and sat with his legs crossed on the floor. The whole room became silent as Nakia lowered his head, allowing his smooth locks to cover his face. Milo are you there, projected Nakia, I want you to protect the cargo ships as best you can. Nakia opened his eyes and sighed. Slowlly he raised himself, only to stumble gracelessly. One of his men caught him. Are you alright sir? asked the technician.
Ill be fine. I think I got through to Milo. Said Nakia.
And it was true. Milo managed to capture the remaining cargo ship with his own tractor beam; the Griffle raced away, as several Post ships chased after it. Milo could sense danger near his pilot and refused to stray too far awayhe circled around the battle area, while trying to evade his attackers. This proved to be too stressing for him, and eventually his slipped up. One of the Post managed to nick Milos exterior with a blast. The ship lost balance and power long enough for the tractor beam to drop the cargo ship. One of the Post nabbed the cargo ship and speed away.
Nakia could sense the failure by the emotions that Milo projected. He would have risked fainting to scold Milo via telepathy, but was sorely interrupted. There was loud thump on the roof of the command tank. Nakia backed away toward the exit. Suddenly there was explosion.
The heat, the glass.
Everybody get out now!! yelled Nakia.
The heat, the glass. The flame and embers.
Through the thick smoke, there was another loud thump and then the gattling of an automatic weapon. Nakia managed to escape out the side door before a volley of shells cascaded through it. There was the sound of hollow shells falling on the groundsizzling in some ferrous fluid. The officer panted as he ran to other side of the tank. All he had on him was a standard issue, Regular Heat. It was a small pistol; powerful but lacking in ammunition. Smoke poured from the side door. The tall, menacing figure of Phi Zero stepped out. Phis helmet briefly lit with a red light, as his visor re-adjusted itself for normal levels of light.
Nakia hid himself behind a battle torrent. Phi walked slowly around the top of the tank. He spoke: [Myself did not want to kill you. Will not kill you..Nakia of the Zamura.]
The Northians heart was beating fast. A bead of sweat dropped from his brow and landed on the searing hot metal surface of the tank. It sizzled.
Phi quickly turned and fired his weapon. Nakia yelped and covered his head as a spray of bullets ricocheted off the torrent. When it stopped, Nakia grunted and yelled, Why are you doing this Phi! This isnt like you! You work alonewhy sell yourself out!?
Phi walked closer: [You will noterroryou dont understand. This is not for the money. This is for my own salvation.]

Phi was now right in front of the torrent. The air was silent, except for sounds of the far away battle. Finally, Nakia slide his gun away. There, Im unarmed. Said Nakia.
Phi began to walk around to the other side of the torrent. Suddenly, Nakia leap out from the other side. There was green, glowing rod in his handsit was almost gaseous in appearance and almost certainly held the characteristic of either being alive or being an extension of Nakias life. Phi looked up and swore in Comlistat. Milo had returned and was now using his tractor beam as a kind of shield around Nakia. He got the message! Its over Phi! The space station boys are probably sending back-up right now! said Nakia.
To which Phi answered: [I have already won. My contract has been resolved. Now, this? This? This is just forfun.]
In orbit around the planet Hofess, there was one of the Local Districts space stations. It was a long tubular structure with an array of solar panels on each side. It housed thousands of workers, scientists, and officers. Thousands of lives.
A black stingray uncloaked nearby. The space station was opening its docking bays to allow its fighters to exit; already the main cannon was preparing to fire.
Underneath the stingray, a port opened and a gun jutted out from beneath it. For an eternity, the gun glowed a deep blue. After an eternity, a single shot fired from the stingray. A blue blast of purgatory entered the side of the space station--letting loose a massive explosion. The explosion started on one end of station, then another erupted a little farther down. Then another. And another.
Nakia could see the explosion all the way from the ground. Phi let out a long beeping noise, before jumping from the command tank and onto one of his mens ships.
Two days later, on the Local Districts main space station, Albatross, Nakia was awaiting his judgment. He stood in a solid white room with floating chairs and desks; Nakia would alternate between sitting down in one of these, then pacing the room five minutes later.

[No sir.]

What's happening!?" asked Nakia.

Get a gripe old man, thought Nakia. Slowly he walked through the bubble and walked out into the middle of the desert. Milo, the curcuit room, the distortion. The wires--the brain. They were all gone. "Aww crap!" cried Nakia.

This the battle cry of the proud Northian people, Nakia leap forward and did a shoulder toss on one of the guards. Someone attacked him from behind--no gun, just a fist. Break the arm, should throw, d-dogde it, move, move, move, thought Nakia.

Why didn't you bring me to the curcuit room?" Nakia called out.
