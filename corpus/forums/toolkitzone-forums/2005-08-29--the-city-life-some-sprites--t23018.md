### venstor · 2005-08-30 03:24 · #B252055

![](http://www.toolkitzone.com/mygames/images/857/4252.PNG)
Correct me if I'm wrong, but isn't your game isometric? Shouldn't the front view of your sprites be more of a 3/4 view?
Anyway, the main problem I see is that your sprites are too low contrast. You've got a bunch of shades in your palette that are just a couple of luminosity points away from each other. If you have different shades of a color, each one should be at least 10 luminosity points darker than the shade above it. (I'm going off the HSL sliders in GraphicsGale. I think that translates to around 20 luminosity points in Paint's color picker)
When I zoom in, I can see the shading you're trying to do, but I can't see it at normal size because you're being too subtle about it. Make your highlights lighter and your shades darker. It's all too close to the medium tone right now. Also, a lot of outlines could stand to be darker. You have the right idea using colors, they just need to be a little darker to be visible. I see that you're trying a bit of selective outlining by making the outlines on the right side lighter, but I think you've made them a bit too light.
The anatomy does look a little odd to me. The way the arms curve out looks a little off. Maybe the lower arms are too beefy? The legs look too skinny and too straight to me. The torsos should maybe be wider at the top and come in a little bit at the waist. I'm no expert at anatomy.
Facially, you'd have room for noses and ears if you wanted them, but they're not necessary. There's quite a bit of individuality shown by each character already, but full faces might set them apart from each other that much more.
Here's an attempt to show you what I'm talking about:
![](http://www.toolkitzone.com/stuff/files/1710/norton1.gif)
The guy in the suit had decent contrast in the hair, but not in his clothing and flesh. I messed with the palette to put more difference between the lights and the darks, put in some anti-aliasing around the edges, and tried to fix the anatomy somewhat. Zoom in on what I did and see if any of it makes sense to you.
I don't know how helpful this was, but that's what I see anyway.

Those are looking worlds better to me. Anything I add now is just going to be nit-picking, because those are some great looking sprites.
On the old guy, a darker outline around his frazzled white hair would make it stand out a bit more from the enviroment. The lines showing his pecs beneath his shirt could probably be a couple of shades lighter. (Same with the guy in the red shirt)
The black guy looks a little odd to me without ears. That's a stylistic choice not to have any, but he'd look more human to me with them. His white shoes could use a darker outline.
The guy in the green shirt's mouth could be darker.
On Cort, I'd darken the lines and shading just a little bit on his white shirt.
As for adding more personality, they already look pretty distinctive to me. You can try different facial expressions, but what might really set them apart is different stances. They are all sort of standing the same way, so you might make one of them kind of slouch or one of them stand kind of bow-legged with his chest puffed out. Just think about how the character acts in story scenes and see if you can think of some way to represent his personality visually.
