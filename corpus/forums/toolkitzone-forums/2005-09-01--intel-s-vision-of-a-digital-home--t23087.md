# Intel's vision of a digital home...

### Spyder · 2005-09-01 09:22 · #B252758

I was reading an article on Tom's Hardware Guide while at work tonight, and it was talking about Intel's vision of the future 'digital home'. Part of this vision is as follows...

**tomshardware.com wrote:**

> Wolfgang's issue with what was presented is that our future family life would have little in common with a typical scenario of today. Availability of various digital devices, ubiquitous broadband and wireless connections will enable every family member to be engaged in their own digital worlds. Just like in Total Recall, we would become trapped inside our own heads.
> For example, Dad would watch his movies and football games in front of the big screen in the family room, Mom has her own TV in the kitchen to watch soap operas while cooking dinner and the kids disappear in their rooms with their portable multimedia players and consume any digital audio and video content that can squeeze through broadband lines.
> Call us both old-fashioned, but being dads ourselves - and in his case with two young children - this is not what we imagined our future family life to be. Intel's vision comes as a surprise and appears to be off track, especially since the company also outlined applications for "user aware" computing that is aimed to support the user and potentially enhance the social component of IT. But it was exactly this social component that was lacking at the demos. Instead, there is a clear social separation in Intel's demo family.
>

Anyways, I was wondering what everyone's opinion on this is. Do you think this is going to be common in the future? Is it a good or a bad thing, or do you even care? Honestly this is already happening. My friend Dennis has a TV in every room in his house except the bathrooms (which he's currently considering doing as well). Even his 7 month old daughter has a 19" TV in her room. He's also considered putting one outside on his back patio.
Normally I wouldn't see a problem with this except that all he does when he's not working is watch TV and play pool. That's all his kids do as well. Now certainly I understand that I sound exactly like my parents did when I was a kid, but my brother and I did plenty of outdoor activity along with playing video games. His kids do absolutely none. He wants them to grow up and be on sports teams, but he won't even take them out to a field to play catch. I'm regarded as basically a 'computer nerd' who 'spends all his time on the internet', while he's an former football player, and yet I do more outdoor activity and exercise in a month than he does in a year.
Basically what I'm getting at is that I don't see how Intel's vision of the digital home of the future is beneficial to society, or anything else other than the companies profiting off all the electronics people are buying. What used to be looked down upon is now being portrayed as a dream.
