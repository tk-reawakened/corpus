Why not? Once could say the same has been done throughout all science. IE: Chemistry being superior to Alchemy. It comes with better understanding. Now while I'll admit that ethology is a more significant alternative school of thinking to psychology than alchemy to chemistry. I believe psychology holds better roots of understanding than what I've read of ethology. The idea of ethology that all of our mental functioning is purely base and animalistic doesn't seem right to me. I understand why it can think that, but I think there's more to it than just base action and reaction. And ethology seems rather closed to the idea that there is more to it than that.

**Quote:**

> The point is, you also can't notice the basic visible patterns of operation, or propably you base them all on the ways of acting of one species in an enclosed, isolated environment, ie. people in the capitalistic country of United States.
>

Actually psychology is studying those basic visible patterns of operation. But it doesn't hold that everything is purely instinctual and purely based on the physical. For one, it doesn't explain to me why different emotions FEEL differently. Example: You say every emotion is based in aggression... love and hate I can understand. But I don't see how feelings such as fear or sadness or happiness can be based AT ALL in agression. I believe a lot of the way we act is based more in reaction to our learning and memories than to pure animal instinctual action and reaction. Otherwise how would we, the human species, have progressed as far as we had. Yeah, it's a little bit of the human arrogance I've bitched about in there, but a little bit of it seems justified, because otherwise, why would certain other species not developed the way we had? It's not like we're the only species with an opposable thumb, like someone who accepts ethology over psychology would seem to think is what makes us special. Nor are we the only species that has learned how to utilize tools (in fact, many primates fit both of those exceptions).
