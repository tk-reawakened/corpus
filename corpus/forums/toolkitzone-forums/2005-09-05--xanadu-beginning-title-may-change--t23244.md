# Xanadu (beginning, title may change)

### Penguin · 2005-09-05 21:38 · #B254440

Alright, I'm trying to write a science fiction story of some sort, obviously this is the start and it ought to suck a bit (I usually re-fix my work two or three times). Though this may be science themed, expect some fantastic elements of some sort.
Here we go, two first short chapters:
One
Xanadu was a small planet. It wasn't very significant either, apart from being home to a few thousand people. It was the closest planet to the Sun of Thousand Names and the only one inhabited in the entire system. The Outer Regions were scarcely populized anyway - the colonies, once serving as the outer borders of the United Systems, have been abandoned by all major fractions as the United Systems fell apart. Most citizens of these colonies have learned to organize themselves, and Xanadu was one such colony. Under the theocratic, militaristic rule of the paladins, the citizens were relatively safe from what the paladins themselves viewed as a danger - which included the two greatest forces of the known Universe, namely the Free Republic and the followers of Logos.
Xanadu was scorched in the heat of the Sun of Thousand Names for the most of time. Only the northern regions were inhabited, and the citizens of the badlands feared to descend into the wastelands and deserts to the south. What lied beyond the urban regions controlled by the paladins was a subject of legends and speculations, the most common theories were that there is either nothing, or some sort of a hidden base or a shrine belonging to the forces of Logos - neither provided a reason to ever try and check.
It must be said that the paladins did manage to save some of the old technology of Xanadu. Electricity was very common, though the paladins never used any advanced system of electronics. Some said that in some inner areas of the Ziggurat, headquarters of the paladins in their greatest and capital city of Steinmauer, a space shuttle or a rocket of some sort is kept, but it wasn't that necessary anyway. Xanadu had no contact with the outer worlds except for some Free Republic caravans (paladins have always greatly pitied having to deal with the Free Republicansy, but it was necessary for the planet's economy). Most of the Xanadians have never ventured outside of their own planet, and their vision on the outer world was dictated by the marchants' tales.
The native inhabitans of Xanadu were rather 'degenerated', as someone from the Inner Regions could have said. They were of a relatively small size, their hair was scarce and short, their carnation rather dark (though some of them had blond hair), and they had four digits, both on arms and legs. A common legend spoke that once all of the human beings lived on a single planet, Terra, which was destroyed as a punishment for human pride, and its inhabitants have been banished to different lands and began to speak different tongues, but majority of the educated population of the colonized Universe regarded this theory as nothing more than a myth of the creationists. Of course, at least on Xanadu, no one openly spoke about disproving such theories, as the paladins have been doing their best to ensure the faith in 'Our Lord' (as they called him), though the more rebellious part of the population was rumored to prepare an opposition to faith. Paladins usually thought it's all the evil influence of the Free Republic, which in their regard was a land of a bunch of immoral sinners anyway.
Usher was one of such rebellious Xanadians, though. He was living in an abode situated on the outer regions of the city of Steinmauer, along with his parents and a sister, and as usually woke up early in the morning. He stood up from his sleeping mattress, opened the sliding metal door of his room with a button, and headed towards the eating room. On his way, he met up with his father Zho, who was passing nearby.
'Usher, we have to talk.' his father began.
'Not now, please... I need to eat something.'
'Alright. But after you finish eating, please come to the living room. We have some matters to discuss...'
In the Xanadian culture, eating in the public or even in front of other people was considered highly impolite, if not immoral - comparable to how excrementing was treated in some of the old cultures. That was of course originating in the paladin philosophy of morality, and a reason beyond why Xanadian abodes always had separate rooms used for eating and debating. A family would have never talked during a dinner - though eating together was a common occurence, the family members during such act were always remaining silent and tried not to look at one another.
Usher has finished eating soon, and hurried towards the living room. His parents and his sister were already sitting around a circular table.
'Son,' his father began. 'I hope you are perfectly aware that you have matured at the age of sixteen yesterday.'
'Father, the religious calendar makes no sense. It does not fit the scheme of the planet's rotation around the Sun of Thousand Names.'
'Silence!' his mother Case spoke out. 'We're not going about insulting the faith in Our Lord now, are we?'
'Our Lord? Paladins apparently managed to distort the teachings of Vassermann to make him some sort of a messiah, eh?' said Usher in fury, standing up. 'Not to mention it doesn't make any sense, it in fact is the violation of the lessons of Vassermann.'
'How dare you call Our Lord by his mortal name?' shouted his father. 'Sit down!'
Usher sat down. 'You're under influence of all these controversial denominations.' his father went on. 'But that is not what I want to speak with you about! You are mature now, son, I want you to have a future.'
'Father... I have a future. I was going to find some work at drilling the wells-...'
'My son shouldn't drill wells! You know what vision of a future I have for you.'
'No!' Usher stood up again and slammed his fist against the table. 'I shall not join the paladins-...'
'You shall. That would teach you some more discipline and respect for religion. And of course, it would give us all a steady source of income.'
'Go join them yourself!'
'You know it's the only way to get out of our poverty! Me and mother thought about moving to closer to the Ziggurat...'
'I am not your money machine.'
'You are our son.'
'They wouldn't agree on me to join... I'm physically weak.'
'You're not. They need soldiers, always. If the rumors are true, the Logos may want to strike
'The Logos make more sense than the paladins...' Usher whispered.
'Silence!' shouted his mother. 'For the sake of the return of the Black Plague,
'Mom, is the Black Plague gonna come again?' all of sudden asked the five year old sister of Usher. 'And many people are going to die?'
'No, honey.' the mother said, calming down. 'It's just an expression. Black Plague is not going to come.'
'You and your sad beliefs!' Usher screamed and headed towards his room, turning back one last time. 'I am going to pay these paladins a visit tommorow, and I'll be glad to return to tell you they didn't allow me to join.'
'You're going to thank us later, son!' his father spoke out as Usher dissappeared from sight.
Two
Usher was woke up by his father on the next day. 'Know that I do not approve it...' the father said, strangely. 'But you have a visitor waiting for you outside. I still do hope you're going to head to the Ziggurat today.'
'I will, dad.' said his son, stood up from the mattress, put on some clothes and quickly headed outside.
In front of the automatic metal doors of the abode stood a male of his age, of white skin, taller, and having five digits on each hand. He was dressed in some loose linen clothes and smiled as he saw Usher come out.
'Khal. - said Usher, smiling as well. 'The hell you're doing here?'
'My father's doing some business with the paladins. I came along. He said he predicts something evil to happen'
'Evil? Yeah. I am going to join the paladins, or at least ask for it. Father's goddamn will.'
'No joke, man? Damn. Sucks for you. I'm not going to tell you some another 'we at the Free Republic...' kind of tale, don't worry. May I assist you on the way?'
'Uh, I wasn't going to go right now, but... alright. The Ziggurat is pretty far away. When are you leaving?'
'Tommorow.' said Khal as they began to walk.
The outer regions of Steinmauer weren't very appealing. It was hot, very hot, almost unbearable, some people were sunbathing iin the open on their mattresses, some were taking water from the well, some paladin guard in a red uniform robe with the lower part of his face wrapped around with a silk cover (all paladins wore that sort of cover) was arguing with a man caught on eating chocolate in the open. The poorer Xanadians had electricity provided, but they could never afford any type of a vehicle to drive around. Only the paladins were officially allowed to have driving licenses anyway.
'I like this place.' said Khal. 'I mean, it's unique. Non-global. Sure, you're under a theocratic regime,' he lowered his voice on these words. 'but I think it's bearable. Beside the fact that you can't take photos here... damn.'
'Yeah. As the pallies say, photography steals one's soul. I know you love photography. Do you have your camera?'
'Yes. I always carry it around. I'm not going to use it here, though.'
It took about three hours of a local time to enter the Temple District. 'I'll wait for you here.' said Khal as they have reached the gate that lead into the outer area of the Ziggurat. Usher nodded. He passed through the ornamental gate. He went through a straight road between the paladin gardens, around which the paladins dressed in all violet were wandering (their robes signifying their higher position, red was used by street guards, violet and blue by the military, green by the non-combatants). Before him, he saw the Ziggurat in its full glory, as he never did before - a pyramidal structure of many levels and balconies, accessible through the lifts from the inside, and only one enterance at its base, through which he was just going to enter with a soft sigh, as he heard his friend Khal scream in the back.
He turned around. Above the line of the walls separating the Ziggurat from the streets of Steinmauer, he could see an object, a shuttle, falling down from the sky in bright sunlight. He hurried towards the gate along with the paladin troopers, who kept saying about 'Plan B3' and 'Crowd Control'. He passed the gate. His friend was standing there with amazement. They could not tell what was really happening, as some paladin grabbed them by their hands and led out to the left. From the gate leading into the Ziggurat, paladins armed with automatic rifles began to emerge and run towards where the object may have fallen.
The paladin that led them out of the gate picked up a mobile phone. 'Area 272, recieved.' he said.
'Area 272, shit.' said Usher. 'That's where my house is.'
'No cursing, please.' the paladin reminded him. 'Sadly, we are obviously closing all optional draft activity until the case is solved.' he added, his voice slightly uffled through the silk cover of his face, then turned around and headed back into the Ziggurat, passing the last forces that marches out. His clothes were blue. He was a general, or a commander of some sort.
'Great.' said Khal, staring at Usher. 'I mean, I feel sad for you, you know... but don't you think we should head and check ourselves?'
'Uh-uh.' said the other one, trying to fight the shock. 'Most fucking certainly we should.'
