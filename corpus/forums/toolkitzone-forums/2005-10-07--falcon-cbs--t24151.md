### p90m16mp5a4 · 2005-10-10 19:06 · #B263826

You have to design your system first. Plan everything out, then start designing the program. You dont have to design the system first, you could just start typing, but that usually doesn't get you very far unless you have an incredibly sharp mind and memory.
I have a small part of a design, tells you three parts of the bs.
The turn system.
The battle formula.
The text and picture system.
you do those three things, you can pretty much make any turn based system. To further it, start the next level of detail, define the turn system, aka player turn, enemy turn. Battle formula, wich variables go into it, how is hp reduced. The text system is pretty much last, because once you have the core stuff, you can then show everything and let the user visually see whats going on. Make sure you organize your code, and be sure to place comments! for personal commenting, hardly ever use "what you are doign" with comments, explain "why you are doing it". I find that to be very useful.
//We need a key to decide which action to do.
Mwin("Press akey") //Shows text
I usually put the "why" above the block of code, and the "what" to the right of the command. When making tutorials, or explaining stuff, always do both.
