### Colin · 2006-01-31 23:33 · #B294097

Shakespeare's existence is supported by his acquaintances, multiple contemporary accounts of his life, and many other things that leave a paper trail. Jesus's existence, on the other hand, is supported by no contemporary accounts (the New Testament is written at least thirty years after his life based on letters supposedly written by his disciples). There is a semicontemporary account that also mentions Jesus (though it obviously omits the fictional miracles), and based on that and just the fact that it's not that far-fetched to believe he existed in the first place, I think it's safe to say he existed.
With regards to proving Jesus didn't perform miracles, it's probably not required to safely say he didn't. It's far more likely that he didn't and it's (generally) more logical to prove the less likely option. For example, if somebody said "I'm Elvis", we would expect him to present proof that he is; we would not believe it if we could not find evidence that disproved it (say, because it's an unknown person). Religion is based on faith, though, not proof, and for patrons of a given religion, their faith alone (not the actual existence of a god [regardless of whether their god exists]) compels them in some way.
As for "pushing religion out", it's inevitable that the world become secular. I think, however, that there will always be a place for at least the morality systems of religions, which are useful to many people.

### Ersuis · 2006-02-01 01:06 · #B294117

But likewise, if you want to continue with the Shakespeare example, you have to compare Shakespeare with the authors of the Bible; the existence of the Bible is the proof of there being authors, and the collected works of Shakespeare is the proof of Shakespeare living (or alternatively, the proof of a writer or group of writers we call Shakespeare).
To compare the existence of Jesus from accounts in the Bible would at best mean you'd have to compare Jesus to a character in a Shakespearean play based on history, such as Henry IV or Julius Caesar, or at worst to a fictional play like Macbeth or King Lear. Biblical passages referring to Jesus don't prove his existence, much like passages in Julius Caesar don't prove the existence of Caesar and passages in Macbeth don't offer support for Lady Macbeth (odd... every 'passages' I've written here all line up on the left side of the text box).
Likewise, there need to be other proofs that someone existed besides accounts in what, by all standards accepted for normal stories, is fiction (note I'm not disputing the claim that the Bible is written on the basis of historial supernatural events, but that by the measures we hold to other literature it appears to be fiction). To find proof of Caesar, you go back to historical records of the times, to find proof of Shakespeare and ancient Jews you go back to the historical records, and to find proof of Jesus you would go back to historical records (of which, I don't dispute, there seem to be some). Saying that Jesus existed due to accounts in the Bible doesn't follow.
Logically, if what we think we know about the universe holds true (that is, that mathematical principles govern how things behave, and it certainly seems to hold true because things stay the same), then dark matter would logically follow from a mathematical model. The placebo effect logically follows from theories of the brain and its governance over chemicals that control pain, and cold fusion doesn't logically follow at all because nobody trustworthy has ever successfully validated claims of cold fusion. My reasoning is that Jesus didn't perform miracles because it appears to us that what has happened previously continues to happen into the future and that miracles currently don't happen now; inductive reasoning isn't proof solid, however, so you're very much free to dispute my interpretation. I won't question your reaction, since quite frankly I don't care what you believe and everyone is free to have their own beliefs on how things work; perhaps you don't feel that the past is a good indication of how the present works, or that you believe that miracles on the magnitude of Jesus' gigs still happen.
I wish you the best of luck.
