# Crits on my story?

### Lisha · 2006-02-12 19:40 · #B297233

In the quote box below is my story. I'm pretty proud of it, though it's not my best work, because for me to write anything even close to being under ten pages long is a big accomplishment. Laughing Hehe. It's really metaphorical, so see if you can figure out what it's about. It's probably really easy, but being the one who wrote it it's hard for me to tell. Wink
Here you go:

**Quote:**

> The land was colorless, flat, and empty. It looked as if God had forgotten to paint this plot of earth with life, as if he had somehow skipped over it. The dirt was hard and crusty, and the air felt silent and dead. Yes, the place was lifeless.
> Except for one spot, one long, curving, neat road, where God must have dragged his paintbrush while moving across this spot. The road had a floor of thriving green grass, and along the sides of it trees and vines stretched and reached to lock their thin, twisted fingers together over the top. Flowers wound themselves around the bases of the trees. Gods paintbrush surely had swept across the earth to make this road.
> She stood there, at the beginning of the road, staring down its curled path. It frightened her, yet it comforted her. She was drawn to it. It called to her in great rasping whispers. Part of her felt content and willing to walk the road; the other part pulled against the other like a dog tethered to a burning post.
> She couldnt tell which part of her was right. Should she tread this road, this mysterious road, or should she turn around and leave? She fought with herself violently; it seemed whatever reason there was to just leave, there was an equally good reason not to.
> But either way, whatever she would have chosen, she found herself taking a single step onto the road. And another. And another. Before she knew what she was doing, before she could tell herself to stop, she was walking the road with quick, easy steps. And she couldnt bring herself to halt.
> She gazed over the horizon, wondering how long this road was, and where it led. But there was no way to tell. The horizon masked the destination of the road, and hid it from sight. Her stomach twisted in fear a bit when she saw this, but she kept on walking.
> She walked for a long, long time. She walked through twists and loops and curves, through rain and snow and bitter cold and blistering heat. But she never stopped, even though she longed to sometimes. She couldnt stop. Her legs kept moving, her feet kept making rhythmic thumps on the ground, and she kept moving forward.
> Sometimes people walked with her. When she first started out, a man and a woman were with her, walking just ahead of her and smiling back at her sometimes. Then, after quite some time of walking, they veered off the road together. As soon as they left the road, she could no longer see them.
> There were other people too. A little blonde girl, an old, frail man, they all came and walked with her for awhile. But they all left, too, and she could see them no more.
> Until a boy, actually more of a young man, came, and he walked next to her. He came on to the path almost right after the man and the woman had left, and he stayed there. Unlike the couple, who had walked just ahead of her, or the little girl, who had walked just behind her, he walked right beside her.
> They still walked, walked for a long time. More people came and went. One child, then another, came and walked with them for a very long time, then left. But she held her eyes on the horizon, wondering when they would reach their destination.
> Until once, when she looked away from the horizon and at the boy, she saw that wrinkles were at the corners of his eyes and on his face and hands, and that his hair was thin and white and wispy. She looked at her own hands and saw that the fingers were almost as thin as those of the trees and that knobby knuckles showed through the skin. She felt her face and felt folds of rough skin. She was older.
> But she kept walking.
> It wasnt much longer, though, before she began to see, just as a speck on the horizon, the road rise up into the clouds. The speck got bigger and bigger, and as they were almost there the boy - an old man now - gave her a sad wave and left the path, leaving her alone.
> The part where the road rose into the sky came closer and closer with every step she took. Before long she was there, at its base, and finally, finally, she was able to stop herself.
> She stood there, at the beginning of the ascent, staring up its curled path. It frightened her, yet it comforted her. She was drawn to it. It called to her in great rasping whispers. Part of her felt content and willing to walk the ascending road; the other part pulled against the other like a dog tethered to a burning post.
> She couldnt tell which part of her was right. Should she tread this ascent, this mysterious ascent, or should she turn around and leave? She fought with herself violently; it seemed whatever reason there was to just leave, there was an equally good reason not to.
> But either way, whatever she would have chosen, she found herself taking a single step onto the ascent. And another. And another. Before she knew what she was doing, before she could tell herself to stop, she was walking the ascent with quick, easy steps. And she couldnt bring herself to halt.
> It was a short walk, and she came to the top within minutes. When she got there, she suddenly was relieved of all her fatigue, and a wave of peacefulness washed over her and soothed her. The world around her blurred. Her eyes were closing. Her body was relaxing.
> She died then. She died happy, though, happy that she had walked the road, happy that she had felt it beneath her feet, happy for the people that had walked beside her.
> Happy that she had tread that road, that road where Gods paintbrush had dripped the paint of life.
