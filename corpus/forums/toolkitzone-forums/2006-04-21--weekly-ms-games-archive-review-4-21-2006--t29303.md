This week's Review
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_1.gif)
Jewel of Destiny
Release date: 1999, 2002, 2005
Creator: Awesome Computing
Author: CBM
When the TK2 made its arrival, it was probably a big stepping stone to the current TK community. I wasn't around during that time so I wouldn't know but I do know that ppl were looking forward to it. The idea of anyone creating games is the TK's philosophy. With an easy to navigate interface, easy to learn language, and the forums to ask for aide, this was possibly with the TK. Another item that was available was a demo game. Almost every game engine has a demo game but the TK is prob the only one who has a demo game that is worthy of being a game itself. I wouldn't be certain though since I only tried like 3 other game makers.
Jewel of Destiny isn't groundbreaking. The programming isn't like Deceit and Conquest, the graphics aren't Heraldry material, and the music isn't something you can dance to but this game is a nice demo. Very clean and cut. Though the game will forever remain unfisnished since the TK Dev only gave us a dinky little demo.
Jewel of Destiny has 2 versions, millenium and powerhouse. The millenium version was also ported to TK3 by Dude Man. So there are 3 JOD games, which I shall review them here.
Plot
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_2.gif)
Millenium (2 & 3) = In a small village of Shal, there is a jewel that protects the fair town but one day, a young lad has a weird dream. He and his pal go to investigate it and find out the jewel is gone. The only thing they find is a key on the floor called the Keystone. What is the keystone and what happened to the jewel? Our 2 heroes set out to discover answers.
Powerhouse = The people of Bushhole discovered a land called Hylanka. In this town, a jewel protects them from giants that live underground. A young man woke up after dreaming of the jewel. Getting an odd feeling, he and his friend go to the hall where the jewel is held, only to find that it's gone. The only thing they find is a key on the floor. They seek the audience of the king to find out answers.
The stories are sorta cliche but not so much as to say it comes from other sources.
Rating: 8/10 for having a very nice story.
Graphics
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_3.gif)
Millenium (2 & 3) = The tiles are very cartoony and bright. I dunno who drew the char tilesets but they must be well made since newbs keep using them for their projects. Yeah you know what I mean. The majority of the games in MS use these tile sets, which in a way, demeans them. These tilesets have been used so much, only JOD uses them well. I have not played another TK game that reuses these tiles in a well managed fashion. Anyways they are bright and beutiful. They look kinda akward in the way they move but it's all good.
I dunno where CBM get these graphics or if he made him but I wish he made them look a bit better. Not that they look terrible, it's just that they look like they dont blend with the game.
Powerhouse = These char tilesets are very plain. As if they were taken from version 1. They is no dynamics in them. Well maybe the knights in the castle. Little effort was put in this. The outside tilesets have obviously been edited with the tile editor. All of these tilesets are really boring. To compesate for them though is the use of lighting. Look inside the building where the jewel is suppose to be stored. It looks like it is glowing a red light. Very nice touch.
Rating: 7/10 for at least having good tiles.
Media
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_4.gif)
Millenium (2 & 3) = The music was developed by the Reeves brothers. They are nice to listen to. Though there are upbeat and smooth sounds, they are too low of tempo to stand out. Well regardless they have become classics in the TK community. Even if you change the name of the file, ppl will automatically recognize it's a Reeves creation.
The SFX are too recognizable since ppl will usually just take these since SFX are hard to find. They aren't too impressive. The basic ones like the opening of the chest are alright but the monster sounds are pathetic.
Powerhouse = The music is mostly classical including Chopin, Pachable, and Champion. Very good selection. There are 3 MP3's and they are techno tunes. The SFX here are pretty much the same as the millenium version.
Rating:
Music- 4/5 for having good sounding music to go with the game.
Sound- 3/5 for having dull sounds.
Programming
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_5.gif)
Millenium (2 & 3) & Powerhouse = The programming is all very basic. Of course this is just a demo game for the TK so you gotta keep it simple. It's simple but it's enjoyable as well. The use of variables are done well. There are some places you cannot enter or leave without activating another variable, like the school to learn RPGCode. You'll need Tano to enter the building because without him, you cannot enter. Also version 2 has paralaxing effects. It's mainly used for the oversight of the mountain in the hill. It's a nice touch.
Rating: 8/10 for keeping it simple and still making it impressive.
Enviroment
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_6.gif)
Millenium (2 & 3) = Not so much. Your mostly spending all your gaming time in the town of Shal. The overworld is really small. It's only a single island with a bunch of trees and another town that's empty too. There's not much to do in shall but it does get to the main points of RPGCoding so it's a plus.
Powerhouse = A bit bigger her but it's the same. Mountains, trees, two towns, and a pier. There is more stuff to do but with limits. Most of the towns have buildings you cannot enter (either the door was locked or the programmer was to lazy to make more boards). The world does seem kinda small, even though you do alot. You can meet the king, escape monsters through the forest, explore a cave, all in the same place.
Rating: 7/10 for being small yet having the feeling of being spacious.
Characters
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_7.gif)
Millenium (2 & 3) = The hero of this game is Frap. He has a dream about the jewel and investigates. His friend Tano is a member of the RPGCode school and knows #AttackAll(), making him a good companion. What is the background between the two? Who knows. The demo doesn't realease much.
Powerhouse = Frap is a young man living in the village of Hylanka who is the decendant of the first wiseman. He has a dream about the jewel and discovers it missing. He meets up with Argat who happens to be a decendant too, making the too related. Argat is in charge of keeping the jewel safe. Tano is Frap's friend and that's pretty much it. There is a char in the game files called Habra but he's not in the game. Lastly, there is also a cameo appereance from Jed. Whose Jed? Play the demo game from Version 1.
Rating: 7/10 for good protagonists but lacking material.
Menu
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_8.gif)
Millenium (2) = What's this? Tano's profile pic is of a black man and his tile is that of a caucasian. That's just weird. Frap's looks alright at least. Where'd CBM get these pics? Anyways, the menu looks bland. It's the same old from every other game but since this is a game demonstrating the TK, I guess it's alright.
Millenium (3) = The menu was ommited for some reason.
Powerhouse = Some things dont change. The char profiles are the same as the Millenium's version, and their tileset pics are WAY OFF! Tano looks more whiter here and Frap does not look anything like the Millenium's version. BAH! Whatever. The menu is the same here.
Rating: 5/10 for having a somewhat troubling profile pics.
Battles
![](http://i19.photobucket.com/albums/b187/TKGB/jod_9.gif)
Millenium (2) = Well you fight overgrown bugs and rodents. What fun. The battles here are really balanced. Everyone is not too strong or too weak. The #AttackAll() has a explosion effect, which is pretty nice. The animations are well too.
Millenium (3) = The battle was ommited. Dude Man, what were you thinking?
Powerhouse = What's with the techno music here? The battles are pretty much the same. You fight giant bugs and rodents but also witches and eyes. This game even has a boss. There are little powers from the main chars. Frap throws a rock and Argat heals.
Not the most impressive display but it's good enough to be playable.
Rating: 7/10 for making good use of the default BS but could of been better.
Gameplay
Millenium (2) = It's kinda choppy if you turn off the paralaxing but if you turn it on, it runs smoothly but it's slow. ACK! Well you can't win em all. Everything goes fine. No bugs or anything which is assumed since this was created by the creator of the TK.
Millenium (3) = You can walk by pixels which doesn't really make any difference in the gameplay. Everything runs much faster here.
Powerhouse = ACK! It's über slow. When you leave the first board which is Tano's house, it takes like half a minute to load up the board. OI! The chars walk really slowly and laggy. This is only experienced in the towns though. Everywhere else it's fine.
Rating: 7/10 for having bumpy gameplay but still being playable.
Fun Factor
Millenium (2) = This game I enjoy so much for some reason. I remember playing it back when I was newbie. This game is so nice. The music, the tiles, the breaking of the 4th wall and having the chars interact with the chars. I always get a nostalgia feeling when playing this game.
Millenium (3) = The port has it's chinks like the menu and battles were ommited. Everything is pretty much the same except for the performance of the gameplay. For some reason this game does not give you the same feeling as playing the original version.
Powerhouse = I belive this game is better at some parts and worse in others. The gameplay is worse here since it's a bit more laggy and maybe too fast. The music is so-so. The battles are way better here. The length is also better. The tilesets are WAY WORSE! To play this game, you gotta choose which aspect is more important to you.
Rating: 9/10 for being fun in it's own nolstalgic way.
Conclusion
This game is obviously old but still being mentioned. It's been mentioned in some old TK comics, one of the Aljo bros was once creating a story for it, and most notaceble of all, ppl keep using the graphics, music, and even some of it's programs. Put all those things aside though and focus on what this game really is: a demo. A demo that comes with version 2. Version 2 has come and had it's run but now remains on the shelf but this game will alway sbe remembered.
Recomendation?
Millenium (2) = Well it comes with version 2 so it's not even a question.
Millenium (3) = Not really but it makes good on any TK collection, that is if you collect TK games.
Powerhouse = Only came with the powerhouse edition so I doubt you'll be able to play it, though if some how you manage to get a chance, I say yes.
Overall Rating: 72/100. This game gets a Excellent rating.
