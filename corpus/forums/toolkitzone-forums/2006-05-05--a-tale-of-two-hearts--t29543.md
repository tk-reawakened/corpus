The gernal idea is to show what a person is capable of under pressure. People have survival instincts, just like animals; it's also a metaphor. Sarus tries to get to his mother, intending to be angry with her, and he confronts flames on his way there. Untimately he rises to meet his anger and charges through, but is humiliated because of it, and is stripped down to the point where he has nothing but love left for Para Pa. Then, in an instant, he begins to nurse another grudge.
It's critical that you try to think metaphorically while reading this. I advise taking it a bit more slowly; my target audience really is adults. The story deals with high levels of maturity, morality, love and insanity. There are so many variables I've never even used before; it's quite a challenge. Read it again from the words 'A fire...?' and see if you can spot the metaphor this time.
I also have to make the metaphors blend in perfectly with the story, or it'll be too divided. It's really challenging. Having read through it quickly again, I've spotted some of those confounded confusing spots; I'll iron them out tommorrow. Taking on a project like this is quite a large amount of work. I'm too used to idle fantasies for my own good.
This might please you, though: I used Saphyra's journals - the more recent ones - as inspiration for the more emotional segments of the chapter. It worked pretty well, if I do say so myself. Wink
