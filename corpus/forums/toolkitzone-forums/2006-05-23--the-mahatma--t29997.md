# The Mahatma

### Darwin · 2006-05-23 06:52 · #B322296

Fire.
Her husband was somewhere in the kitchen, calling the fire brigade. If only they woke up earlier, theyd probably be able to deal with it all faster and easier  and now the situation turned desperate. Once again, she summed up everything in her mind. Theres no way to get up the stairs because the whole upper floor burns; her husband got the fire extinguisher from the basement and as soon as he finishes the call, theyll break through the main door and wait for help; her child at the time is with him in the kitchen and waiting for instructions. Itd hopefully all be well.
Done, said her husband as he came back from the kitchen with the fire extinguisher in his hand. The wooden stairs have apparently caught fire by now  if the fire spread to the lower floor, theyd suffocate in a matter of minutes.
Took you long enough. Wheres the boy?, she asked.
I dont know. He left. I thought he was scared and wanted to see you, or something. He moved out of the kitchen without a word. You didnt notice him?
Oh, God, no. God, no!
Screaming his name, she headed to the basement as her husband fought with the flames on the stairs to check the upper floor. But the basement was empty, the frontal yard before the house decorated with the burning logs that fell from the upper floor, smoke rising above the grass.
He cant be on the upper floor, he couldnt have got through the wall of fire. No kid would be stupid enough to do it anyway, and he's four years old already. He was real scared, you saw him.
The hell you mean, he cant be on the upper floor?! Well he isnt here, too. Oh God, oh God.
Have you seen the cat?
I didnt think about the cat, for Gods sake.
Must have been left on the upper floor.
Oh God, shut up about the cat! Where is my son?
The fire outside has been extinguished, they heard a voice from the megaphone. Help has arrived. Can you come out? Is there someone on the upper floor? I repeat...
Yes!, her husband said and pulled her hand. She resisted. He looked at her. If hes alive, theyll find him.
Crying, she followed him.
The minutes or hours  she could not tell  as she waited for the fire brigade to search the house were the longest ones in her life. All until they finally returned, having found two bodies laying among the smoke on the upper floor, a child and an animal. Then more waiting, in the white interior of the hospital as the doctors tried to stop the inevitable. And then nothing, no outside world anymore  only her fury and sadness.
He went for the cat, she thought, he went for the goddamn cat.
