### Spyder · 2006-06-14 09:22 · #B327451

You should be able to get just about any [Socket LGA775](http://www.newegg.com/Product/ProductList.asp?N=2010200280+1070509908&Submit=ENE&SubCategory=280) motherboard. That is, if it fits in your case. Dell's cases should certainly be ATX compliant, but they might use micro-ATX motherboards, depending on the size of your case. You might have to measure your original board to find out what size you can use. Even if it only takes micro-ATX, there should still be plenty of boards out there that use PCI-Express. Hell, if my Shuttle has one, there's sure to be one that can fit in your Dell. [Here's a list](http://www.newegg.com/Product/ProductList.asp?Submit=ENE&N=2010200280+1070509908+1073607587+1075707619&Subcategory=280&description=&srchInDesc=&minPrice=&maxPrice=) of Micro-ATX LGA775 boards supporting PCI Express. Make sure and pay attention to whether or not the board has standard PCI slots or not. Some of them don't, and if you use any kind of PCI cards, you'll certainly want to keep that in mind. [This one](http://www.newegg.com/Product/Product.asp?Item=N82E16813131565) looks like a good choice to me, but I'm not as up to date on what motherboards are good and what is bad, but judging by it's popularity and reviews at NewEgg, as well as the feature set, I don't think you could go wrong with it.

### Spyder · 2006-06-14 14:06 · #B327482

I'm pretty sure MicroATX boards are all pretty much the same size. They mostly look square in demension, while standard ATX boards are quite obviously rectangles. Wikipedia says that the MicroATX standard is 9.6" by 9.6", so if your motherboard is anywhere close to that, then that's what you have. If it's more like 12" by 9.6", then it's a standard ATX. Typically standard ATX boards have more expansion slots as well, with 4 or 5 PCI slots, and an AGP or PCI-Express slot as well, give or take depending on the configuration, whereas a MicroATX board will have 2 or 3 PCI slots, and an AGP or PCI-Express slot.
As for Windows XP, I'm really not sure. I know this question has been brought up before though, but I'm not sure if it was ever answered. If it was any other operating system, even any other version of Windows, I'd say you'd probably have a good chance of being able to swap boards without any problem other than having to install new drivers, but with Windows XP, I swear I've read that Microsoft did something to prevent moving a single installation of Windows from moving between computers as some useless form of copy protection.
On the other hand, if you can swap out every other bit of hardware without problems, I see no reason why you couldn't swap the motherboard out as well. Someone else probably has a stronger answer though.
