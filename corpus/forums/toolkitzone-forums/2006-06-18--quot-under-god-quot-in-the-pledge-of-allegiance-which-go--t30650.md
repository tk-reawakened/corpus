### Dont_Think_So · 2006-06-19 20:03 · #B328935

Indeed; the problem isn't that we could choose not to recite it, the problem is that the government is backing it, which is clearly unconstitutional.
Those who say "it could refer to any God" are really dodging the point; not every believes in a single God, and the government should endorse a group of religions either. Not all of us follow a Hebrew-derived religion, and really, for the most part that's the only group that "God" could refer to. Most other religions involve either no god or many, and though they may not be as popular that doesn't mean they should be ignorred.
As for the whole afterlife thing; for the sake of the religious, I'm going to say there's no way we can definitively know what happens to us after we die. However, by observing the world from a purely scientific, objective view (or as objective as possible; one not affected by religion), Colin's view is by far the most likely. If modern discoveries about the brain and how it works had been made before the birth of these religions, then they wouldn't have needed to come up with hypotheses of what happens to us; we would (hopefully) have used reason as opposed to guesswork.
