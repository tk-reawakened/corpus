# Seraph

### Darwin · 2006-06-19 20:50 · #B328951

Another story I began recently, and which I am not too happy with. It is put in the same setting as "The New Level", though it can't be really told apart from ideology. I tried to reduce "outside world" and its mechanisms in the story to a minimum.
The story, shortly put, is about brainwashing. A psychopath and a terrorist is spared from death sentance to be sent into a facility virtually separated from outside world, where he is subject to attempts of his "re-education". The personell, reduced to 8 people, is forced to change Seraph's personality from a religious fanatic to a believer in their pseudoscientific ideology.
One
Three men stood before the door. Two of them were muscular, of pretty dark skin  their uniforms and weapons on the back revealed that they were soldiers. The third one was held by them tightly and had handcuffs on his arms behind his back. His skin was pale, across the face and the chest were signs cut in blood  well visible, as the man had no shirt on and only trousers. His feet, also, were bare.
The door, a massive pressure-controlled transparent pane, opened to the sides. The three man marched in. The door closed behind automatically. There was no return now, at least for one of the men...
The room they were in was rather spatious, white and without any furniture, except for the desk and two seats on its sides. One man, dressed in white trousers and a white shirt, sat on one of the seats, another one dressed in a same manner was standing. There were touch-sensitive computer panels built-in on two opposite walls, one with the massive pressure door, and the other one behind the desk and the men in white  there was a smaller pressure door on that wall as well.
Let him sit, said the sitting man and the soldiers forced the handcuffed one to sit down on the empty seat. Alright. The man folded his hands on the desk and looked in the eyes of the newcomer. What is your name?
The prisoner was silent. The man in white lowered his sight, reached under the desk and pulled out a notebook computer which he put on the desk and turned on. Ah, I see. Youre not very talkative, arent you? Its sad, considering that I wanted to hear everything from yourself first. But, I dont want to force you to do anything. Ill just have to resort to the information provided to me from other sources... Pity. If youd like to comment on something, feel free to talk.
The man started to type on the keyboard and stare at the screen. Ah, yes. Seraph.
The man looked up suddenly. Oh, yeah, I forgot to mention my own name. I am Prenou. The man he talked to just stared at him with disgust but didnt utter a word.
Yes, Prenou looked back at the screen again and his face got a more serious look. Seraph, the famous Seraph, at least famous in the outside world. According to the information Ive received, youre a, let me quote, a dangerous psychopatic murderer focusing on aggression and destructivity due to incapability of supressing personal desires and biological elements of nature according to the system of values worshipped. Would you agree with that, Seraph?
The handcuffed man raised his sight a little. Several seconds passed in silence as the two men stared at themselves.
I see, Prenou said. I can understand your incomfortable situation and your attitude. Id also want to excuse for my nonsensical question, of course nobody would agree with such a description of a human being, any human being, am I right?
Not waiting for a response that wasnt to come at all anyway, the man looked up on two soldiers and ordered, Stop holding him.
S-sir, said the left soldier in a deep voice, Im not sure if thats allowed. Hes a-...
Stop holding him, youve heard me. No, wait. Youre dismissed. We have our own guards, you may leave.
The soldiers slowly left the grasp, then took several steps back and stared blankly at the handcuffed man. Seraph rose his hand, stenghtened his back, and shook his handcuffed hands.
Your childish attempts to win my trust with demonstrations of false kindness are going nowhere, he said. I suggest you just resign and send me back for a penalty I deserve. I have no time for this... circus.
Very well, said Prenou and turned off, closed his laptop and put his hands on it. But I cant say I see any attempt of you proving the description of you in the files is wrong, my friend. Youre creating an entire, I can say... false image of yourself.
Seraph shudded, lower his sight a bit and for a moment he looked as if he regreted saying anything.
The order remains, now said Prenou to the soldiers, you may go.
Unsure, looking at one another, the soldiers nodded an headed towards the door. The other person in white in the room headed for the computer panel and opened the pressure doors out. The soldiers left. Prenou put the notebook under the table.
An image that doesnt even look well on you, he started talking again. Your self-destructivity and a desire to look like a martyr dont work here. If you didnt get it already, you are here not because somebody respects your life or thinks killing you would be too drastic.
No, my friend., Prenou stood up and started to slowly head towards Seraph, the other person in white stepped back not to interrupt his path. You are here because death penalty is not enough. Its because death penalty is what you want and what youre not going to get. Youre here to face a fate worse than death, at least what probably should seem worse than death to your rather specific mind now... youre going to be cleared, my friend. Purified entirely and completely. Cleansed of any imperfection. Youre going to fall down on your knees and accept yourself and your biological animalistic nature then to raise and become a perfect construct of logic and reason.
I guess Ive broken the illusion about me so far, he went on, standing straight before the handcuffed mans sit, if you deserve to maintain yours, fine, but its not going to get you anywhere. You arent going to be sent back. We care for your tormented soul and until it becomes purged, its not going to leave this building.
And what would you call this, Seraph spoke, if not a torturous pervention of a mind?
I would call it sanctity. Let me tell you something, youve left your god or gods the moment you stepped into this building. In this limited reality, we are the God. We are the omnipotent, benevolent God who is not going to rest until its child, who transgressed against its will, is led back on the straight path.
Blasphemies!, Seraph shouted and turned his head to the side, starting at the floor.
Obviously., Prenou said, again sitting down behind the desk and folding his hands before the notebook. Indeed, I got a bit carried over by my egoistic nature aspirating for what it can never become. I forgot that in this reality, you are in the centre. And now youre disillusioned, so let me get back to the original point: What is your name?
Seraph.
Its not your real name.
No. Is Prenou your real name?
I dont even know this. I am using it since Ive been here, so it became one. I know no other.
Neither do I.
A smart response. Youre not an idiot, arent you?
My wisdom is nothing compared to the wisdom I serve.
Bang, wrong. Prenou stood up and put hands on the table, starting at Seraph from above. The wisdom you serve is a cancer upon your mind. Its a trap for your personality, a leech sucking out your intellect. Youre going to get rid of it, Seraph.
A leech, sucking? Ha, Seraph made a naturalistic expression of hatred on his face, only an idiot would use such terms to refer to God himself!
Is it God that wanted you to kill?
Yes.
Wouldnt you call such a God an idiot?
Id call ourselves idiots compared to his wisdom.
Are you aware that most representatives of your religion condemn your act and consider it opposing the words of your God?
Yes, and I consider religion personal. A thing of true believers, not rotten institutions.
Do you consider your actions opposing the words of God?
No. Truth is in the scriptures. Or are you going to argue about this?
No, I dont care about the scriptures. And in the end, youre not going to either. Youre going to view scriptures as nonsense, nothing of importance greater than childhood fables.
Theyre holy. Im not going to believe otherwise.
Its not their holiness, its your point of view. Nothing unchangeable. But I guess youll understand after its all over.
Ill never understand. I found my faith and nothing is going to change it.
Would you be ready to suffer for your faith? Would you view it as a virtue, of a sort?
Yes.
Very well.
Prenou stood up again and whispered something to the other man in white for a longer time. Then, using the computer panel he opened the door on the back of the room and they both left. Seraph was left in silence for a while. He made no attempt to struggle or try to get out. It was hopeless and unnecessary. Sitting, he tried to relax, and looked up at the ceiling separating him from the sky somewhere above.
God., he said aloud, and knew that no more words were necessary.
Two
The back doors opened again and another man in white, one he has never seen before, emerged. He was much more muscular than the rest, and wore some sort of leather armor. His hands were covered in gloves and Seraph could hear his metal boots make sound s he walked on the white marble floor.
The man moved the desk back a bit and stood straight before Seraph.
God, he said aloud, all of sudden. As Seraph, surprised, raised his eyes to look at the man and ponder why would he say such a word, he struck his face with a fist. Seraph was knocked from the chair, which with a loud sound fell on the floor due to the impact, and laid knocked on the floor, rising his head as the man drew closer again.
God is my strength!, he shouted.
God is my strength!, the man repeated, raising his arms and seconds later, kicking Seraphs face with the metal boot. Now he could feel that it was also spiked. Red scratches marked his face.
This is Gods will! shouted the man and this time kicked Seraph in the crotch. He curled, but couldnt bear it. He screamed in pain as with the mans next words, Salvation! or something of that sort  he didnt seem to care anymore  a kick landed on his chest and he had trouble getting air. Struggling, he felt the man lifted him and holding him tightly, repeating some more words mentioning God and containing religious terms, banged his head on a white wall. As Seraph started to lose consciousness and fell on the floor, the man opened the door and grabbed him again, this time to carry him to a completely different place of residence.
Three
When Seraph woke up, he didnt have handcuffs on. He was in a light, entirely white room, much smaller than the one he was in earlier, and he was completely naked. He could feel his head ache, but he managed to sit down on the floor. The room was extremely bright, there seemed to be a mirror on its ceiling, and the only door, metal, but also painted white, appeared locked. Suddenly, the light turned off for a second and went back on. The metal door opened and some man, he couldnt tell whether one he has already seen, threw a white sleeping back and a white pillow at him. Then, the doors closed.
Seraph grabbed both things  they were comfortable in touch, very comfortable, and not thinking much, he got in the sleeping back and put his head on the pillow, laying on the marble floor similar to the one his previous laying-on was very unpleasant. But now he could ignore the pain. As if subject to his desires, the lights turned out and he was alone again.
He had no idea what could happen when he woke up, but neither did it matter. It seemed to him as if this night  or at least the time of day that he was unable to precisely know  was his last. I could spend an eternity like this, he thought, resting.
God, he whispered again, starting at the ceiling.
Four
Prenou was standing in a dark room and looking at the computer screen. The floor contained a large glass pane in the middle, under which another light room existed  the one Seraph was currently sleeping in. Prenou sighed, and turned as somebody entered his room. It was another person in white, a younger one.
Greetings, Labbe., Prenou said. What is it?
News. Melanogasters coming.
Oh, damn it! Prenou turned around for a while, but turned back soon and looked straight at Labbe. When?
Tommorow.
Whats that supposed to mean? Oh, this doesnt mean anything very good.
He doesnt want us to get prepared.
Theres no physical possibility for us toget prepared! Seraph arrived this morning. That human is insane.
Hes going to determine the time boundaries of the therapy.
Oh. So its going to happen...
Prenou turned, walked a few steps and stared at the pane behind which Seraph was visible. His room was significantly smaller than the one they were now in, but such were the demands for the proper observation of the subject.
What are your prospects, Labbe?
My prospects? The man became rather surprised by the question.
Yes, Prenou turned around, youre relatively new here and you probably still bear enough sanity to answer logically. The whole plan, the whole minimalistic approach of the system melanogaster represents... do you think its going to work?
I dont know. I hope so.
Hopes should be left along with religions at the front door when coming here... Prenou took a step back and returned to the computer. The information we have  and the situation, overally  doesnt fill me with any hope. The personell is reduced to eight people, and Seraph. A psychopath. A murderer. A biological factor-responding animal of no childhood and no education, not to mention a mind filled with nonsensical ideology. And finally, Melanogasters verdict. Considering the situation  lets be realistic. How much time can he give us? Seven, six years?
Look, Prenou, I really dont know. I presume you also dont except me to but rather seek somebody to hear your thoughts.
Youre pretty much right. Oh well, Im disillusioned about the situation, at least. How do you think it goes so far?
A little bit drastic, if I were to talk.
The speech?
No. The beat-up part.
Prenou looked at Labbe and laughed. Ive asked him a question, he responded. He wanted this. He needs to learn that in a logical universe, and this place is one of these, the choices he makes  choices no other than these of factors influencing his mind, which are for a major part, heritage of his idiotic system of values  bear consequences.
Is it a part of the conditioning? To make him hate his religion?
No. Were going to start the purging of his mind soon, though. Its sad we dont have time, really. Is everything prepared?
Yes. All the chemical substances, that is. Guards are ready for the tasks as well.
Good work, Labbe. You should get some sleep.
And you?
Im going to plan the whole scheme out before Melanogaster arrives.
Well do it, Prenou.
We better do it...
Five
Seraph was awakened by a melodic doorbell sound, although a very loud one as well. He didnt got out of his sleeping bag, though he noticed the doors opened and a guard appeared in them.
Stand up and come here, he said.
Im naked, Seraph responded.
The guard sighed and disappeared for a while, closing the door behind. Half a minute later, he reappeared, threw white trousers and a white shirt at Seraph and closed the door again.
Seraph dressed up, then calmly waited until the guard opened the door again. He put handcuffs on him, though he was too sleepy or weak to offer any resistance. Slowly, on his bare feet, he followed the guard through a short corridor and into another room. It looked like a kitchen  there was a white table, behind which Prenou sat down, and near which the same man in white he saw the previous day stood. The seat before the table was empty, presumably reserved for him, and a bowl of rice as well as one of water were standing on his side.
He sat on his own, through the guard still stood nearby.
Like a dog, Seraph said, in a bowl, and how am I supposed to eat without my hands?
Prenou nodded, and Seraphs handcuffs were removed. Prenou handed him a spoon and he started to lazily eat the rice. When he ate around half of the amount in the bowl, he put the bowl aside and with both hands held the one with water and drank everything.
And by the way, there is no toilet in my room, he commented.
The room is temporary. Once we realise you understand the behaviour asked of you, you will gain access to a different part of the facility where you will be free to roam. As for now, knock on the door to signify any needs.
What is this? Seraph, already apparently having finished eating, raised his sight at Prenou. A prison? A lab? A torture chamber? What am I doing here?
Youre undergoing a mental change.
Brainwashing. I hope you understand it works just for people of small faith!
Seraph loudly banged his hands on the table. Prenou picked both bowls up and gave them to the other man in white, who got out of the room through a door on the side with them.
Oh, and by the way, Prenou went on, I hope you didnt misunderstand the yesterdays occurance. Youve made a particular decision which consequences you have been demonstrated. I am not going to judge that decision. You have to understand choice you are put before and their results.
Youre going to need a force stronger than that to break me!
Seraph stood up.
Youre acting too aggressively. Excuse us, but we wont be able to deal with you for a while, as we have a particular visit within an hour or so. You may keep the clothes. Knock whether you need anything.
After these words, and while the other man entered the room again, the guard drew closer to Seraph and held his hands to put his handcuffs on. Seraph made a grinning expression and started at the ground for a while, after which he surprisingly stared at Prenou and smiled in a rather unfriendly manner. The guard put handcuffs on his nonetheless and he turned to follow him back into the room.
As they left, Prenou sighed and stood up.
Hes a psychopath, he commented. The other man nodded. Alright, lets make sure we got eerything ready when Melanogaster comes. Right answers and right questions.
