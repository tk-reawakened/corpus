**About Me:**
I was born on March 5th, 1986 in Cincinnati, Ohio, USA (Not exactly something to be proud of). I became very interested in electronic devices (namely video games) at a young age, due to my father's hefty collection of tape players, stereo receivers, and various other devices that played music or did something cool. My first game system came was the original NES, and it started me on my way. I played Super Mario Bros. all the time, due to the fact that I was five years old and it was not only my first game, for a time it was my only game. I moved up in the world of video games as fast as the industry did, obtaining a SNES 3 days before it's release date, and a MegaDrive (known as the Sega Genesis in the US) for Christmas that year. Games like Sonic the Hedgehog and The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past pushed me a little further into the gaming field. After a bit of time in my elementary school, I ran head first into a brand new device: the computer. The first computer I ever worked on was a Macintosh (don't quite remember the model number..), and I loved it. I just about instantly learned the system and all its idiosyncracies, and I loved it. I moved on to high school and obtained an old machine: An HP Vectra. 66MHz processor, 24 MB of RAM, 1 MB of Video RAM, and not much else. But it was enough to run DOS and QBASIC. I learned how to use QBASIC and made my first attempt at making a game. It ended pretty badly, due to my inexperience with any sort of programming language. I eventually had to scrap this machine and I moved on to an HP Pavillion with a 400MHz Celeron and an 8MB nVidia video card. It was the sweetest machine I had owned and I loved it. However, due to my inexperience with maintaining machines, I also had to scrap this one. I got ahold of another Vectra and used Windows 98 with it. This machine didn't last long because I chose to rid myself of it. For awhile, I was without a computer. Then, through the generosity of a neighbor, I obtained an IBM 300GL, with a P233 MMX and 2 MB of video memory. This computer became my workhorse, being pushed to it's limit everyday running Windows XP and using the RPG Toolkit v2.08 all the way up to 2.2b. I even had version 3 running on it for a while. This machine lasted longer than any other that I had owned, because by the time I obtained it, I was very well experienced in the maintainence of machines. Despite it's age (the BIOS dated the computer back to 1994, and the processor would reflect that), I kept it running very well until 2006 (I obtained it in 2001~2002), when I optimized it as much as possible and gave it to a friend. I now own a monster gaming machine which is currently being used as a file server (which is a big insult to the machine, but due to circumstances eyond my control, that's all it can be used for), and a cheap little Compaq iPaq desktop, which has been upgraded from a PIII 500 to a PIII 850, memory from 128MB PC133 (why it was in the machine is beyond me) to 256 MB PC100 (much better due to the FSB), and from a Maxtor 5400RPM 8.4GB HDD to a hefty Western Digital 7200 RPM 80 GB HDD. Much nicer, in my opinion. I am currently enrolled at Dayton Job Corps Center, attending the A+/Computer Repair class, and working towards the CompTIA Net+ Certification. My next stop is Wright State University, targeting Game Design (or Graphic Design) for my major and Computer Programming for my minor.
