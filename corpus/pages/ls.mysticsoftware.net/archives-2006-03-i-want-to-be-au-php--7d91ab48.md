# "I want to be Audrey Hepburn." - REC 1

March 2006 - 19:57
Ah, my first actual episode. And now, I'll start you off with some screenshots, from the OP, Cheer ~Makka na kimochi~. A rather catchy piece that grows on you, sung by our upstart seiyuu, Kanako Sakai. If you haven't already, obtain a copy of the single.
And we're through the opening. This first episode opens up with a guy standing outside of a building (well, a movie theater actually, but you can't get that from first looks). He makes note of how it looks to rain in one dit-rection, and how it is just a normal sunset in the other dit-rection. After having a sign blow into his leg, mud get splashed on his face, and slightly mocked by a theatergoer, he gives up thinking that he was stood up. He's about to throw away the tickets when a cute voice interrupts him, saying to watch the movie instead of throwing them away.
After saving the tickets from a gruesome death, (Yippee!!!!), our kawaii girl asks for one. The guy reluctantly gives one to her, and she then proceeds to drag him into the theater. (I hope it's a good movie!) Okay, so they're inside now, and to the guy's dismay, kawaii girl reads all the subtitles aloud. (Anybody wish you had even a foam bat for people like that?) Fortunately for the guy, the movie ends, and they exit to the lobby, where he asks kawaii girl why she read all the subtitles. Her reply, a rather interesting one I might add, "I want to be Audrey Hepburn." Cue the ever classic "huh?" response. This conversation is short lived though, as the guy sees the girl who stood him up outside the theater, Tanaka-san, from accounting. (Why is it that they're always from accounting? Or seem to have a similar name to Tanaka? The world shall never know.) Oh, and to slam him into a window, she's with another guy. To the guy: Tough luck there, but hey, maybe one day!
Thought the guy was totally slammed in the dumps? Thought he didn't have a chance in the world? Well think again, kawaii girl offers to comfort him in exchange for the movie. Cue scene change: Restaurant! Now we get to see a slightly drunk kawaii girl, and the guy, talking about dreams. We now learn that kawaii girl is a seiyuu...in training. (Is it a coincidence that Kanako-san is rather new on the scene too? Nah, didn't think so.) Or, depending on how you want to translate it, she's an "egg" of seiyuu, or a 'shell' of a seiyuu, or a seiyuu 'egg.' There's gotta be some pun involved in that, but alas, my Japanese is still very poor.
Well, I digress, ahem, the guy ends up making a comment about how she's young and can have dreams. Kawaii-seiyuu-in-training girl responds by calling him old and not having any dreams (ever the optimist) while he replies that he's only 26. A pessimist, that young? What is the world coming to? After leaving the restaurant, with the guy paying for most of it, they travel home together, when they learn that they live in the same neighborhood. (Can somebody smell, setup? I should stop with the quips. I really like the series so far, I do, but I can't help but to add in these quips.) They part ways, after it starts to rain, with kawaii-seiyuu-in-training girl quoting an Audrey Hepburn line.
The guy then has a nightmare, losing his own voice, with kawaii-seiyuu-in-training girl doing his voice, giving up on Tanaka-san, from accounting.
After waking up, to sirens and flashing lights, the guy proceeds to have a quick run of bad luck, from somebody or something stumbling over him, to breaking his glass. Ouch, what luck. He then walks outside, sees that there's a fire in the area, and looks for kawaii-seiyuu-in-training girl. He finds her sitting on a swing, holding a pillow, in a rather...catatonic state. She's got nowhere else to go, no relatives, and nothing's left in the apartment. He offers her his room for the night, and they return to his apartment. Following a shower and getting dressed, he offers her his bed for the night. She then starts to cry about all she's lost, while he tells her that he'll buy everything, well, what can be replaced at least.
Our scene then progresses to what many other blogs have hated, the alluded sex scene. (My opinions on this are to follow.) Finally, we get to introductions. We learn that kawaii-seiyuu-in-training girl is Onda Aka, age 20. We also learn that the guy is Matsumaru Fumihiko, age 26, and that he works for a sweets company. Now it's morning. Matsumaru wakes up to find Aka-chan missing, save for a note that says she's returned to the agency. (We must have a spy. Nah.) End Episode 1.
I didn't mean for this post to turn out the way it may seem. I really do love the series so far, especially Aka-chan. Aka-chan kawaii yo Aka-chan. It may seem like I ridiculed it a little, even in an MST3k style, but that wasn't my intent. Sure some of it is a little agressive humor at t-rec's expense, but would you rather a boring, drab post? It adds a little flavor, I think.
About the alluded sex scene. I don't really see what's the problem. Many other bloggers find it out of place, crude, and uncalled for. Sure, it out of place. Although crude and uncalled for seems to attack it a little harshly. On the mention of crude - it's all alluded, and as says, it could've just been one long kiss all night. I doubt that, but I don't think things got as heated as the others do. *is* [Phoenix512](http://phoenix512anime.blogspot.com/2006/02/t-rec-1.html)
I like the series so far, having watched up to episode 4, which leaves me a bit of catch up to do. At 12 minutes per episode, and only 9 episodes, it seems a little short. It's interesting though, I have an attraction to short series though. I'm sorry if I offended any other lovers of the series with my quips, I didn't mean anything that strongly, but once something's written, one tends not to retract it, a few things do make some sense. Then again, none of that matters really because of Aka-chan! Aka-chan kawaii yo Aka-chan!
