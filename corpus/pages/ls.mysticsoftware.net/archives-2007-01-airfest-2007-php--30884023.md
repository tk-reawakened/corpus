AirFest 2007 - My Air Rewatch
One of the advantages of broadband is the ability to download large files quickly. While quickly is a relative term (I remember the time it took to download a single episode, at least 12 hours if I was lucky), it still serves to make a point. It can be said that most of my anime collection is "computer based," that being the case, there are backups of it everywhere. Unfortunately, the disc containing Air could not be found. This led to my taking advantage of my broadband connection, and "restocking" my collection.
Even at the time of writing this post, I'm not sure what I want to do with it. So, what you're about to experience in reading this, what I'd like to get across, are some of my thoughts on a number of things. Read on, I don't bite.
![I'd be remiss not to mention Kanna!](/images/anime/post_kanna_01.jpg)
**I'd be remiss not to mention Kanna!**
About a week and a half ago, I had decided to do an Air rewatch. Of course, this was brought on by somebody else's rewatch (of whom I'd link, but can't find at the moment.)
This rewatch was rather interesting, to say the least. I had intended to marathon the entire series last weekend, but events sorta led me to going day by day with as much as I could. Even to date, I haven't finished the rewatch on the Summer Special, but that doesn't concern this post so much as it'd only reinforce my love of Kanna. You need only look at the picture above...or the banner above that. You could even look at the fact that I have preordered a Kanna PVC figure due out this March, available from , but this post isn't about Kanna -- yet. [HLJ](http://www.hlj.com/)
I suppose I'll get onto the meat of what I want to write about. After a quick synopsis, going block by block.
- Episode One: This really serves to introduce us to the main characters. We get to see some clumsy Misuzu action, learn that she's on summer vacation, but still has to attend summer classes. We meet Kirishima Kano and Poteto. We also meet Tohno Minagi and Michiru. As most, it's a simple introductory episode. Oh, we meet the protagonist - Kunisaki Yukito.
- Episodes Two through Four: Kyoto Animation (yes, Air's TV adaptation is KyoAni's work) moves right into Kano's character arc. For those unfamiliar with Air, there are 3 character arcs in the first overall story arc of the game -- and series. We learn that she has lost her mother while young, her older sister and father have raised her. We learn that she wears a ribbon on her wrist that, when she takes it off after growing up, will allow her to use magic. We learn that this is not actually the case as she had an interesting experience regarding a feather at the shrine, and afterwards, tried to slit her wrist. We learn that the feather contains memories from hundreds of years ago that gave Kano a split personality. We learn that Yukito has interesting spiritual powers and resolves the situation surrounding Kano and the feathered possession.
- Episodes Five and Six: Now we move into Minagi's character arc. This is a little more involved than Kano's arc, in a way. Minagi was supposed to have had a little sister, Michiru, but her mother miscarried and shortly thereafter, the relationship between her father and mother got strained. Minagi's father had left to another town remarried. Minagi's mother had been traumatized by the events, leading to her forgetting about Minagi and thinking that she's Michiru. It reaches a critical point when she forgets about having any children at all. We now learn that the Michiru we've seen throughout the series is really and manifestation of a dream, both Minagi's and her mother's. Resolution is achieved by this realization, and Minagi's mother regains her wits, and recognizes Minagi as her daughter, Minagi. Michiru was brought to us from the power of another feather, which had to return to the sky.
- Episode Seven: This is really all we get of Misuzu's character arc for now. We learn more about Yukito's power, that Misuzu has a "condition" that prevents her from really getting close to people, and that it may kill her. Yukito leaves, comes back, and makes realizations about what his mother had told him, and how he can 'save' Misuzu, and the girl in the sky. The end of this episode takes us back 1000 years, and brings the end of the Dream story arc.
- Episodes Eight and Nine: Eight really starts the Summer story arc, of which more detail is available in the summer special. We're introduced to Ryuuya, Uraha, and Kanna (Kannabi no Mikoto). We learn Kanna is a winged being, and despite being in a palace, is actually being imprisoned to keep her powers in check. We also learn that her mother is imprisoned on a mountain elsewhere. Ryuuya, Uraha, and Kanna break out of the palace in search of Yaobikuni, Kanna's mother. They make their way to meet her mother, and finally do. The meeting results in disaster - Yaobikuni is shot and killed by cheap arrows, and in an effort to escape, Kanna is trapped in the sky by a Buddhist curse. We learn of Ryuuya and Uraha's legacy and "magical" power. The Summer story arc is now "finished".
- Episodes Ten through Twelve: The final Air story arc begins. We get a flashback to Yukito's "sacrifice" and his subsequent reawakening as a crow, whom Misuzu finds and names Sora. We see the events of Misuzu's character arc from the Dream arc through Sora's perspective. Later on, we see more of Yukito's realization in the transformation. The final elements of this arc are Haruko's realization of her desire to be Misuzu's mother, the conflict when Misuzu's father wants to take her back, Haruko's efforts to be Misuzu's mother, and Misuzu's passing. Air has been completed.
AirFest 2007 also included the theatrical release, of which I should note, has voicing Yukito ( ), as he did in the game, as opposed to voicing in the tv series. The movie wrap's Misuzu's Dream and Air arcs with the Summer arc being told from the perspective of a town legend, by Misuzu. There are also some differences in the movie that change, ever so slightly, what goes on. Instead of summer classes, Misuzu is conducting field research as a summer project, enlisting Yukito's aid to travel from place to place. Kanna's story is more apparent with her love surrounding Ryuuya, but vague on how she gets trapped in the sky (by being shot by arrows, and not falling.)
Falling back to the core of things I wanted to say, with regards to the anime, if one pays attention to the end of episode twelve, where the two children are talking together, you see a switch to Misuzu and Yukito on the seawall (with Misuzu waving). Many have thought that the children represent Misuzu and Yukito, which I won't deny, and I'll further to add Ryuuya and Kanna into the mix, but I'd like to say that this scene is the same as one in episode ten where she's waving at kids on the beach while sitting next to a sleeping Yukito.
Now, if you're expecting some philosophical insight or something, I don't think I have any of that. Rather, I'll go on about KyoAni's production values. The way they animated Air was masterful, albeit, with only 13 episodes, some parts were a little rushed. The only thing that's a little...off...are the tears. They seem more like rivers, and not even so 'realistic' in how they are, but the effect of the scenes remain. The animation from KyoAni is quite memorable that when I got to some flashbacks in episode twelve, I actually considered, and have expressed the desire to myself, to rewatch it again. The initial reaction was more of a rewatch now, but I'll give it a little time and do a summer rewatch probably.
For those who don't know, Air is Key's third (well, technically second, but I count ONE as a Key work), game, following after Kanon. Using Kanon 2006 as a benchmark for accuracy in the story, it's safe to say that Air is pretty true to the original game. Key's gift of storytelling is rather insane. They have a way of creating characters that you get wrapped up with, and start to pull on your emotional strings. Such as Misuzu's Aozora scene. For someone unprepared, yet totally engrossed in the story, they're likely to be in tears by the end.
Key's style tends to end with one of bittersweet melancholy, while retaining (what seems to me) an underlying theme of hope. All one has to do is just lookup Kanon summaries for each character, and they'll find little taglines at the end of each that give a sense of hope about each character. Air's execution of hope is different. Kano's story doesn't end with hope, really, more of life. Minagi's story definitely has a hope element to it with her half-sister, who's name is never revealed. One is lead to conclude, though, that it is Michiru, given the cutout of when she says her name, and the visual reference. She looks like a younger Michiru. Misuzu's flag of hope is her "goal." Air's overall hope is apparent in the scene I called out above, with the young kids on the beach, and Misuzu waving. I think it's safe to say that Key has hope in the bag.
I think that takes care of most of what I wanted to do. I didn't really have a planned direction for this post, and I'll prolly have more pop-up as I think of it, but I think this is it for now. Maybe some day this week, or next weekend, I'll play pseudo-catchup with Kanon.
