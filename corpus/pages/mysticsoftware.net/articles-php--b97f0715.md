# Hello

Mystic Software and its staff have been a part of the community for some time now. We have seen countless numbers of new people, and new games, most of which don't stick around long enough to get a single demo onto the internet, let alone make an impact on the community. However, there are a rare few that stand above the crowd, and such is the case for Cyrus Martin's "Deceit & Conquest". First released when the TK was still taking baby steps on the world wide web, Deceit & Conquest was soon to become the most acclaimed TK game available. Today, after no less than six releases of the game, "Deceit and Conquest" has evolved beyond the capabilities of the RPG Toolkit, and is instead being written as a series of novels that will much more accurately and completely, detail the world of Seor, and the characters that inhabit it. This interview was not only conducted to raise interest in the game and novels, but to possibly give others inspiration for their own projects, in hope that others may see the same success that Mr. Martin and "Deceit and Conquest" have seen. Now that the RPG Toolkit is open source, every successful game is not only a success for the author(s), but for the entire community as well. **Hello**
> Cyrus: Hey man!
**So, how did you come up with the world of 'Seor'?**
> Cyrus: Well I was actually interested in creating an entire world from scratch for a long time but I didn't really expect to actually do it. I think that if I had never stumbled on The RPG Toolkit that I would probably have never written anything of any real length. It sort of reinvigorated me and pushed me to go through with my dream.
**Did you plan on making the game first, or did it actually start off as a novel and work it's way to becoming an RPG?**
> Cyrus: I had zero interest in writing a novel when I came up with the idea of D&C. I honestly just didn't feel adequate as a writer. Authoring a book is a huge undertaking and a big responsibility. I feel that if I am to write a book that I owe the reader a lot more than if I were to make a small little game. It took me years before I got up the confidence to actually write it as a book and I might never have done so if I hadn't already maximized the RPG Toolkit's potential.
**Now that the book is written, would you say that your satisfied with the content?**
> Cyrus: Well I'm actually not done. I ran behind a little because I have been trying to finish my film "Dawn of Twilight" and I had to spend a lot more time on it than I expected. I am in the process of finishing it up though so I'll reserve judgment on that. I will say though that I am MUCH more satisfied with the way the story can be told as a novel than as a game. I really just started completely over when I decided to write this out as a book and I think the story and characters have benefited from that immensely. The reader is in for a treat.
**Do you think the story has changed to the extent that a D&C fan may not enjoy it as much as they did the game?**
> Cyrus: That's a good question and it has been one of my main concerns when writing this book. There's not a simple way to answer that actually so I'll put it this way... I was personally not happy with the way I had to summarize the story when it was in the game. I was also not happy with building a story around a typical video game architecture. I really painted myself into a few corners and I was VERY eager to get out. The book is much darker than the game. The violence is much more intense. The characters are a lot more real and the way things unfold is (in my opinion) much more interesting. I think if you were a fan of the game that you will appreciate this but honestly the younger readers might feel a bit overwhelmed ...though I have tried to avoid writing a story that was too much different than the original. I love this story and because of that I think that I made changes that make it better. I hope everyone will agree.
**Darker, more violence, more realistic characters; sounds interesting to me =D**
> Cyrus: Me too. I think that when you read it that you will probably understand why I made the changes that I did even if I'm not explaining it all that well right now.
**What inspired you to make the game 'Deceit & Conquest'? Such as games, books, drugs and alcohol, a good sandwich?**
> Cyrus: I think my central inspiration for Deceit and Conquest (The game) was probably Final Fantasy 6. That story really is at the heart of everything I did in the original game. In fact if you were around back then, you probably remember that I started off with graphics I ripped out of that game. Once I got past the initial phase of development though I think I was more inspired by stories like Les Miserables, Excalibur, and The Lord of The Rings films. I think that those really helped me visualize where I wanted to go with the world I was creating and the characters. I basically just started over and built characters that I thought fit into this atmosphere and then started letting the history of Seor unfold in my mind. I actually have the entire saga in my head right now. It's all there. I know how it all began and I know how it will all end. That took me about 6 years... but like I said, I love this story. I think in the end, the reader will recognize some of my influences yet will find something fresh in these pages.
**Yeah, I do remember the FF6 tiles. There are a lot of people in the community who use ripped tiles because they don't think they can draw/pixel well enough to do custom. What made you decide to ditch the FF6 look and do custom artwork for D&C, and what might you say to someone that is currently using ripped tiles/artwork, but has considered going original?**
> Cyrus: Hmm... I think that there is nothing wrong with using ripped tiles in a video game as long as you give some sort of credit as to where you got the graphics and don't try to sell the game or anything. Eventually I think anyone who is serious about making a game though will want to move past that phase. In fact, I think that if you really care about your game then the sight of those ripped tiles will begin to annoy you so much that you'll replace them... besides you'll end up making tiles here and there anyway for things you can't find in another game. That's why I started replacing the graphics in D&C. If I was asked, I would suggest starting with ripped tiles and then just let nature take its course. The ripped tiles will either get filtered out over time or the author was probably not very serious about the project. That's' my opinion though.
**How is expressing a story in a novel form different than through a game? Is it what you expected, or did some unforeseen aspects arise in the transition?**
> Cyrus: Heh. Well it's totally different. I really was not all that aware of just how different it was going to be but there are some fundamental differences in how you can write and how you have to write in order to get the story across. In a game, there are visual representations for most of the environments and characters. Dialog is confined to a script of "he said" and "she said" boxes with character's names pasted on them. That all goes straight out the window when crafting a book. Also, a game is interactive and not entirely linear... meaning that the story doesn't necessarily move in a straight line. A book is totally linear so I had to come up with ways to keep the reader moving along and interested in things that were going to have to be imagined. That was much more fun than drawing semi-crappy graphics and putting test into boxes. There are dozens of examples I could list but the point is that these are not very similar formats. It was a huge task to convert D&C into a novel and I had to totally throw out a lot of things that just don't make sense unless you're playing a game... but then I think that all makes the story better anyway... so it was not a bad thing in the end. I had to really alter the way the story and the imagery is described but I think it was all worth the end result.
**Do you think it would be easier to make a game out of a novel?**
> Cyrus: I think that is a complicated question. There is no way you can make a game out of a novel and remain 100% faithful to the book unless the book was written in such a way that the interactivity could be fit in without changing things too much. If you aren't all that concerned with being faithful to the book though, then I think it would be very helpful to have the story of the book as a blueprint to work from. Making a game is hard though and involves a lot of concepts and knowledge that writing a book does not. Coding, drawing, working around bugs, obtaining sound effect and music... all of these can really make the process difficult. Making a game is more difficult for me than writing a story as a novel, but I think that is something that is different for everyone. I'd say it would be generally a lot harder though to make a game from a book than a book from a game simply because of all the factors involved in game production.
**Can we expect any more from you in the way of games, novels, or movies? Whether D&C related or not?**
> Cyrus: Absolutely. I am releasing my first feature film "Dawn of Twilight" in January and then I am going to begin pre-production on my second movie called "Nest of Vipers" which will shoot this coming summer. I plan on doing 1 movie a year from now on. Additionally, I will be working on "D&C Volume 2: Vyr" which will be released some time in 2005. I'll continue publishing each volume as quickly as I can write them until all 10 are completed at which point I'll write books about other things. I am just getting started on all of this and it may be a little bumpy at first but just hang in there. It's a long way to the end of this road.
**10! I thought it was a trilogy?!**
> Cyrus: Heh oooh... well... things change. I had to rethink all of that because there's just waaaay too much to tell in 3 books. The first 3 are a trilogy to themselves but not the end of the story. There's a lot more to tell after that. I had 3 books in my head and ended up splitting those into separate trilogies... and then adding one final volume to blow it all out of the water.
**Wow. I can't even think up a paragraph's worth of story, and your writing 10 books =/. That's crazy.**
> Cyrus: heh yeah I know. I guess I just overachieved when I was thinking about the story. I just have a lot to tell and 3 books is just not enough.
**Well I'm not going to complain, that's awesome. I'm really looking forward to getting your first book and movie. Just for re-iteration, when are they coming out? Or do you have exact release dates yet?**
> Cyrus: Dawn of Twilight will be released then first week of January. I'm still hammering out the details on that. D&C Vol 1 will be released just as soon as I can finish it. I was hoping for a January 3rd release but I may miss that. I'll make it up to everyone that pre-orders the book though. I have a special surprise for everyone who did that. I feel bad about making people wait but it really could not be avoided. I promise though that it will be worth it.
**Ah, no worries about the wait time. I seem to be doing a lot of that lately. It builds patience =D. Ok, one last question =D. Do you plan on submitting Dawn of Twilight to any film festivals, such as Sundance?**
> Cyrus: I might submit it to a couple of festivals. I missed the entry for Sundance though. I missed a couple of others too that I actually intended to enter... but that's how this business goes. Right now I am focusing on getting this movie out there and getting the book done. After I get those 2 things done, then I will have a lot more time to worry about competitions. We'll see. I cant say what will happen... so we'll see.
