# Mystic Software

Note: This is a tutorial on using tiles in a unique way, not the actual creation of tiles! In RPGs, the game can be well defined and improved by using good light effects throughout the game. For example, if you were to make a tile, it would look better and more professional if part of the tile was shadowed, and the other lightened, depending on the rises and lowers of the object in the tile. Another way is to not actually change the tile itself, but change how it is used on the board, mainly in the RPG Tool Kit. Now, if you were to look around the room you are in right now (this will only work if its day time and you in a room with a window) and look at how it is shadowed, the chances are that mostly around the corners and maybe the top of the wall with the windows are shadowed enough to be noticeable. Now, most people these days strive for realism in the game they are helping to or are producing themselves. So, if one were to implement good, well thought out shadowing system, it would much improve the general appearance of the game. Thus, coming to the reason of this tutorial: making a game with the kind of shadows in a room or outside how is looks in the real world, though, I will only cover the room for right now, you can be creative and create a way for shadowing the outside yourselves! : ) First, draw the basics of a room with the board editor in the Tool Kit (that would be pretty much just the floor and maybe the back wall). Now as you saw earlier when you looked around the room, the corners were the most shadowed. So, you would think that it would be best to use the Tile Lightener on the board editor to darken the corners right? No. In life, the floor (or rug, for instance) its not the actual floor thats darkening, but merely that the center of the room (for this example) is becoming lighter because of a light source. To prove this, shut of the light emitting from the windows. Youll notice that the corner is not what changes, but the center or wherever the light was going to does. So, when working with light, change the center of the board, not the corners. So, heres how to do that. First, go up to the Tile Lighting effecter on the upper left side. Its usually best to increase the 0 (or whatevers there) by maybe two (increasing it by too much will cause the difference to be too noticeable, and too little to be unnoticeable). Then, only the middle of the room with the new lighted tile, skipping all of the edges. Then, return the Tile Lighting to its original position. And then go to the 2,2 position from where the floor starts. Then place the darker tile there, and follow the same pattern throughout the board. In the end, it should look something like this example: N- normal tile, unchanged. L- lightened tile, changed.
