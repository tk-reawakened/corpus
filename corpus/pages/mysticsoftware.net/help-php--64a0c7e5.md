Chap.2: Basic Backgrounds Chap.3 :Monster Tutorial Chap. 4:Last Words ******************************** Chapter 1: Why 3D? Welcome, to my guide on how to add basic 3D graphics and enemys to a Toolkit Game. But when I posted this on the forum, responses were mixed. About half think 3D has ruined many games, and will continue to do the same. Others, think 3D adds a better perception of realism. The program that you will be using in this tutorial will allow you to create animated movies and stills. This can create more appeal, because, it's something differnt, and people like realism. Also, it allows you to create concept charectar art in 3D. Future devlopments in the Toolkit program may allow it to run a .Blend movie file. There is a plugin that claims to run Movie files, but it is still unknown if it runs .blends. If it does, you can create full 3D, near CG and FMV quality cutscenes. I'm about to take you through the basics of adding 3D elements to your game. ********************************* Chapter 2: Basic Backgrounds Ok, by now, you need to know basic RPG coding and TK navigation, and basic game building skills. The first thing you do is go to http://www.blender3d.org and download the lastest version of Blender. No, not into one of your game folders, normal program files. You need to get VERY accoustemd to Blender. It has a very steep learning curve. You should spend about 2 and a half hours on it every day for 2 weeks. Follow most of the tutorials in the tutorial section of Blender 3d . If 2 weeks seems long, just close out of this guide. Your not serious about your game. 2 weeks is nothing if your serious about the development of your game. So, after learning Blender, we know cameras, vertices, lamps, metas, spheres and so on. The first thing you'll want to do is make a plane. Then, go out to google and find a nice top-down camera shot of some grass. TExture the plane with it, then add some bumps. Make a mountain or two, possibly a castle on the plane, ( there's a nice guide to making a caslte on blender3d.) and all that fun stuff. You can try to add a sun, not too hard. Then, align your camera to a side view ( or any other good background view) and save it as a jpeg. Now, just go use it as a background picture in TK, and you got the hard part done! ! Good job!! Next, we need to make it so it does not seem like a background, but a actual game environment. So, were you made trees and other reletivly solid objects, make the empty tile over it solid. Repeat as nessecary. If you want to add hills, make the first half of your hill the "ladder" to layer two. then, just make sure the layer two tiles are normal. And the empty air all around it solid. Now, you have a basic 3D runtime environment.. *********************** Chapter 3: Monster Tutorial For anyone who dosent like making monsters using paint or something similar, and don't wanna seem cheap by ripping some, Blender can make you a nice monster in a matter of minutes. ********************** This will teach you how to create a basic snake. Delete the dafult plane. Set a Meta Ball. Enlarge it to a medium-small size. ( S is the function) Then, place another meta ball. move it so you can see it then enlarge it to roughly the same size as the other one. ( G to move, S to size) drag your second M-ball to the other one until a strand connects between them. Move it however close or far away you want. continue this way for a few more M-Balls. After you've gotten that, you can add two more mballs for the eyes, ( descend them into the 'head' ball until only half is showing. You may need a side camer view for this.) You can texture the body and then find a nice eye on google for the eyes. Pull your camera back and keep looking at the snake until you find a good size for your monster. Save it as a jpeg, open your favorite photo editor, and crop the snake to be only the snake, no blue background. To integrate the snake, go to your favorite TK version. Open up the edit enemy window. Go to graphics and 'import' the jpeg. Woohoo!!! You just made your first 3D enemy! ********************* Chapter 4: Last Words. Well, my time with you has come and gone, and we've made many improments to your game. The possiblites with TK is pretty much unlimitied, as is with Blender. So, experiment, and you may come up with something new and innovative. Like a first person view. :P . There are many more feautures you can add, like fireworks in the background for a in game scene or a cutscene. Those will come in the second part of this guide.
