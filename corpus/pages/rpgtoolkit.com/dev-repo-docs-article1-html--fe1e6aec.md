# Originality

The Art of RPG Production
An Essay by Cyrus Martin
This is by far the most challenging part of making an RPG. The initial thinking is, "My game original. There's not another one like it."...but soon the developer realizes that this simply isnt true. There are no 100% original RPGs any more. The reason is simple...the very fact that it is classified into a pre-existing category like "RPG" means that there is something about it that been done before. Lets not forget the basics of plot... There are NO original plots. What I mean to say is, the basics of every plot are based on either other plots from the past or personal experiences...which in the realm of RPG making means...very few new ideas. A completely original concept is still only a concept. The bulk of a story and its characters have to be based on something...and to some degree they may also be original...but only to a degree. The ego of the creator often causes a conflict between creating an original plot and living with the fact that it is basically impossible to be TOTALLY original. This can be a bitter pill to swallow and a really hard lesson to learn. If originality is the most challenging, Gameplay is definitely the most difficult part of RPG development. Characters and enemies have tons of factors that have to be concidered in order for the game to function properly...let alone be fun to play. All of the small statistical information that we love so much about RPGs actually becomes a monumental task when making one of these games. Level progression, HP, MP, experience, the cost of items, Defense considerations, attack abilities, special moves, magic, weaknesses, and many other such pieces make up a puzzle that really has no clear solution. The most a programmer can do is try and reach a general grey area that will keep the game challenging but also easy enough to be fun. Lets not forget level design, map design, the size of boards and the most elusive thing of all... interest. Keeping the player interested in what is happening in the game is not easy by any means. These are the challenges of developing good gameplay. - Programming - The code in which the game is written causes the developer to rethink the RPG as it is being developed. You may say to yourself,"I really want a huge dragon to appear here and blow up everything on the screen"...which sounds great in concept...but the actual implementation of an idea can be tedious, difficult and sometimes flat out impossible. The developer learns the confines of the engine he is using or at least the code. While it may be true that anything CAN be done given the time and knowledge, the creator will find himself/herself choosing what is and isnt worth the effort. That dragon blowing up a screen may be possible but if it takes a solid week of coding, recoding, graphic design, working around bugs and such and all for maybe 15 seconds on the screen...well... In the long run, coding can be added to bring the game up to the desired level of quality that the creator has in mind...so planning a coding strategy will save you time and frustration. How long should an RPG be? Some would say that an RPG cant be long enough...but as a dveloper you must recognize that the project must have an end...or at least a point where the game will reach the first level of completion. The first level should be basically a rough draft version... This is the point where the game is basically complete as far as coding, graphics and gameplay and from that point on you can begin changing things to fit your original vision. The second level of completion will come when the game is ready for the public...which is basically done. The final stage will be once the game has been tested and corrections have been made to improve gameplay and weed out any last bugs. This is probably the best way to go about a game release but as anyone who has followed the progression of my game (Deceit and Conquest) realizes...it may not be the best way ALL the time. If your plot and characters are not clearly defined or if the releases themselves do not follow a traditional "Whole game" format then you will need to relook this plan. In any case, the game must still have a clear end so that the last 2 stages can begin. How long should the game be? Well, it should be long enough to tell the story while holding the interest of the player. Contrary to what some would say, an RPG CAN indeed be too long. If the player looses interest...it is too long...regardless of the hours required to complete it. ...So game legnth is really a question of keeping the player into whats happening. Role Playing Games...
The name itself is a bit misleading...after all... it suggests that you (the player) are portraying yourself as another person or character.
This may be true in the beginning of the genre...when cardboard and dice were the canvass to which the tapestry of the story was laid out...and the players referred to themselves as "Toulok" or "Meldrid" even outside the confines of the game itself.
...and it is true that the name of the genre is owed to this format of play...
...but as with time, change occurs in the realm of The RPG.
Today, we know a different meaning behind this. The old games are still there in the background...but the inception of video game consoles and home computers has led to the expansion of RPGs into a new and (in my opinion) much more entertaining experience.
Anyone who has ever been a "Dungeon Master" in D&D or has ever written out a paper RPG has a mild grasp of the fundamentals of making a workable RPG...and now that we can transfer that experience into the digital world, making a Role Playing Game has suddenly become a possibility.
...but making a video game...especially an RPG...is no easy task.
There is a lot envolved in the process of turning an idea into a complete and enjoyable experience.
Many who start down this path find themselves conflicted with the realization of several key areas in RPG construction.

When thinking of art, the first thing that comes to mind is the literal connotation...meaning drawings...paintings...cinematics...and such... ...but the Art of an RPG is so much more than this. Artistic expression is vital in RPG development because it sets a look for the game. The art can be founf in tiles, boards, drawings, borders... ...in the writing...sounds...and music... This is an area with no system or method to it. It is an area that can only be defined by the creator and the style in which he/she presents the game. Many RPGs choose a dark mood and correlate this with serious villians...somber music...and overly detailed graphics... While others choose a less serious tone...as in the traditional "Working Designs" RPG with humor and bright colors abound. This is something that is very personal to the developer and will reflect his/her thinking about what an RPG is supposed to be. No book or tutorial can teach artistic expression....which is why it can be so difficult to define yourself. To create an artform within a video game is soemthing that takes experience, knowledge and passion...but most of all...it takes talent. Learnign to express that talent can only help in this genre. Emotion is something that must be conveyed to keep the player interested in the game. When something is supposed to be funny...it must BE funny...sad moments must hold the player's heart...and evil villians should make the player hate them. To convey these felings, you will have to learn how to use them effectively. Study the basics of plot development and character interaction. Emotions are a human trait and so dialog should be filled with as much emotion as possible. Remember the old phrase also..."Less is More". Learn when to keep the emotions relaxed and when to add high drama to the arena. A game should never push any single emotion...although some will take precedent over others. Keep the player into the game. Keep them interested. Show them the souls of the characters and the emotions will come out on their own. As a creator, you must not be afraid to use emotions to carry a scene...and you must remember that even the most cold-hearted of heroes must still have some degree of feeling or else they become boring. The mood of an RPG can be set by your art and emotion. Let the music define scenes and keep the actions of the characters consistent with the flow of the story. If you can look into the hearts of the characters and see them as people or individuals rather than tools in a plot, they will guide your story instead. Let the mood flow...dont try and force it. As you begin to develop the game you may find yourself compelled to change some major parts of it becase it "feels" right even though it wasnt what you had originally planned. This is good. Let the story go where it will. Every plot hs the possibility to take on a life of its own...let it. Now that we have a better understanding of the challenges and burdens of RPG development, lets take a look at the less defined and most important factors in creating a Role Playing Game.

The above paragraphs are only a guide to help those who wish to add as much substance and art to their works as possible...but even this is just the thoughts of one person. Learn as you go. Don't make up your mind about anything.
