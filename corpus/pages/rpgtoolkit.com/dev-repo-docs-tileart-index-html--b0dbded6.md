# BY: JERRY LIANG (THE HEAD TAKER)

** DRAWING TILES
**BY: JERRY LIANG (THE HEAD TAKER)**
**-INTRODUCTION-**
Besides good story and combat systems, an RPG (or any other game) needs some good visuals. Many people have resorted to ripping Tiles from different games that are already made (FFVI is a popular one) and are satisfied with what they get. Many others, however want their RPGs to stand out from the rest and have a personality of its own. Not all of these people know how to create their own tile sets however. If you happen to be one of those people, then keep reading. This guide is to help you get a basic understanding of where to start, and how to proceed once you know you what. Remember, tile drawing is an extreamly subjective thing, so anyting goes.
**-GETTING AN IDEA-**
Before doing anything, you need an idea of what type of tilesets you want. For example, Mario RPG is nice and bright, while Shadowrun or Final Fantasy are darker and not as vivid. So once you've gotten an idea of what kind of tileset you want, it's a good idea to make a coustom pallet for it. The pallet should have the basic colors, including skin colors. With that settled, you'll now need to think of how your RPG is going to look. You can make it like a cartoon where all the characters heads are bigger than their bodies, or you can make it a bit more realistic. Weather you choose one or the other might also have some relevence to the colors you picked for your pallet. A darker pallet might match nicer with a realistic tileset, while a brighter tileset might match nicer with a cartoonish one.
**-THE BASICS-**
The first thing to do is find a good drawing program. In this guide, I will be using MS paint, because not everyone has something better. Once you are in your program, load up your pallet from the ptions menu, and set the drawing window to 32x32 from the mage menu.
