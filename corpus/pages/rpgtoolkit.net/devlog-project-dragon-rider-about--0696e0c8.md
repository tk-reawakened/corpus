Once upon a time, in the valley of Kaemarr, a people called the Dura-Long lived. Peaceful and wise, they spent their time making wonders and beautiful things. But it was not to last. The Dura-Long grew careless in passing on their ways and wisdom to the next generations, and the kingdom became shrouded in darkness. Not too long after the dynasty of Shonto has come to an end the kingdom of the Dura-Long turned brother against brother. Dividing into five factions, each united under their own banner and began warring against each other for supremacy of the valley. And so it was that when the barbarians from the south came, there was not strength enough to match them. The barbarians attacked, burning and plundering at will. Many of the Dura-Long were killed, and those who survived were taken into captivity as slaves for the barbarians. During the years of captivity, the valley of Kaemarr lay abandoned and desolate. But the memory of the furtile fields and lush pastures stayed alive in the hearts of the survivors longer than the memory of even their own language. So it was inevitable that sooner or later, a leader of the rebels would rise, and lead the slaves to freedom. About ten generations after the fall of the Dura-Long, a man by the name of Alfred arose, igniting the hearts of his people with his vigor and passion. The barbarians saw his effect on the people and, in fear of losing their highly profitable slaves (not to mention their own lives) put him to death on public display. But if anything this only served to aid the rebal sentiment. So a few years later, when Alfred's son was grown, Alfred the Younger united the three thousand kinsmen with strength left to fight under one banner. Together they resisted the barbarian forces and escaped into the valley of Kaemarr, taking over five thousand women and children with them. But there were still more left in captivity, and Alfred's heart would not rest until all had been safely recovered. So mustering his forces once more, he and his brother Vivaro led the charge against the barbarians once more. But the barbarian's were ready this time. Armed and numerous as sand on the seashore, they resisted the rebels' charge, scattering the army far and wide. Alfred himself only just escaped from capture, saved by his brother Vivaro and the two fled into the hills of Kaemarr. While Vivaro went back to the rebel camp to comfort and encourage the people, Alfred began walking. He walked and thought and thought and walked. But no answer came to his mind. There was no way they could win against the barbarians. But in his walking, he stumbled across the Utaru. A mostly peaceful tribe, small in number, who had dwelt in the foothills of the Spire Range even before the DÃ»ra-Long had settled. When Alfred explained his plight to the Utaran chief, Levi, he pledged his people's aid in recovering the surviving slaves. Alfred went home with a lighter heart, even though he had misgivings about what good a handful of natives could do to aid in such a great battle. When the day of the battle arrived, Alfred's army stood on their side of the battle-field, armed and ready, but the Utaru still hadn't arrived. Finally giving up all hope, Alfred determined to give his life fighting for the freedom of his people. Raising his sword, he let out his battle cry, "For freedom!" and was surprised to hear it repeated among his army. Yet even more surprising was the cry that followed. It seemed almost an echo of his army; faint, as if far, yet strong. Alfred turned to see an amazing sight. About three dozen dragon were flying toward the battlfield, each with an Utaran warrior on it's back winning themselves the name "DragonRiders". A cheer rose among the rebel army, and Alfred fell to his knees, thanking his God. The battle was won with ease. The dragons were incredible allies, and the barbarian army's morale had been shattered as soon as they caught sight of them. The rest of the slaves were liberated, and returned safely to the valley. It was then that Alfred the Younger completed his greatest work yet, united all the rebels under one banner, he named them a new people, the Dura-Yu, which the scribes said meant "the free people". So in the kingdom of Kaemarr, there was peace. But when the good King Alfred mysteriously disappeared and his brother, Prince Vivaro, took the throne, the kingdom fell into darkness. For envy of his brother had eaten at Vivaro's heart. Working away slowly it eroded all the good that had been there before. Monsters, no longer held back by the royal guards, became bold, their raids more frequent and devastating. Even worse, as soon as Vivaro gained the the crown, he revealed his loyalty not to the people of Kaemarr, but to the barbarians of the south, bringing them in as his riotous brute squad, raiding the people and causing fear amoung the populace. It was then that Levi, the selfsame chief who had liberated the people in the past, reunited his scattered nomadic people into a powerful force once more able to combat the skirmishers of Vivaro. But, evil though he was, Vivaro was cunning, and realized that the Utaru must be stopped or his power would be shattered. So in a secret night attack, Vivaro's forces ambushed the Utaran camp, slaughtering the men and women, young and old, leaving none alive... Except a young boy named Aaron, son of Levi, who had escaped the carnage with his father's dragon, Ryuu. Together, they fled into the mountains, determining to hide themselves there until the kingdom was once again in the hands of a just king...
This game has a custom battle system where a character's position on the battlefield not only changes during the fight, but is important to what moves each character can use. Though the game uses a charge-bar system, each character has their own charge rate before their bar fills. Watch out for those quick little goblins now!
Your allies, as you pick them up, join your party as in almost any other RPG game, but unlike most, they remain on the board. When you walk around, your allies will follow you, an interesting addition to some puzzle challenges, though they will usually get out of the way.
