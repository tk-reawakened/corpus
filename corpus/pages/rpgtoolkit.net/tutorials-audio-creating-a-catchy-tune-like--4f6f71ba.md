**Part 1 - The Knowledge**
** Creating a Catchy Tune - Music Tutorial
*By Perry Swain ( aka Pezzer )*
**Introduction:**
Welcome to a series of tutorials made by me, where I shall guide you through some of the basics on how to create a good piece of music. This is the first part and I shall be showing you how to make a catchy tune in you music that people will remember. Remember this isn't a "get good quick" tutorial, it just shows the basics. Good luck and enjoy ;)
Note: All music in this tutorial was made by me, you may use this music in your game. **not**

**The 6 points to make a catchy tune:**
1. **Repetition :**
This is one of the most vital things to have, repetition. You may think repeating something over and over again is boring, however when done correctly, it can be affective. If you play a tune twice make the second slightly different, or higher or even play it completely backwards! *very*
2. **The Notes:**
This is the killer, the notes that you play will be a way for a person to recognise your piece. You must base your tune around a scale, and at that a good scale! Also depending on what kind of mood your trying to create you need to think whether you want a Major or a Minor tune. *always*
Listen to the difference.
**C Scale (Simple, all the white notes, happy (major), not very exciting) - *Major* [C Major Scale](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_mus2.mid)**
![](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_img2.gif)
*above, C major 1 octave*
**C Scale (Sad (Minor... no not that type of minor...), some sharp notes, good for lots of things) - *Minor* [C Minor Scale](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_mus3.mid)**
![](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_img3.gif)
*above, C minor 1 octave*
You'll notice how changing a few notes to sharps or flats you can completely change the mood of something, heard here as two versions of Mary Had a Little Lamb, the first Major and the Second Minor.
[Example](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_mus4.mid)
3. **Rhythm:**
It's alright having repetition and good notation, but without rhythm a piece will sound boring. Don't be afraid to experiment with weird and wacky rhythms as long as they work with the pieces Time Signature/Metre

4. **The Tempo.**
A slow piece would make it a long, usually sad/depressing tune, a fast tempo would give action/tension. Tempo is measured in BMP (Beats Per Minute).
[Example Slow Piece](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_mus5.MID)
[Example Fast Piece](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_mus6.mid)
Notice also in both pieces I used Minor keys, Minor in my opinion is fantastic. It is great for creating tension. Feel free to open these up in Noteworthy and check out the rhythms and workings behind them.
5. **The Instrument**
A tune will sound good, if you play it on the right instrument. Too many times have I seen people using a flute to play a bass part; it just can't happen... Ever, not in MIDI at least. Make the tune for the instrument, using the range of the instrument. It's no good making a piece really low and getting a glockenspiel to play it; it would sound HORRID!
A trumpet wasn't made to play a violin concerto...
**Brass Instruments (Trumpets, Trombones, French Horns, etc...)**
These are the instruments that should be used for supporting, or even playing the main tune. I usually use them for chords (clusters of notes from a scale). They give a "punchy" feel to the music.
![](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_img4.gif)
**Woodwind Instruments (Flutes, Oboes, Clarinets, Saxophone etc...)**
A common misconception is that Saxophones are brass instruments, they are woodwind instruments because of 1 of 2 reasons:
Saxophones use something called a reed to produce the sound, whereas brass instruments have a metal mouth piece which they blow a raspberry (ok like a fart noise :P) into. **1.**
The saxophone is a derivative of a clarinet, it was created by a man who played jazz but wanted his clarinet to be louder, so he added a bell (the horn on the end) to make it louder and made the whole thing out of metal. Thus the Saxophone was born. **2.**
Woodwind is great for main tunes that are not fast, I usually use them for sweet, soft melodies rather than action.
![](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_img5.gif)
**String Instruments - Classical (Violins, Violas, Cellos and Harps) - Guitars (Distortion, Overdriven, Clean Strat, 12-stringed etc...)**
These are amazing instruments when used together for good backing. Individually they can be used to create different moods; when pizzicato strings (plucked strings) are used - good for happy tunes and lively ones , tremolo strings (vibrating almost) are great for tension, and spiccato? (short notes) good for little breaks in happy tunes.
A Harp is brilliant for happy, relaxing, calm tunes. If you use them give them arpeggio's of chords to sound great (IMO).
Guitars are great power chord players (fast chords repeated). Distortion and Overdriven are both affects that are used on guitars, not unique instruments, I use them mainly for Solos where the guitar goes wild and gets smashed into the back of a SUV.
![](http://www.intrepidmusic.co.uk/tutorial/musictutorialpart_1_img6.gif)
6. **The Dynamics**
Dynamics give tunes a great... erm dynamicness! No really, volume change throughout a piece is a good idea to keep in interesting. This is a point they I don't usually use a lot, I should but it's not that important. However it can be very effective.
Quiet pieces can give a eerie feel, or a sad feel. Loud pieces can give an action feel or even be used to make someone jump out of their seat (or turn off their speakers before their parents/partners shout at them :P)
