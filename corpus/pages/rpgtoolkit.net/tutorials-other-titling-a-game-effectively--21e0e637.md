Titling A Game Effectively
**OBCT takes a look at the do and don'ts in relation to giving your game project a name, especially avoiding those often used words such as "Dark" or "Final".**
**Titling a Game Effectively**
I was looking through the new games section, and eventually found my way to the new search page. The new sorting method allowed me to notice something startling. Looking at the profiles sorted A-Z, I found some similarities in the titles of TK games.
For example, take a word like 'Dark'. You have Dark Age, Dark Age 2, Dark Age of Camelot: Mini Ques, Dark Ages, Dark and Light, Dark Destiny X, Dark Dragon Guardians, Dark Ego, Dark Forces, Dark Realms, Dark Revolution, Dark stories, Dark Stories Zero, Dark Sun, Dark Times, Dark Times, Dark Warrior, DarkEdge1, and Darkened Fate.
As another example, games starting with 'Final' include Final Blunt 4, Final Destiny, FInal Dimension Chapter 1, Final Fanasty, final fantasy, Final Fantasy Reunions, final fantasy series, Final Fantasy VIII - Prologue, Final Fantasy XII Origins, Final Fantasy: hero's make, Final Fantasy: Revenge of..., Final Legend, Final Quest, FinalDestiny, and FinalFantasyReunions.
If you didn't bother to read through all those names, then my point probably got across. These words get redundant and boring! Of all the games I mentioned, only 5 have been updated in the last month. I suspect, however, that at least one of those updates was the profile being created. I assume by now that you realize why those games are named as such, and why they fail to be continued (or updated, in the least).
I think it's safe to assume that these people (with some excpetions) named their games so clich?ly is that they sound 'cool'. Surely a game with 'Final" or 'Dark' in the name will attract people. The fact is that they don't. I, for one, usually skip over all these profiles, unless I have seen the game somewhere before, and was interested.
So how can you prevent your game from being ignored straight from the beginning? Plan out your story. If you know what will happen late in the game, rather than use the 'Oh, I know what will happen, it's all in my head.' excuse, then you should be able to make a good title for your game. Actually, you should have your basic story written down before you develop your game's title. By basic, I don't mean a three sentence plot. Someone reading this 'basic' story should be able to have the game spoiled for them by this document.
This may come as a shock to some of you. How, you ask, can you create the whole story without a title? Well, the real question is why, since it's fairly easy to create a whole story. To go on a related side note, my sister wrote a novel a few years ago. She named the book when she started, and ended up changing it 4 times before she finished. You can assume, then, that she changed the title of the story itself. *because*
To answer the question previously stated, by planning out your story, you can make the title meaningful to the story. This may actually include some commonly used words, but more than likely you can create a more interesting title. You can use a place's name or an important character's name as the game's title. You could use a seemingly meaningless phrase several times in the game, and it could be the title. If you've taken a class in poetry, then you will understand this concept. Be creative with the title. If your game's title can't attract people, then you'll have a hard time finding anything else to attract them.
To provide an example of this concept, I'm going to use the game 'Talona'. What exactly does the title mean? You can't say that a 'Talona' is a thing that you know. Is it a person? A place? In the demo, you don't ever see the word mentioned, except for the title itself. It stimulates your curiosity, and makes you want to know what 'Talona' is. This is a great example to show you how a title can attract players.
Chances are, if you plan out your story, you will find a much better title than something with 'Dark' or 'Final' in it. I'm not saying that those words should not be used in a title, but the multitude of games with them outweighs the defense of using them, in my opinion. At the very least, you can pull out a thesaurus.
