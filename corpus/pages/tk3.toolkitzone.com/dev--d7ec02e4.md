# Development Principles

These are requirements for potential developers.
General programming experience.
Understand non-platform specific principles of programming, such as memory management considerations, and the Windows API.
Fluency in Visual Basic 6 and/or C++.
The two languages the Toolkit is written in.
Some knowledge of the codebase.
Understanding of the overall structure of the editor and engine. The Toolkit game file types; how a Tk game works; the idea of the MDI and child forms; the use of DirectX in game graphics.
Specific and well-planned development ideas or bug solutions.
New developers must have a specific reason for joining the team, not just joining it because they want to be part of the process. Developers must have tangible development ideas and a plan of implementation, for instance the AutoTiler tool.
Time to invest in the development team.
Developers must have time to devote to the Toolkit—if developers do not make contributions for a long period of time, they will be removed from the team. One-time developers are invited to submit patches.
Agree to release contributions under the Toolkit's . [licence](?page=licence_main)
Released contributions shall be released under the or its where applicable. [AC Open Licence](?page=acol) [derivative](?page=acold)
Current team members will assess applicants based on these criteria and vote on admittance of the user, requiring a majority for entry.
Users can also for bugs or small features instead of joining the team, if they are not able to or want to commit to long-term development. Submitted patches will be assessed by the developers and integrated if acceptable; credit will be given to the user in the release documentation and the developer records. Only developers can commit to the CVS repository (although anyone can access it through anonymous CVS and tarball download). [submit patches](?page=submit_patch)
