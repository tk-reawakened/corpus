# I N T E R V I E W - by MrG Martin

> I will start by a general question. What are your current and future projects (even outside the TK) ?
> Right now Zeo-K is taking most of my time. Thanks to Sophia's dedication, perserverance, and pointy stick, I've been able to really make some progress on a project for once in my TK life. Sophia, by the way, is doing terrain and character graphics for the game, and is doing an excellent job at that.
> That will look great ! Will there be special features in Zeo-K ?
> Plenty.. The main draw is the non-linear gameplay. You can build an extremly unique character with many varities on skills, stats, appearance, etc. Most every situation in the game can be roughly gotten around in three ways: shooting, sneaking, and sabatoge (hacking.) There's plenty to do in the game world, with the many colorful and unique locales you would expect from a developing, but ravaged Wasteland.
> Of course the game features a very spiffy battle system. It's fully realtime, with destructable and usable environments. There are tons of different weapons, armor, and items to use, and there are several levels of enemy AI. There's even a special slo-mo mode similiar to bullet-time on Max Payne or The Matrix.
> The NPCs in the game are smart. They all have their own schedule, affiliation, agenda, temperment, and swervy, swervy haircut.
> And the Wasteland isn't just for saving. There is a huge variety of mini-games to play to entertain you during your time. You can play pool, drag race, gamble, and there's even a fully featured Laptop to play and work on.
> Every single interface system in the game is custom. From the shop to the menu to the prompts.
> As you might be able to tell, we're basically planning the most advanced game ever done with the TK, and all the baggage that entails.
> I have no choice ;). By the way, what will be the game's settings ?
> The game is set in the near-future. World governments are in dissarray thanks to ever increased power from anarchial terrorist groups. Several years before the game begins, one large, prominent terrorist group hit Southern England with a nuclear warhead, just west of London. They called this a warning. Since then, the world has been slowly slipping further and further into anarchy.
> In the meantime, the Wasteland has been shut off from the rest of the world and remains uncontrolled by any government. As such, it attracts heavy criminal elements and gangs and wasteland vigiliantes, along with pioneers trying to build a new society. It is, in short, a very colorful place.
> In Zeo-K you play a mercenery hired by the UN's international defense initiative (IDI) division to go into the Wasteland and recover a device called the Puritron. The Puritron was brought to the Wasteland a few years after it became the Wasteland, but Wasteland gangs stole the device and dissassembled it. Of course, this is only the beginning. The mysteries delve much deeper than that.
> Is there any release date scheduled ?
> Well, originally I was planning an end of the summer release for an initial demo, but that didn't go according to plan. You see, the Wasteland is divided into zones for both gameplay and story purposes. In terms of gameplay, as your character develops he can brave the new zones. In terms of story, the zones often mark the boundries of the territory of a particular gang.
> Right now, I'm only planning on doing one demo before the final game. This is the way most professional games do it, after all. This demo should hopefully come out around the Holidays, just in time for the TKGR Awards! Hehe.
> Just what I was thinking ;). Well to get back on general questions : are you going to use exclusively professionnal tools to make games later, or will you still use the TK as a sidetool to make home-made games ?
> I never really intended to go into the professional games industry. I'm getting a degree in creative writing and that's the field I'm entering. As for the TK becoming a professional tool someday, I think with the advances Chris is making it should one day become good enough to be a commercial product intended for semi-pro games. Hopefully he'll retain a free version as well, and knowing Chris, I'm pretty sure he will. But I do see a commercial future for the TK.
> Ok. What are your opinion about the way the TK Community expand ever and ever and how it (basically) works, with oldies running sites to help newcomers ?
> I think the community works well. I'm American, so naturally I love big business. Yeah. Erm. I think having a few designated sites everybody knows to go to for resources (like tilesets, RPGCode, etc.) is a good idea, because then you know you'll never have to scour the entire community searching for that one elusive tileset or program or plugin or whatever.
> I think the community gets along fairly well, and I think we're lucky to have so much peaceful coexistance among the people that do own all the sites, and that there are a healthly lack of petty power squabbles (cough the QB community cough)
> That's true. Now about the program itself...A lot are giving up with it just because it's hard to learn. Do you think it is the first "test" before creating a really good game, or do you think the Toolkit should be more user-friendly ?
> I think the TK could be more user friendly. As certain people have said time and time again (hi Kaimu), the interface is very disorganized. With a more streamlined interface, and easier access to the features, the TK should attract more users that will then stick around to learn the more advanced features and hopefully be hooked. Of course, there is the speed issue, and I think this is the other major thing Chris needs to improve to really make the TK an attractive product for the users of something like RM2K.
> So you do think Chris should hire or ask help from other people to get the TK to a commercial stage ?
> Hmm, tough question. I think that Chris is very dedicated to his programming of the TK, and he should retain that fully, if for no other reason than he seems to write it attuned to his mind and his mind only. But the homebrew RPG Maker scene has always been dominated by one act shows, though this trend seems to be gradually shifting somewhat, particularly with the up and coming products (see RPGDS). I think it would help Chris very much if he got some outside talent to work on default graphics, RPGCode, animation, and music (although we already have one for that). Having all these resources readily available in the TK when you download it (or even as one big default pack that comes with it as another download or something) would really do a lot to attract users.
> Did you ever eat belgian waffles ?
> Yes! I use to enjoy Dennys's version, but then the Waffle Shop opened up a local branch, and I fell in love with their tens of thousands of waffle variations and other breakfast goodies. Breakfast is, actually, my favorite meal of the day, which isn't a good thing on a student's budget. Still, I don't mind going without toothpaste or toilet paper if I can squeeze in a few extra dollars that lets me avoid eating icky icky cereal! Mmmm, breakfast!
> Yes, I also think breakfast is the most important meal of the day. BTW my grandma makes excellent chocolate waffels. Well, thanks for your time and thanks twice for the answers ! I enjoyed interviewing you =)
> Thanks Martin, I had all the pleasure.
