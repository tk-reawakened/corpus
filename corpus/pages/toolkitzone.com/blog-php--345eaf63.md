And to be honest, my internet came back up Wednesday, and I never let you guys know. Ha ha ha... We've got wireless now! Anyways, I'm here to tell you guys how I think the phenomenon called Pokemon died. A long time ago, Game Freak released a game called Pokemon, and it had two versions: Red and Blue. The concept was brilliant: You catch creatures called Pokemon, and you train them to fight for you. Both versions have Pokemon that are exclusive to them, and you'll have to trade with other players to get them. While you walk around, other trainers will see you and try to battle you, and you will be required to fight gym leaders to get badges, so that you can prove your skill and get traded Pokemon to obey you at certain levels. The only parts I didn't like about the game were the corny ending, the fact that traded Pokemon don't obey you at certain levels, and how freaking hard it is to beat the first gym leader when you choose Charmander. Blah! The game was heading for massive sucess. Then something happened. The Pokemon show came to be. Here's a piece of advice for anyone who's contemplating making a show of a game, that goes through the same storyline and everything... DON'T!!! The show appeared and completely screwed the entire concept using a horribly made show. Then the massive propaganda followed, which also ruined it. The Pokemon rap, the 2.B.A Master CD (Which I still have x_x), and all the other mass propaganda. The show and the game were supposed to be doing the same thing. Instead, Team Rocket became comprised of two blundering idiots and the only Meowth that could talk, who did nothing but try to capture Pikachu, 2 of the Gym Leaders followed Ash, and Pikachu was suddenly Ash's bestest friend. I don't know if it was the fact that there WAS a show, or if it was made badly, but I know I could do better if I made the show. I would mess with as little details from the game as possible. You know how Scott Ramsoomair of VGCats draws his n00bie type people? That's how we were back then. And our link to the show, and the show's link to the game, killed the entire thing off. The fact that Yellow came out, which was based on the show, didn't help. Gold and Silver weren't particularly bad directions to go, but by the time those came out, the entire concept of Pokemon had been completely destroyed. Thus is the fall of a brilliant game.
