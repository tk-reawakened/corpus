# User Entries (1)

14 June, 2005
I recently had a chance to watch this film, and I was unimpressed. I've missed the main topic, so here are my thoughts. (This is similar to what I posted on Thanian Nation; I accidentally wrote a more comprehensive post than I meant to.) First, the actors. Palpatine is quite good. He should have played every character. Obi-wan is better than last time, but that really doesn't mean much; that man could say any line and I'd never get closer to understanding his character's personality. It's all so dull, so poor. Anakin is sickening, as usual. He's greasy and unkept, makes me sick to look at. His acting is quite atrocious too ('No, that's because I love you.'). Ugh. Padmé is whiny and almost as sickening as Anakin, but to her credit, not quite. Lucas still hasn't got a handle on the camera, and it tends to do strange swirls for no real reason in the middle of scenes. The sky has always got some ships moving in it as to draw your eyes away from the centre, and everything is always way too bloody busy. There is no one place to look; there are always a million things happening at once. The battle choreography is particularly terrible, and the Jedi tend to do twirls for no reason, even though it makes their backs susceptible to light saber hit. When they aren't spinning, they're putting so little effort into moving the sabers that it's almost comedic to watch. The story is riddled with problems. Order sixty-six is joke; you'd think having thousands of drones with it programmed in somebody might just have noticed. And although they can take on hundreds of enemy droids, the Jedi are defenceless against their own. Anakin's transition to the Dark Side was a little like this: 'The dark side, you say? Sign me up, baby!' Then Anakin doesn't even question his first order as Vader, despite it involving the slaughter of innocent children. Nothing changed in the kid, but three seconds later he's ready to kill innocents. This was of course to save his wife, whom he then returns to attempt to kill. Finally, Vander's conclusory 'Nooo~' deserves a special note for its badness. Haha. Abysmal.
Colin
