I was bored this weekend, so I decided that I should try and scan some of my earlier black and white pieces. There's nothing new in this batch, but I don't think anyone here has seen any of these before. This is a photo of a bench taken just outside of Old Stronach Cemetary, near Manistee, Michigan. I like it for some reason, but out of the batch of four that I turned in to my photo professor, it wasn't his favorite ("starts to work" was his criticism). I tweaked the contrast slightly and toned it in Photoshop. The actual photo was shot sometime in February, since I was hurrying to fill out a Valentine card. Birds over a building. The contrast was also tweaked a bit, and again, I toned it in Photoshop. When I was going through my contact sheets, I really didn't like the photo much at all, but as I worked in the darkroom, trying to get the photos ready for class, this one grew on me more and more. Taken in January, I believe. A lit window on a local church. I like this photo, and it's an earlier work of mine, having been taken in summer of 2004, even before I bought my Minolta. Tweaked the contrast, of course, and toned it, althought I'm not quite sure I like the toning much now. I like the cross set against the tree, and expanse with crosses dotting behind it. Overall, I like it, but it feels a little muddy. I think when I get a chance I'm going to reprint this one and make another scan. This was taken in April. A dance at my old high school. My little brother was DJing the dance, so I got in free to take some pictures for my class. This is a picture of the commons area of my high school, where the students eat lunch during the day and lots of stuff happens. The photo was shot in January. Another shot of the same dance. You can see my brother's equipment on the side. A photo of the deck at my house at night. The porch light illuminates one side of the railing quite harshly, and leaves the other side in shadow. Makes for an interesting photo. I think I shot it March of 2004. Conceptually, this is a favorite photo of mine, although it's a little obscure in it's meaning. The tree's branch arches over a series of stones, almost making it look like it's protecting them. The tall stone is the mother of the family, and the three smaller stones are her children. Taken in April. My professor liked this one, even though I didn't explain the concept. Trees in a forest. A simple composition, but it gives (me) a feeling of panic and being lost. The original copy I made was poorly exposed, but I was able to correct the exposure in Photoshop. Taken around March of 2004. I hope you enjoyed what I have. Questions, comments, and any criticism is certainly welcome (I love to hear what people have to say). This work is the kind of work that I really like to do. While it's fun to take pictures of bugs and flowers, I'm fond of doing weirder or more artistic photos than just wildlife. After veering off into the wildlife direction over the summer, I'm trying to work my way back into doing the artistic sort of stuff again. I shot a roll of Tri-X last Sunday, and I'm hoping that I get a chance to develop it later this week, with another showcase of anything interesting next Sunday, provided I get time to print.

A classmate of mine asked the professor today where he could go to find out about the Unix commands for the Solaris boxes we have in the lab. "You can type man ." And I saw the Fear in his eyes. In my CPS 210 class (Assembly Language and Computer Architecture), we had a nice pretest on basic concepts we should know. It was quite fun. I'm always astonished at the people that completely fly through computer science tests, wondering how they can go so damn fast. I consider myself to be experienced with the language we're using (C++), and decent at logical operators, following loops by hand and whatnot, but it still took me an hour to do the ten question test. How is it these people can breeze through in thirty-five minutes? Are they really that fast, that good, or do they just not have a clue about what's going on? Backing up through time, I had a nice weekend. It was Labor Day weekend, which means a three day weekend with really nothing to do. I did some homework, worked on senior pictures for a little while, and had some hamburgers (the Swiss Olive Burger at the Osprey Grill at Manistee National Golf and Resort is quite tastey, by the way). Kelly's pictures turned out fantastic, and I'm looking forward to sending some back to the lab for enlargements. My return to my dorm Monday evening is the stuff on which poetry is written about. It was a calm, peaceful journey. I had the windows rolled down the entire way, taking in the cool evening air. The dial on the radio was turned to an NPR station, from which I could listen to the BBC World Report. It was quite lovely. I've been really reluctant to post lately, with the death of QuickCat's father and the disaster events of the southern United States; how can I possibly not post anything that sounds pale, selfish, petty, and inane compared to death and destruction?
