07 January, 2006
As I was sort of talking about in my last entry, the TK is changing. Always has been, always will. (That's not a complaint Spyder) And we have little "fads" you could call them. I remember there was one month (somewhere in 2002 I'm guessing) that the idea of online TK games just went crazy. Most brushed it off with a "impossible you idiot", but some really chased after the dream. Or rather, they just wanted attention. Anyway, it was damn hilarious at times. TK doesn't really have good, or beneficial, fads though really. The only one I can really recall is the whole drawn boards idea that, in my opinion, Heraldry really started. Sure, it wasn't the first, but I still remember that first screenshot of the house that had some going crazy. There's nothing really wrong with this fad - sure, the followers don't even approach Heraldry's and Quint's quality, but at least they're doing something. The problem I have though is people calling pixeled, tiled boards outdated, or worst, inferior to drawn boards. It's just silly. People have always tried to create their own community sites (like TKZ), but with success. Besides TKZ and MS, the closest thing to get any share of the "market" would be TK-X, Dan's little run. But there was a stretch of a month or two that there was literally a handful of new sites each week. All hideous with pages filled with "under contruction" and "content coming soon" of course. It was a little embarrassing, but sort of funny. ANYWAY (sorry, if I don't reminisce, I just forget it), there's a new fad I've noticed as I scavage through the games section looking for some quality games to watch (actually found some). The fad is this - posting tons of screenshots of ripped images. Some images big enough to be desktop backgrounds, some random character portraits - all ripped, and nearly all excessively large. If any screenshots of boards or menus at all, they're terrible (and usually ripped). I guess some people just love finding huge ripped artwork on Google.

30 June, 2005
Little warning... this entry has no point. It's mere ramblings. This is for me... so I can read in two or three years and see how everything was going back then. When I first downloaded TK, I used the default tiles for about a month. The next month, I discovered MS and downloaded RM2K tilesets. Back then for me, it was like striking gold. It felt like a new beginning. So I went crazy... worked on a game called "Time" for a few months, and basically had a blast. Pretty soon after that, I was getting more into the community, met Sophia Hsu, and started messing around with original tiles. With some beginner tips from Sophia, I was creating almost mediocre stuff. So I ditched "Time", which was about a nice half an hour game at the time, and started a new game - "Final Fate". I created trees, grass, houses, sprites, and enemies... working hours a day on my game... and had the best three or four months I ever had using the TK. I had a good game going... again, about 30 minutes of gameplay using original tiles and default systems. I still remember just putting my heart and soul into that game for hours on end. At this point, I was really into pixeling. And of course, everytime I made a full set of tilesets that could basically support a game, I needed a whole new game, right? So I did that for about a year... switching from project to project as my pixeling skills improved. At this time, I was "Cyan" in the community about as one annoying 14 year old kid. I supported original tiles incredibly - and any game that used ripped tiles was frankly a piece of shit. I became a fan of playing and reviewing TK games also, had a site called "TKSpot" that no one will remember. It actually had about 20 reviews on it. I had "Majikal Gaming" going and making new layouts for the company site every few months. And this went on for about a year or two... focusing on pixeling, programming, and learning from the mistakes of other TK games while playing and reviewing them. Mid 2002, I figured I knew what I was doing. I couldn't keep a project going more than maybe six months if my life depended on it, but I learned so much from my project failures, and each project looked and played better. I made my first custom BS for The Dragoon (not the Goon I'm working on now - it had original tiles and a very simple 1v1 custom BS). I worked on The Dragoon (old still) for a few months, morphed it into The Ranger and worked on it for another few months, then morphed it into The Wanderer when I decided to redo the BS. Most of you probably remember The Wanderer, a project that is anything but dead and something I plan to finish next year. People were excited about the project - it had good original graphics, and nice custom 1v1 BS going for it... and I think people would still love to see it done (which again, it will be finished, as I promised :D). Then late 2003, early 2004, I created Revival Gaming. At that time (this was about a month before TKZ became official TK site and TK went OS), there was a drought of games. Everyone was waiting for a stable TK3, at least that was the excuse. 2003 saw not even a dozen released games I would guess. It was crap. So I created RG, and I think most will remember RG... but we had it going pretty well. I created The Dragoon (the new one) basically just to have something to do for the group, and to live by the "smaller is better" concept we had going, I used ripped tiles. A decision that I regret constantly. For years I had hated ripped tiles. It was cheating - it was only making half a game - it was taking any originality the game could have away - it was an instant killer to any project. Well, my opinion had dimmed in intensity I guess you could say, so I did it anyway. And I admit, it was MUCH easier making the game with ripped graphics. But I feel guilty. Like I openly told people that used them back in 2002, it was like cheating. It was like only making half a game. And in a way, I still feel like that. And yet here I am working like mad on a project with ripped tiles. TK really isn't that big of a part of my life anymore fortunately, but in some ways, using ripped tiles in Goon is the biggest dilemma of my life right now. On one hand I just want to finish the game - the general concept of the game isn't really what I want to do anymore (never really was - Goon was suppose to be a small project just to promote RG), on the other hand, well - I feel guilty as hell for using ripped tiles. What really gets me is people that think the first demo for Goon was incredible because they don't realize I didn't make the tiles. And people outside of TK thinking that I made the tiles. Or showing it to my brother and at first he thinks it's great cause I made the tiles, but then I have to tell him I didn't. It just doesn't feel like I'm making a complete game... like it's not completely my game. Everytime I see an indie RPG game using original tiles, be it a TK game or VB game or C++ game... I feel guilty for using ripped tiles. And simply... it sucks. A few weeks ago I was debating what to do with this all this... should I just ditch Goon? Should I create original tiles and, for complicated reasons, redo the boards I had already done? Should I just keep working on the game as it is and just finish it as fast as I could and move on? For now, the latter is what I'm basically doing. I still haven't decided if I'll be doing original tiles after demo 2 is done, but for now, I'm just focusing on getting this new demo out. We'll see what happens... For anyone who just read all this... idiot. :)

25 May, 2005
I've always been a little bitter towards some people. And I've always been a little bitter towards the whole damn community in a way. Why? Well, I've always felt that we don't give enough interest to TK games. We don't give the attention and time that TK games and their developers deserve. I mean, people spend a lot of time on their games. For some, over a hundred hours. So when they finally release something, either be it a demo or full release, I think we should at least be willing to download the game and give it a shot. Some people do - some people have played dozens of TK games. Most people fall into the catagory of playing only a handful of games since they've been in the community. And I've never liked that. Well, in a way I still don't, but now I'm going to be much more forgiving. Why? I've always known TK games haven't been that entertaining. Most just aren't fun. A lot of them seem like a chore to play at times. And I've always known that people are both lazy and busy. So, I'm finally going to put the two facts together and come to the conclusion that it isn't as much the community's fault that there isn't interest in TK games as much as it being the actual creators of the games. Because their games just ain't fun. Some are, like Dustball and Dark Age, and consequentially, a lot of the community have at least downloaded the games. And I also know that actually making a TK game is very hard. Most of us are all alone, and of course, it's just a hobby for all of us. So now if it's not the community's fault or the developers' fault, whose fault is it? No ones; it's just the way it goes I guess. So until the games start getting better, the community's interest in the 90% of the TK games is going to remain generally low. And I guess that's how it should be and will be. And to quote one of my favorite movies ever... and that's all I have to say about that. (who knows the movie?) Please don't yell at me again... =P
22 May, 2005
I remember talking to Indy about the general TK community and the games and everything, and we were basically thinking of a way to improve it. Sounds cheesy and whatever you want to say, but that's what we were doing. And Indy thought of the expo idea. Of course, I went crazy and called it a stroke of genius because, in many ways, it was. But really, the TK Expo isn't much at all, thanks to a few stupid mistakes. First of all, the community doesn't understand that content isn't going to magically appear in their morning cereal bowls the morning before the expo. And it's not really that they just don't do work, but they need to understand that the Expo would be MUCH more exciting if it wasn't just screenshots that we saw the preceding week in the screenshots forum or story info that's been collecting dust in a game profile for the past six months. STOP POSTING SCREENSHOTS! Save them for the damn expo for God's sake. Don't release your game just a few weeks before the expo either. Come on guys, the content provided by the community makes the expo, not the fancy forums. It's too long too. It should be a week long. There really isn't THAT much to see people, and a game expo is suppose to be a huge rush of brand new content and games. I know you can't follow the same rules as game expos that take place in real life, but if you want to capture the huge level of excitement that events like E3 deliver for the commercial gaming world, you can't be dragging it on for a month when even the biggest of "real-life" expos is only three days long. Sure, the current TK Expo is better than no expo, but it's depressing to see it fail to reach its potential. Sorry, I just had to get that out.
