09 February, 2005
Ok, I recently read a post on one of the message boards asking a few simple questions. The problem is, a few of the questions could have been answered by the person asking, if they opened their baby blue eyes, and looked. I have found this to be a treand with a lot of new members who ask questions like "Where's the item editor", "How do I make an animation", "Where's the bathroom?". We're not their parents. They should go out and acctually try to do something, and not make people do it for them. I've noticed this in one of my classes where I was in a position where I could help the teacher with answering questions about the software that we were using. There were a few people, particularly one, who constantly asked me for help with things that were super simple to figure out, or wanted me to do the thing for them. So, I've made a list of things that people should and shouldn't do when asking questions about something they don't know about, or just want to ask a question. 1) If you are shown how to do something, pay attention. If there are a few small details that you forget how to do, then that's cool, I don't expect you to remember everything. But if you want to be shown how to do the same thing again, and again, then that gets annoying. Chances are, your question will be ignored, or you would be told to figure it out yourself. 2) Your problem isn't the biggest problem. If you can wait, then you will wait. Unless someone is available. Then, chances are, you will be helped. 3) Don't be rude. If you have a problem, don't try to distract the person who you're asking while they are doing something which is important to them. Also, if they give you a critique, and you completly blow up in their face, then you probably didn't deserve their time. If someone gives you a critique on a drawing, or etc, then that's time that they are spending on you. If you ask for a critique, don't expect them to say "OMG! THAT ROXXORZ! I'M GOING TO HANG IT ON MY WALL AND MAKE A SHRINE TO THE WORK, AND IT'S ARTIST!". That's not going to happen, unless they are being sarcastic. If they say that something like the image needs a ton of work, and they give good ideas on how to fix it, don't try to make them feel bad about themselves. Chances are, you'll make yourself look like an idiot. 4) Lastly, before you ask, try figuring out what is wrong yourself. If you are asking how do you do something, try it first. If that doesn't work, then ask a friend near you. If that doesn't work, ask someone, like a teacher, who probably knows how to do what you are asking very well. If you're trying to find out what you could do to make something better, try thinking of how first (self critique). If you expect to have someone sit at your desk, and teach you/ work on your project in your future career, then you've got it all wrong. There aren't going to be people who will just sit around helping you with everything, or do the work for you. You're expected to know what you're doing, and if you don't know, then experement first. If that doesn't work, then ask. Don't ask before. So, if you read this, and continue to ask questions that could be answered just by looking around for yourself, then I may just go nuts and direct you to this rant.
