12 October, 2005
In a game, I was able to obtain a powerful, enchanted sword that I wanted to use immediately. Though I'll be damned if I can equip it without a "Your sword skill is required to be 246 to equip this item.", when my sword skill is 100. So what? I know how to use the sword I already have. I even have the sufficient strength to wield it. But the game is treating me like if I have never used one before. The sword skill could be used to improve your chances of hitting, but to know how to use a sword is enough to use that new sword. Too bad people are adding these ridiculous requirements to games. This problem irritated me while I was playing Anarchy Online, when I was unable to equip a certain pistol just because my pistol and fling shot skills did not meet the requirements. I could spend my skill points on them, but they are starting to take up more skill points to increase. And I had to increase other skills too, which costs plenty of skill points too. Well I believe there are certain exceptions for this kind of feature; for example when a warrior with 8 strength is attempting to wield an enourmous sword that requires about 16 strength. Well those are my views, feel free to agree or disagree. In other news, I was able to complete Myst and Riven. It is because of my experience of playing Myst when I was little. I had to use a game guide for Riven since it was more complex. It may be due to how I have to keep in mind what happened in all five islands. In other Myst games, you only have to mind what is going on in that Age. So that when you go to another Age, you can just forget about the previous Age and keep in mind what may be important later. Well I am not sure how to describe it. Now I'm planning to play Myst 3 without using a game guide.
11 August, 2005
There are good games, and there are bad games. And some game developers added fake reviews of their bad games in magazines, their websites, etc, just to get the customer to think it's a good game. Here's one for Spec Ops: Red Mercury: "...the 'surprise' hit of the Summer" Xbox Magazine "...one summer bockbuster that you'll want to be part of..." TeamXbox.com "The Xbox's first-person shooter lineup will be one game stronger this summer..." Gamespot.com "...intense action and cinematic flair in every square inch of the game." GameInformer.com "...rises above the glut of first-person shooters..." Gamespot.com Gamespot gave it a 6.8 and EGM gave it a 4.0, 4.5, 4.0. So someone might just look at that advertisement and think this game is awesome just because it looked like it was getting good reviews. How about I of the Dragon? When I looked at the game box, I thought it was a good game since a piece of Gamespot's review was on there. But I went ahead on the Internet and checked for the actual review, and Gamespot gave it a 6.0. More lies. So how about Shellshock: Nam '67? A review from CGW is on the box. But checking on Computer Gaming World's website, they actually gave that game a 1.5. For those people who might be game designers in the future, don't put fake reviews on your game box. Even if it's just for profit, you're getting a baaad reputation.

I think the part where we add these humanoid races in fantasy rpgs/stories/etc is overused. Stories like Harry Potter, Eragon, Lord of the Rings, there's always an elf. The only problem about I have about humanoid races is that it is seems to be a cliche. I'm always expecting dwarves, elves, gnomes, orcs, etc. in every fantasy game. Some MMORPGs even take animals like frogs (Everquest), lizards (Everquest again), some bull creature (WoW), felines (Horizons), etc. and make them into humanoid characters. I'm not saying fantasy games and stories should not make these humanoid beings, but it is starting to be a cliche. Another of my viewpoints brought to you by Neol.
