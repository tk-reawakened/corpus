Yeah, 3rd may is polish shity constitution day and today we had reminder in school. We also had a speech by 2 members of Siberiak Society (or however to translate that), people who survived being sent to Sibir (or Kazachstan) my the commies. It wasn't as bad as US Patriot would portrait that - when you got arrested, you had 20 minutes to pack all the stuff you want and in wagons, drive to the destination. There, you had to work - wasn't bad at spring (especially in Kazachstan), altough winters were harsh. Usually, forest cutting or work in kolhoz'es - collective government farms. The ideology was simple and fitting with communistic "labor race" - each day you had to fill the norm. Those who didn't fill the norm got rotten bread, soup from some shit and water. Those who did got better soup, regular bread, some potatos and water. Those who worked ABOVE the norm got sausages and vegetables. Pretty motivating, huh? Children were forced to work from age of 13 years (that's a bit harsh, altough children below 13 were provided "social care" and could even go to schools in the local area near where their parents were forced to work). Majority of deaths was from cold (obviously, -40 C isn't nice temperature to work at cutting trees) and during transport (women and children, mostly). And that was during stalinism, the most harsh period of communism ever. After Stalin's death, the siberian prisoners were released (amnesty), altough it was still "secret" and well hidden that such gulags ever existed until the fall of USSR. Honestly, if some basic reforms to the system would be made, like: 1. Informing the arrested about what will await them and giving them 1-2 hours to fully preppare, informing about taking more clothes and food 2. Increasing labor age to 18 from 13 3. Estabilishing norms based on medical observation (lower for the younger and the women) ...then it would sound so fucking humane that it would beat american prison system where prisoners are beaten and raped. Honestly. Big Brother says... Do not crush your enemies: Make them work for you.
