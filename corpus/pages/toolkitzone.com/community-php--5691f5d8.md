**The only thing more annoying than...** [Colin](community.php?blogs&user=226)comments
The only thing more annoying than clich?d logic puzzles is people who present said clich?d puzzles without knowing the right answer and then belittle you when you give it. A common problem tells of a story where three people visit an inn where rent will be $30 for the group. They decide to split the fee evenly, paying $10 each. But a discount is available, they learn, after paying the money. This week it's only $25. So the teller refunds them $1 each and they let him keep the remaining $2 as a tip. The recounter of this puzzle will then say something along the lines of: multiply the amount each of the renters paid ($9) by the number of renters to obtain $27 and then add in the tip, $2, to obtain $29 of money in somebody's hands. But where did the $1 that came in go? The answer is, of course, that it went nowhere; the final calculation makes absolutely no sense. Supposing the renters and teller both had no other money, the renters each hold $1 at the conclusion of the story (for a total of $3) and the teller holds $27, the amount they paid. Even Marc could add $3 and $27 to find that no money disappeared. But yet people approach this problem as though it were altogether mysterious and worthy of my time. And then, after I give them my brilliant and succinct solution (as presented here), they tell me I'm wrong and spout off garbage about $5 not being divisible by three. Shocker. So, if you ever feel the need to waste someone's time with a clich?d logic puzzle, please do them a favour and be able to recognise the correct response.
