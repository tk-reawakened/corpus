**The possibilities are not endless** [Colin](community.php?blogs&user=226)comments
Let us define a "possibility" as a list of the position of each elementary particle within the universe. The "state" of each particle (e.g. its speed) is irrelevant because such state data could not be expressed relativistically in any non-self-referential manner and because its only use would be to determine what possibilities follow if the model were allowed to "run". The number of possibilities is clearly directly related to the number of elementary particicles in the universe (finite: see the second law of thermodynamics) and the number of positions in which each elementary particle can exist (finite: see Hubble's Law, but Delano has pointed out that we need to postulate a quantised universe to make the connection here) and it is related only to these things. I therefore conclude that the number of possibilities is finite, and ergo, not endless, as the popular phrase would have us believe.

The actual universe is, quite possibly, endless. Particles may possibly extend infinitely in space. Thus, how far they go is limited by their speed and factors influencing them. Considering the time, however, is limited between Big Bang and the thermal death of the universe, they may never expand to a maximum theoretically possible distance.
