I'm Korean and we have a saying in Korea that says you only argue with those you care about. But the people who thought that up are the same people who said that you can only be hurt by those you love. If that were the case, there's too much love going around. And in the psychology of a relationship, it generally goes that you heat up relationships and cool down pretty bad then meet in the half point. It is also theorized that you usually get to know a person AFTER the third month of regular meetings and contact with them. But this varies with age. Generally, the younger you are, the shorter the time periods are... the older you are, the longer the time periods are. I say with that in mind, you talk to her with a cool head. You must keep in mind that there's always two people in your relationship. It's you and your girlfriend. But keep in mind whether or not she's the type who makes up her mind or wants a bit of fight for it. Speaking as a girl myself, I know I'm the type who says she's made a decision but can be swayed if the persuasion was good enough. There are girls who are completely the opposite. But I'm glad you're trying to talk to her... just maybe you need to let HER cool off a bit before contacting her. Attempt at friendly conversations and inch your way towards the more pressing subjects. Tell her how you feel in the end. Don't rush the 'I love you's. They can feel very forced in situations like this. But give it a go. If you're willing to fight for this relationship to work, you'll probably make it work, given that she's willing also. Good luck.

I know the situation quite well, actually. It's happened before, and Stephanie and I got through it. It's just something you both have to work through. I guess you could say it's a phase. Stephanie and I had this period of time were arguments were rampant and often led to a lot of annoyance and anger. One thing that helped us was just spending less time together, and generally just talking about it, and figuring out how we could communicate better. Like you mentioned, you'd think it makes you stronger, and I believe it does as well. We thought our relationship was fairly strong beforehand, and it was, but there was a lot of things we didn't know about each other, and that's something that's gotten much better. So, my advice is to talk together and figure out what you can do to improve yourselves, and I actually even recommend spending less time together, but more time talking. Eventually you'll both just miss each other to death. And if it helps you out, Stephanie felt we should break up too, but look - we got through that. Sorry about the long comment.
