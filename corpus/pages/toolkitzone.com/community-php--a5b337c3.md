# Blogs

by (4 , 57 views) Sunday 4.20am, January 28th 2007
**Blagging in my blog** [Asimir](community.php?blogs&user=310)comments
Not very long ago, Megazone - the company that sold us our laser tag equipment and sells us spare parts - closed their headquarters and the owners buggered off. This sucks for any site using Megazone equipment, but it especially sucked for the Leicester arena, which is in the same building as the Megazone headquarters. They ended up with their electricity being cut off because nobody was paying for it any more. Anyway, about a week after this happened, Quasar sent out letters to all Megazone sites advertising their latest equipment, Quasar Elite. I've played at two Quasar Elite sites before, and the equipment really sucks. The games are much less customisable than with Megazone's equipment - Infusion (though Infusion is still very restrictive. More on that later) - and the basic game has loads of annoying things which can't be disabled, such as reflex shots, two-stage deactivation periods, recharging and special weapons for hitting people on the gun five times in a row. These make the game less fun and harder to explain. If we switched equipment, we'd lose many of our members instantly (I know a lot of them have used Quasar Elite and hated it too) and we'd have to raise our minimum age because the younger kids just wouldn't understand. We'd obviously also have to pay a load of money to make the switch in the first place. We did phone around some other Megazone sites though, to ask if they were planning on switching, and if they were, if we could buy their old equipment. None of them were switching though. Megazone is just part of Zone Empire, which is based in Australia, so some people came over from there to try to sort out the situation. Those people were the owner of P&C Micros - the company which owns Zone Empire - and a guy called Paul who used to live in this town (long before we opened the arena), then moved to Leicester (where Megazone hq was). He recently got a job with Zone Empire and went to Australia, but now he's had to come back. (He came back just in time for our Christmas All-Nighter, and he brought some lovely prizes for the tournament) The reason Paul was in Australia was because he was working on the new Megazone system, Nexus. Now that him and the company owner are here in England, they're finishing the system here. They had a stand at the ATEI exhibition at London to show off the new equipment. This was the day after my sister's birthday. Since my sister's at university in London, we went down for two days to celebrate her birthday and visit the ATEI exhibition. The exhibition was mostly of arcade machines, so I got to go on some of the latest arcade machines for free, which was fun. I got also some free hot chocolate from a hot drinks dispenser. The Megazone stand was small, but the new equipment was interesting. The pack design was good, though I don't like the new guns, but the main thing that interested me is that the games are coded in Python and can be written by site operators. With the current system, Infusion, you have a list of precoded games and all you can change is a few settings. The two basic games both have a lot of settings, which let you change things like gun speeds, lives and deactivation times for different teams, and settings for bases and other miscellaneous stuff. This gives you a lot more control than with other systems (like Quasar Elite) but you're still very restricted, and most game ideas can't be implemented. Although we won't be getting Nexus at least for a few years, I'm (probably) going to learn Python so I can (maybe) write some games for the beta Nexus site which will be at Leicester in a month or so. Whether I actually do make games or not depends on how much trial and error it'll take for me to write them. We took a bunch of brochures from arcade machine companies too, since we're thinking of buying our own machines. If we do, we'll set up a new company, which I'd be the main owner of, then trade between the two companies. We worked out that if we buy the same machines that we already have, with the average money we'd make from them, we'd have them paid off in about a year, not considering repairs. We'll want to get different machines though, since having the same machines all the time is boring, but we should still make the money back in much less than two years. The kitchen at work seems to be dying right now. One of the freezers stopped freezing, so we've had to stuff everything in the other freezer, which will probably cause it to break too. The pizza warmer exploded a couple of weeks ago, just as I was about to start cooking for seven parties. (or, to be more accurate, the switch on the pizza warmer exploded) so I had to blu-tac it together until I had time to fix it properly :/ There are other things I could blog about, but this is already too long, so I'll leave it at this.
