**Coffee Developer** [Ersuis](community.php?blogs&user=34)comments
Some time ago I came across telling how to create black and white film developer using items you could get at the grocery store. I mulled it over for a few months, and finally on Tuesday I set into motion the steps necessary to experiment with coffee developer. This evening I finally conducted the experiment. The Shutterbug recipe calls for eight ounces of water, four teaspoons of coffee crystals, and two teaspoons of sodium carbonate (washing soda). For the coffee I used Spartan brand regular instant coffee, it being the cheapest coffee available at the store (for good reason; it's not any good to drink). I had trouble finding washing soda, but reading materials online led me to believe that Borax could be used as a substitute. This was mixed at 100F (38C). Developer was stopped using cold water baths, and the film was fixed for three minutes using Kodak Rapid Fixer diluted 1:4. I used Kodak 400TX (formerly Tri-X) type 135 rated at ISO 100 for the experiment. My test photos were shot on a sunny day with different subjects and manual exposure. The developer was used straight from the mixing container at 100F (38C). A development time of 30 minutes with tank inversions every fifteen seconds was used. The test was successful, but not as much as I would have liked. Through most of the film clear lines were visible, indicating that the exposed frames had begun to develop. Several frames begin to show the subject, most notable an image of a stop sign. However, they were all too thin to print. Obviously a stronger developer is needed. The obvious variable to change is the Borax; sodium carbonate, as opposed to sodium perborate (Borax), is the chemical of choice for most experiments. Development time could also be increased, but I'd rather not spend an hour or even two hours developing a roll of film. A higher water temperature could also be used, but I worry about the effects it will have on the emulsion. Finally, a stronger coffee mix could be used. From what I understand, caffeine is the active agent in the mix. I don't understand the chemistry of the process very much, but apparently caffeine has properties similar to the developing agents in manufactured developers. In addition, coffee (and tea) have tannins which support development. Finally, the Borax (or other basic substance, like baking soda or washing soda) is used to keep the mixture as an alkali. Interesting enough, vitamin C can also be used to create a developer; there's quite a bit of information online about doing so. The final link I give under the "other resources" explains this in slightly more detail (and, it just so happens, is where I learned this from).
