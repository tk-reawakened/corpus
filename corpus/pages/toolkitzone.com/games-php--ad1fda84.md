# Stats and Personality: How will they affect my horse?

How things like stats and personality will affect training and showing.
**Stats and Personality: How will they affect my horse?**
A lot, that's how. I'll divide this page into two parts to show you.
**-Stats**
As of now, I'm not entirely sure which stats I'll have in the game. Things like speed, agility, disipline, and strength will definitely be included, along with a few others. Everything starts at zero, and you train up in certain areas to increase them. What increases what, you ask? Well, that's up to you to find out, isn't it?
In showing, I want to have certain stats averaged together to decide the winner. However, I don't know if this is possible yet. If not, I'll just use those stats as are and whoever has the highest wins (though I'd have to narrow it down to one stat). You will be competing against numerous NPC horses, whose owners you can meet in town. Sometimes an owner will even form a rivalry with you, but that's off the subject. Back on subject, another way I may judge shows is by a level system. I may instead have you train a horse in a certain area and give levels rather than points (please comment on this page which you'd like better: point stats or level stats). I'm not sure which yet; I think the level stats would be easier to code but harder to figure out how to make work the way I'd like. And that was a run on sentence, I think. Anyway, stats aren't the only thing that affects your horse's performance...
**Personality-**
Every horse has a "Sims Style" personality (selected randomly at the start of the game), meaning it has a stat out of ten for each personality stat. Example: Shy- 8/10. That means this particular horse is pretty darned shy. I'll may have stats as the following, but it's definitely open to change: Shyness, Inteligence, Friendliness, Grace, Lazyness, and Toughness. Again, definitely open to change. How will these affect shows and training? Well, you certainly don't want a shy racehorse. That's just asking for trouble, and in real life it would be asking for someone to get hurt. Your driving horse should be tough. Your dressage horse should be smart and gaceful, as should your cutting horse. "But what," you're asking, "do I do with a horse?" Well, in actuality, you don't want a hyper dressage horse. Noooooooo way! You don't want a hyper halter horse, either. You do, however, want a hyper racehorse. And now you ask, "What about a mean horse?" Well, that one's slightly tougher. There isn't anything that mean-ness helps, no way. But there are things that it doesn't affect as much. Racing, for example. A mean horse is usually a competitive one, meaning it won't affect racing. It will affect halter, for instance, though. You aren't going to get a good score if your horse snaps at the judge. Nah-ah. Friendliness doesn't really help anything much, except for maybe halter. *lazy*
Well there you have it. A little write-up on personality and stats. Remeber, though, that everything is subject to change and nothing is cast in stone until the game is realeased (which, at this rate, won't be too much longer! Yay!). I hope you enjoyed this, and I hope it was a good preview to the only horse simulation game (maybe the only simulation game at all) on TKZ, The Horse Smell!
