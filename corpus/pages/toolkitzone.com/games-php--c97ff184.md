**Tack of the Trade**
What tack there is, and how it'll help you
**Introduction -**
I'm not going to give you all the answers here - after all, that's where all the fun its! - however, I will tell you what each saddle & bridle is, exactly, so you can figure it out even you aren't a horse-savy person. ;-) I may drop a hint here or there, too, so pay attention to what you read.
**Dressage Saddle -**
This is a saddle made for dressage (duh). Dressage is like dancing on horseback, I guess. It's very refined and percise; your aids (signals to the horse) cannot be seen, yet the horse must be able to perform amazing, advanced movements such as peruettes(sp?), half-passes, shoulder-ins, and much more. The dressage saddle has a flat flap, since your legs won't be as bent as they would be if you were jumping. It also has a deep seat, to help keep the rider sitting straight and tall.
**Hunter/Jumper Saddle -**
This saddle is made for jumping (again, duh). It has a flatter seat than the dressage saddle, and the flap is FAR more exaggerated to give the rider plenty of room for the bent leg he/she will need for jumping.
- **Racing Saddle**
Made for racing. Duh. Very small (VERY small), very light (VERY light), and made of little more than a layer of thin leather. Extremely exaggerated flap, because the rider is practically crouching on the horse when sitting (watch a horse race - or even for that matter - and you'll see what I mean). Built to weigh as little as possible, as the horse needs to go fast. *Seabiscuit*
**Saddleseat Saddle -**
I don't know as much about this saddle... it's made for saddleseat, which is when gaited horses strut their stuff and lift their legs as high as possible, and the rider wears that long tailcoat-ish thing, and I can't think of any better way to explain, so sue me. And what a run-on sentence that was, huh? The saddleseat saddle is very very flat, and also has a straight flap like a dressage saddle. I think it's designed to keep the rider in his/her place during the bouncy trots without getting bruised in the backside, but again I don't know much about this saddle.
Those are all the english styled saddles. I'll add the western-style saddles later, as I'm sick of writing right now. *weak smile*
