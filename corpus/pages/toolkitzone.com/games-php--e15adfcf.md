# Browse

Posted on by (24 views, 4 ) Special Features
**Saturday 6.06am, November 26th 2005** **[7LegionArmy](community.php?user=7614)**comments
Just wanted show that The Lost Epic will hopefully have a few unique features that set it apart from other games.
So everybody knows about my graphics but what about the game. What is the story behind these characters who's bad, who's good, who's who. Well in all actuality there are no villians or heroes in The Lost Epic. Sure there is a protagonist and an antagonist. But that doesn't neccassarily mean their heroes and villains. The main character Absalon De'Adluss. A boy loses his home, the basic and generic beggining but thats what sets him on his path. He is investigating the destruction of his home. In this time period Absalon is unstable, he is becoming obsessed with this. He is willing to kill innocent lives and even kill his friends if neccessary to uncover the truth behind the destruction of his home. He meets many characters through out the game, who help him to do this. In the process he uncovers a much more twisted conspiracy. The destruction of his home only a plays a small part. Even discovering that he is a pawn in a plot himself. In the begging of the game Absalon murders a Cadmus Vilehart a seer. When a person takes the life of a seer he gains their power. Absalon gains this power which puts him in position to be used by those in power. The stage shifts when Absalon goes rogue agiant those who would use him. The antagonists, include Arch Bishop Vladislas Valasko, several nobility and Parliament Representatives. Also Including members of the elusive and mysterious cult Merlincroft. The Arch Bishop is the main antagonist. The Arch Bishop is delusional yet, charasamatic, inspiring, caring, giving, selfless at times. He is the main figure in the conspiracy which I won't give away. The game primarily evolves around the human element, human nature, corrupt or elevated. That human beings can do great or terrible things. As Absalon discovers, through his own actions and the actions of others. You decide the fate of a man who can do great or terrible things. What about Game Features. A. One of my all time favorite ideas the TENT. The Tent in the BS acts as a player. When the TENTS counter reaches 0 a plaer is allowed in the TENT. Inside the TENT a character can. 1. Heal Themself 2. Restock Items 3. Change Equipment 4. Repair Equipment (That is if you have the Blacksmith) In the Battle menu there is a option called Escape this allows the Character to escape to the TENT. Where they will stay. They can be allowed back in battle but that takes up the TENTS turn. Characters can still enter the TENT even when a escaped character is in the TENT. B. Special Battle Menu. Which options include 1. (The character points and clicks using the cursor. A character's range of movement depends on their class, level and speed. 2. (Just the basic attack, depending on the job class, a character can attack twice) 3. (The Name of the skill such as Elemental Sword or Holy Sword is seen not Use Skill. Skills need Sp, Sp is gained by attacking normaly. Dependig on your level and job class more or less Sp can be gained by your normal attacks.) 4. (If you have a magic wielding job class, you can cast spells. Magic takes MP and has CT, Casting Time. Depending on the size of the spell will determine the spells CT) 5. (This simply allows the player to check the selcted characters stats) 6. (This allows the charcter to escape to the TENT where they cannot return to battle until the TENT counter reaches Zero. C. A Little extra fun are the Skill Types. There are three. 1. (An attack skill is a skill that simply does some sort of damage to the target, for example SEVEN SWORDS ADD: Posion, Sleep and Petrify. 2. (Support skills are skills that give the character a plus stat or a extra boost. For instance, IGNORE GRAVITY ADD: Float. 3. ( Reaction Skills only work when triggered and their success rate is determined by your LUCK. For example SCROOGE HEART damage recieved equals money gained. Trigger: Hp Damage.
