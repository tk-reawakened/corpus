Over time, pipes degrade and fall into disrepair no matter how much you've worked to keep them in good shape. Either they freeze or they simply wear down over time and become a liability to your home. Whatever the cause, it's a fact that you will have to deal with failing pipes as a regular part of owning a home. When your pipes or any other part of your plumbing system needs to be worked on, it's important to take it very seriously. There are far too many risks associated with failing pipes for you to move slowly on getting these issues taken care of.
The Dangers
It is widely understood that water damage is both common and extremely damaging to a home. When there is a problem with your plumbing, your shower, your faucet or any other part of your water system it can lead to massive problems. This is because water can get into just about anything and it can cause . Even a small problem with your pipes or with the seal on your bathtub can become an enormous issue very quickly. Unfortunately, many homeowners don't always understand just how problematic water damage can be. Mold, rot and corrosion are the three most common issues that come up with water damage. Mold is a natural agent of decomposition, which means that it will degrade natural fibers like the wood in your walls. However, it is also physically dangerous and can cause numerous issues for your respiratory health if you are exposed to it in large quantities. Even when wood does not become moldy, water can cause it to become soft and rot away very quickly. Depending on the type of minerals in your water, it can even cause metal to begin corroding.
Preventative and Reactive Pipe Repair
When it comes to repairing pipes, there are generally two classes people refer to. These classes are "preventative" and "reactive" pipe repair. They reflect the different situations in which people generally look for pipe repair services. While they are indeed two opposing sides of the same issue, they are also not mutually exclusive. In fact, most homeowners will be for both preventative and reactive service at some point. However, it is generally better to be searching for preventative service instead of reactive service. [in the market](http://www.esfs.org/pipe-repair)
Preventative Pipe Repair
As the name would suggest, preventative pipe repair defines work that you do on your pipes to keep them from failing. In these situations, you may be doing regular yearly maintenance on seals or replacing a section of pipe that you notice is starting to create issues. This is work that you do to help prevent failure in your plumbing system. Regular repair work is often less expensive than emergency repair work, so this can be a great way to ensure long-term savings. When you do preventative work on a regular basis, you are doing a lot to help ensure that you won't have to engage in reactive repair very often.
Reactive Pipe Repair
Reactive repair is repair work that you commission during an emergency. If a pipe bursts during the winter and creates a mess, you would be forced to bring out a contractor with a greater level of immediacy. Reactive pipe repair is a last resort that you will have to deal with every now and then since you can't prevent every disaster. However, it's important to do everything you can to avoid it. Not only is this type of emergency repair work significantly more expensive, but it's also much more stressful on you.
