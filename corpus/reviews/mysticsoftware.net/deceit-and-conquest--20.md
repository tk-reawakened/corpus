# Deceit and Conquest

These reviews do not reflect the opinions of Mystic Software and/or it's staff, and we will not be held responsible for the content of the reviews.
> *Review by Rueben*
> Proclaimed by some as the greatest RPG Toolkit game of all time. Certainly D&C does not lose marks for scope and ambition, but does the game live up to the legend? Chapter 1 of the game is perhaps one of the most slated toolkit games of recent times. Not undeservedly so, and I will get the negatives about the game out of the way first. Graphically, D&C has received the most critism. While not terribly bad, there is a little left to be desired by the bland textures and environments. But this is certainly not a point I would rate the game down for. The main point would be the RPG elements. The battles feel absurdly easy and added as though they were an aferthought, and rarely do you feel like your character is developing stats-wise. The limited number of enemies only compounds this problem. However, explore the game further and you find that the game really is worth your time and effort. While some of the graphics are a little dry, the sprites themselves and the in-game cinematic shots are dripping with style and substance. Certainly not to everybody's tastes, but very well done. However, where this game really shines is the story. The epic, which revolves around two warring nations, focuses primarily on Hal Geran, a general for his own nation, the empire of Seor Yul. Geran has lots of character and you warm to him quickly when he interacts with other less-competent soldiers. The systems in D&C are brilliantly done, with a well done custom battle system and a nice-looking menu system. The message window also is unique, which can be set to either mouse-based or keyboard-based. These help take away the feeling of a 'thrown together' game which those that use the default systems tend to have about them. The music is hugely atmospheric, particularly the main theme tune which was composed by the talented Chris Chiu alongside Randy. It all feels well-placed and works wonderfully, producing one of the best soundtracks in any Toolkit game. Overall, this game is perhaps a little too deep for a lot of players, who may prefer to take the better RPG and combat elements and sacrifice such a good story. But for those that can overlook some initial problems, there is a game in here that will live in memory for a long time to come. Pros: - Original and deep storyline - Unique style Cons: - Poor RPG elements
